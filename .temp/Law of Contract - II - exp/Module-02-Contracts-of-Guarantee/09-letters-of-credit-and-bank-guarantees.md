## 📘 Letters of Credit and Bank Guarantees

🎚️ Difficulty: 🔴 Hard

**Statute / Rules:** Contract Act guarantee chapters + **commercial practice**; UCP/contract terms (course-dependent)

---

### 🧠 Introduction

**Letter of credit (LC)** is a **bank’s undertaking** to pay seller against documents (not typically analysed purely as ICA “guarantee,” but syllabus links them). **Bank guarantee (BG)** is often **independent** undertaking: bank pays on **demand** if conditions met, **de-linked** from underlying dispute (subject to **fraud exception**).

---

### 📖 Main Content

#### 🔹 Letter of credit

Parties: **applicant, issuing bank, beneficiary**. Payment against **strict compliance** of documents. Principle: **autonomy** of banking engagement.

#### 🔹 Bank guarantee

Types: **performance guarantee**, **financial guarantee**, **advance payment guarantee**. Keywords: **unconditional**, **on first demand**—courts still police **fraud**.

#### 🔹 Relationship to Sections 126–147

Academic questions: BG may be **guarantee-like** but often treated as **independent** contract—examiners may ask “is it contract of guarantee under Section 126?” Discuss **tripartite** vs **bank’s independent promise**.

#### 🔹 Fraud exception

**Hindustan Construction Co. v. Satluj Jal Vidyut Nigam** line (verify spelling)—injunction against encashment only on **established fraud** or **egregious** grounds as per case law your faculty teaches.

---

### 🧩 Elements / Principles

1. **Autonomy principle** in LC/BG.  
2. **Strict compliance** of documents.  
3. **Fraud** as narrow exception to payment.
