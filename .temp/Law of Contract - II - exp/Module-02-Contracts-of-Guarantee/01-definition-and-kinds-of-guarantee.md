## 📘 Definition of Contract of Guarantee and Kinds of Guarantee

🎚️ Difficulty: 🟡 Medium

**Statute:** Sections 126–127, Indian Contract Act, 1872

---

### 🧠 Introduction

A **contract of guarantee** is a contract to **perform the promise** or **discharge the liability** of a **third person** in case of his **default** (Section 126). It is **tripartite**: **surety**, **principal debtor**, and **creditor**. The surety’s promise is **collateral** to the principal obligation.

**Kinds** commonly examined: **specific guarantee** (one debt) vs **continuing guarantee** (series of transactions—Section 129); **fiduciary vs commercial** phrasing may appear in advanced notes.

---

### 📖 Main Content

#### 🔹 Section 126 essentials

- **Three parties** with distinct roles  
- **Surety** undertakes liability **if principal debtor defaults**  
- **Consideration** must move from creditor or debtor contexts as per Section 127

#### 🔹 Kinds

1. **Specific / simple guarantee** — covers a **single** loan or fixed obligation.  
2. **Continuing guarantee** (Section 129) — extends to **series** of transactions until **revoked** as per law.

#### 🔹 Fidelity guarantee (course context)

Employee dishonesty bonds—often discussed as **guarantee** products (fact-pattern).

---

### 🧩 Elements / Principles

1. **Collateral promise** — not primary debt promise by surety in the same sense as debtor’s primary undertaking (conceptually).  
2. **Default trigger** — central to guarantee vs indemnity.  
3. **Tripartite consent visibility** — creditor must know surety’s role (contrast **undisclosed** ideas in agency—don’t mix chapters).
