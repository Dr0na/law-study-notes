## 📘 Principle of Indemnity in General

🎚️ Difficulty: 🟢 Easy

**Statute:** Indian Contract Act, 1872 — Chapter VIII (Sections 124–125)

---

### 🧠 Introduction

**Indemnity**, in ordinary language, means **security against loss**. In commercial life, one party often promises to **make good** another’s loss if a stated event occurs (for example, loss from litigation, from acting on instructions, or from a regulatory condition). The Contract Act codifies a narrow type of indemnity contract in **Sections 124–125**, while many commercial indemnities are still governed by **general contract law** plus **wording of the clause**.

---

### 📖 Main Content

#### 🔹 General principle

The core idea is **reimbursement**: the indemnified person should not be left **out of pocket** because of the **risk** the parties allocated to the indemnifier. The allocation is **consensual** (contract), though statute fixes some incidents for the defined “contract of indemnity.”

#### 🔹 Indemnity vs insurance (conceptual)

**Insurance** is a species of indemnity in outcome (often), but **contract of indemnity** under the Act is a **private promise** between two contracting parties, not necessarily an insurance policy regulated under insurance law—useful distinction in viva.

#### 🔹 Risk allocation in commerce

Banks, landlords, publishers, and local bodies use indemnity to shift **legal exposure** (duplicate certificates, title assurances, construction permissions). Examiners like **risk + event + reimbursement** framing.

---

### 🧩 Elements / Principles

1. **Loss-based** — Liability is typically tied to **actual loss** unless contract says otherwise.
2. **Consensual allocation** — Freedom of contract subject to public policy/legality.
3. **Narrow statutory definition** — Section 124 covers promises to save from **loss caused by promisor** or **any other person** (textual reading essential in exams).

“Principle of indemnity in general” is the **policy foundation** for Sections 124–125: **contractual shifting of loss** within lawful limits, later refined by **definition, formation, extent, and commencement** topics.
