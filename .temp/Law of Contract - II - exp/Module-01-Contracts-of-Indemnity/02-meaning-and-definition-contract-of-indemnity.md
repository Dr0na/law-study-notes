## 📘 Meaning and Definition of the Contract of Indemnity

🎚️ Difficulty: 🟢 Easy

**Statute:** Section 124, Indian Contract Act, 1872

---

### 🧠 Introduction

A **contract of indemnity** is defined in **Section 124** as a contract by which one party promises to **save the other from loss** caused to the other by the **conduct of the promisor himself**, or by the **conduct of any other person**. The definition is **narrow** compared to English commercial “indemnity,” but exams require **precise reproduction** and **explanation of each element**.

---

### 📖 Main Content

#### 🔹 Parties

- **Indemnifier** — the promisor who undertakes to save from loss  
- **Indemnity-holder** / **indemnified** — the promisee whose loss is to be made good

#### 🔹 Promise must target “loss”

The promise is to **save from loss**, not merely to perform a service unrelated to loss. However, **actual loss** issues tie to **Section 125** and **commencement** case law.

#### 🔹 Conduct element

Loss may be caused by:

1. **Promisor’s own conduct**, or  
2. **Any other person’s conduct**

This captures third-party claims (for example, litigation by a stranger affecting the indemnity-holder).

#### 🔹 Contract, not tort

The remedy foundation is **contract**; tort principles may overlap only incidentally in fact situations.

---

### 🧩 Elements / Principles

1. **Lawful consideration** and **competent parties** (general law).  
2. **Promise to save from loss** — definitional core.  
3. **Causation** must fit the contract’s words and Section 124’s frame.  
4. **Narrow statutory definition** — many bank indemnities still analysed as contracts with indemnity **clauses**.

---

### 🧾 Conclusion

Section 124 defines indemnity as a **promise to compensate loss** flowing from **promisor or third-party conduct**. Exam answers must quote **key words** and then **interpret** each element briefly.
