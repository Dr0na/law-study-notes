## 📘 Formation, Essential Features, and Parties to Bailment

🎚️ Difficulty: 🟡 Medium

**Statute:** Sections 148–149, Indian Contract Act, 1872

---

### 🧠 Introduction

Formation needs **agreement** (express/implied) plus **delivery** of goods (Section 149: how delivery made—actual or constructive). Parties: **bailor** (delivers) and **bailee** (receives). Consideration may exist (**non-gratuitous** bailment) or bailment may be **solely for one party’s benefit** affecting duty standard.

---

### 📖 Main Content

#### 🔹 Essential features

1. **Goods** identifiable  
2. **Delivery** of possession to bailee  
3. **Purpose**  
4. **Return/disposal** obligation  
5. **Contract** (including implied-in-fact)

#### 🔹 Delivery (Section 149)

Possession may pass without physical transfer if **constructive** delivery completes legal possession shift.

---

### 🧩 Elements / Principles

- **Gratuitous vs reward** bailment changes **duty of care** (Sections 151–152 cluster).  
- **Title** usually remains with bailor unless sale/hire-purchase facts.

---

### ⚖️ Case Laws

**State of Gujarat v. Memon Haji Hassan** — bailment vs agency—use if in notes.

---

### 🧾 Conclusion

Party labels and **delivery proof** decide formation disputes.
