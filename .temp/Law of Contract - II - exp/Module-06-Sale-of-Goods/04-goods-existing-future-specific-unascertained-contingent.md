## 📘 Goods: Meaning, Existing and Future, Specific, Ascertained, Unascertained, and Contingent

🎚️ Difficulty: 🟡 Medium

**Statute:** Sections 2(7), 6, 15–18, Sale of Goods Act, 1930

---

### 🧠 Introduction

**Goods** means every kind of **movable property** other than actionable claims and money (unless consideration) — **Section 2(7)**. Classifications drive **passing of property** rules (**Sections 18–25**).

---

### 📖 Main Content

#### 🔹 Existing vs future goods

**Future goods** are goods to be **manufactured/acquired** after contract (**Section 2(6)**).

#### 🔹 Specific goods

Identified and agreed upon at contract time (**Section 2(14)**).

#### 🔹 Ascertained

Goods become **ascertained** when **identified** to contract (exam language).

#### 🔹 Unascertained / generic

Sale of **10 tons** from bulk—property rules under **Section 20A** (if applicable in your amended Act) / **Section 18**—follow **current bare Act**.

#### 🔹 Contingent goods

Goods acquisition depends on **contingency** (part of future goods concept).

---

### 🧩 Elements / Principles

Property cannot pass in **unascertained** goods until **ascertainment** (default rule).

---

### ⚖️ Case Laws

**Re Wait** (English—bulk goods)—historical; compare with Indian amendments.

---

### 🧾 Conclusion

Classification decides **when buyer becomes owner**.
