# Comparison: Sale vs. Agreement to Sell

**Context:** Module 6, Sale of Goods Act, 1930.

| Basis of Difference | Sale | Agreement to Sell |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Transfer of Property**| The property (ownership) in the goods passes to the buyer **immediately** at the time of making the contract. | The property in the goods passes to the buyer at a **future time** or upon fulfillment of some conditions. |
| **Nature of Contract** | It is an **Executed Contract** (the act is done). | It is an **Executory Contract** (something remains to be done). |
| **Risk of Loss** | Risk follows ownership. The risk falls on the **buyer**, even if the goods are still in the possession of the seller. | The risk falls on the **seller**, even if the goods are in the possession of the buyer. |
| **Remedy for Breach by Buyer** | The seller can sue the buyer for the **price** of the goods. | The seller can generally only sue the buyer for **damages** for non-acceptance (not the price). |
| **Remedy for Breach by Seller**| The buyer can sue the seller for damages and can also sue the third party who bought the goods, for recovery (conversion). | The buyer can only sue the seller for **damages** for non-delivery. They cannot claim the goods. |
| **Right of Resale** | The seller has no right to resell the goods (except an unpaid seller in specific cases). | The seller can resell the goods to a third party (though liable for damages to the original buyer). |
| **Insolvency of Buyer** | If the buyer becomes insolvent before paying, the seller must deliver the goods to the Official Receiver, but can claim a dividend. | The seller can refuse to deliver the goods to the Official Receiver unless the price is paid in full. |
| **Insolvency of Seller** | If the seller becomes insolvent, the buyer can recover the goods from the Official Receiver. | The buyer cannot recover the goods, but can only claim a dividend as a creditor for any money paid. |

---
**Exam Tip:**
Always link "Risk passes with ownership". This single phrase helps derive the answers for "Risk of Loss" and "Insolvency" points.
