## 📘 Partition: Mitakshara and Dayabhaga; Subject Matter; Who May Claim; Kinds; Survivorship and Representation; Reopening and Reunion

🎚️ Difficulty: 🔴 Hard

---

### 🧠 Introduction

**Partition** under Hindu law is the **division of joint status** and **metes-and-bounds** (or by shares) separation of **joint family/coparcenary property** among those entitled. **Mitakshara** and **Dayabhaga** differ on **who** may demand partition **when**, and how **shares** are reckoned. Two allied ideas frequently tested are **survivorship** (Mitakshara classical) and **representation** (branch claims through a deceased coparcener’s line).

**Reopening** partition is exceptional (fraud, mistake, omitted property). **Reunion** is recreating joint status after partition—rare and tightly constrained.

---

### 📖 Main Content

#### 🔹 Concept of partition

Partition has:

- **Status severance**: end of jointness (subject to reunion)
- **Property division**: actual division or **partition by suit** leading to allotments

#### 🔹 Partition under Mitakshara

- Generally, **any coparcener** may demand partition (subject to classical disabilities—minor coparcener sues through guardian; some persons had limited rights historically—follow your textbook list).
- **Partition by metes and bounds** vs **partition by agreement** vs **oral partition** (fact-sensitive; evidentiary issues).
- **Survivorship**: until partition, on death of coparcener, interest may pass to **survivors** (classical Mitakshara)—now modified by **HSA 1956** in many intestacy situations; exam answers should mention **statutory overlay**.

#### 🔹 Partition under Dayabhaga

- Because coparcenary unity forms differently, **father’s lifetime** rules differ; sons’ expectations crystallise after his death in many problem patterns.
- Shares and **simultaneous** rights differ—always apply **Dayabhaga** only when facts say **Bengal school**.

#### 🔹 Subject matter of partition

What can be partitioned:

- **Coparcenary property** (primary)
- **Joint family property** broadly considered in settlements
- **Separate property** only if **voluntarily pooled** or agreed

Not subject: purely **personal** rights unless parties contractually settle.

#### 🔹 Persons entitled to partition / share

- **Coparceners** (including **daughters** as coparceners post-2005 in Mitakshara coparcenary)
- **Mothers/widows** may get **share** or **maintenance/quasi-rights** depending on arrangement and school/statute
- **Minor**: partition possible; court protects interest

#### 🔹 Kinds of partition

1. **De facto** (actual division of possession)
2. **De jure** (severance of status even if property not physically divided)
3. **Partial partition** (some items/branches separated)
4. **Partition by arbitration/family arrangement**

#### 🔹 Survivorship and representation

- **Survivorship**: classical Mitakshara idea that coparcener’s interest enlarges survivors until partition.
- **Representation**: if a coparcener **dies** leaving heirs in line, **branch representation** concepts affect share calculations in many problem sets (link to textbook charts).

Post-amendment exams: always integrate **daughter’s coparcenary** share and **notional partition** discussions where taught.

#### 🔹 Reopening partition

Courts allow reopening for:

- **Fraud**
- **Mistake** as to property
- **Omitted assets** / **accidental omission**
- **Undue influence** vitiating consent

Mere **inadequacy of share** is usually **not** enough.

#### 🔹 Reunion

- Only **adult coparceners** who **intend** reunion can reunite; strict **consent** requirements.
- Not all separated members may reunite into same coparcenary structure easily—exam answers should stay cautious and textbook-faithful.

5. **Reunion** — rare; needs clear intent among qualified coparceners.

### 🧾 Model Answers

#### 🎯 15 Mark — “Explain partition under Mitakshara and Dayabhaga systems.”

##### 🧾 Answer Synopsis

- Define **partition** (status + property).
- Mitakshara: **birth coparcenary**, **unilateral demand**, **survivorship** classical.
- Dayabhaga: **death-oriented** community among sons; different **lifetime** of father’s powers.
- Contrast **who partitions when** and **share logic**.
- Mention **statutory changes** where they affect Mitakshara outcomes.

##### 🧠 Introduction

Partition is the mechanism by which a Hindu joint family’s **joint status** ends and **joint property** is divided among those entitled. Because Hindu law on joint families is **school-based**, Mitakshara and Dayabhaga prescribe **different default rules** on when coparcenary unity exists and therefore **what partition disrupts**.

##### 📖 Body

**1. Mitakshara partition**  
Coparceners exist **by birth**; any coparcener may ordinarily seek **partition** of coparcenary property. Severance may be **de jure**—even without immediate physical division, **intention to separate** can end jointness in many fact patterns. Classical Mitakshara also discussed **survivorship** until partition.

**2. Dayabhaga partition**  
Dayabhaga’s coparcenary structure differs: sons’ **coparcenary community** often emerges after **father’s death** in textbook formulations. Consequently, **father’s lifetime** management and **sons’ expectations** differ from Mitakshara. Partition problems must therefore use **different timelines** for “who has a coparcenary interest now?”.

**3. Subject matter contrast**  
Both schools partition **joint/coparcenary** property, not ordinarily another’s **bona fide separate** property unless blended or agreed.

**4. Shares**  
Mitakshara branch-based shares (with **representation** concepts in problems) differ in detail from Dayabhaga inheritance-flavoured calculations—present through a **simple hypothetical** in exam (e.g., father + sons + predeceased son’s child) only if asked.

**5. Statutory overlay**  
For Mitakshara regions, **HSA 1956** amendments alter membership (**daughters**), affecting partition parties and shares; mention briefly to show modern law awareness.

##### ⚖️ Case Laws Section

Use **one** partition precedent your notes emphasise (e.g., **partial partition** / **oral partition** cases) plus **Vineeta Sharma** hook for daughter as coparcener-partitioner.

##### 📊 Comparison Table

| Basis | Mitakshara | Dayabhaga |
|------|------------|-----------|
| Coparcenary unity | Strong **birth** community | More **death-oriented** among sons |
| Typical demand timing | During father’s lifetime (sons/daughters as coparceners now) | Problem-dependent; often post-death emphasis |
| Survivorship emphasis | Higher in classical descriptions | Lower/different |
| Exam approach | Use unless Bengal facts | Use only when Bengal/school stated |

##### 🧾 Conclusion

Partition is universal as an idea—**sever jointness, divide property**—but **Mitakshara** and **Dayabhaga** diverge on **coparcenary timing** and therefore on **who may partition when** and how **shares** are conceptualised. Modern Mitakshara answers must also reflect **statutory coparcenary inclusivity**.
