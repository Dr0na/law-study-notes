# Module 7: Oral Evidence & Examination of Witnesses

## Introduction
Oral evidence is governed by Sections 54 to 55 and Sections 140 to 168 of the Bharatiya Sakshya Sanhita, 2023 (BSS). Under evidence law, oral evidence is the most common way of bringing facts to the record. This module covers the general principles of oral evidence, the hearsay rule, the process of examination of witnesses in court, and refreshing memory.

---

## 1. General Principles of Oral Evidence (BSS Sections 54 & 55)

*   **Proof of Facts by Oral Evidence — Sec. 54 BSS (formerly Sec. 59 IEA)**: All facts, except the contents of documents or electronic records, may be proved by oral evidence.
*   **Oral Evidence must be Direct — Sec. 55 BSS (formerly Sec. 60 IEA)**: Oral evidence must, in all cases whatever, be direct; that is to say:
    - If it refers to a fact which could be seen, it must be the evidence of a witness who says he saw it.
    - If it refers to a fact which could be heard, it must be the evidence of a witness who says he heard it.
    - If it refers to a fact which could be perceived by any other sense, it must be the evidence of a witness who says he perceived it by that sense.
    - If it refers to an opinion or the grounds on which that opinion is held, it must be the evidence of the person who holds that opinion on those grounds.

### Hearsay Evidence:
- **Meaning**: Hearsay is second-hand or derivative evidence. It is a statement made by a person who is not called as a witness, reported by a witness in court.
- **Rule of Exclusion**: Hearsay evidence is strictly inadmissible.
- **Exceptions to Hearsay Rule**:
  - Res Gestae (Sec. 4 BSS)
  - Admissions and Confessions (Sec. 15-24 BSS)
  - Dying Declarations (Sec. 26(1) BSS)
  - Public records or expert opinions in books.

## 2. Examination of Witnesses (BSS Sections 140 to 168)

Witnesses are examined in court in a structured order to ensure fairness and logical flow:

*   **Order of Examinations — Sec. 141 BSS (formerly Sec. 138 IEA)**:
    1.  **Examination-in-chief**: The examination of a witness by the party who calls him.
    2.  **Cross-examination**: The examination of a witness by the adverse party. (Crucial for testing truthfulness).
    3.  **Re-examination**: The examination of a witness, subsequent to the cross-examination, by the party who called him. (Used to explain matters introduced in cross-examination).
*   **Leading Questions — Sec. 144-146 BSS (formerly Sec. 141-143 IEA)**: A leading question is a question which suggests the answer which the person putting it wishes or expects to receive (e.g., "Was the suspect wearing a red shirt?").
    - *When they must NOT be asked*: In Examination-in-chief or Re-examination, if objected to by the adverse party, except with permission of the Court.
    - *When they MAY be asked*: In Cross-examination.
*   **Hostile Witness — Sec. 157 BSS (formerly Sec. 154 IEA)**: A witness who turns against the party that called them. The Court may, in its discretion, permit the party who calls a witness to put any questions to him which might be put in cross-examination by the adverse party (including leading questions and impeaching their credit).
*   **Impeaching Credit of Witness — Sec. 158 BSS (formerly Sec. 155 IEA)**: The credit of a witness may be impeached by the adverse party or, with the consent of the Court, by the party who calls him:
    - By evidence of persons who testify that they believe him to be unworthy of credit.
    - By proof that the witness has been bribed.
    - By proof of former statements inconsistent with any part of his evidence.

---

## 3. Refreshing Memory — Sec. 162 BSS (formerly Sec. 159 IEA)

A witness may, while under examination, refresh his memory by referring to any writing made by himself at the time of the transaction concerning which he is questioned, or so soon afterwards that the Court considers it likely that the transaction was at that time fresh in his memory.
- The witness can also refer to a writing made by any other person, and read by the witness within the time aforesaid, if when he read it he knew it to be correct.

## 4. Landmark Judgments

1.  **State of Rajasthan v. Kartar Singh (AIR 1970 SC 1305)**
    - *Ratio*: Discussed the rules for declaring a witness hostile and the evidentiary value of a hostile witness's statements (their testimony is not completely discarded; parts that support the prosecution or defence can still be accepted).
2.  **Kalyan Kumar Gogoi v. Ashutosh Agnihotri (2011 2 SCC 532)**
    - *Ratio*: Defined hearsay evidence and discussed the rationale behind its exclusion (unreliability, lack of oath, inability to cross-examine).

## 5. Model Answers (10 Marks & 15 Marks)

### A. Model 10 Marks Answer: What are Leading Questions? When can they be asked and when can they not be asked?
**Answer**:
1.  **Definition**: Section 144 BSS defines a leading question as any question which suggests the answer which the person putting it wishes or expects to receive.
2.  **Rules for Asking Leading Questions**:
    - **In Examination-in-Chief / Re-examination (Sec. 145 BSS)**: They must not, if objected to by the adverse party, be asked in examination-in-chief, or in a re-examination, except with the permission of the Court. The court will readily permit leading questions on matters which are introductory, undisputed, or have already been sufficiently proved.
    - **In Cross-Examination (Sec. 146 BSS)**: They may be asked in cross-examination. This is because the witness is being tested by the opposite party, and leading questions are essential to check consistency and probe for lies.
3.  **Rationale**: In chief-examination, the witness is friendly to the lawyer calling them; hence, leading questions would allow the lawyer to put words into the witness’s mouth. In cross-examination, the witness is hostile to the cross-examiner, so leading questions are necessary to test their veracity.
4.  **Conclusion**: Leading questions are vital cross-examination tools, but are strictly controlled in chief-examination to preserve the purity of witness testimony.

### B. Model 15 Marks Answer: Explain the order of examination of witnesses. What is a "Hostile Witness" and what is the evidentiary value of their testimony under BSS, 2023?
**Answer**:
1.  **Introduction**: Witnesses are examined sequentially to establish facts. Section 141 of BSS lays down the three stages: Examination-in-chief, Cross-examination, and Re-examination.
2.  **Order of Examination**:
    - *Examination-in-chief*: Conducted by the party calling the witness. Focuses on bringing out positive facts.
    - *Cross-examination*: Conducted by the opposite party. Focuses on testing accuracy, credibility, and detecting bias.
    - *Re-examination*: Conducted by the party calling the witness to clarify any new facts brought out in cross-examination.
3.  **Hostile Witness (Sec. 157 BSS)**:
    - A hostile witness is one who is unwilling to tell the truth at the instance of the party calling him, showing bias against that party.
    - Under Section 157, the court may permit the party who called the witness to cross-examine their own witness (ask leading questions and impeach their credit).
4.  **Evidentiary Value of Hostile Witness**:
    - In English law, a hostile witness’s evidence is completely rejected.
    - In Indian law (*Kartar Singh case*), a hostile witness's testimony is **not completely wiped off** the record.
    - The court can rely on those parts of the hostile witness’s testimony which conform to other evidence and support either the prosecution or the defence.
5.  **Conclusion**: The concept of hostile witness ensures that a party is not sabotaged by their own witness turning rogue, allowing the court to still extract truth from the testimony.
