# Q04 (5 Marks) — Austin’s command theory of law

**Module:** 04 — Legal Positivism
**Marks:** 5
**Difficulty:** 🟡 Medium
**ID:** JUR-5M-Q004

## MODEL ANSWER

### 🧠 Introduction

John Austin (1790–1859), in *The Province of Jurisprudence Determined* (1832), defined law as **"a command of the sovereign backed by sanction."** His theory is the classical statement of **legal positivism**.

### 📖 Four elements (CSSD)

1. **Command** — an expression of wish by a superior to an inferior.
2. **Sovereign** — a determinate human superior who receives **habitual obedience** from the bulk of society and is not habitually obedient to any other.
3. **Sanction** — the threat of evil for non-compliance.
4. **Duty** — the addressee’s legal obligation to obey.

### 📖 Significance

- Austin sharply separated *what the law is* from *what it ought to be* — the **separation thesis**.
- Inspired codification (IPC, CPC) and modern legal drafting.

### 🔻 Criticism

- Cannot explain **customary law** (no command).
- Cannot account for **constitutional limits** on the sovereign or federal systems.
- **Power-conferring rules** (contract, marriage) are not commands.
- Hart’s gunman analogy — coercive command alone does not make law.

### 🧾 Conclusion

Austin remains the **starting point** for positivism but has been refined by Hart’s rules-and-recognition model and Kelsen’s Pure Theory.
