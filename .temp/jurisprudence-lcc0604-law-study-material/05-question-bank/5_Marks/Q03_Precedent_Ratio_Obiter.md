# Q03 (5 Marks) — Ratio decidendi and obiter dicta

**Module:** 02 — Sources of Law
**Marks:** 5
**Difficulty:** 🟡 Medium
**ID:** JUR-5M-Q003

## MODEL ANSWER

### 🧠 Introduction

A judgment contains many statements, but only one part carries **binding force** — the **ratio decidendi**. The rest, by way of illustration, observation, or argument, is **obiter dicta**.

### 📖 Ratio decidendi

Ratio decidendi is the **legal reason for the decision** — the principle deduced from the material facts. It binds all lower courts under the doctrine of *stare decisis* and, in India, under **Article 141** of the Constitution.

- Identified by: material facts + the rule applied to them.
- Test: would the decision have been the same without this statement?

### 📖 Obiter dicta

Obiter dicta are **incidental observations** by the judge that are not necessary to the decision. They are **persuasive** and may guide future development of the law but do not bind.

### ⚖️ Examples

- *Donoghue v Stevenson* — the **neighbour principle** is the ratio of the case.
- *Maneka Gandhi v UoI* — the observation on "due process" implicit in Article 21 was ratio, while observations on natural justice exceeded the immediate point and have been treated as persuasive obiter.

### 🧾 Conclusion

> 🧠 **Mnemonic — RB-OP:** *Ratio Binds, Obiter Persuades.* Careful separation of the two is the difference between a sound and a sloppy use of precedent.
