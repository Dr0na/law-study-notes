# Q05 (5 Marks) — Hohfeld’s jural relations

**Module:** 06 — Concept of Person, Right, and Duties
**Marks:** 5
**Difficulty:** 🟡 Medium
**ID:** JUR-5M-Q005

## MODEL ANSWER

### 🧠 Introduction

Wesley Newcomb Hohfeld in *Some Fundamental Legal Conceptions* (1913) unpacked the loose word *"right"* into **eight precise jural relations**, arranged in four **correlative** pairs and four **opposite** pairs.

### 📊 Table

| ⚖️ Right-side | 🤝 Correlative | ❌ Opposite |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Claim (right) | Duty | No-right |
| Privilege (liberty) | No-right | Duty |
| Power | Liability | Disability |
| Immunity | Disability | Liability |

### 📖 Worked illustrations

- **Claim ⇄ Duty:** A buyer’s right to delivery; seller’s duty to deliver.
- **Privilege ⇄ No-right:** Walking on one’s own land; the world has no-right to stop.
- **Power ⇄ Liability:** Parliament’s power to tax; taxpayers’ liability to be taxed.
- **Immunity ⇄ Disability:** Diplomatic immunity; the prosecuting state’s disability.

### 🧾 Conclusion

Hohfeld’s schema sharpens legal language and exposes hidden ambiguities. A right rarely walks alone — every right is accompanied by a precise correlative on someone else.

> 🧠 **CPPI** — Claim, Privilege, Power, Immunity.
