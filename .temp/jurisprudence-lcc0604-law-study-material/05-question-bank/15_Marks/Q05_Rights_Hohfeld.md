# Q05 (15 Marks) — Discuss the nature, characteristics, and Hohfeldian classification of legal rights

**Module:** 06 — Concept of Person, Right, and Duties
**Marks:** 15
**Difficulty:** 🔴 Hard
**ID:** JUR-15M-Q005

## MODEL ANSWER

### 🧾 Synopsis

- Definition of legal right (Salmond, Holland, Hohfeld).
- Five characteristics — **SAOPT**.
- Three theories — Will, Interest, Composite.
- Hohfeld’s eight jural relations — **CPPI**.
- Applications and Indian touch.

### 🧠 Introduction

A **right** is an *“interest recognised and protected by the rule of law”* (Salmond). Rights are the **building blocks** of legal personality — they identify what each person may claim, do, alter, or resist. The most precise modern analysis is **Hohfeld’s scheme** of eight jural relations.

### 📖 Definitions

- **Salmond** — right is an interest the violation of which is a legal wrong.
- **Holland** — capacity residing in one person of controlling, with the consent and assistance of the State, the actions of others.
- **Hohfeld** — *right* is a loose word for at least four distinct positions (claim, privilege, power, immunity).

### 📖 Characteristics of a legal right (SAOPT, COUNT = 5)

1. **Subject** — person of inherence (who has the right).
2. **Act / forbearance** — content of the right.
3. **Object** — subject-matter (what the right is over).
4. **Person of incidence** — bearer of correlative duty.
5. **Title** — facts giving rise to the right.

### 📖 Theories of legal rights (COUNT = 3)

1. **Will theory** — Kant, Hegel, Savigny: rights protect human will and autonomy.
2. **Interest theory** — Ihering, Salmond: rights protect legally recognised interests.
3. **Composite (modern)** — Maccormick, Raz: rights have both will-enabling and interest-protecting functions.

### 📖 Hohfeldian classification (CPPI)

Hohfeld in *Some Fundamental Legal Conceptions* (1913) breaks "right" into four pairs.

| ⚖️ Right-side | 🤝 Correlative | ❌ Opposite |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Claim** | Duty | No-right |
| **Privilege** | No-right | Duty |
| **Power** | Liability | Disability |
| **Immunity** | Disability | Liability |

#### Worked examples

- **Claim ⇄ Duty:** Buyer’s right to delivery; seller’s duty to deliver.
- **Privilege ⇄ No-right:** Walking on one’s own land.
- **Power ⇄ Liability:** Parliament’s power to tax; taxpayers’ liability.
- **Immunity ⇄ Disability:** Diplomatic immunity.

### 📖 Kinds of rights (further classifications)

- Perfect vs imperfect.
- Positive vs negative.
- Real (in rem) vs personal (in personam).
- Proprietary vs personal.
- Vested vs contingent.
- Public vs private.

### 📖 Indian application

- *Maneka Gandhi v UoI* (1978) — expanded Article 21 into a cluster of claim-rights.
- *Justice K.S. Puttaswamy v UoI* (2017) — recognition of **privacy** as a fundamental right; uses Hohfeldian language of claim and immunity against the State.
- Constitutional **disabilities** of the State (Articles 14, 19, 21) correspond to **immunities** of the citizen.

### 🔻 Criticism

- Hohfeld’s scheme is **technical** and abstract; non-lawyers find it impenetrable.
- Does not by itself say which rights are *important* — that is a question for political philosophy.

### 🧾 Conclusion

Legal rights are best understood through their **structure**. Hohfeld’s eight jural relations supply the most precise vocabulary; the five characteristics (SAOPT) and the three theories of rights (Will, Interest, Composite) round out the picture. A topper answer must always use Hohfeldian language for at least one paragraph.
