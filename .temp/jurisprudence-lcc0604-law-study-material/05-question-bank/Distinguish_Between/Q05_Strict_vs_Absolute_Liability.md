# Distinguish — Strict Liability vs Absolute Liability

**ID:** JUR-D05

## MODEL ANSWER

### 🧠 Introduction

**Strict liability** is the classical no-fault liability of *Rylands v Fletcher* (1868). **Absolute liability** is its modern Indian counterpart, evolved by the Supreme Court in *M.C. Mehta v UoI* (1987) for **hazardous enterprises**, and admits **no defences**.

### 📊 Table of distinctions

| 🔍 Basis | Strict liability | Absolute liability |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Foundational case | *Rylands v Fletcher* (1868) | *M.C. Mehta v UoI* (1987) |
| Activity covered | **Non-natural** use of land with escape | **Hazardous / inherently dangerous** activity |
| Defences | Available — act of God, plaintiff’s default, third-party act, statutory authority, consent | **None** — duty is absolute and non-delegable |
| Burden of proof | On plaintiff to show non-natural use and escape | On enterprise (deemed liable once activity + injury established) |
| Quantum of damages | Compensatory | **Exemplary** — capacity-of-enterprise rule |
| Jurisprudence | Tort of strict liability | Constitutional tort recognised under Article 21 |
| Statutory follow-on | None directly | Public Liability Insurance Act, 1991; Environment Protection Act, 1986 |
| Topper one-liner | "Strict — with defences" | "Absolute — no defences" |

### 📖 Why the SC departed from *Rylands*

In *M.C. Mehta* (Oleum gas leak), Chief Justice Bhagwati held that the *Rylands* defences are anachronistic for modern hazardous industry; the *enterprise must bear the entire cost of any harm* as **part of its overheads**. This is sometimes called the **enterprise liability** doctrine.

### ⚖️ Cases

- *Rylands v Fletcher*, (1868) LR 3 HL 330.
- *Read v J Lyons & Co.*, [1947] AC 156 — limited *Rylands* by requiring escape from land.
- *M.C. Mehta v UoI*, (1987) 1 SCC 395.
- *Indian Council for Enviro-Legal Action v UoI*, (1996) 3 SCC 212 — applied absolute liability principle (Bichhri industries pollution).

### 🧾 Conclusion

Strict and absolute liability share a **no-fault** foundation, but the Indian Supreme Court made absolute liability *truly absolute* by stripping it of defences and tying it to the **capacity of the enterprise** to compensate.
