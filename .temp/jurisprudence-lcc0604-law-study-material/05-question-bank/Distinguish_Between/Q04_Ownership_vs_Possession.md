# Distinguish — Ownership vs Possession

**ID:** JUR-D04

## MODEL ANSWER

### 🧠 Introduction

**Ownership** is the *de jure* right to a thing; **possession** is *de facto* control of that thing combined with the intention to hold it for oneself. The two often coincide but can be separated (e.g., lessor vs lessee).

### 📊 Table of distinctions

| 🔍 Basis | Ownership | Possession |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Nature | *De jure* right | *De facto* control + intention |
| Ingredients | Bundle of rights — user, disposition, residue | Corpus possessionis + animus possidendi |
| Duration | Permanent until alienated | May be temporary |
| Protection | Through title-based suits | Through possessory remedies (Specific Relief Act, Sections 5 & 6) |
| Origin | Acquired by inheritance, transfer, prescription, original modes | Acquired by taking or delivery |
| Capacity | Requires legal personality | Even unborn / corporations / minors can possess through agents |
| Maxim | *Dominium* | *Possessio* |
| Mode of proof | Title deeds, registration, evidence of right | Physical control + intent |
| Topper one-liner | **Right** to the thing | **Control** of the thing |

### ⚖️ Cases to cite

- *Asher v Whitlock* (1865) — possession good against all but the true owner.
- *Yashwant Singh v Jagdish Singh* (1968) — Section 6, Specific Relief Act, protects possession without proof of title.

### 🧾 Conclusion

Owners are *entitled*; possessors are *in control*. The law protects both — title-based actions for the owner, possessory remedies for the possessor — because **peaceful possession** itself is a public good.
