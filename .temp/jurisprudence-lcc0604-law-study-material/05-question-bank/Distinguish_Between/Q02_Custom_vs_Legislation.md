# Distinguish — Custom vs Legislation

**ID:** JUR-D02

## MODEL ANSWER

### 🧠 Introduction

**Custom** is law that emerges from long-continued practice in a community. **Legislation** is law deliberately enacted by a competent authority. Both are sources of law but operate very differently.

### 📊 Table of distinctions

| 🔍 Basis | Custom | Legislation |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Origin | Community practice over time | Deliberate enactment |
| Form | Unwritten (until codified) | Written and promulgated |
| Speed | Slow, organic | Fast, can keep up with change |
| Authority | Antiquity, reasonableness, acceptance | Constitutional competence |
| Validity test | Six essentials (ACPRCC) | Procedural validity, constitutionality |
| Flexibility | Hard to change | Easy to repeal and amend |
| Indian example | Hindu / Muslim personal laws | IPC, 1860; CPC, 1908 |
| Place under Constitution | "custom" in Article 13(3)(a) | Articles 245-255 |
| Limits | Must be reasonable, not against statute | Cannot violate fundamental rights / basic structure |
| Topper one-liner | Old, unwritten, organic | Modern, written, deliberate |

### 🧾 Conclusion

In the modern Indian state, **legislation is dominant** but does not displace **custom** entirely — personal laws and trade usage continue to draw on customary norms. The two operate in **complementary** registers.
