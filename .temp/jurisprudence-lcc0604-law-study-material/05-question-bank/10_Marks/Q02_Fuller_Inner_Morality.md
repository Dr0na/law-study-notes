# Q02 (10 Marks) — Lon Fuller’s "inner morality of law"

**Module:** 03 — Natural Law Theories
**Marks:** 10
**Difficulty:** 🟡 Medium
**ID:** JUR-10M-Q002

## MODEL ANSWER

### 🧾 Synopsis

- Fuller’s *The Morality of Law* (1964) re-stated natural law in **procedural** form.
- Eight desiderata = **internal morality of law**.
- Distinction between the **morality of duty** and the **morality of aspiration**.
- Implications for Nazi-era statutes and Hart–Fuller debate.
- Conclusion: legality and morality are connected by craft, not metaphysics.

### 🧠 Introduction

Lon L. Fuller (1902–1978), American legal philosopher at Harvard, treated law as a **purposive enterprise of subjecting human conduct to the governance of rules**. Calling something law presupposes the legal system has cleared certain **internal moral** hurdles.

### 📖 The eight desiderata (GPPC-CPCC)

1. **Generality** — laws must be expressed as general rules, not ad hoc commands.
2. **Publicity** — rules must be promulgated.
3. **Prospectivity** — no retroactive rules.
4. **Clarity** — rules must be understandable.
5. **Consistency** — non-contradictory.
6. **Possibility of compliance** — must not require impossible acts.
7. **Constancy through time** — must not change too rapidly.
8. **Congruence** — official action must match declared rules.

### 📖 Morality of duty vs morality of aspiration

- **Morality of duty** sets minimum demands — what one *must* do.
- **Morality of aspiration** points to excellence — what a person *should* aspire to.

The eight desiderata are aspirational; total failure of any one **destroys** the legal system as a system of rules.

### ⚖️ Cases / examples

- **Nazi grudge-informer cases (post-1945)** — secret retroactive rules failed publicity and prospectivity; Fuller would deny them the title of *law*.
- **Indian context:** *Maneka Gandhi v UoI* (1978) — procedural fairness implicit in Article 21 mirrors Fuller’s desiderata.

### 🔻 Criticism

- **Hart’s reply** (1965 *Harvard Law Review*) — Fuller confuses **efficacy** with **morality**. A wicked but efficient system can satisfy all eight desiderata.
- **Dworkin** — Fuller’s desiderata are too procedural; substantive morality must enter the picture.

### 🧾 Conclusion

Fuller’s genius is to relocate natural law from theology to **craftsmanship**. Even on Hart’s positivist premises, a legal system that ignores publicity, prospectivity, or clarity is not just *bad* law — it is not law at all.
