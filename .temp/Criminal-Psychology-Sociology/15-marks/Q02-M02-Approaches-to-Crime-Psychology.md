## 🎯 Introduction

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

Criminal psychology seeks to understand why individuals engage in criminal behavior. Three major psychological approaches have emerged to explain crime - the Psychological approach (cognitive and personality factors), the Psycho-analytical approach (unconscious motives and childhood experiences), and the Behaviourist approach (learned behavior through conditioning). Each offers unique insights into criminal behavior causation.

## 🧠 Psychological Approach to Crime

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

The psychological approach focuses on individual mental processes, personality traits, and cognitive functioning that predispose individuals to criminal behavior.

### Core Concepts:

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

**Focus:** Intelligence, personality, cognition, moral development

**Assumption:** Criminals differ from non-criminals in psychological makeup - personality traits, thinking patterns, decision-making processes

</div>

### 4 Key Elements:

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

**Element 1: Intelligence and Crime**

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

Early theories linked low intelligence to crime.

**Goddard's Theory:** Feeblemindedness causes crime - low IQ individuals lack judgment, easily influenced

**Modern View:** Relationship complex - low IQ may correlate with getting caught, not necessarily committing crime. Intelligence affects type of crime (white-collar vs. street crime).

</div>

<br>

**Element 2: Personality Traits**

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

Certain personality traits associated with criminality:
- **Impulsivity** - acting without thinking
- **Risk-taking** - thrill-seeking behavior
- **Aggression** - hostile tendencies
- **Lack of empathy** - cannot feel others' pain
- **Antisocial personality disorder** - persistent pattern of violating others' rights

**Eysenck's Theory:** Criminals score high on: Extraversion (E), Neuroticism (N), Psychoticism (P)

</div>

<br>

**Element 3: Cognitive Processes**

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

How criminals think differently:
- **Cognitive distortions** - twisted thinking patterns
- **Moral disengagement** - justify harmful behavior
- **Hostile attribution bias** - see threats where none exist
- **Poor problem-solving** - cannot resolve conflicts legally

**Example:** Criminal may think "They deserve it" (moral disengagement) or "Everyone does it" (cognitive distortion).

</div>

<br>

**Element 4: Moral Development**

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

**Kohlberg's Theory:** Criminals stuck at lower stages of moral development
- **Pre-conventional:** Avoid punishment, seek reward
- **Conventional:** Follow rules, seek approval
- **Post-conventional:** Universal ethical principles

Criminals operate at pre-conventional level - focused on personal consequences, not morality.

</div>

</div>

### Application:

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

- Psychological testing for risk assessment
- Cognitive-behavioral therapy in prisons
- Personality profiling for investigations
- Understanding white-collar vs. violent crime differences

</div>

</div>

## 🧠 Psycho-analytical Approach to Crime (Freudian)

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

The psycho-analytical approach, founded by **Sigmund Freud**, emphasizes unconscious mental processes, childhood experiences, and internal conflicts as causes of criminal behavior.

### Freud's Structural Model of Personality:

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

**Component 1: Id**

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

**Nature:** Primitive, instinctual, unconscious

**Operates on:** Pleasure principle - seeks immediate gratification

**Contains:** Basic drives - hunger, sex, aggression

**No morality:** Doesn't distinguish right from wrong

**Example:** Infant crying for food immediately - pure id

</div>

<br>

**Component 2: Ego**

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

**Nature:** Rational, conscious, mediator

**Operates on:** Reality principle - considers consequences

**Function:** Balances id's demands with reality

**Develops:** Around age 2-3 years

**Example:** Child wants toy but knows stealing is wrong - ego weighs options

</div>

<br>

**Component 3: Superego**

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

**Nature:** Moral conscience, internalized values

**Operates on:** Morality principle - right vs. wrong

**Function:** Enforces moral standards, creates guilt

**Develops:** Around age 5-6 through parental/social influence

**Example:** Feeling guilty after lying - superego at work

</div>

</div>

### How Psycho-analytical Theory Explains Crime:

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

**Explanation 1: Weak or Underdeveloped Superego**

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

**Cause:** Poor parenting, lack of moral guidance in childhood

**Result:** No internal moral compass - cannot distinguish right from wrong - no guilt after wrongdoing

**Criminal Type:** Psychopathic criminals who feel no remorse

</div>

<br>

**Explanation 2: Overactive/Harsh Superego**

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

**Cause:** Excessively strict parenting, harsh punishment

**Result:** Overwhelming guilt → commits crime to be punished → relieves guilt

**Criminal Type:** Neurotic criminals who unconsciously seek punishment

</div>

<br>

**Explanation 3: Weak Ego**

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

**Cause:** Cannot control id's impulses

**Result:** Acts on immediate urges without considering consequences

**Criminal Type:** Impulsive criminals - crimes of passion, unplanned offenses

</div>

<br>

**Explanation 4: Unresolved Childhood Conflicts**

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

**Oedipus Complex (Boys):** Unresolved sexual attraction to mother, hostility to father → aggressive, antisocial behavior

**Electra Complex (Girls):** Similar conflict with parents → behavioral issues

**Result:** Adult criminality as manifestation of childhood conflicts

</div>

</div>

### Defense Mechanisms and Crime:

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

Criminals use defense mechanisms to cope with guilt/anxiety:

**1. Rationalization:** "They had it coming" / "I needed the money"

**2. Projection:** Blame others for own impulses

**3. Displacement:** Take out anger on innocent victims

**4. Denial:** Refuse to acknowledge wrongdoing

</div>

### Application:

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

- Psychotherapy for offenders
- Understanding childhood trauma's role
- Addressing underlying psychological conflicts
- Treatment of sex offenders, violent criminals

</div>

</div>

## 🧠 Behaviourist Approach to Crime

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

The behaviourist approach views criminal behavior as **learned** through environmental experiences, conditioning, and reinforcement. Crime is not innate - it is acquired.

### Core Principle:

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

**All behavior (including criminal behavior) is learned through interaction with the environment.**

**Key Thinkers:** B.F. Skinner, Ivan Pavlov, John Watson, Albert Bandura

</div>

### 3 Learning Mechanisms:

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

**Mechanism 1: Classical Conditioning (Pavlov)**

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

**Process:** Pairing neutral stimulus with response

**Pavlov's Experiment:** Dog salivates at bell sound (paired with food)

**Application to Crime:**
- Fear responses conditioned
- Anxiety in certain situations
- Phobias and compulsions

**Example:** Child punished in specific place → fears that place → anxiety-related behavior

**Limited explanation for crime:** Explains emotional associations but not complex criminal behavior

</div>

<br>

**Mechanism 2: Operant Conditioning (Skinner)**

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

**Process:** Behavior shaped by consequences (rewards/punishments)

**Key Concepts:**
- **Reinforcement** (positive/negative) - increases behavior
- **Punishment** - decreases behavior

**Application to Crime:**

**Criminal Behavior Reinforced When:**
- Crime brings rewards (money, status, excitement)
- No punishment or delayed punishment
- Peer approval for criminal acts

**Example:** Shoplifting → Gets away with it (reinforced) → Repeats behavior

**Delinquency:** If antisocial behavior rewarded (peer acceptance) and prosocial behavior ignored → delinquency develops

</div>

<br>

**Mechanism 3: Social Learning Theory (Bandura)**

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

**Process:** Learning through observation and imitation (modeling)

**Bobo Doll Experiment:** Children who observed adults hitting doll later imitated aggressive behavior

**Application to Crime:**

**Criminal Behavior Learned Through:**
- **Observation:** Watching others commit crimes
- **Imitation:** Copying criminal behavior
- **Vicarious reinforcement:** Seeing others rewarded for crime

**Four Processes:**
1. **Attention:** Notice the behavior
2. **Retention:** Remember it
3. **Reproduction:** Ability to perform it
4. **Motivation:** Expect positive outcome

**Example:** Child growing up in criminal environment - observes family members committing crimes - sees them benefit - learns crime is acceptable - imitates behavior

**Media Violence:** Exposure to violent media can increase aggression through modeling (controversial but research-supported).

</div>

</div>

### Application:

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

- Behavior modification programs in prisons
- Token economy systems (reward good behavior)
- Social skills training
- Anger management (replace aggressive responses)
- Understanding gang membership (peer reinforcement)

</div>

</div>

## ⚖️ Comparison of Three Approaches

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

| **Aspect** | **Psychological** | **Psycho-analytical** | **Behaviourist** |
|-----------|------------------|----------------------|-----------------|
| **Focus** | Personality, cognition | Unconscious, childhood | Learning, environment |
| **Cause of Crime** | Abnormal personality/thinking | Unresolved conflicts, weak superego | Learned through conditioning |
| **Key Factors** | Traits, IQ, moral development | Id-Ego-Superego imbalance | Reinforcement, modeling |
| **Criminality** | Internal traits | Unconscious drives | Learned behavior |
| **Treatment** | Cognitive therapy | Psychoanalysis | Behavior modification |
| **Free Will** | Partial | Deterministic (unconscious) | Deterministic (environment) |
| **Criticism** | Ignores environment | Unscientific, untestable | Ignores internal factors |

</div>

## 🎯 Conclusion

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

The three psychological approaches offer complementary perspectives on criminal behavior. The Psychological approach focuses on individual differences in personality and cognition. The Psycho-analytical approach reveals unconscious motivations and childhood origins of criminality. The Behaviourist approach demonstrates how criminal behavior is learned through environmental conditioning and social modeling.

Modern criminal psychology integrates insights from all three:
- **Psychological:** Assessment of personality disorders, cognitive distortions
- **Psycho-analytical:** Understanding deep-rooted conflicts, trauma therapy
- **Behaviourist:** Behavior modification, social learning interventions

Understanding these approaches helps in:
- Assessing criminal risk
- Designing treatment programs
- Explaining different types of crime
- Developing prevention strategies

No single approach fully explains crime - combination provides comprehensive understanding of criminal behavior's complexity.

</div>
