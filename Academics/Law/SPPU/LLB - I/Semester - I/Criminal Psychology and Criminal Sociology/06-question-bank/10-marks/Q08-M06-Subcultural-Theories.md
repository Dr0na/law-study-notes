## 🎯 Introduction

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

Subcultural theories explain crime and delinquency as result of distinct cultural systems that develop within larger society. Cohen and Miller both focus on lower-class youth gangs but offer different explanations for their formation and behavior.

## 👥 Cohen's Theory of Delinquent Subculture (1955)

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

**Albert K. Cohen** in "Delinquent Boys" (1955) explained gang delinquency as collective solution to status frustration experienced by lower-class boys.

### Core Argument:

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

Lower-class boys face **status frustration** when they cannot meet middle-class standards valued by schools and society. They form delinquent subcultures with inverted values where they can achieve status.

</div>

### Process of Formation (5 Steps):

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

**Step 1: Middle-Class Measuring Rod**

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

**Schools impose middle-class values:**
- Academic achievement
- Ambition and hard work
- Delayed gratification
- Respect for property
- Verbal skills

**Lower-class boys judged by these standards**

</div>

<br>

**Step 2: Status Frustration**

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

**Lower-class boys ill-equipped to compete:**
- Poor socialization for school success
- Lack verbal skills
- Family values immediate gratification
- Cannot meet middle-class standards

**Result:** Fail in school, rejected by teachers, feel inadequate - experience **status frustration**

</div>

<br>

**Step 3: Problem of Adjustment**

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

Boys face psychological problem - how to cope with failure and low status?

**Cannot succeed in conventional world** - need alternative way to gain status and self-respect

</div>

<br>

**Step 4: Collective Solution**

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

Boys with similar problems **come together** - form delinquent gang/subculture - create their own value system

**Collective, not individual:** Subculture provides group solution to shared problem

</div>

<br>

**Step 5: Reaction Formation (Value Inversion)**

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

**Delinquent subculture inverts middle-class values:**

| **Middle-Class Value** | **Delinquent Subculture Value** |
|----------------------|--------------------------------|
| Respect property | Steal, vandalize |
| Academic achievement | Reject school, mock students |
| Delayed gratification | Immediate pleasure |
| Non-violence | Toughness, fighting |
| Courtesy | Hostility, disrespect |

**Purpose:** Gain status by doing opposite of what middle class values - what is condemned by middle class is celebrated in gang

</div>

</div>

### 3 Characteristics of Delinquent Subculture:

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

**1. Non-Utilitarian:**

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">
Crime not for economic gain - for status, fun, rebellion. Vandalism, joyriding have no practical purpose.
</div>

<br>

**2. Malicious:**

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">
Deliberately spiteful, hostile - "get back at" middle-class society.
</div>

<br>

**3. Negativistic:**

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">
Defined by opposition to conventional norms - "wrong" in conventional terms is "right" in subculture.
</div>

</div>

### Application:

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

**Explains:** Juvenile delinquency, gang vandalism, school dropouts joining gangs

**Policy:** Address status frustration through alternative success opportunities, vocational training, mentorship

</div>

</div>

## 👔 Miller's Theory of Lower-Class Gang Delinquency (1958)

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

**Walter B. Miller** disagreed with Cohen, arguing lower-class has its **own distinct culture** (not reaction to middle-class values).

### Core Argument:

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

Lower-class gang delinquency results from conforming to **focal concerns** of lower-class culture - not rebelling against middle-class values, but following their own class values.

**Key Point:** Lower-class culture has existed for generations with distinct values - delinquency is natural expression of these values, not pathology.

</div>

### 6 Focal Concerns of Lower-Class Culture:

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

**Focal Concern 1: Trouble**

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

Ability to handle trouble and stay out of it valued - "street smarts" - knowing how to avoid/manage dangerous situations

**Link to Crime:** Sometimes "handling trouble" involves violence or illegal acts

</div>

<br>

**Focal Concern 2: Toughness**

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

Physical prowess, masculinity, bravery highly valued - cannot show weakness - "real man" doesn't back down

**Link to Crime:** Proving toughness through fighting, violence - defending reputation

</div>

<br>

**Focal Concern 3: Smartness**

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

Street smarts - ability to outsmart others, con people, make easy money - not academic intelligence but practical cunning

**Link to Crime:** Using wits to make money (illegal schemes), hustling, conning

</div>

<br>

**Focal Concern 4: Excitement**

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

Seeking thrills, action, risk - life alternates between boring routine and exciting weekend activities

**Link to Crime:** Joyriding, fighting, risky behavior for excitement - crime provides thrills

</div>

<br>

**Focal Concern 5: Fate**

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

Belief that life controlled by luck/fate, not hard work - "what will be, will be" - limited control over destiny

**Link to Crime:** Fatalistic attitude → less concern about consequences - gambling, risky illegal activities

</div>

<br>

**Focal Concern 6: Autonomy**

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

Value independence - resist external control - "nobody tells me what to do"

**Link to Crime:** Resisting authority (police, teachers, parents) - defying rules as assertion of autonomy

</div>

**Mnemonic: "TTSEFA"** - Trouble, Toughness, Smartness, Excitement, Fate, Autonomy

</div>

### Why Delinquency Occurs:

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

**Not Reaction:** Boys aren't reacting against middle-class values - they're conforming to their own class culture

**Natural Expression:** Delinquent behavior is natural outcome of adhering to lower-class focal concerns

**Example:** Fighting to prove "toughness" is valued in lower-class culture - boy fights to gain respect within his community

</div>

</div>

## ⚖️ Comparison: Cohen vs. Miller

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

| **Aspect** | **Cohen** | **Miller** |
|-----------|----------|-----------|
| **Cause** | Reaction to middle-class rejection | Conformity to lower-class culture |
| **Status Frustration** | Yes - cannot meet middle-class standards | No - have own standards |
| **Values** | Inverted middle-class values | Distinct lower-class values |
| **Delinquency** | Rebellion, negativistic | Natural expression of culture |
| **Crime Type** | Non-utilitarian (vandalism) | Utilitarian & non-utilitarian |
| **Target** | Middle-class institutions | Variable |

</div>

---

## 🎯 Conclusion

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

Both Cohen and Miller explain lower-class gang delinquency but differ in perspective. Cohen sees it as **reaction to failure** in middle-class system (status frustration → delinquent subculture). Miller sees it as **conformity to distinct lower-class culture** (focal concerns naturally lead to behaviors labeled delinquent).

**Practical Implications:**
- **Cohen approach:** Expand opportunities for lower-class success in conventional system
- **Miller approach:** Understand and work within lower-class cultural framework, provide legitimate outlets for focal concerns (sports for toughness, entrepreneurship for smartness)

Both theories highlight how class culture shapes behavior and that delinquency must be understood in cultural context, not just as individual pathology.

</div>
