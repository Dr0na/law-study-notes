# 📝 Question: Explain Garofalo's concept of "Natural Crime". (5 Marks)

---

# ✍️ Answer:

## 🎯 Introduction

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

Raffaele Garofalo (1851-1934), Italian criminologist and jurist, developed concept of "Natural Crime" distinguishing truly harmful acts from mere legal violations.

</div>

---

## 📖 Concept of Natural Crime

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

**Natural Crimes** are acts that violate two basic human sentiments found in all civilized societies:

**1. Sentiment of Pity (Altruism):**

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">
Compassion for others' suffering - violated by crimes against person: murder, rape, assault, causing pain/suffering
</div>

<br>

**2. Sentiment of Probity (Honesty):**

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">
Respect for property rights - violated by crimes against property: theft, fraud, robbery, dishonesty
</div>

</div>

---

## 🆚 Natural vs. Artificial Crime

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

**Natural Crimes (Mala in Se):**
- Wrong in themselves
- Universally condemned
- Violate basic human sentiments
- **Examples:** Murder, theft

**Artificial Crimes (Mala Prohibita):**
- Wrong because law says so
- Vary by time/place
- Don't violate natural sentiments
- **Examples:** Traffic violations, licensing offenses

</div>

---

## 👤 Psychic Anomaly

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

**Garofalo's Theory:** Criminals have **psychic anomaly** - lack basic moral sentiments (pity/probity) - moral deficiency prevents feeling empathy or respecting property

</div>

---

## ⚖️ Treatment Approach

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

**Garofalo advocated harsh treatment:**
- Death penalty or life imprisonment for incorrigibles
- Elimination of defective individuals
- Reparation to victims

**(Note: Modern criminology rejects this harsh approach)**

</div>

---

## 🎯 Conclusion

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

Garofalo's "Natural Crime" concept distinguished truly harmful acts from technical violations. While his treatment recommendations were harsh, his emphasis on psychological factors and victim rights influenced modern criminology and victimology.

</div>
