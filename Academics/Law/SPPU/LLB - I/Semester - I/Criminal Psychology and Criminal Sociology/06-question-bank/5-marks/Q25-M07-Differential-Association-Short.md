# 📝 Question: Write a short note on Sutherland's Differential Association Theory. (5 Marks)

---

# ✍️ Answer:

## 🎯 Introduction

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

Edwin Sutherland's Differential Association Theory (1939) is one of most influential criminological theories, explaining crime as learned behavior through social interaction.

</div>

---

## 📖 Core Principle

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

**"Criminal behavior is learned in interaction with others."**

**Key Idea:** Person becomes delinquent when exposed to **excess of definitions favorable to law violation** over definitions unfavorable.

**Simple Terms:** Associate more with people who view crime favorably → More likely to commit crime

</div>

---

## 🔑 Key Propositions (9 Total)

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

**Main Points:**

**1. Criminal behavior is learned** (not inherited)

**2. Learned through communication** with others

**3. Learning occurs in intimate personal groups** (family, friends, peers)

**4. Learning includes:**
- Techniques of committing crime
- Motives, drives, rationalizations, attitudes

**5. Excess of definitions:** Balance tips toward crime when pro-criminal associations outweigh anti-criminal

**6. Associations vary in:** Frequency (how often), Duration (how long), Priority (how early in life), Intensity (importance of source)

</div>

---

## ✅ Example

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

**Youth in Gang:**
- Associates with gang members daily (frequency, duration)
- Exposed to pro-crime attitudes ("stealing is smart")
- Learns techniques (how to steal)
- Learns rationalizations ("rich don't miss it")
- Pro-criminal definitions exceed anti-criminal
- **Result:** Engages in crime

</div>

---

## 💡 Policy Implications

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

- Positive peer group programs
- Mentorship with pro-social adults
- Early intervention before delinquent associations form
- Family strengthening programs

</div>

---

## 🎯 Conclusion

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

Differential Association Theory explains crime as learned through intimate associations. Those who associate with criminal patterns learn to commit crime. Prevention requires changing association patterns and strengthening pro-social relationships.

</div>
