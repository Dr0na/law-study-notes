# 📝 Question: Explain the concept of Self-Control and its relationship to crime. (5 Marks)

---

# ✍️ Answer:

## 🎯 Introduction

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

Gottfredson and Hirschi's General Theory of Crime (1990) identifies low self-control as the primary cause of criminal behavior. Individuals with low self-control are more likely to engage in criminal and analogous behaviors.

</div>

---

## 📖 Concept of Self-Control

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

**Self-Control:** Ability to restrain impulses, delay gratification, consider consequences before acting

**Development:** Formed in early childhood (ages 0-10) through effective parenting - remains relatively stable throughout life

</div>

---

## 🔑 6 Characteristics of Low Self-Control

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

**1. Impulsivity:** Act without thinking

**2. Simple Tasks:** Prefer simple over complex

**3. Risk-Seeking:** Seek excitement, physical activities

**4. Short-Term:** Present-oriented, don't plan ahead

**5. Self-Centered:** Insensitive to others' suffering

**6. Low Frustration Tolerance:** Quick to anger, cannot handle frustration

</div>

---

## 🔗 Link to Crime

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

**Why Low Self-Control → Crime:**

Crime provides:
- Immediate gratification
- Easy/simple way to get wants
- Excitement and risk
- Requires little planning
- Doesn't require concern for others

**Matches perfectly** with characteristics of low self-control individuals.

</div>

---

## 📋 Causes of Low Self-Control

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

**Ineffective Parenting:**
- Lack of supervision
- No recognition of deviant behavior
- No effective punishment/correction
- Parents don't care

**Result:** Child doesn't learn self-control

</div>

---

## 🎯 Conclusion

<div style="margin-left: 20px;">

Low self-control, developed through poor parenting in childhood, predisposes individuals to crime and deviant behavior throughout life. Prevention requires effective early childhood parenting programs teaching discipline, supervision, and development of self-control.

</div>
