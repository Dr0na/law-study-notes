# Module 04: Contracts of Pledge

**Statutory Provision:** Sections 172–179 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872

---

## 1. Meaning and Definition of a Contract of Pledge

**Section 172** defines Pledge as:
> *"The bailment of goods as security for payment of a debt or performance of a promise is called a 'pledge'."*

A pledge is a special kind of bailment where the purpose is strictly to serve as a security.

### Parties (2 Parties)
1. **Pawnor (or Pledgor):** The bailor (the person who deposits the goods as security).
2. **Pawnee (or Pledgee):** The bailee (the person who takes the goods as security).

---

## 2. Essential Features and Creation

**3 Essential Features:**
1. **Delivery of Possession:** Just like bailment, there must be delivery (actual or constructive) of movable goods.
2. **Purpose:** The delivery must be *strictly* for the purpose of serving as security for a debt or performance of a promise.
3. **Returnable:** The goods must be returned upon the payment of the debt or performance of the promise.

**Creation:**
A pledge is created by the delivery of goods. It can be an express agreement or inferred from the conduct of the parties. It applies only to movable property (goods, documents of title, valuables).

---

## 3. Distinction: Pledge vs. Bailment vs. Hypothecation

| Basis | Pledge | Bailment | Hypothecation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Meaning** | Bailment of goods as security. | Delivery for a specific purpose (repair, custody). | Creating a charge on movable property without delivering possession. |
| **Possession** | Transfers to Pawnee. | Transfers to Bailee. | Remains with the debtor (borrower). |
| **Right of Sale**| Pawnee has a default right to sell the goods on notice. | Bailee generally has no right to sell, only right of lien. | Creditor can take possession and sell only upon default. |
| **Statute** | Indian Contract Act (Sec 172) | Indian Contract Act (Sec 148) | Sec 3(n) Securitisation Act 2002 |

---

## 4. Rights, Duties, and Liabilities of the Pawnor

### Rights of the Pawnor
1. **Right to get the goods back:** Upon payment of the debt or performance of the promise.
2. **Right to redeem (Sec 177):** Even if the time for payment has expired, the pawnor may redeem the goods at any time *before* their actual sale by the pawnee, but he must pay any additional expenses incurred due to his default.
3. **Right to surplus:** If the pawnee sells the goods, the pawnor is entitled to any surplus money remaining after the debt and costs are settled.

### Duties/Liabilities of the Pawnor
1. **To pay the debt:** Including interest and expenses.
2. **To disclose flaws:** In the goods pledged.
3. **To meet any deficit:** If the sale of pledged goods yields less than the debt amount, the pawnor remains personally liable to pay the balance.

---

## 5. Rights, Duties, and Liabilities of the Pawnee

### Rights of the Pawnee
1. **Right of Retainer (Sec 173 & 174):** The pawnee may retain the goods not only for the payment of the debt or the performance of the promise, but also for the interest due, and for all necessary expenses incurred in respect of the possession or preservation of the goods. Note: Cannot retain for a *subsequent* debt unless agreed.
2. **Right to extraordinary expenses (Sec 175):** Entitled to receive from the pawnor any extraordinary expenses incurred for the preservation of the goods.
3. **Right on Default of Pawnor (Sec 176):** If the pawnor makes default, the pawnee has two choices:
   - *Choice A:* Bring a suit against the pawnor and retain the goods as a collateral security.
   - *Choice B:* Sell the pledged goods, provided he gives the pawnor *reasonable notice* of the sale.

### Duties of the Pawnee
1. **To take reasonable care** of the pledged goods.
2. **Not to use** the pledged goods (unless authorized).
3. **Not to mix** the pledged goods with their own.
4. **To return the goods** along with any accruals upon settlement of the debt.

---

## 6. Pledge by Certain Specified Persons (Exceptions to "Nemo Dat Quod Non Habet")

Normally, only the true owner can make a valid pledge. However, under specific sections, a non-owner can make a valid pledge to protect an innocent pawnee who acts in good faith.

1. **Pledge by Mercantile Agent (Sec 178):**
   Where a mercantile agent is, with the consent of the owner, in possession of goods or documents of title, any pledge made by him, acting in the ordinary course of business, is as valid as if expressly authorized by the owner, provided the pawnee acts in good faith and has no notice of lack of authority.

2. **Pledge by person in possession under voidable contract (Sec 178A):**
   If a person obtains possession of goods under a voidable contract (e.g., by fraud or undue influence), but the contract has not been rescinded at the time of the pledge, the pawnee acquires a good title to the goods, provided they acted in good faith.

3. **Pledge where pawnor has only a limited interest (Sec 179):**
   Where a person pledges goods in which he has only a limited interest, the pledge is valid to the extent of that interest. (e.g., a finder of goods pledging them for expenses incurred).
