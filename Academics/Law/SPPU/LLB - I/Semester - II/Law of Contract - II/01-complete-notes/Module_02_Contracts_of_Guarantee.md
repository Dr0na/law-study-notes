# Module 02: Contracts of Guarantee

**Statutory Provision:** Sections 126–147 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872

---

## 1. Definition and Kinds of Guarantee

**Section 126** defines a contract of guarantee as:
> *"A contract to perform the promise, or discharge the liability, of a third person in case of his default."*

A guarantee may be either oral or written (in India, oral guarantees are valid, unlike in English law where they must be in writing). The primary purpose is to provide security to the creditor.

### Kinds of Guarantee
1. **Specific / Simple Guarantee:** Given for a single, specific debt or transaction. It comes to an end when the guaranteed debt is discharged.
2. **Continuing Guarantee (Section 129):** A guarantee which extends to a series of transactions.
3. **Retrospective Guarantee:** Given for an existing debt.
4. **Prospective Guarantee:** Given for a future debt.
5. **Absolute Guarantee:** Where the surety unconditionally promises to pay if the principal debtor defaults.
6. **Conditional Guarantee:** Where the liability is contingent upon some event happening.

---

## 2. Formation, Essential Features & Parties

### Parties to a Contract of Guarantee (3 Parties)
1. **Surety (Guarantor):** The person who gives the guarantee.
2. **Principal Debtor:** The person in respect of whose default the guarantee is given.
3. **Creditor:** The person to whom the guarantee is given.

### 5 Essential Features of a Contract of Guarantee
1. **Tripartite Agreement:** It involves three separate contracts:
   - Between Creditor and Principal Debtor
   - Between Creditor and Surety
   - Between Surety and Principal Debtor (implied promise of indemnity)
2. **Existence of a Principal Debt:** There must be an underlying debt or liability. If there is no principal debt, there can be no valid guarantee.
3. **Consideration:** A guarantee must be supported by consideration (Section 127).
4. **No Misrepresentation or Concealment:** Any guarantee obtained by misrepresentation (Section 142) or by keeping silence regarding material circumstances (Section 143) is invalid.
5. **Writing not necessary:** Can be oral or written (Section 126).

---

## 3. Contract of Guarantee vs. Contract of Indemnity

| Basis of Difference | Contract of Indemnity (Sec 124) | Contract of Guarantee (Sec 126) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Number of Parties** | 2 (Indemnifier, Indemnity Holder) | 3 (Creditor, Principal Debtor, Surety) |
| **Number of Contracts** | 1 (Between indemnifier and holder) | 3 (C-PD, C-S, S-PD) |
| **Nature of Liability** | Primary and independent. | Secondary and conditional (arises only on PD's default). |
| **Purpose** | Reimbursement of suffered loss. | Security for a debt. |
| **Right to Sue 3rd Party**| Indemnifier cannot sue third party in his own name. | Surety, after paying the debt, steps into the shoes of the creditor and can sue the PD. |

---

## 4. Consideration for a Contract of Guarantee

**Section 127:** *"Anything done, or any promise made, for the benefit of the principal debtor, may be a sufficient consideration to the surety for giving the guarantee."*

- The surety does not need to receive direct, personal benefit.
- The loan given or goods sold to the principal debtor on the surety's recommendation is valid consideration for the surety's promise.
- Past consideration is generally not sufficient for a fresh contract of guarantee unless explicitly covered.

---

## 5. Continuing Guarantee and its Revocation

**Section 129:** A guarantee which extends to a series of transactions is called a "continuing guarantee".
- *Example:* A guarantees payment to B, a tea-dealer, to the amount of Rs. 100, for any tea he may from time to time supply to C.

### Revocation of Continuing Guarantee
A continuing guarantee can be revoked in two ways:
1. **By Notice (Section 130):** The surety can revoke it at any time regarding *future transactions*, by giving notice to the creditor. The surety remains liable for transactions that occurred *before* the notice.
2. **By Death of Surety (Section 131):** The death of the surety operates, in the absence of any contract to the contrary, as a revocation of a continuing guarantee, so far as regards *future transactions*.

---

## 6. Nature and Extent of Surety's Liability & Discharge

### Extent of Liability (Section 128)
- **Co-extensive:** The liability of the surety is *co-extensive* with that of the principal debtor, unless it is otherwise provided by the contract.
- This means the surety is liable for the principal amount plus any interest or charges the principal debtor owes, but not more.

### Discharge of Surety
A surety is discharged from their liability under the following circumstances:
1. **Revocation** (Sec 130, 131)
2. **Variance in terms of contract (Sec 133):** Any variance, made without the surety's consent, in the terms of the contract between the principal debtor and the creditor, discharges the surety as to transactions subsequent to the variance.
3. **Release or discharge of principal debtor (Sec 134):** If the creditor releases the PD, the surety is also released.
4. **Compounding by creditor with principal debtor (Sec 135):** If the creditor makes a composition with, or promises to give time to, or not to sue, the principal debtor, without the surety's consent.
5. **Creditor's act or omission impairing surety's eventual remedy (Sec 139).**
6. **Loss of security (Sec 141):** If the creditor loses or parts with security given to him, the surety is discharged to the extent of the value of the security.

---

## 7. Surety's Rights Against the Principal Debtor

1. **Right of Subrogation (Section 140):** Where a guaranteed debt has become due, or default of the principal debtor to perform a guaranteed duty has taken place, the surety, upon payment or performance, is invested with all the rights which the creditor had against the principal debtor.
2. **Right to Indemnity (Section 145):** In every contract of guarantee, there is an implied promise by the principal debtor to indemnify the surety. The surety is entitled to recover from the PD whatever sum he has rightfully paid under the guarantee.

---

## 8. Surety's Rights Against the Creditor

1. **Right to Securities (Section 141):** A surety is entitled to the benefit of every security which the creditor has against the principal debtor at the time when the contract of suretyship is entered into.
2. **Right to set-off:** The surety has a right to claim any set-off or counterclaim which the principal debtor may possess against the creditor.

---

## 9. Letters of Credit and Bank Guarantees

While the Indian Contract Act does not explicitly detail them, they operate on guarantee principles.
- **Bank Guarantee:** A promise by a bank to a beneficiary to pay a specified amount if the bank's customer defaults on a contract. It is an independent contract between the bank and the beneficiary. Courts generally do not grant injunctions stopping bank guarantees except in cases of egregious fraud or irretrievable injustice.
- **Letter of Credit (LC):** Used in international trade. The buyer's bank guarantees payment to the seller's bank upon presentation of specified documents.

---

## 10. Co-surety and Manner of Sharing Liabilities

When two or more persons are sureties for the same debt, they are called co-sureties.

1. **Equality of Burden (Section 146):** Co-sureties are liable to contribute equally, subject to any contract to the contrary. If the debt is 30,000 and there are 3 sureties, each is liable for 10,000.
2. **Liability of co-sureties bound in different sums (Section 147):** Co-sureties who are bound in different sums are liable to pay equally as far as the limits of their respective obligations permit.
3. **Release of one co-surety (Section 138):** A release by the creditor of one of the co-sureties does NOT discharge the others, nor does it free the released surety from their responsibility to the other co-sureties.
