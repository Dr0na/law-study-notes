# Module 05: Contracts of Agency

**Statutory Provision:** Sections 182–238 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872

---

## 1. Meaning and Definition of a Contract of Agency

**Section 182** defines an Agent and Principal:
> *"An 'agent' is a person employed to do any act for another, or to represent another in dealings with third persons. The person for whom such act is done, or who is so represented, is called the 'principal'."*

The fundamental principle of agency is **"Qui facit per alium facit per se"** (He who acts through another acts himself). In law, the acts of the agent within the scope of their authority bind the principal as if the principal had performed them personally.

---

## 2. Essential Features, Parties & Kinds

### Essential Features
1. **Principal must be competent (Sec 183):** Must be of the age of majority and of sound mind.
2. **Agent may be incompetent (Sec 184):** Any person may become an agent (even a minor). However, a minor agent is not personally responsible to the principal.
3. **No consideration necessary (Sec 185):** A contract of agency does not require consideration to be valid.

### Kinds of Agents
1. **General Agent:** Employed to do all acts connected with a particular business or employment.
2. **Special Agent:** Employed to do a specific act or for a specific transaction.
3. **Universal Agent:** Has unlimited authority to do all acts that the principal could lawfully do.
4. **Mercantile Agents:** E.g., Factors, Brokers, Auctioneers, Del credere agents (who guarantee the solvency of the third party for an extra commission).

---

## 3. Creation of Agency

An agency may be created in the following ways:
1. **Express Agreement:** By spoken or written words (e.g., Power of Attorney).
2. **Implied Agreement:** Arising from the conduct, situation, or relationship of the parties.
3. **Agency by Estoppel (Sec 237):** When a person by his conduct or words leads a third party to believe someone is his agent, he is stopped from denying it later.
4. **Agency by Holding Out:** Similar to estoppel, where affirmative conduct by the principal creates the impression of agency.
5. **Agency of Necessity:** In an emergency, a person can act as an agent to save the principal from loss without prior authority (Sec 189).
6. **Agency by Ratification (Sec 196):** When an act is done without authority, but the principal later accepts and ratifies it.

---

## 4. Distinction between Agent, Servant, and Independent Contractor

| Basis | Agent | Servant / Employee | Independent Contractor |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Control** | Principal directs *what* to do, but agent decides *how* to do it. | Master directs *what* to do and *how* to do it. | Employs own methods; principal only cares about the final result. |
| **Creation of Legal Relations**| Creates legal relations between principal and third parties. | Generally does not create legal relations between master and third parties. | Does not bind the employer with third parties. |
| **Liability** | Principal is liable for authorized acts. | Master is liable for acts in the course of employment (vicarious liability). | Employer is generally not liable for contractor's torts. |

---

## 5. Agent's Authority

1. **Express Authority (Sec 187):** Given by words spoken or written.
2. **Implied Authority (Sec 187):** Inferred from the circumstances of the case; things spoken or written, or the ordinary course of dealing. An agent has authority to do every lawful thing necessary for the purpose.
3. **Apparent / Ostensible Authority (Sec 237):** Authority that the principal induces a third party to believe the agent possesses, even if not actually granted.
4. **Authority in Emergency (Sec 189):** An agent has authority, in an emergency, to do all such acts to protect the principal from loss as would be done by a person of ordinary prudence.

---

## 6. Delegation of Authority: Sub-agent & Substituted Agent

The general rule is **"Delegatus non potest delegare"** (a delegated power cannot be further delegated). Section 190 states an agent cannot delegate unless justified by trade custom or nature of agency.

1. **Sub-agent (Sec 191):** A person employed by, and acting under the control of, the original agent.
   - *Properly Appointed:* The principal is represented by the sub-agent and is bound by their acts. The agent is responsible to the principal for the acts of the sub-agent.
   - *Improperly Appointed:* The principal is not bound by the acts of the sub-agent. The agent is responsible to both the principal and third parties.
2. **Substituted Agent (Sec 194):** Where an agent, holding an express or implied authority to name another person to act for the principal in the business of the agency, has named another person. Here, privity of contract is established directly between the principal and the substituted agent.

---

## 7. Rights, Duties, and Liabilities of an Agent

### Duties of Agent to Principal
1. To conduct business according to principal's directions or custom (Sec 211).
2. To work with reasonable skill and diligence (Sec 212).
3. To render proper accounts (Sec 213).
4. To communicate with the principal in cases of difficulty (Sec 214).
5. Not to deal on his own account without principal's consent (Sec 215).
6. Not to make secret profits (Sec 216).
7. To pay sums received for the principal (Sec 218).

### Rights of Agent
1. **Right of retainer (Sec 217):** To retain money for advances made or expenses properly incurred.
2. **Right to remuneration (Sec 219):** For work done, unless gratuitous.
3. **Right of Lien (Sec 221):** On principal's property for commission and expenses.
4. **Right to be indemnified (Sec 222 & 223):** Against consequences of lawful acts and acts done in good faith.

---

## 8. Personal Liability of Agent & Undisclosed Principal

**General Rule (Sec 230):** An agent cannot personally enforce contracts entered into on behalf of his principal, nor is he personally bound by them.

**Exceptions (Personal liability arises when):**
1. Contract expressly provides for it.
2. Agent acts for a foreign principal.
3. Agent does not disclose the name of the principal.
4. Principal cannot be sued (e.g., minor, foreign sovereign).
5. Pretended Agent (Sec 235): A person falsely represents himself to be an agent. If the principal does not ratify, the pretended agent is liable to compensate the third party.

**Undisclosed Principal:**
When an agent makes a contract without disclosing that they are an agent. If the third party discovers the principal, they may sue either the principal or the agent, or both (Sec 233). The principal can also require the performance of the contract from the third party, subject to the rights and obligations between the agent and third party.

---

## 9. Revocation and Termination of Agency

An agency is terminated by (Sec 201):
1. Revocation by the principal.
2. Renunciation by the agent.
3. Completion of the business of agency.
4. Death or insanity of either the principal or the agent.
5. Principal being adjudicated an insolvent.

### Irrevocable Agency
1. **Agency coupled with an interest (Sec 202):** Where the agent has an interest in the property forming the subject matter of the agency, the agency cannot be terminated to the prejudice of such interest.
2. **Where agent has incurred personal liability:** The principal cannot revoke to leave the agent exposed to liability.
3. **Where authority is partly exercised (Sec 204):** The principal cannot revoke the authority for acts already done.

### Effect of Termination
- **Between Principal and Agent (Sec 206, 208):** Takes effect when it becomes known to the agent. Notice must be given for reasonable revocation.
- **Towards Third Parties:** Takes effect when it becomes known to the third parties. Until then, the agent can still bind the principal.
