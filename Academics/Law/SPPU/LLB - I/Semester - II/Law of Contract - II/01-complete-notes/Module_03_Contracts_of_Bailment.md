# Module 03: Contracts of Bailment

**Statutory Provision:** Sections 71, 148–171, 180–181 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872

---

## 1. Definition of a Contract of Bailment

**Section 148** defines a Bailment as:
> *"The delivery of goods by one person to another for some purpose, upon a contract that they shall, when the purpose is accomplished, be returned or otherwise disposed of according to the directions of the person delivering them."*

### Parties (2 Parties)
1. **Bailor:** The person delivering the goods.
2. **Bailee:** The person to whom they are delivered.

*Note:* Bailment only deals with movable goods. It does not apply to immovable property or actionable claims.

---

## 2. Formation and Essential Features

**4 Essential Features of Bailment:**

1. **Contract:** There must be a contract, express or implied, between the bailor and the bailee. (Exception: Finder of goods acts as a bailee by implication of law).
2. **Delivery of Possession:** There must be a transfer of possession (actual or constructive) from bailor to bailee. Transfer of ownership does *not* occur; only possession changes hands.
   - *Actual delivery:* Physical handover.
   - *Constructive delivery:* Doing something that puts goods in the possession of the intended bailee (e.g., handing over warehouse keys).
3. **Specific Purpose:** The goods must be delivered for some specific purpose (e.g., repair, safe custody, carriage).
4. **Return or Disposal:** Once the purpose is accomplished, the specific goods must be returned to the bailor or disposed of according to his directions. (If the bailee has the option to return *different* goods or money, it is a sale, not bailment).

---

## 3. Kinds of Bailment

Bailments can be classified based on reward (consideration):

1. **Gratuitous Bailment:** A bailment without any reward or remuneration for either party (e.g., lending a book to a friend).
2. **Non-Gratuitous (Bailment for Reward):** A bailment where either the bailor or bailee receives a reward (e.g., giving a car to a mechanic for repair, renting a bike).

Bailments can also be classified by purpose (as per the syllabus examples):
1. **For the benefit of the bailor:** Leaving your goods at a friend's house for safe custody while you are out of town.
2. **For the benefit of the bailee:** Lending your calculator to a friend for their exam.
3. **For mutual benefit:** Giving clothes to a tailor for stitching.

---

## 4. Rights, Duties, and Liabilities

### A. Duties of the Bailor
1. **To disclose faults in goods (Sec 150):** 
   - *Gratuitous Bailment:* Must disclose known faults that materially interfere with the use of them or expose the bailee to extraordinary risks.
   - *Non-Gratuitous:* Bailor is responsible for faults whether he was aware of them or not.
2. **To bear necessary expenses (Sec 158):** In gratuitous bailment, the bailor must repay the bailee the necessary expenses incurred for the purpose of the bailment.
3. **To indemnify the bailee (Sec 164):** For any loss sustained due to the bailor's defective title.
4. **To receive back the goods:** When the purpose is fulfilled.

### B. Duties of the Bailee
1. **To take reasonable care (Sec 151):** The bailee is bound to take as much care of the goods as a man of ordinary prudence would take of his own goods. If such care is taken, the bailee is not liable for loss/damage (Sec 152).
2. **Not to make unauthorized use (Sec 154):** If the bailee uses the goods in a way not authorized by the contract, they are liable for any damage arising from such use.
3. **Not to mix goods (Secs 155-157):** The bailee must keep the bailor's goods separate from their own.
4. **To return the goods (Sec 160):** Must return or deliver them according to the bailor's directions when the time expires or purpose is accomplished.
5. **To return accretion/profits (Sec 163):** In the absence of a contract to the contrary, the bailee must deliver to the bailor any increase or profit that accrued from the goods (e.g., a calf born to a bailed cow).

### C. Rights of the Bailor
1. Right to claim damages for negligence.
2. Right to terminate the bailment if the bailee makes unauthorized use (Sec 153).
3. Right to demand return of goods.

### D. Rights of the Bailee
1. Right to recover necessary expenses.
2. Right to be indemnified by the bailor for defective title.
3. **Right of Lien (Secs 170-171):** The right to retain possession of the goods until due charges are paid.
   - *Particular Lien (Sec 170):* For labor or skill exercised on the goods (e.g., tailor).
   - *General Lien (Sec 171):* For any general balance of account (applicable only to bankers, factors, wharfingers, attorneys, and policy brokers).

### Rights against Third Parties (Sec 180-181)
If a third party wrongfully deprives the bailee of the use or possession of the bailed goods, or injures them, both the bailor and the bailee have the right to bring a suit against the third person. Any compensation obtained is divided between them according to their respective interests.

---

## 5. Termination of Bailment

A contract of bailment is terminated under the following circumstances:
1. **Expiry of Time / Accomplishment of Purpose:** When the stipulated time ends or purpose is fulfilled.
2. **Unauthorized Use (Sec 153):** A bailment becomes voidable at the option of the bailor if the bailee does any act inconsistent with the conditions of the bailment.
3. **Termination of Gratuitous Bailment (Sec 159):** The bailor can demand the goods back at any time, even before the specified time, provided the bailee is compensated if premature return causes loss exceeding the benefit received.
4. **Death (Sec 162):** A gratuitous bailment is terminated by the death either of the bailor or the bailee.
5. **Destruction of subject matter:** If the goods are destroyed or lose their identity.

---

## 6. Finder of Goods as a Bailee

**Section 71:** A person who finds goods belonging to another, and takes them into his custody, is subject to the same responsibility as a bailee.

**Duties & Rights (Secs 168-169):**
- Must take reasonable care and try to find the true owner.
- Has a right to retain the goods against the owner until he receives compensation for trouble and expenses incurred in finding the owner and preserving the goods (Lien).
- Cannot sue the owner for such compensation unless a specific reward was offered.
- Can sell the goods if:
  1. The owner cannot be found or refuses to pay lawful charges.
  2. The goods are in danger of perishing or losing greater part of value.
  3. The lawful charges of the finder amount to 2/3rds of the value of the goods.
