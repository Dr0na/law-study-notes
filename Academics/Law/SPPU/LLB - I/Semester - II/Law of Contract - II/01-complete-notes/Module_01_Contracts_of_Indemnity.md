# Module 01: Contracts of Indemnity

**Statutory Provision:** Sections 124–125 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872

---

## 1. Introduction & Principle of Indemnity in General

The term "indemnity" literally means "security against loss" or "to make good a loss" or "to compensate for damage". In commercial transactions, a contract of indemnity is frequently used to safeguard one party against potential risks or losses that might arise due to the actions of the other party or a third party.

The general principle of indemnity is based on the idea of placing the indemnified party in the same position (financially) as they would have been if the loss had not occurred. Note that insurance contracts (except life and personal accident insurance) are generally contracts of indemnity, though the Indian Contract Act’s definition is narrower.

---

## 2. Meaning and Definition

**Section 124 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872** defines a Contract of Indemnity as:
> *"A contract by which one party promises to save the other from loss caused to him by the conduct of the promisor himself, or by the conduct of any other person, is called a 'contract of indemnity'."*

### Parties involved (2 Parties):
1. **Indemnifier:** The person who promises to make good the loss or compensate the other party.
2. **Indemnity Holder (or Indemnified):** The person whose loss is to be made good, or the person protected by the promise.

**Example:**
*A* contracts to indemnify *B* against the consequences of any proceedings which *C* may take against *B* in respect of a certain sum of 200 rupees. This is a contract of indemnity.

---

## 3. Formation and Essential Features

A contract of indemnity is a species of the general contract. Therefore, it must possess all the essential elements of a valid contract (Section 10).

**5 Essential Features of a Contract of Indemnity:**

1. **Two Parties:** There must be at least two parties—the indemnifier and the indemnity holder.
2. **Promise to Save from Loss:** The core object is to protect the promisee from a loss.
3. **Loss Caused by Human Agency:** As per Section 124, the loss must be caused by the conduct of:
   - The promisor himself, or
   - Any other person (a third party).
   *(Note: The Indian definition does not strictly cover losses arising from accidents or acts of God (like fire or perils of the sea), although English Law includes them. However, Indian courts have often applied English principles to expand the scope to cover implied indemnities and other losses).*
4. **Valid Contract:** It must have free consent, lawful object, lawful consideration, and competent parties.
5. **Express or Implied:** An indemnity can be express (written/spoken) or implied from the circumstances of the case or the relationship of the parties (e.g., *Secretary of State for India v. Bank of India*).

---

## 4. Nature and Extent of Liability of the Indemnifier (Rights of Indemnity Holder)

The liability of the indemnifier is equivalent to the rights of the indemnity holder. These are enshrined in **Section 125**.

If the indemnity holder is sued within the scope of the indemnity, they are entitled to recover the following from the promisor (Indemnifier) - **(3 Types of Recoveries)**:

1. **All Damages (Section 125(1)):** All damages which he may be compelled to pay in any suit in respect of any matter to which the promise to indemnify applies.
2. **All Costs (Section 125(2)):** All costs which he may be compelled to pay in bringing or defending such suits.
   - *Condition:* He must not have contravened the orders of the promisor, and he must have acted as it would have been prudent for him to act in the absence of any contract of indemnity, or the promisor must have authorized him to bring or defend the suit.
3. **All Sums Paid under Compromise (Section 125(3)):** All sums which he may have paid under the terms of any compromise of any such suit.
   - *Condition:* The compromise must not be contrary to the orders of the promisor, and it must be one which it would have been prudent for the promisee to make, or the promisor must have authorized it.

---

## 5. Commencement of Liability of the Indemnifier

**When does the liability to pay arise?**
The Indian Contract Act is silent on the exact moment when the indemnifier's liability commences. Historically, under English common law, the rule was "you must be damnified before you can claim to be indemnified" (i.e., you must actually suffer/pay the loss before claiming it).

However, courts of equity established a more progressive rule, which Indian courts have adopted:
**The liability commences as soon as the liability of the indemnity holder becomes absolute and certain.**
The indemnity holder can compel the indemnifier to pay the claim or protect him even *before* he has actually paid the money out of his pocket. 

**Landmark Case:** *Gajanan Moreshwar Parelkar v. Moreshwar Madan Mantri (1942)*
- **Held:** The Bombay High Court held that the Indian Contract Act is not exhaustive. If the indemnity holder has incurred a liability which is absolute, he is entitled to ask the indemnifier to pay the amount or pay it into court. He need not wait to pay the amount himself and then sue the indemnifier.

## 6. Examples & Practical Applications of Indemnity

The syllabus specifies 4 common practical examples of indemnity:

1. **Indemnity for issuing duplicate share certificate, or bank deposit receipt:**
   When a shareholder loses their original share certificate, the company will issue a duplicate only if the shareholder signs an indemnity bond. This bond protects the company from any loss if the original certificate is found and misused by a third party.

2. **Indemnity by owner of a plot of land to a local authority:**
   When a landowner asks the local authority (Municipal Corporation) for a commencement certificate for construction, the authority may demand an indemnity bond. This protects the authority against any claims, damages, or neighbor disputes arising due to the permitted construction.

3. **Indemnity clause in a contract between a publisher and the printer:**
   A publisher may indemnify the printer against any liability for defamation, copyright infringement, or sedition arising from printing the book. If the printer is sued by an author for copyright infringement, the publisher will bear the loss.

4. **Indemnity clause in a sale deed of land:**
   A seller of land may include a clause indemnifying the buyer against any loss if a defect in the seller's title is discovered later, ensuring the buyer is protected if a third party claims ownership of the land.

---

## 7. Conclusion
A contract of indemnity is a foundational commercial safeguard. While the Indian Contract Act defines it restrictively (limiting it to human conduct), Indian courts have broadened its application to align with English equitable principles, ensuring robust protection for the indemnity holder once their liability becomes absolute.
