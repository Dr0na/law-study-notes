# Module 06: Contracts of Sale of Goods

**Statutory Provision:** The Sale of Goods Act, 1930

---

## 1. Definition and Essential Features

**Section 4(1)** defines a Contract of Sale of Goods as:
> *"A contract whereby the seller transfers or agrees to transfer the property in goods to the buyer for a price."*

### 6 Essential Features of a Contract of Sale:
1. **Two Parties:** Buyer (who buys or agrees to buy) and Seller (who sells or agrees to sell). A person cannot buy their own goods.
2. **Goods:** The subject matter must be "goods" (movable property). Actionable claims and money are excluded.
3. **Price:** The consideration must be money (price). If it is goods for goods, it is a barter. If it is partly goods and partly money, it is a sale.
4. **Transfer of Property:** "Property" here means ownership. There must be a transfer of ownership, not just possession (as in bailment).
5. **Absolute or Conditional:** The contract may be absolute or conditional.
6. **All essentials of a valid contract:** Free consent, competency, lawful object, etc.

---

## 2. Sale vs. Agreement to Sell

**Sale (Executed Contract):**
Where under a contract of sale the property in the goods is transferred from the seller to the buyer immediately, the contract is called a sale.
- *Risk:* Risk passes with property; buyer bears the loss if goods are destroyed.
- *Remedy for Breach:* Seller can sue for the price of the goods.

**Agreement to Sell (Executory Contract):**
Where the transfer of the property in the goods is to take place at a future time or subject to some condition thereafter to be fulfilled.
- *Risk:* Risk remains with the seller.
- *Remedy for Breach:* Seller can generally only sue for damages, not the price.

---

## 3. Goods: Meaning and Classification

**Section 2(7)** defines Goods: Every kind of movable property other than actionable claims and money; includes stock and shares, growing crops, grass, and things attached to or forming part of the land which are agreed to be severed before sale.

### Classification of Goods:
1. **Existing Goods:** Goods owned or possessed by the seller at the time of making the contract.
   - *Specific Goods:* Goods identified and agreed upon at the time a contract of sale is made.
   - *Ascertained Goods:* Goods identified subsequent to the formation of the contract.
   - *Unascertained Goods:* Goods defined by description only, not specifically identified.
2. **Future Goods:** Goods to be manufactured or produced or acquired by the seller after making the contract of sale.
3. **Contingent Goods:** Goods the acquisition of which by the seller depends upon a contingency which may or may not happen.

### Effect of Perishing of Goods
- **Before contract (Sec 7):** If specific goods perish before the contract is made without the seller's knowledge, the contract is void (mistake of fact).
- **Before sale but after agreement to sell (Sec 8):** The agreement is thereby avoided if the goods perish without fault on either side.

---

## 4. Conditions and Warranties

A stipulation in a contract of sale may be a condition or a warranty (Sec 12).

- **Condition:** A stipulation essential to the main purpose of the contract, the breach of which gives rise to a right to treat the contract as repudiated (cancelled) and claim damages.
- **Warranty:** A stipulation collateral to the main purpose of the contract, the breach of which gives rise to a claim for damages but not to a right to reject the goods.

**Implied Conditions (imposed by law):**
1. Condition as to Title (Sec 14): Seller has the right to sell.
2. Sale by Description (Sec 15): Goods must correspond with the description.
3. Sale by Sample (Sec 17): Bulk must correspond with the sample, buyer must have opportunity to compare.
4. Condition as to Quality or Fitness (Sec 16): Generally, no implied condition (Caveat Emptor), but applies if buyer relies on seller's skill and judgement for a specific purpose.
5. Condition as to Merchantability (Sec 16(2)).

**Implied Warranties:**
1. Warranty of quiet possession.
2. Warranty of freedom from encumbrances.

---

## 5. The Rule of 'Caveat Emptor'

**Meaning:** "Let the buyer beware."
It means that it is the buyer's duty to select the right goods. The seller is not bound to supply goods fit for any particular purpose of the buyer unless the buyer expressly makes it known.

**Exceptions to Caveat Emptor:**
1. Fitness for buyer's purpose (if made known to the seller and reliant on seller's skill).
2. Sale under a patent or trade name (no implied condition as to fitness, but merchantability applies).
3. Merchantable quality (for goods bought by description).
4. Usage of trade.
5. Consent obtained by fraud or active concealment.

---

## 6. Transfer of Title: Nemo Dat Quod Non Habet

**Rule (Sec 27):** "No one can give what he has not got." A seller cannot transfer a better title to the buyer than they themselves possess.

**Exceptions (where a non-owner can pass valid title):**
1. Sale by a mercantile agent (acting in ordinary course).
2. Sale by one of joint owners (Sec 28).
3. Sale by a person in possession under a voidable contract (Sec 29) before rescission.
4. Sale by one who has already sold the goods but continues in possession (Sec 30(1)).
5. Sale by buyer obtaining possession before the property in the goods has vested in him (Sec 30(2)).
6. Sale by an unpaid seller.

---

## 7. Delivery of Goods

Delivery means voluntary transfer of possession.
- **Actual Delivery:** Physical handover.
- **Symbolic Delivery:** Delivery of a symbol (e.g., keys to a warehouse).
- **Constructive Delivery (Attornment):** When a third person in possession acknowledges they hold it on behalf of the buyer.

**Rules regarding delivery:**
- Payment and delivery are concurrent conditions.
- Seller must deliver upon buyer's application.
- Delivery must be made at the agreed place and time.
- Buyer has the right to examine/inspect goods before accepting (Sec 41).

---

## 8. Rights of an Unpaid Seller

A seller is "unpaid" when the whole of the price has not been paid or tendered, or a bill of exchange has been dishonored (Sec 45).

**Rights against the Goods:**
1. **Right of Lien (Sec 47):** Right to retain possession until payment.
2. **Right of Stoppage in Transit (Sec 50):** Right to stop goods while they are in the course of transit to the buyer, if the buyer becomes insolvent.
3. **Right of Re-sale (Sec 54):** The seller may re-sell the goods if they are perishable or if notice is given to the buyer and buyer fails to pay within a reasonable time.

**Rights against the Buyer Personally:**
1. Suit for price (Sec 55).
2. Suit for damages for non-acceptance (Sec 56).
3. Suit for interest and special damages (Sec 61).

---

## 9. Auction Sales

**Rules for Auction Sales (Sec 64):**
- Where goods are put up in lots, each lot is prima facie deemed to be the subject of a separate contract.
- The sale is complete when the auctioneer announces its completion by the fall of the hammer or other customary manner. Until such announcement, any bidder may retract their bid.
- A right to bid may be reserved expressly by or on behalf of the seller.
- If the seller makes use of pretended bidding to raise the price, the sale is voidable at the option of the buyer.
