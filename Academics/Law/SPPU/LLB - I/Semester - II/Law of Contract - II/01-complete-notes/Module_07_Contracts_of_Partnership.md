# Module 07: Contracts of Partnership

**Statutory Provisions:** The Indian Partnership Act, 1932 and The Limited Liability Partnership Act, 2008

---

## 1. Meaning and Definition of Partnership

**Section 4 of the Indian Partnership Act, 1932:**
> *"Partnership is the relation between persons who have agreed to share the profits of a business carried on by all or any of them acting for all."*

- Persons who have entered into partnership with one another are called individually **"partners"** and collectively a **"firm"**, and the name under which their business is carried on is called the **"firm name"**.

---

## 2. Essential Elements of a Partnership

There are 5 essential elements to constitute a partnership:
1. **Association of Two or More Persons:** Must be at least two competent persons. The maximum limit is prescribed by the Companies Act (currently 50).
2. **Agreement:** Partnership arises from contract, not from status (e.g., Hindu Undivided Family is not a partnership).
3. **Business:** The agreement must be to carry on some business (trade, occupation, or profession). Joint ownership of property does not itself create a partnership.
4. **Sharing of Profits:** The agreement must be to share the profits of the business. (Sharing losses is not essential unless agreed).
5. **Mutual Agency (The True Test):** The business must be carried on by all or any of them acting for all. Every partner is both an agent and a principal for himself and other partners.

---

## 3. Position of a Minor as a Partner

**Section 30:** A minor is incompetent to contract and therefore cannot become a partner.
- However, with the consent of all adult partners, a minor may be admitted to the **benefits** of a partnership.
- The minor's share is liable for the acts of the firm, but the minor is **not personally liable**.
- Upon attaining majority, the minor has 6 months to elect whether to become a full partner or leave the firm. If they remain silent, they automatically become a partner and become personally liable retrospectively from the date of admission to benefits.

---

## 4. Relations of Partners to One Another

### Duties of Partners
1. To carry on business to the greatest common advantage, be just and faithful, and render true accounts (Sec 9).
2. To indemnify the firm for fraud (Sec 10).
3. To attend diligently to duties without remuneration (unless agreed) (Sec 12, 13).
4. To share losses equally or as agreed (Sec 13).
5. To hold and use firm property exclusively for the firm (Sec 15).
6. To account for personal profits derived from firm transactions or competing business (Sec 16).

### Rights of Partners
1. Right to take part in the conduct of the business (Sec 12).
2. Right to express an opinion and be consulted.
3. Right to have access to, inspect, and copy books of the firm.
4. Right to share profits equally or as agreed.
5. Right to interest on capital (if agreed) and interest on advances (at 6% p.a.).
6. Right to be indemnified for ordinary and emergency acts.

---

## 5. Relations of Partners to Third Parties

**Partner as Agent (Sec 18):** A partner is the agent of the firm for the purposes of the business of the firm.

**Implied Authority (Sec 19):** The authority of a partner to bind the firm. A partner can bind the firm by acts done in the usual course of business.
*Acts usually outside implied authority:* Submitting disputes to arbitration, opening a bank account in own name, compromising claims, withdrawing suits, admitting liability in a suit, acquiring/transferring immovable property.

**Liability of the Firm:**
1. The firm is liable for the acts of a partner done within the scope of their apparent authority (Sec 22).
2. Every partner is liable **jointly and severally** for all acts of the firm done while they are a partner (Sec 25).
3. Firm is liable for wrongful acts or omissions (torts) of a partner acting in the ordinary course of business (Sec 26).
4. Firm is liable for misapplication of money or property received from a third party by a partner (Sec 27).

---

## 6. Change in Constitution of a Firm

1. **Admission of a Partner (Sec 31):** Requires the consent of all existing partners. The new partner is not liable for acts done before their admission.
2. **Retirement (Sec 32):** A partner may retire with consent of all, or in accordance with an express agreement, or (in a partnership at will) by giving written notice. Must give public notice, otherwise continues to be liable to third parties.
3. **Expulsion (Sec 33):** A partner cannot be expelled unless the power is conferred by contract and exercised in good faith.
4. **Insolvency (Sec 34):** A partner ceases to be a partner on the date of the order of adjudication. The firm may or may not dissolve depending on the agreement.
5. **Death (Sec 35):** Does not necessarily dissolve the firm if agreed otherwise. The deceased's estate is not liable for future acts of the firm.

---

## 7. Dissolution of a Firm

Dissolution of partnership (change in relations) is different from the dissolution of the firm (complete breakdown and closure).

**Modes of Dissolution of the Firm:**
1. **Dissolution by Agreement (Sec 40):** With consent of all partners or in accordance with a contract.
2. **Compulsory Dissolution (Sec 41):** By adjudication of all partners (or all but one) as insolvent, or by the business becoming unlawful.
3. **Contingent Dissolution (Sec 42):** Subject to contract, on expiry of term, completion of venture, death of a partner, or insolvency of a partner.
4. **By Notice (Sec 43):** If partnership is at will.
5. **Dissolution by the Court (Sec 44):** On suit by a partner for reasons like: partner becoming of unsound mind, permanently incapable, guilty of misconduct affecting business, persistent breach of agreement, transfer of interest to third party, business cannot be carried on except at a loss, or just and equitable grounds.

---

## 8. Registration of Firms

Registration is **optional** under the Indian Partnership Act, but non-registration carries severe disabilities.

**Effects of Non-Registration (Sec 69):**
1. No suit in a civil court by a partner against the firm or other partners to enforce a right arising from a contract.
2. No suit in a civil court by the firm against any third party to enforce a right arising from a contract.
3. The firm or its partners cannot claim a set-off in a dispute with a third party.

*Exceptions:* Non-registration does not affect the right of third parties to sue the firm, or the right of partners to sue for dissolution and settlement of accounts.

---

## 9. Limited Liability Partnership (LLP) Act, 2008

An LLP is an alternative corporate business form that gives the benefits of limited liability of a company and the flexibility of a partnership.

### Essential Features of LLP
1. **Body Corporate:** It is a separate legal entity distinct from its partners.
2. **Perpetual Succession:** Changes in partners do not affect the existence of the LLP.
3. **Limited Liability:** A partner is not personally liable, directly or indirectly, for an obligation of the LLP, except in cases of fraud. No partner is liable for the independent or unauthorized actions of other partners (unlike mutual agency in ordinary partnership).
4. **Registration:** Mandatory to register with the Registrar of Companies (ROC).
5. **Agreement:** Rights and duties are governed by an LLP Agreement.

### Distinction: Ordinary Partnership vs. LLP

| Feature | Ordinary Partnership (1932 Act) | LLP (2008 Act) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Legal Status** | Not a separate legal entity. | Separate legal entity (Body Corporate). |
| **Liability** | Unlimited, joint and several. | Limited to agreed contribution (except fraud). |
| **Registration**| Optional. | Mandatory. |
| **Mutual Agency**| Every partner is an agent of the firm and other partners. | Partner is an agent of the LLP, but not of other partners. |
| **Perpetual Succession**| No. Death/insolvency may dissolve the firm. | Yes. Existence is unaffected by changes in partners. |
| **Maximum Limit**| 50 partners. | No maximum limit. |
