# Topper's Answer Writing Template

Scoring high marks in law exams requires more than just knowing the law; it requires presenting it structured, logical, and examiner-friendly manner.

## General Principles

1. **Use Explicit Counts:** Always write "There are 5 essential elements..." before listing them. It shows clarity and confidence.
2. **Underline Keywords:** Underline case names, section numbers, and legal maxims.
3. **Use Subheadings:** Avoid long paragraphs. Use clear, bold subheadings.
4. **Be Accurate:** If you forget a section number, write "According to the relevant provisions of the Indian Contract Act...". **Do not invent section numbers.**

## Template for a 15-Mark Descriptive Question

*(E.g., "Define Contract of Guarantee and explain the discharge of surety")*

**1. Introduction (3-4 lines)**
- Provide a brief context. 
- *Example:* "A contract of guarantee is a specific commercial contract designed to protect a creditor from the default of a debtor..."

**2. Statutory Meaning & Definition (Very Important)**
- Cite the section and act.
- *Example:* "Section 126 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872 defines a contract of guarantee as..."

**3. Parties Involved (If applicable)**
- Briefly list the parties. (e.g., Surety, Principal Debtor, Creditor).

**4. Essential Elements (Use counts)**
- *Example:* "The 5 essential elements of a valid contract of guarantee are:"
  1. Tripartite Agreement
  2. ...

**5. Main Body / Explanation (The core of the answer)**
- Break this down into headings as asked in the question.
- *Example:* **Discharge of Surety**
  - **A. By Revocation**
    - Section 130...
  - **B. By Conduct of Creditor**
    - Section 133 (Variance)...

**6. Case Laws (Sprinkled or dedicated section)**
- Provide: **Name of Case** -> **Brief Facts (2 lines)** -> **Principle Held (2 lines)**.
- *Example:* *State Bank of India vs. Mula Sahakari Sakhar Karkhana Ltd.* (Bank Guarantee principle).

**7. Conclusion (2-3 lines)**
- Summarize the crux.
- *Example:* "Therefore, while the surety's liability is co-extensive with the principal debtor, the law provides robust protections to discharge the surety if the creditor alters terms or prejudices the surety's rights."
