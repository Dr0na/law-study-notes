# Comparison: Bailment vs. Pledge

**Context:** Module 3 & Module 4 of Indian Contract Act, 1872.

| Basis of Difference | Bailment (Sec 148) | Pledge (Sec 172) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Meaning** | Delivery of goods for a specific purpose, to be returned when the purpose is accomplished. | Delivery of goods strictly as a security for payment of a debt or performance of a promise. |
| **Scope** | Bailment is a wider term. Every pledge is a bailment, but every bailment is not a pledge. | Pledge is a narrower term. It is a specific sub-type of bailment. |
| **Purpose** | Can be for various purposes: repair, safe custody, carriage, use, etc. | The sole purpose is to serve as **collateral / security** for a loan or promise. |
| **Right of Sale** | The bailee generally has **no right to sell** the goods. They only have a right of lien (right to retain possession until dues are paid). | The pawnee (pledgee) has the **statutory right to sell** the goods upon default by the pawnor, after giving reasonable notice. |
| **Use of Goods** | The bailee may use the goods if authorized by the contract or the nature of the bailment. | The pawnee has **no right to use** the pledged goods, as they are kept strictly as security. |
| **Consideration** | Bailment can be gratuitous (without reward) or non-gratuitous (for reward). | A pledge is always supported by consideration (the underlying debt or promise). |

---
**Exam Tip:**
When asked to distinguish between Pledge and Hypothecation as a sub-question: In a Pledge, possession transfers to the creditor. In Hypothecation, possession remains with the debtor.
