## 📦 Q4. Define Bailment. Discuss the rights and duties of a bailee in detail.

### 1. Introduction and Definition
A bailment involves the temporary handover of goods for a specific purpose without transferring ownership.
**Section 148** of the Indian Contract Act, 1872 defines Bailment as: 
*"The delivery of goods by one person to another for some purpose, upon a contract that they shall, when the purpose is accomplished, be returned or otherwise disposed of according to the directions of the person delivering them."*
- **Parties:** The person delivering is the *Bailor*, and the person receiving is the *Bailee*.

### 2. Duties of a Bailee
The law imposes strict duties on a bailee to protect the bailor's property:
1. **Duty to Take Reasonable Care (Sec 151):** The bailee is bound to take as much care of the bailed goods as a man of ordinary prudence would take of his own goods of similar bulk, quality, and value. If such care is taken, the bailee is not liable for loss or destruction (Sec 152).
2. **Duty Not to Make Unauthorized Use (Sec 154):** If the bailee uses the goods in a manner not authorized by the contract, they are liable for any damage arising from such use, even if they were not negligent.
3. **Duty Not to Mix Goods (Sec 155-157):** The bailee must keep the bailed goods separate from their own goods. If mixed without consent, the bailee bears the expense of separation or compensates for the loss if inseparable.
4. **Duty to Return the Goods (Sec 160):** The bailee must return the goods without demand as soon as the time for which they were bailed has expired, or the purpose has been accomplished.
5. **Duty to Return Accretions (Sec 163):** In the absence of a contract to the contrary, the bailee must deliver to the bailor any natural increase or profit accrued from the goods (e.g., a calf born to a bailed cow).

### 3. Rights of a Bailee
To ensure fairness, the bailee is also granted specific rights:
1. **Right to Recover Necessary Expenses (Sec 158):** In gratuitous bailments, the bailee can recover expenses incurred for the purpose of the bailment.
2. **Right to be Indemnified (Sec 164):** If the bailor has a defective title and the bailee suffers a loss as a result, the bailee is entitled to compensation.
3. **Right of Lien (Sec 170 & 171):** The bailee has the right to retain the goods until due charges are paid.
   - *Particular Lien:* Only against goods on which labor or skill was exercised.
   - *General Lien:* For any general balance of account (available only to specific groups like bankers, wharfingers).
4. **Right to Sue Third Parties (Sec 180):** If a third party wrongfully deprives the bailee of the goods, the bailee can bring a suit against them.

### 4. Conclusion
The contract of bailment places a heavy emphasis on trust. The bailee's duties revolve around acting prudently and faithfully returning the goods, while their rights primarily focus on recovering rightful expenses and remuneration through the potent tool of lien.
