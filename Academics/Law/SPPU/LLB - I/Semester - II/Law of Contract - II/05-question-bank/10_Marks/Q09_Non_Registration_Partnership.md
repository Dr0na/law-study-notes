## 🏢 Q9. Discuss the effect of non-registration of a partnership firm under the Indian Partnership Act, 1932.

### 1. Introduction
Under **Section 58** of the Indian Partnership Act, 1932, the registration of a firm is **optional**, not compulsory. There is no penalty for non-registration. However, the consequences of not registering are **severe** — they amount to practical disabilities that can cripple the firm's ability to enforce its legal rights.

### 2. Effects of Non-Registration (Sec 69)
**Section 69** lays down 3 major disabilities that attach to an unregistered firm:

#### I. No Suit by Partner Against Firm or Other Partners [Sec 69(1)]
No suit to enforce a right arising from a contract or conferred by the Partnership Act shall be instituted in any court by or on behalf of any person suing as a partner in a firm against the firm or any person alleged to be or to have been a partner in the firm, unless the firm is registered.
- *Example:* If A and B are unregistered partners and B refuses to share profits, A **cannot file a suit** to recover his share.

#### II. No Suit by Firm Against Third Parties [Sec 69(2)]
No suit to enforce a right arising from a contract shall be instituted in any court by or on behalf of a firm against any third party, unless the firm is registered.
- *Example:* If an unregistered firm sells goods to C and C refuses to pay, the firm **cannot sue C** for the price. (But C can sue the firm.)

#### III. No Right of Set-Off [Sec 69(3)]
A partner or the firm cannot claim a **set-off** (i.e., a claim that a sum owed by one party can be offset against a sum owed to them) in any proceeding instituted against the firm or a partner.

### 3. Exceptions to Sec 69 (Rights NOT affected by non-registration)
Non-registration does NOT affect the following:
1. ✅ **Third parties can sue** the unregistered firm or its partners.
2. ✅ **Right of a partner to sue for dissolution** of the firm and settlement of accounts (Sec 69(3)(a)).
3. ✅ **Right of a partner to sue the official assignee** or receiver of an insolvent partner for realization of their property.
4. ✅ **Power of an insolvent partner** to realize the property of the firm.
5. ✅ **Rights arising from a contract where the value does not exceed ₹100.**
6. ✅ **Suits not arising out of a contract** (e.g., tort actions).

### 4. Conclusion
While registration is voluntary, it is **practically essential.** An unregistered firm exists in a legally disadvantaged position — it can be sued but cannot sue. For any firm that anticipates commercial disputes or needs to enforce contracts, registration is an indispensable first step.
