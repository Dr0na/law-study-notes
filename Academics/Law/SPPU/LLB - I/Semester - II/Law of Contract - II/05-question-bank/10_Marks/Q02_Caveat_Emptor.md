## 🛒 Q2. Explain the rule of 'Caveat Emptor' (Let the buyer beware). What are the exceptions to this rule?

### 1. Meaning of Caveat Emptor
**Section 16** of the Sale of Goods Act, 1930, enshrines the maxim *Caveat Emptor*, which translates to "Let the buyer beware." 
It implies that it is the responsibility of the buyer to carefully examine the goods before purchasing them. The seller is not obligated to point out defects or ensure the goods suit the buyer's specific purpose. If the buyer makes a bad choice, they cannot blame the seller.

### 2. Exceptions to the Rule (When the Seller is Liable)
The strictness of this rule has been diluted by modern commerce. The exceptions are:

1. **Fitness for Buyer's Purpose [Sec 16(1)]:** 
   If the buyer makes known to the seller the particular purpose for which goods are required, and relies on the seller's skill or judgment, there is an implied condition that the goods shall be reasonably fit for that purpose. 
   *(Case: Grant vs. Australian Knitting Mills - Buyer relied on seller's shop for safe underwear).*
2. **Sale under Patent/Trade Name:** 
   If goods are bought under a brand name, there is no implied condition regarding fitness for a particular purpose (buyer relied on the brand, not the seller).
3. **Merchantable Quality [Sec 16(2)]:** 
   Where goods are bought by description from a seller who deals in such goods, there is an implied condition that they must be of merchantable quality (saleable and fit for ordinary use).
4. **Usage of Trade [Sec 16(3)]:** 
   An implied condition as to quality or fitness may be annexed by the usage of trade.
5. **Consent by Fraud/Concealment:** 
   If the seller obtains consent by fraud or actively conceals a defect that could not be discovered upon reasonable examination, the rule does not apply.
6. **Sale by Sample (Sec 17):** 
   The bulk must correspond with the sample. If the sample has a hidden defect (not discoverable by ordinary inspection), the seller is liable.

### 3. Conclusion
While *Caveat Emptor* forces buyers to be diligent, the exceptions (especially fitness and merchantability) provide a heavy counterbalance, shifting the burden to the seller to act honestly and professionally in trade.
