## 💍 Q7. What is a Pledge? Discuss the rights and duties of a Pawnee. Under what circumstances can a non-owner make a valid pledge?

### 1. Definition
**Section 172** of the Indian Contract Act, 1872:
*"The bailment of goods as security for payment of a debt or performance of a promise is called 'pledge'."*
- **Pawnor (Pledgor):** The person who deposits goods as security.
- **Pawnee (Pledgee):** The person who takes the goods as security.

### 2. Rights of the Pawnee

#### I. Right of Retainer (Sec 173 & 174)
The pawnee may retain the goods pledged, not only for:
- Payment of the **debt or performance of the promise**, but also
- The **interest** of the debt, and
- All **necessary expenses** incurred for the preservation of the goods.
⚠️ However, the pawnee cannot retain the goods for any debt or promise other than the one for which they were pledged (unless there is a contract to the contrary).

#### II. Right to Extraordinary Expenses (Sec 175)
The pawnee is entitled to receive from the pawnor extraordinary expenses incurred for the preservation of the goods pledged.

#### III. Rights on Default of Pawnor (Sec 176) — Most Important
If the pawnor makes default in payment, the pawnee has **two remedies:**
1. 📋 **Sue the pawnor** for the amount due and retain the goods as collateral security; OR
2. 🔨 **Sell the goods** after giving the pawnor **reasonable notice** of the sale.
- If the proceeds of the sale exceed the debt, the pawnee must return the **surplus** to the pawnor.
- If the proceeds fall short, the pawnor is liable to pay the **deficit**.

### 3. Duties of the Pawnee
1. **Take reasonable care** of the pledged goods (same standard as bailment — Sec 151).
2. **Not to use** the pledged goods (they are kept strictly as security, not for the pawnee's personal benefit).
3. **Not to mix** the pledged goods with the pawnee's own goods.
4. **Return the goods** (along with any natural accretions/profits) upon the payment of the debt.

### 4. Pledge by Non-Owners (Exceptions to *Nemo dat quod non habet*)
The general rule is that only the true owner can pledge goods. However, the law provides 3 exceptions to protect innocent pawnees:

#### I. Pledge by Mercantile Agent (Sec 178)
A mercantile agent who is, **with the consent of the owner**, in possession of goods or documents of title, can make a valid pledge while acting in the **ordinary course of business**, provided the pawnee acts in **good faith** and without notice of the agent's lack of authority.

#### II. Pledge by Person Under Voidable Contract (Sec 178A)
If a person has obtained possession of goods under a **voidable contract** (e.g., by fraud) and pledges the goods **before the contract is rescinded (cancelled)**, the pawnee acquires a good title, provided they act in good faith and without notice.
- ⚠️ If the contract is void (not voidable), this exception does NOT apply (e.g., goods obtained by a minor's contract or under a fundamental mistake).

#### III. Pledge by Person with Limited Interest (Sec 179)
Where a person pledges goods in which they have only a **limited interest**, the pledge is valid to the extent of that interest.
- *Example:* A finder of goods who has spent money on their preservation can pledge the goods to the extent of their expenses.

### 5. Conclusion
The rights of the pawnee ensure that the security provided by the pledge is commercially meaningful — the pawnee can retain, sue, or sell. The exceptions for non-owner pledges balance the true owner's property rights against the need to protect innocent commercial parties.
