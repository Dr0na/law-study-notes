### Q12. Write a short note on: Bank Guarantees.

**📖 Context:** Module 2 — Contracts of Guarantee (Indian Contract Act, 1872).

**1. Meaning:**
A bank guarantee is an undertaking by a bank (the guarantor) on behalf of its customer (the applicant/principal debtor) to pay a specified amount of money to the beneficiary (creditor) if the customer defaults on a contractual obligation.

**2. Nature:**
- A bank guarantee is an **independent** and **autonomous** contract between the bank and the beneficiary.
- It is separate from the underlying contract between the customer and the beneficiary.
- The bank must pay on demand if the terms of the guarantee are met, **regardless of disputes** in the underlying contract.

**3. Key Legal Principles:**
- 🛡️ **Absolute Obligation:** The bank's obligation to pay is absolute once the demand is made in conformity with the guarantee.
- 🚫 **No Injunction (General Rule):** Courts will NOT ordinarily grant an injunction restraining the encashment of a bank guarantee.

**4. Exceptions (When Court MAY Intervene):**
The courts will interfere only in **two exceptional circumstances**:
1. **Egregious Fraud:** Fraud of an egregious nature which vitiates the entire underlying transaction (established fraud, not mere allegation).
2. **Irretrievable Injustice:** Special equities in favor of the applicant which would make it unconscionably unjust to allow encashment.

**5. Landmark Case:**
*(Case: State Bank of India vs. Mula Sahakari Sakhar Karkhana Ltd.)* — Supreme Court reaffirmed that bank guarantees are independent contracts and courts should not lightly interfere with their enforcement.

**🎯 Conclusion:** Bank guarantees are vital commercial instruments that provide certainty of payment in trade. The law strictly upholds their enforceability to preserve confidence in the banking and commercial systems.
