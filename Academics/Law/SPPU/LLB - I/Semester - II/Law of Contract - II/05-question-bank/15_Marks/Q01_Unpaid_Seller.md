## ⚖️ Q1. Who is an unpaid seller? Discuss in detail the rights of an unpaid seller against the goods and against the buyer personally, with relevant provisions of the Sale of Goods Act, 1930.

### 1. Introduction
The Sale of Goods Act, 1930, recognizes that a seller who has parted with their goods but has not received the price must be protected. The law grants such an "unpaid seller" specific potent rights to recover their dues.

### 2. Definition of Unpaid Seller
**Section 45** of the Sale of Goods Act defines an unpaid seller. A seller of goods is deemed to be an "unpaid seller" when:
1. The whole of the price has not been paid or tendered.
2. A bill of exchange or other negotiable instrument has been received as conditional payment, and the condition has not been fulfilled by reason of the dishonor of the instrument or otherwise.

### 3. Rights of an Unpaid Seller
The rights of an unpaid seller are broadly classified into two categories:
**A. Rights against the goods (Sec 46)**
**B. Rights against the buyer personally**

---

### A. Rights Against the Goods (Jus in Rem)
Even if the property (ownership) in the goods has passed to the buyer, the unpaid seller has three rights against the goods:

#### I. Right of Lien (Sections 47-49)
**Meaning:** The right to retain possession of the goods until payment or tender of the price.
**Conditions:**
- The seller must be in possession of the goods.
- Goods sold without any stipulation as to credit.
- Goods sold on credit, but the term of credit has expired.
- The buyer becomes insolvent.
**Termination of Lien (Sec 49):** The lien is lost when:
- Seller delivers the goods to a carrier for transmission without reserving the right of disposal.
- The buyer or his agent lawfully obtains possession.
- By waiver of the lien.

#### II. Right of Stoppage in Transit (Sections 50-52)
**Meaning:** The right of stopping the goods while they are in transit, resuming possession, and retaining them until payment.
**Conditions:**
- The seller must be unpaid.
- The property must have passed to the buyer.
- The goods must be in the course of transit (neither with seller nor buyer).
- **The buyer must have become insolvent.**
**Transit ends when:** The buyer or their agent takes delivery from the carrier.
*(Case: Great Indian Peninsula Railway Co. vs. Hanmandas)*.

#### III. Right of Resale (Section 54)
**Meaning:** The unpaid seller who has exercised the right of lien or stoppage in transit can resell the goods.
**Conditions for valid resale:**
- The goods are of a perishable nature (no notice required).
- The seller gives notice to the buyer of their intention to resell, and the buyer does not pay within a reasonable time.
- If the resale fetches a lower price, the seller can recover the deficit from the original buyer. If it fetches a higher price, the seller keeps the profit.

---

### B. Rights Against the Buyer Personally (Jus in Personam)

#### I. Suit for Price (Section 55)
- Where property in goods has passed to the buyer, and they wrongfully neglect or refuse to pay according to the terms.
- Where the price is payable on a certain day irrespective of delivery, and the buyer fails to pay.

#### II. Suit for Damages for Non-Acceptance (Section 56)
- Where the buyer wrongfully neglects or refuses to accept and pay for the goods, the seller may sue for damages for non-acceptance.

#### III. Suit for Interest and Special Damages (Section 61)
- The court may award interest on the price from the date of the tender of the goods or from the date on which the price was payable.

### 4. Conclusion
The Sale of Goods Act provides a comprehensive protective umbrella for an unpaid seller. The rights against the goods (lien and stoppage) ensure that the seller does not lose both the goods and the money, while personal rights ensure legal recourse for damages and interest.
