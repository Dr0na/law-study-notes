## 🤝 Q2. Define Contract of Agency. Discuss in detail the various modes by which an agency can be created. Explain the maxim *Delegatus non potest delegare*.

### 1. Introduction and Definition
Agency is a relationship where one person represents another in legal and commercial dealings.
**Section 182** of the Indian Contract Act defines: 
*"An agent is a person employed to do any act for another, or to represent another in dealings with third persons. The person for whom such act is done, or who is so represented, is called the principal."*
**Maxims:** 
- *Qui facit per alium facit per se* (He who acts through another acts himself).

### 2. Creation of Agency
A contract of agency does not require any consideration (Sec 185). It can be created in the following 6 ways:

#### I. Express Agreement (Sec 187)
- Authority is given by words spoken or written.
- *Example:* A Power of Attorney. A executes a written document authorizing B to sell his house.

#### II. Implied Agreement (Sec 187)
- Authority is inferred from the circumstances of the case, the ordinary course of dealing, or the conduct of parties.
- *Example:* A owns a shop, managed by B. B frequently orders goods from C in A's name, and A pays for them. B has implied authority from A to order goods from C.

#### III. Agency by Estoppel (Sec 237)
- When a person, by their conduct or words, willfully leads a third person to believe that a certain person is their agent, they are "estopped" (prevented) from later denying the agency.
- *Example:* X tells Y in the presence of Z that X is Z's agent. Z stays silent. Later, X buys goods from Y on Z's credit. Z is liable to pay Y.

#### IV. Agency by Holding Out
- Similar to estoppel. It requires some affirmative conduct by the principal over time. If a master generally allows his servant to buy goods on credit, the master is liable if the servant misuses this authority, because the master "held him out" as an agent.

#### V. Agency of Necessity (Sec 189)
- Created under pressing emergency circumstances to save the principal's property from loss.
- **Conditions:** There must be a real emergency, inability to communicate with the principal, and the agent must act in good faith.
- *Example:* A carrier is transporting perishable tomatoes. Due to a landslide, the journey halts. The carrier sells the tomatoes locally to prevent them from rotting. The carrier becomes an agent of necessity.

#### VI. Agency by Ratification (Sec 196)
- When an act is done by a person on behalf of another, but without their authority, the person on whose behalf the act is done may elect to ratify or disown it.
- **Effect:** Ratification relates back to the date of the act.
- *Condition:* The agent must have professed to act as an agent. *(Case: Keighley, Maxsted & Co. vs. Durant)*.

### 3. Maxim: *Delegatus non potest delegare*
**Meaning:** A delegated power cannot be further delegated.
**Explanation (Sec 190):** An agent cannot lawfully employ another to perform acts which he has expressly or impliedly undertaken to perform personally. The principal appointed the agent based on personal trust and skill.
**Exceptions (When a sub-agent CAN be appointed):**
1. **Trade Custom:** If it is customary in that specific trade (e.g., architects hiring surveyors).
2. **Nature of Work:** If the work is purely clerical or ministerial requiring no special skill.
3. **Emergency:** To protect the principal from unforeseen loss.
4. **Consent:** If the principal expressly or impliedly consents to the delegation.

### 4. Conclusion
Agency is a highly flexible legal relationship that facilitates global commerce. While an agency is usually formed by express mandate, the law accommodates commercial realities by recognizing implied, necessary, and ratified agencies, while strictly protecting the principal through rules against unauthorized delegation.
