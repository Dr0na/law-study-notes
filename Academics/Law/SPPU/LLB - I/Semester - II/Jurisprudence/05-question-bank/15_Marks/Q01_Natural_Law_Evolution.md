# Q01: Natural Law Theories

## 📝 Question
Trace the evolution of Natural Law theories from the classical period to its modern revival. (15 Marks)

---

## 🧾 MODEL ANSWER

### 1. 🧾 Answer Synopsis
- **Introduction**: Definition of Natural Law (Morality + Reason).
- **Classical Period**: Plato, Aristotle, Aquinas.
- **Social Contract Period**: Hobbes, Locke, Rousseau.
- **Decline and Revival**: 19th-century decline and 20th-century revival (Fuller, Finnis).
- **Conclusion**: Enduring relevance of justice.

### 2. 🧠 Introduction
Natural Law is the philosophy that certain rights or values are inherent by virtue of human nature and can be universally understood through human reason. It is the "Higher Law" that serves as a benchmark for man-made laws.

### 3. 📖 Body
The evolution of Natural Law can be divided into four major stages:

#### A. Ancient/Classical Period
- **Greek Thinkers**: Plato and Aristotle viewed nature as a source of justice.
- **St. Thomas Aquinas**: The peak of medieval Natural Law. He synthesized reason with faith. He divided law into Eternal, Natural, Divine, and Human.

#### B. The Social Contract Period (Renaissance)
- **Hugo Grotius**: Secularized Natural Law.
- **Thomas Hobbes**: Natural law as a tool for survival and security under an absolute sovereign.
- **John Locke**: Natural law as the source of inalienable rights (Life, Liberty, Property).
- **Rousseau**: Natural law expressed through the "General Will."

#### C. The Decline (19th Century)
With the rise of the Industrial Revolution and Scientific Method, **Legal Positivism** (Austin, Bentham) became dominant. Natural Law was dismissed as "nonsense upon stilts."

#### D. The Modern Revival (20th Century)
The horrors of the World Wars led to a realization that law without morality can lead to tyranny.
- **Lon Fuller**: Focus on the "Internal Morality of Law" (Procedural fairness).
- **John Finnis**: A modern restatement based on "Basic Human Goods."
- **H.L.A. Hart**: Admitted a "Minimum Content of Natural Law."

### 4. ⚖️ Case Laws
- **Nuremberg Trials**: Applied Natural Law principles to punish "crimes against humanity" which were legal under the then-existing Nazi laws.
- **A.K. Gopalan v. State of Madras**: Early Indian case discussing "Procedure established by law" vs "Due process" (Natural Justice).

### 6. 🧾 Conclusion
Natural Law has survived for over 2,500 years because it addresses the fundamental human quest for justice. Today, it forms the backbone of the **International Human Rights** movement and the "Basic Structure" doctrine in India.
