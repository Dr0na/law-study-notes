# Q02: Ratio Decidendi vs Obiter Dicta

## 📝 Question
Distinguish between Ratio Decidendi and Obiter Dicta. (5 Marks)

---

## 🧾 MODEL ANSWER

### 1. 🧾 Answer Synopsis
- **Ratio**: The "Reason" (Binding).
- **Obiter**: "By the way" (Persuasive).
- **Key Differences**: Authority, Necessity, Precedent.

### 2. 🧠 Introduction
A judicial judgment consists of various parts. For the purpose of Precedent, it is essential to distinguish between what is binding and what is merely illustrative.

### 3. 📖 Body (Comparison Table)

| Basis | ⚖️ Ratio Decidendi | 🗣️ Obiter Dicta |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Meaning** | The "Reason for the decision." | "Things said by the way." |
| **Binding Force** | Binding on lower courts. | Not binding; only persuasive. |
| **Necessity** | Essential to decide the case. | Not essential to the decision. |
| **Precedent** | Creates a binding precedent. | Does not create a precedent. |
| **Authority** | High legal authority. | Lower authority (unless from SC). |

### 4. ⚖️ Case Laws
- **B. Shama Rao v. UT of Pondicherry**: Held that it is the *Ratio* that is binding under Article 141, not every word in a judgment.

### 5. 🧾 Conclusion
Identifying the *Ratio* is the primary task of a lawyer. While *Obiter* can be helpful for arguments, only the *Ratio* has the force of law.
