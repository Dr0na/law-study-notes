# Distinguish — Natural Law vs Positive Law

**ID:** JUR-D01

## MODEL ANSWER

### 🧠 Introduction

**Natural law** holds that law derives its binding force from its conformity to a higher moral order discoverable by reason. **Positive law** is law that has been *laid down* — *positum* — by human authority. The split is the oldest controversy in jurisprudence.

### 📊 Table of distinctions

| 🔍 Basis | Natural Law | Positive Law |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Source of authority | Moral order, reason, divine will | Sovereign, constitution, social source |
| Validity depends on | Moral content | Source / pedigree |
| Famous maxim | *Lex iniusta non est lex* | *Lex est lex* |
| Key thinkers | Plato, Aristotle, Aquinas, Grotius, Hobbes, Locke, Rousseau, Fuller, Finnis | Bentham, Austin, Hart, Kelsen |
| Method | Normative, reasoning from human nature | Analytical, descriptive |
| Approach to unjust laws | May deny them the title of "law" | Recognises them as valid law (citizens may have moral grounds to disobey) |
| Strength | Moral check; foundation of human rights | Predictability; clarity |
| Weakness | Disagreement on moral content | Cannot judge wicked-but-valid law |
| Indian touch | Basic-structure doctrine; *Kesavananda* | Routine statutory interpretation |

### 🧾 Conclusion

Modern jurisprudence has moved towards a **dialogue** between the two — Hart concedes a *minimum content* of natural law; Fuller relocates natural law to internal morality. The exam-friendly answer: *natural law tells us when to challenge positive law; positive law tells us what is presently in force.*
