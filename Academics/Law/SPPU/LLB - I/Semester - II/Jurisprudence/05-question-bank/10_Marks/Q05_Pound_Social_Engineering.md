### 🧠 Introduction

Roscoe Pound (1870–1964), American jurist, founded the **sociological school**. He treated law as a **social institution** that engineers competing interests with minimum friction and waste. His phrase **"law as social engineering"** has become a slogan for instrumentalist jurisprudence.

### 📖 The engineer’s task

Pound likened the lawyer to an **engineer** who must reconcile, satisfy, or compromise the interests of competing parties. Just as an engineer balances loads, the law balances **claims, demands, and desires**.

### 📖 Classification of interests (IPS)

1. **Individual interests** — personality, domestic relations, substance (property and contract).
2. **Public interests** — interests of the state as juristic person and as guardian.
3. **Social interests** — general security, security of social institutions, general morals, conservation of social resources, general progress, individual life.

### 📖 Jural postulates of civilised society

Pound articulated five **jural postulates** that civilised people are entitled to assume:

1. Others will not commit intentional aggression against them.
2. Others will allow them to control resources they have acquired peacefully.
3. Others will keep their reasonable promises.
4. Others will act with due care so as not to cause unreasonable risk of harm.
5. Others will keep dangerous things they maintain on their land from escaping.

### 📖 Application to India

- **Public Interest Litigation** developed by the Indian Supreme Court is a textbook application of Pound — courts engineer relief for unrepresented social interests (*S.P. Gupta v UoI*, 1981; *Bandhua Mukti Morcha v UoI*, 1984).
- The **welfare-state directive principles (Articles 38–51)** translate sociological aims into constitutional text.
- *M.C. Mehta v UoI* (1987) — courts engineer environmental protection by recognising absolute liability for hazardous industry.

### 🔻 Criticism

- **Vague** — the metaphor of engineering does not tell us *how* to balance interests.
- **Underestimates conflict** — social interests cannot always be reconciled; sometimes one must override another.
- **Pro-judicial activism** critique — gives judges too much policy power.

### 🧾 Conclusion

Pound’s theory remains a powerful lens for understanding modern welfare law. India’s constitutional jurisprudence — directive principles, PIL, public-interest absolute liability — reads like a casebook of Pound’s social engineering.
