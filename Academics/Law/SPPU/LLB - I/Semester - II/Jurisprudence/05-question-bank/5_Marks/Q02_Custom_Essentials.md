# Q02 (5 Marks) — Essentials of a valid custom

**Module:** 02 — Sources of Law
**Marks:** 5
**Difficulty:** 🟢 Easy
**ID:** JUR-5M-Q002

## MODEL ANSWER

### 🧠 Introduction

A **custom** is a uniform practice of a community followed for so long that it has acquired the force of law. To be enforced, six **essential conditions** (mnemonic **ACPRCC**) must be satisfied.

### 📖 Essentials (6)

1. **Antiquity** — observed from time immemorial. English common law fixes the date at 1189; Indian courts accept reasonable antiquity.
2. **Continuity** — practised without interruption.
3. **Peaceable enjoyment** — *nec vi, nec clam, nec precario* (without force, secrecy, or permission).
4. **Reasonableness** — must satisfy a rational person’s sense of fairness.
5. **Certainty** — clear and definite in scope and content.
6. **Consistency** with statute and public policy — custom contrary to express law is void.

### ⚖️ Recognition in India

Hindu and Muslim personal laws recognise custom; *Article 13(3)(a)* of the Constitution includes "custom or usage having the force of law" in the definition of *law*. Courts are reluctant to enforce customs against fundamental rights (e.g., abolition of *Devadasi* practices).

### 🧾 Conclusion

Custom remains the **oldest source** of law and continues to operate where statute is silent — provided all six essentials are proved.
