# Q01 (5 Marks) — Briefly explain the sources of law

**Module:** 02 — Sources of Law
**Marks:** 5
**Difficulty:** 🟢 Easy
**ID:** JUR-5M-Q001

## MODEL ANSWER

### 🧠 Introduction

A **source of law** is anything that gives a rule its binding force. Salmond divides sources into **formal** (the authority that validates the rule) and **material** (where the content comes from). In modern legal systems the four material sources are **custom, legislation, precedent, and juristic writings (CLPJ)**.

### 📖 Body

1. **Custom.** Uniform practice of a community followed since time immemorial, satisfying six essentials — antiquity, continuity, peaceable enjoyment, reasonableness, certainty, and consistency with statute (**ACPRCC**). Survives in personal laws and trade usage.
2. **Legislation.** The deliberate making of rules by the legislature. Supreme legislation (Parliament/State legislature) and subordinate legislation (delegated rules). Today, the dominant source.
3. **Precedent.** The ratio decidendi of a higher court binds lower courts (*stare decisis*). In India, **Article 141** makes the Supreme Court’s law binding on all courts.
4. **Juristic writings.** Treatises by Salmond, Pollock, Hart, and Mulla act as persuasive secondary authority.

### 🧾 Conclusion

The four sources operate in a **layered authority** — legislation primary, precedent dominant in courts, custom residual, juristic writings persuasive.
