# 📘 Module 05: Sociological, Realist, Historical Schools of Law and Critical Legal Studies

🎚️ Difficulty: 🟡 Medium

---

### 🧠 Introduction

While Positivism focuses on the *structure* of law, these schools focus on the *context* of law—its history, its social impact, and the reality of how judges decide cases.

---

### 📖 Main Content

#### 1. Sociological School: Roscoe Pound

- **Core Idea**: Law is a tool for **Social Engineering**.
- **Social Engineering**: The process of balancing competing interests in society (Individual, Public, and Social interests) to achieve maximum satisfaction with minimum friction.
- **Jural Postulates**: The basic assumptions on which a civilized society is based (e.g., people should not commit intentional aggression).

#### 2. Realist School: American Realism

- **Core Idea**: "Law is what the judge says it is."
- **Focus**: The actual practice of courts rather than the rules in books.
- **Thinkers**:
  - **Oliver Wendell Holmes**: "The life of the law has not been logic; it has been experience." (The **Bad Man Theory** - a bad man only cares about what the court will do).
  - **Karl Llewellyn**: Focus on "Law Jobs" (resolving disputes).
  - **Jerome Frank**: **Fact-Skepticism** (judges are influenced by their own biases and the way facts are presented).

#### 3. Historical School: Savigny and Maine

- **Savigny**: Law is not made; it grows. It is found in the **Volkgeist** (Spirit of the people). Law is like language—it evolves with the people.
- **Sir Henry Maine**: Studied the evolution of legal systems.
  - **Status to Contract**: In progressive societies, the legal position of an individual moves from being determined by birth/status (e.g., slave, son) to being determined by voluntary agreement (Contract).

#### 4. Critical Legal Studies (CLS)

A modern movement that challenges the "neutrality" of law, arguing that law is politics.

- **Feminist Legal Theory**: Analyzes how law has historically been used to subordinate women and advocates for gender equality in legal structures.
- **Critical Race Theory**: Examines how law and legal institutions are used to maintain racial hierarchies and systemic racism.

📐 **Diagram: Comparison of Schools**
| School | Primary Focus | Key Concept |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Historical** | Past/Tradition | Volkgeist (Savigny) |
| **Sociological** | Social Impact | Social Engineering (Pound) |
| **Realist** | Court Practice | Judge-made reality (Holmes) |
| **CLS** | Power/Politics | Law is not neutral |

---

### ⚖️ Case Laws

- **Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala**: An example of "Social Engineering" where the court balanced the state's power to amend the Constitution with the protection of its "Basic Structure."

---

### 🧾 Conclusion

These schools remind us that law does not exist in a vacuum. It is shaped by history, driven by social needs, and interpreted by human beings with their own perspectives.
