# Module 4: Legal Positivism

## Introduction

Legal Positivism is a school of jurisprudence that views law as **separate from morality**. Positivists believe that law is what is **"posited" (laid down) by human authority**, not what ought to be based on morality. The key principle: **"Law is law, regardless of whether it's moral or immoral."** This module covers three major positivist thinkers: Bentham and Austin (classical positivism), Hart (modern positivism), and Kelsen (pure theory).

---

## 1. Bentham and John Austin's Theory of Positivism

### A. JEREMY BENTHAM (1748-1832) 📊

**English Philosopher - Father of Utilitarianism and Legal Positivism**

**Main Work:** "An Introduction to the Principles of Morals and Legislation" (1789)

#### **1. Utilitarianism - Foundation of Bentham's Theory** ⚖️

**Principle of Utility (Greatest Happiness Principle):**
**"The greatest happiness of the greatest number"**

**What it means:**
- Actions are right if they produce happiness
- Actions are wrong if they produce pain/suffering
- Measure goodness by consequences (pleasure vs pain)
- Aim: Maximum pleasure for maximum people

**Felicific Calculus:**
Bentham created a method to calculate pleasure and pain:
- Intensity
- Duration  
- Certainty
- Proximity
- Fecundity (leads to more pleasure?)
- Purity (unmixed with pain?)
- Extent (how many affected?)

#### **2. Law Should Maximize Utility** 💡

**Bentham's view of law:**
- Law is a tool for achieving greatest happiness
- Good law = produces maximum happiness
- Bad law = produces more pain than pleasure
- Law should be judged by consequences

**But:**
- Law's validity NOT dependent on being moral
- Even bad law is law (if made by proper authority)
- Should be changed, but it's still law

#### **3. Separation of Law as "Is" and "Ought"** 📖

**Two Questions:**
- **"What is law?"** - Expository jurisprudence (descriptive)
- **"What ought law to be?"** - Censorial jurisprudence (prescriptive)

**Bentham's Point:**
- Must distinguish between describing law and evaluating law
- Law "is" separate from what law "ought to be"
- Foundation of positivist thinking

#### **4. Codification** 📋

**Bentham's project:**
- Favored comprehensive codes
- Law should be clear and accessible
- System of punishment based on utility

---

### B. JOHN AUSTIN (1790-1859) ⚖️

**English Jurist - Father of Analytical Jurisprudence**

**Main Work:** "The Province of Jurisprudence Determined" (1832)

**Most Influential Positivist - His theory dominated for century**

### Austin's Command Theory of Law

#### **1. Law is Command** 📜

**Austin's Famous Definition:**
**"Law is the command of the sovereign backed by sanction"**

**Four Elements of Law:**

**A. Command** 🗣️
**What it means:** Expression of wish/desire that another should do/not do something.

- Not request or advice
- Order backed by threat
- Example: "Pay taxes or face penalty"

**B. Sovereign** 👑
**Who is sovereign:**
- Person or body whom people habitually obey
- But who obeys no one else
- Ultimate political superior

**In Democracy:** Parliament/Legislature is sovereign  
**In Monarchy:** King/Queen is sovereign  

**Characteristics of Sovereign:**
- **Determinate** (specific, identifiable)
- **Habitually obeyed** by bulk of population
- **Obeys no one** (not subject to any authority)
- **Unlimited power** (no legal limits)

**C. Duty** ⚖️
When sovereign commands, subjects have duty to obey.

**D. Sanction** ⚠️
**What it is:** Threat of punishment for disobedience.

- Evil/harm threatened
- For not obeying command
- Example: Imprisonment, fine

**Austin's Formula:**
**Law = Command + Sovereign + Duty + Sanction**

---

#### **2. Classification of Law**

**Austin distinguished:**

**A. Law Properly So Called:**

**i. Divine Law:**
- Commands of God
- For humans
- Through revelation

**ii. Positive Law:**
- Commands of human sovereign
- What we normally call "law"
- Austin's main focus

**B. Law Improperly So Called:**

**i. Positive Morality:**
- Social rules not commanded by sovereign
- Custom, etiquette
- Not law in Austin's strict sense

**ii. Laws by Metaphor:**
- Laws of nature (gravity, motion)
- Not law in Austin's sense

**Austin's Strict View:**
Only Positive Law (sovereign's commands) is true law. Rest are called "law" loosely or metaphorically.

---

#### **3. Types of Positive Law**

**A. Laws of Command (Imperative):**
- Direct commands: "Do this" or "Don't do this"
- Create duties

**B. Laws of Revocation:**
- Repeal previous commands
- Remove duties

**C. Laws of Permission (Permissive):**
- Allow what was prohibited
- Grant liberty

**D. Laws Declaratory:**
- Declare or explain existing law
- Don't create new law

---

#### **4. Separation of Law and Morality** 🔀

**Austin's Clear Position:**
- Law and morality are **separate and distinct**
- Law's validity doesn't depend on morality
- Even immoral law is law (if commanded by sovereign)

**Austin's Distinction:**
- **Law as it is** (what law actually is) - Expository jurisprudence
- **Law as it ought to be** (what law should be) - Censorial jurisprudence

**Must keep separate:**
- Don't confuse legal validity with moral goodness
- Immoral law is still law (but should be changed)

**Example:** Slavery laws were immoral but legally valid (made by sovereign)

---

### Criticism of Austin's Theory

**Major Problems:**

#### **1. Cannot Explain Custom** 📜
- Custom is law but not sovereign's command
- Austin had to twist definition to fit custom
- Unsatisfactory

#### **2. Limited View of Sovereign** 👑
- In democracy, no one person is sovereign in Austin's sense
- Constitution limits Parliament
- Cannot identify sovereign in modern states

#### **3. Ignores International Law** 🌍
- No world sovereign
- Yet international law exists
- Austin couldn't explain

#### **4. Too Narrow** 📏
- Reduces all law to commands
- Ignores law's social dimensions
- Mechanical view

#### **5. Ignores Morality** ❌
- Complete separation unrealistic
- Law does have moral content
- Cannot ignore justice

---

## 2. H.L.A. HART'S CONCEPT OF LAW (1907-1992)

**British Philosopher - Most Important 20th Century Jurist**

**Main Work:** "The Concept of Law" (1961)

**Hart's Project:** Refine and modernize positivism, answering Austin's critics.

### Hart's Theory: Law as Union of Primary and Secondary Rules

#### **Core Idea:**
Law is a system of **primary rules** (rules of conduct) and **secondary rules** (rules about rules).

### Primary Rules of Obligation 📋

**What they are:**
Rules that impose duties - tell people what to do or not do.

**Examples:**
- Don't kill
- Don't steal
- Pay taxes
- Perform contracts

**Simple Society:**
- Only primary rules exist
- Elders/community enforce
- Like primitive tribes

**Problems with Only Primary Rules:**

**3 Defects (COUNT = 3):**

**1. Uncertainty** ❓
- Rules not written down
- Disputes about what rules are
- No authoritative text

**2. Static Character** 🔒
- Rules cannot be changed deliberately
- No procedure for change
- Slow evolution only

**3. Inefficiency** 🐢
- No organized enforcement
- No courts or officials
- Diffused social pressure only

---

### Secondary Rules - Hart's Innovation 🎯

**What they are:**
Rules about how to create, change, and apply primary rules. They remedy defects of primitive system.

### 3 Types of Secondary Rules (COUNT = 3):

#### **1. Rule of Recognition** ✅

**What it does:** Identifies which rules are valid law.

**Function:**
- Provides test for legal validity
- Example: "What Parliament enacts is law"
- Ultimate criterion in legal system
- Removes uncertainty

**In India:**
- Constitution is rule of recognition
- Any law consistent with Constitution is valid

**In UK:**
- "What Queen-in-Parliament enacts is law"

**Importance:** Most fundamental rule - tells us what counts as law

#### **2. Rules of Change** 🔄

**What they do:** Provide procedures for creating new rules and changing old rules.

**Functions:**
- Legislative procedure (how to make law)
- Amendment procedure
- Repeal procedure
- Removes static character

**Examples:**
- Constitutional amendment procedure (Article 368 in India)
- Legislative process (Bill → Debate → Pass → Assent)
- Contract-making rules

**Empowers:**
- Legislature to make law
- Individuals to make contracts, wills
- Private law-making also

#### **3. Rules of Adjudication** ⚖️

**What they do:** Provide procedures for determining if rules have been broken and applying sanctions.

**Functions:**
- Establish courts
- Grant judging powers
- Define procedures
- Provide for enforcement
- Removes inefficiency

**Examples:**
- Court procedures (CPC, CrPC)
- Evidence rules
- Sentencing guidelines

**Empowers:**
- Courts to adjudicate
- Officials to enforce
- Organized sanction system

---

### Hart's Complete Theory

**Legal System = Primary Rules + Secondary Rules**

**Simple Society:** Only primary rules → Defects (uncertain, static, inefficient)

**Advanced Legal System:** Primary + Secondary rules → Remedies defects

**Rule of Recognition:** Remedies uncertainty (tells what's law)  
**Rules of Change:** Remedies static character (allows deliberate change)  
**Rules of Adjudication:** Remedies inefficiency (organized enforcement)  

---

### Hart vs Austin - Improvements

| Feature | Austin | Hart |
|---------|--------|------|
| **Law is** | Commands of sovereign | Union of primary + secondary rules |
| **Focus** | Sovereign and commands | Rules and recognition |
| **Can explain custom?** | No (problematic) | Yes (some customs recognized) |
| **Can explain constitution?** | No (who commands sovereign?) | Yes (rule of recognition) |
| **International law?** | No | Better (though debates) |
| **More sophisticated?** | No | Yes |

**Hart refined positivism** while keeping core idea (law and morality separate).

---

### Hart on Law and Morality

**Hart's Position: Separation Thesis But...**

**Separation:**
- Law and morality are **conceptually separate**
- Can have immoral law (legally valid)
- Legal validity not dependent on morality

**BUT Acknowledges:**
- Law and morality often overlap
- Minimum content of natural law necessary (survival rules)
- Law influenced by morality
- Morality influences law-making

**Hart vs Natural Law:**
- Natural Law: Law must be moral
- Hart: Law and morality separate but connected in practice

## 3. HANS KELSEN'S PURE THEORY OF LAW (1881-1973)

**Austrian Jurist - Most Radical Positivist**

**Main Work:** "Pure Theory of Law" (1934)

**Kelsen's Goal:** Create **pure** science of law - free from all non-legal elements.

### What "Pure" Means:

**Purify law from:**
- ❌ Morality (like other positivists)
- ❌ Sociology (social facts)
- ❌ Politics
- ❌ History
- ❌ Psychology

**Focus only on:** Legal norms themselves

---

### Kelsen's Pure Theory

#### **1. Law as System of Norms** 📋

**What is norm:**
A rule prescribing certain conduct as obligatory.

**Law consists of:**
- System of norms
- Not facts or commands
- "Ought" propositions ("You ought to pay taxes")

**Norm vs Fact:**
- Norm: "Ought" (prescriptive) - "You should not steal"
- Fact: "Is" (descriptive) - "People steal"

**Kelsen's Focus:** Law is system of "ought" norms

---

#### **2. Hierarchy of Norms** 🏛️

**Kelsen's Key Insight:**
Norms are arranged in hierarchy - each norm derives validity from higher norm.

**The Pyramid Structure:**

```
                    GRUNDNORM (Basic Norm)
                           ↓
                    CONSTITUTION
                           ↓
                    STATUTES/ACTS
                           ↓
              RULES/REGULATIONS (Delegated Legislation)
                           ↓
                   JUDICIAL DECISIONS
                           ↓
              INDIVIDUAL NORMS (Court orders, Contracts)
```

**How it Works:**
- Each lower norm derives validity from higher norm
- Constitution derives from Grundnorm
- Statutes valid if consistent with Constitution
- Rules valid if authorized by Statute
- Orders valid if based on Rules

**Example:**
- Income Tax Act valid because Constitution authorizes Parliament to make tax laws
- Income Tax Rules valid because Act authorizes Central Government to make rules
- Tax officer's order valid because based on Rules

---

#### **3. Grundnorm (Basic Norm)** 👑

**What is Grundnorm?**
- **German word:** "Grund" (basic/ground) + "norm" (norm)
- **The ultimate norm** from which all other norms derive validity
- **Hypothetical** (assumed, not proved)

**Why Needed:**
- Cannot have infinite chain (each norm validated by higher norm)
- Must have ultimate norm
- Grundnorm stops the regress

**What it States:**
"The Constitution (or first Constitution-makers) ought to be obeyed"

**In India:** Grundnorm might be: "The Constitution of India as adopted in 1950 is binding"

**Nature:**
- Not positive law (not laid down by anyone)
- Presupposed/assumed
- Necessary assumption for legal system
- Changes with revolution (new Grundnorm)

#### **4. Revolution and Grundnorm** 🔥

**What happens in revolution:**
- Old legal order overthrown
- New order established
- **New Grundnorm** comes into being

**Example:**
- Before 1947: Grundnorm = "British Crown's authority ought to be obeyed"
- After 1947: Grundnorm = "Indian Constitution adopted by Constituent Assembly ought to be obeyed"

**Kelsen's Insight:** Legal continuity breaks in revolution; new grundnorm emerges

---

#### **5. Separation from All Non-Legal Elements** 🔀

**Kelsen's Radical Position:**

**Law is:**
- System of norms
- Logically connected
- Pure legal science

**Law is NOT:**
- About morality (normative ethics)
- About social facts (sociology)
- About psychological facts
- About political power

**Why "Pure":**
- Scientific study of law
- Focus only on legal norms
- Avoid contamination from ethics, politics, sociology

**Criticism:**
- Too abstract
- Ignores social reality
- Law cannot be studied in vacuum
- Unrealistic

---

### Kelsen vs Austin - Both Positivists But Different

| Feature | Austin | Kelsen |
|---------|--------|--------|
| **Law is** | Commands of sovereign | System of norms |
| **Focus** | Sovereign, commands, sanctions | Norms, hierarchy, validity |
| **Grundnorm** | No such concept | Central concept |
| **Separation from** | Morality | Morality + all social facts |
| **Can explain constitutional law?** | Difficult | Yes (hierarchy) |
| **Radical?** | Moderate positivist | Extreme positivist |

## Summary: Three Major Positivists

### 1. Jeremy Bentham (1748-1832)

**Key Ideas:**
- Utilitarian theory: Greatest happiness principle
- Law should maximize utility
- Separation of "is" and "ought"
- Favored codification

**Contribution:** Foundation of positivism and utilitarianism

---

### 2. John Austin (1790-1859)

**Key Ideas:**
- Law is command of sovereign
- 4 elements: Command, Sovereign, Duty, Sanction
- Strict separation of law and morality
- Analytical jurisprudence

**Contribution:** Systematic positivist theory, influenced generations

**Problems:** Cannot explain custom, constitution, international law

---

### 3. H.L.A. Hart (1907-1992)

**Key Ideas:**
- Law is union of primary and secondary rules
- Rule of recognition (identifies valid law)
- Rules of change and adjudication
- Minimum content of natural law

**Contribution:** Modernized positivism, answered Austin's critics

**Improvement:** Can explain custom, constitution, more sophisticated

---

### 4. Hans Kelsen (1881-1973)

**Key Ideas:**
- Pure theory of law
- Law as hierarchy of norms
- Grundnorm (basic norm) - ultimate validity source
- Separation from all non-legal elements

**Contribution:** Most systematic legal theory, influenced jurisprudence worldwide

**Problems:** Too abstract, ignores social reality, unrealistic purity

---

## Core Principles of Legal Positivism

**All positivists agree on:**

✅ **Separation Thesis**: Law and morality are separate  
✅ **Social Thesis**: Law is social fact (what's laid down), not moral ideal  
✅ **Validity from Authority**: Law valid if made by proper authority (not moral content)  
✅ **Descriptive**: Focus on law as it is, not as it ought to be  
✅ **No Necessary Connection**: Law can be immoral and still be law  

---

## Positivism vs Natural Law - Core Debate

| Aspect | Natural Law | Positivism |
|--------|-------------|------------|
| **Law and Morality** | Inseparable | Separate |
| **Unjust Law** | Not true law | Still law (but bad law) |
| **Validity** | From moral content | From proper authority |
| **Focus** | What ought to be | What is |
| **Higher Law** | Exists | Doesn't exist |
| **Emphasis** | Moral evaluation | Scientific description |
