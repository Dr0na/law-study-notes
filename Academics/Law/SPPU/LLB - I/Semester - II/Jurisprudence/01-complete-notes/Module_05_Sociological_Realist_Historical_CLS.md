# 🌐 Module 05 — Sociological, Realist, Historical Schools and Critical Legal Studies

> Syllabus cluster: Sociological (Roscoe Pound) • American Realism (Holmes, Llewellyn, Frank) • Historical (Savigny, Maine) — comparison with analytical school • CLS (Feminist Legal Theory, Critical Race Theory).

## 1. Sociological school — Roscoe Pound

Law is a **social institution** that must answer to social needs. Pound coined the metaphor of **"social engineering"** — law balances conflicting interests with minimum friction.

### Pound’s classification of interests

1. **Individual interests** — personality, domestic relations, property/contract.
2. **Public interests** — interests of the State as a juristic person.
3. **Social interests** — general security, security of social institutions, general morals, conservation of resources, general progress, individual life.

> 🧠 **Mnemonic — IPS:** *"Individual, Public, Social"* — Pound’s three buckets of interests.

### Jural postulates

Pound listed five **jural postulates** of civilised society (e.g., people must control resources peacefully, must keep promises, etc.). Courts and legislatures should weigh interests against these postulates.

## 2. American Realism

The realists were a movement of judges and academics at Columbia and Yale (1920s-1940s) reacting to formalist “legal science.” Their central claims:

- **Law-in-books ≠ law-in-action.**
- **Judges make law** — appellate decisions depend on personality, politics, and facts as much as rules.
- The study of law is the **prediction of what courts will do**.

### Oliver Wendell Holmes Jr.

- *"The life of the law has not been logic; it has been experience."*
- **Bad-man theory** — law is what officials do; we predict it the way a *bad man* predicts the consequences of his acts.

### Karl Llewellyn

- **Rule-skepticism** — paper rules underdetermine outcomes; the *real* rules are how judges decide.
- Authored the *Uniform Commercial Code* — combining realism with practical drafting.

### Jerome Frank

- **Fact-skepticism** — even if rules were certain, trial fact-finding is fluid (witnesses, judge’s mood, jury bias).
- *Law and the Modern Mind* (1930) — rule-myth as a *father-figure* substitute.

> 🧠 **Mnemonic — HLF:** *"Holmes, Llewellyn, Frank"* — the three realists you must name.

## 3. Historical school

### Friedrich Carl von Savigny (1779–1861)

- Law develops like **language and custom**, expressing the **Volksgeist** (spirit of the people).
- Hostile to **codification** without organic readiness.
- Sources: custom first, jurists second, legislation last.

### Sir Henry Maine (1822–1888)

- *Ancient Law* (1861) — comparative method.
- Famous thesis: *"the movement of progressive societies has hitherto been a movement from status to contract."*
- Sequence: themistes (divine commands) → customary law → law of priestly codes → era of fictions → equity → legislation.

> 🧠 **Mnemonic — VS-MC:** *"Volksgeist → Status-to-Contract"*
> V = Volksgeist (Savigny), S = Status, M = Maine, C = Contract.

### Historical vs Analytical school

| 🔍 Aspect | Historical | Analytical |
|:---|:---|:---|
| What is law? | Organic growth of the people’s spirit | Command/rule with formal validity |
| Source priority | Custom → juristic writings → legislation | Legislation → precedent |
| Method | Historical, comparative | Logical, definitional |
| Theorists | Savigny, Maine | Bentham, Austin, Hart |
| Attitude to codification | Sceptical | Welcoming |

## 4. Critical Legal Studies (brief study)

CLS (1970s onwards) treats law as **politics by other means** — apparently neutral doctrine masks power relations.

### Feminist Legal Theory

- Law has historically reflected a **male standpoint**; categories such as "reasonable man", privacy, and consent need rethinking.
- Strands: liberal feminism (equal rights), radical feminism (Catharine MacKinnon — law as instrument of male dominance), difference feminism (Carol Gilligan — ethics of care), and intersectional feminism (Kimberlé Crenshaw).

### Critical Race Theory (CRT)

- Race is a **social construction**; law has helped construct and maintain it.
- Key ideas: *interest convergence* (Derrick Bell), *intersectionality* (Crenshaw), counter-storytelling.
- Indian uses: caste, reservation jurisprudence, hate-speech regulation.

> 🧠 **Mnemonic — F-CRT:** *"Feminism + Critical Race Theory — twin CLS branches in your syllabus."*

## 5. Why these schools matter

Sociological, realist, and CLS schools together remind us:

1. Law is **about society**, not just internal logic.
2. **Who decides** is as important as what rules say.
3. Apparently neutral doctrines carry **distributive consequences**.
4. A jurisprudence answer should always close with a **policy** or **social** evaluation if the question invites one.

## 6. Conclusion

These schools complete the **trilemma** of jurisprudence:

- *What is law?* — Analytical and natural-law schools (Modules 03–04).
- *How does it function?* — Sociological and realist schools.
- *Whom does it serve?* — Historical and Critical schools.

A topper answer maps the question onto one of these three lenses before writing.
