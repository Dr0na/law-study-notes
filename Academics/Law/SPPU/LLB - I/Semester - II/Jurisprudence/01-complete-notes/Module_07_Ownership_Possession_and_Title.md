# 📘 Module 07: Concepts of Ownership, Possession and Title

🎚️ Difficulty: 🟡 Medium

---

### 🧠 Introduction

Ownership and Possession are two of the most important concepts in property law. While they are often used interchangeably in common speech, they have distinct legal meanings. "Title" is the link between a person and an object.

---

### 📖 Main Content

#### 1. Ownership

Ownership is the "entirety of the powers of use and disposal allowed by law."

**Characteristics of Ownership**:
- It is indefinite in point of user.
- It is unrestricted in point of disposition.
- It is unlimited in point of duration.

**Kinds of Ownership**:
1.  **Corporeal vs. Incorporeal**: Corporeal is ownership of physical objects (land, car); Incorporeal is ownership of rights (copyright, patent).
2.  **Sole vs. Co-ownership**: Owned by one person vs. multiple people.
3.  **Trust vs. Beneficial**: Legal title is with the trustee, but the benefit is for the beneficiary.
4.  **Vested vs. Contingent**: Vested is immediate and certain; Contingent depends on an event that may or may not happen.

#### 2. Possession

Possession is the "prima facie evidence of ownership." It has two elements:
- **Corpus Possessionis**: Physical control over the object.
- **Animus Possidendi**: The intention to exclude others from the object.

**Kinds of Possession**:
- **Mediate vs. Immediate**: Mediate is through another person (e.g., landlord through tenant); Immediate is direct control.
- **Constructive Possession**: Legal possession without physical contact (e.g., having the keys to a warehouse).
- **Adverse Possession**: Possessing property without the owner's permission for a statutory period (usually 12 years in India), which can lead to ownership.

**Comparison: Ownership vs. Possession**
| Basis | Ownership | Possession |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nature** | De Jure (By Law) | De Facto (In Fact) |
| **Right** | Absolute right | Physical control |
| **Remedy** | Proprietary remedy | Possessory remedy |

#### 3. Title

Title is the "deed or ground" by which a person acquires a right. It is the evidence of ownership.

**Classification of Titles**:
- **Original Titles**: Acquire a right for the first time (e.g., creating a painting, catching a fish).
- **Derivative Titles**: Acquire a right from a previous owner (e.g., Sale, Gift, Inheritance).

---

### ⚖️ Case Laws

- **Armory v. Delamirie (1722)**: A chimney sweep found a jewel. The court held that the finder has a right to the property against everyone except the true owner. (The "Finders Keepers" principle).
- **Bridges v. Hawkesworth**: Found money on the floor of a shop. Held that the finder had a better title than the shopkeeper because the shop was a public place.

---

### 🧾 Conclusion

Ownership is the ultimate right, but Possession is the physical reality. Law protects possession to prevent people from taking the law into their own hands, even if they are the true owners.
