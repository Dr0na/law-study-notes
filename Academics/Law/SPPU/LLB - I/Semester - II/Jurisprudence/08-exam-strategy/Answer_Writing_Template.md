# ✍️ Answer Writing Template - Jurisprudence

To score high in Jurisprudence, your answers must be structured, logical, and supported by juristic views and case laws.

## 1. 🎯 Structure for 15-Mark Questions (Long Answers)
1.  **Introduction**: Define the concept. Mention the school of thought it belongs to.
2.  **Juristic Views**: Quote at least 2-3 jurists (e.g., Austin, Salmond, Pound).
3.  **Main Body**:
    - Use headings and sub-headings.
    - Explain the core principles.
    - Use a **Mermaid Diagram** or Flowchart if applicable.
4.  **Case Laws**: Cite at least 2-3 landmark cases. Use the format: *Case Name* → *Facts* → *Held* → *Principle*.
5.  **Critical Analysis**: Mention merits and demerits (criticisms).
6.  **Conclusion**: Summarize the modern relevance of the concept.

## 2. 🎯 Structure for 10-Mark Questions (Medium Answers)
1.  **Introduction**: Brief definition.
2.  **Key Points**: 4-6 major points with brief explanations.
3.  **Comparison Table**: If the question is "A vs B," always use a table.
4.  **Case Law**: At least 1-2 relevant cases.
5.  **Conclusion**: A short closing paragraph.

## 3. 🎯 Structure for 5-Mark Questions (Short Notes)
1.  **Definition**: 1-2 sentences.
2.  **Core Explanation**: 3-4 bullet points.
3.  **Mnemonic**: Mention the mnemonic for recall.
4.  **Example/Case**: 1 small example or case name.

---

## 💡 Pro-Tips for Jurisprudence
- **Don't Invent**: Never make up jurist names or quotes. If you forget, use general terms like "Analytical jurists believe..."
- **Use Maxims**: Sprinkle Latin maxims (e.g., *Stare Decisis*) to show depth of knowledge.
- **Draw Diagrams**: Even a simple pyramid or flowchart can fetch you 1-2 extra marks.
- **Focus on 'Why'**: Jurisprudence is about the philosophy of law. Always explain the *reason* behind a rule.
