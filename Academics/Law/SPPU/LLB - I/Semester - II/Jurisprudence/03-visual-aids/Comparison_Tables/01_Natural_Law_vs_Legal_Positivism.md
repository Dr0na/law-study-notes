# 📊 Natural Law vs Legal Positivism — comparison

| 🔍 Aspect | Natural law | Legal positivism |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **What is law?** | Higher moral order discoverable by reason; positive law must conform | Social-fact rule recognised by source criteria |
| **Validity** | Depends on **moral content** | Depends on **social source** (separation thesis) |
| **Test** | *Lex iniusta non est lex* (unjust law not law) | Rule of recognition / sovereign command / Grundnorm |
| **Key thinkers** | Plato, Aristotle, Aquinas, Grotius, Hobbes, Locke, Rousseau, Fuller, Finnis | Bentham, Austin, Hart, Kelsen |
| **Method** | Normative, reasoning from human nature | Analytical, descriptive |
| **Reaction** | Inspired human-rights and post-WWII trials | Modernised by Hart’s “soft positivism” |
| **Indian touch** | Used in *Kesavananda* basic-structure reasoning | Used in routine statutory interpretation; *Dosso* invoked Kelsen |
| **Strength** | Provides moral check | Provides clarity and predictability |
| **Weakness** | Disagreement on moral content | Cannot account for wicked-but-valid law |

> 🧠 **Take-away:** map any "law and morality" essay onto the **separation** vs **overlap** spectrum, then add Fuller-internal-morality and Finnis-basic-goods as bridges.
