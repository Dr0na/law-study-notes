# ⚖️ Important Cases - Jurisprudence

## 📘 Module 01: Introduction
### **Queen v. Dudley and Stephens (1884)**
- **Facts**: Three sailors and a cabin boy were stranded at sea. To survive, the sailors killed and ate the cabin boy.
- **Issue**: Is "Necessity" a defense to murder?
- **Held**: No. The court held that there is no absolute necessity to preserve one's own life at the cost of another's.
- **Principle**: Law and Morality are distinct but the law must uphold high moral standards.

---

## 📘 Module 02: Sources of Law
### **Bengal Immunity Co. v. State of Bihar**
- **Facts**: Dispute regarding the binding nature of Supreme Court decisions on itself.
- **Issue**: Is the Supreme Court bound by its own previous decisions under Art 141?
- **Held**: No. The Supreme Court is not bound by its own decisions and can overrule them for the public good.
- **Principle**: Flexibility of Precedent at the highest level.

---

## 📘 Module 04: Legal Positivism
### **State of Rajasthan v. Union of India**
- **Facts**: Challenge to the central government's power to dissolve state assemblies.
- **Issue**: Where does the ultimate legal authority lie?
- **Held**: The Constitution is the "Grundnorm" of the Indian legal system.
- **Principle**: Application of Kelsen's Pure Theory in India.

---

## 📘 Module 06: Legal Personality
### **Salomon v. Salomon & Co. Ltd. (1897)**
- **Facts**: Mr. Salomon incorporated his business. When it failed, creditors tried to hold him personally liable.
- **Issue**: Is a company a separate person from its owner?
- **Held**: Yes. A company is a separate legal entity.
- **Principle**: Corporate Personality.

---

## 📘 Module 07: Ownership & Possession
### **Armory v. Delamirie (1722)**
- **Facts**: A chimney sweep found a jewel and took it to a goldsmith, who refused to return it.
- **Issue**: Who has a better right to the jewel?
- **Held**: The finder has a right against everyone except the true owner.
- **Principle**: Protection of Possession (Finders Keepers).

---

## 📘 Module 08: Liability
### **Rylands v. Fletcher (1868)**
- **Facts**: Water from a reservoir escaped and flooded a neighbor's mine.
- **Issue**: Is the owner liable even if not negligent?
- **Held**: Yes.
- **Principle**: Rule of Strict Liability.

### **M.C. Mehta v. Union of India (1987)**
- **Facts**: Oleum gas leak from a factory in Delhi caused death and injury.
- **Issue**: Is the Rylands rule sufficient for hazardous industries?
- **Held**: No. In such cases, liability is Absolute and has no exceptions.
- **Principle**: Absolute Liability (Indian Innovation).
