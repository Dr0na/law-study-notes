# Module 02: Intestate Succession

**Key Statutes:** Hindu Succession Act, 1956; Indian Succession Act, 1925; Muslim Personal Law.

---

## 1. Hindu Succession Act, 1956

### Application (Sec 2)
Applies to any person who is a Hindu by religion (includes Buddhists, Jains, and Sikhs). Does NOT apply to Muslims, Christians, Parsis, or Jews.

### Succession to Property of a Hindu Male (Sec 8-13)
When a Hindu male dies intestate (without a will), his property devolves in the following order:

**Class I Heirs (Sec 8 read with Schedule):**
Son, daughter, widow, mother, son of a predeceased son, daughter of a predeceased son, son of a predeceased daughter, daughter of a predeceased daughter, widow of a predeceased son, son of a predeceased son of a predeceased son, daughter of a predeceased son of a predeceased son, widow of a predeceased son of a predeceased son.

**Rules:**
1. Class I heirs take simultaneously and to the exclusion of all other heirs.
2. They take the property as **tenants-in-common** (not as joint tenants).
3. If no Class I heir exists, the property goes to **Class II heirs** (father, specified relatives).
4. If no Class II heir, then to **Agnates** (related through males).
5. If no Agnates, then to **Cognates** (related through females).
6. If no heir at all, property **escheats** to the Government (Sec 29).

### Succession to Property of a Hindu Female (Sec 15-16)
Property of a Hindu female dying intestate devolves in the following order:
1. Sons and daughters (including children of any predeceased son or daughter) and the husband.
2. Heirs of the husband.
3. Mother and father.
4. Heirs of the father.
5. Heirs of the mother.

**Special Rule (Sec 15(2)):** If the property was inherited from her father/mother, it goes to the heirs of the father. If from husband/father-in-law, it goes to heirs of the husband.

### Stridhana and Women's Estate
- **Stridhana:** Property over which a Hindu woman has absolute ownership — gifts from parents, husband, in-laws, etc.
- **Women's Estate (abolished by the 1956 Act):** Under old Hindu law, women held a "limited estate" (life interest). The Hindu Succession Act, 1956, converted all limited estates into **absolute estates** (Sec 14).

### Disqualifications from Inheritance (Sec 25-28)
1. **Murderer (Sec 25):** A person who commits murder of the intestate is disqualified.
2. **Convert (Sec 26):** If a Hindu converts to another religion, their children born after conversion are disqualified (but the convert themselves can still inherit).
3. **Disease/Disability:** NOT a disqualification under the 1956 Act (unlike old law).

### Escheat (Sec 29)
If the intestate has left no heir qualified to succeed, the Government takes the property.

## 2. Muslim Law of Inheritance

### General Principles
Muslim law of inheritance is governed by the **Quran** and **Hadith** (not codified by statute in India, except the Shariat Act, 1937 makes Muslim personal law applicable).

**Key Features:**
1. No distinction between movable and immovable property.
2. No concept of joint family property.
3. The estate vests in heirs **immediately** upon death.
4. A Muslim cannot dispose of more than **one-third (1/3rd)** of their property by will (the rest must go to heirs by inheritance).

### Three Classes of Heirs

#### I. Sharers (Quranic Heirs / Dhul-Faraiz)
Heirs who are entitled to a **prescribed share** (fixed fraction) of the estate.
- *Examples:* Husband (1/4 or 1/2), Wife (1/8 or 1/4), Father, Mother, Daughter, Son's daughter.

#### II. Residuaries (Asabah)
Heirs who take the **residue** after the sharers have received their shares.
- *Examples:* Son, Father (as residuary), Brother.

#### III. Distant Kindred (Dhul-Arham)
Heirs who are neither sharers nor residuaries. They inherit only if there are no sharers or residuaries.

### Sunni vs. Shia Distinction
| Basis | Sunni (Hanafi) | Shia |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Doctrine** | Follows Quranic rules strictly. | Follows rules from the Quran and Imam's teachings. |
| **Representation** | No right of representation (grandchild cannot represent predeceased parent). | Right of representation is recognized. |
| **Distant Kindred** | Inherit if no sharers/residuaries. | More elaborate classification. |

### Disqualifications
1. **Murderer:** Disqualified under Sunni law (not under Shia law in all cases).
2. **Difference of Religion:** A non-Muslim cannot inherit from a Muslim.
3. **Child in Womb:** Can inherit if born alive within 6 months (Sunni) or 10 months (Shia).

## 3. Indian Succession Act, 1925

### Application
Applies to intestate succession of **Christians, Parsis,** and **persons of other religions** (except Hindus and Muslims who are governed by their own personal laws).

### Domicile (Sec 5-17)
Domicile is relevant for determining which law of succession applies. Every person has a domicile of origin (acquired at birth) which can be changed by acquiring a domicile of choice.

### Consanguinity
- **Lineal Consanguinity:** Persons in a direct line of descent (parent-child-grandchild).
- **Collateral Consanguinity:** Persons sharing a common ancestor but not in a direct line (siblings, cousins).

### Intestate Succession for Christians (Sec 31-49)
- Widow gets **1/3rd**, children get **2/3rds** (if both survive).
- If only widow, no children — widow gets **1/2**, rest to kindred.
- If no widow — children take the whole property equally.

### Intestate Succession for Parsis (Sec 50-56)
- If intestate leaves a widow and children — widow and each child take equal shares.
- If only widow, no children — widow gets **1/2**, rest to next of kin.
- Special provisions for distribution among parents, siblings.
