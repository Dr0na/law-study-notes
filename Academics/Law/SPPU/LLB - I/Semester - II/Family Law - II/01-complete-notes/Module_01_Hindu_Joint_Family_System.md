# Module 01: Hindu Joint Family System

**Key Statutes:** Hindu Succession Act, 1956 (as amended by Amendment Act, 2005); Mitakshara and Dayabhaga Schools of Hindu Law.

---

## 1. Evolution of Joint Family System in India

The Hindu Joint Family is a unique institution of Hindu law. It consists of all persons lineally descended from a common ancestor and includes their wives and unmarried daughters. The joint family is not a creation of contract but arises from **status** — by birth into the family.

Historically, the joint family performed social, economic, and religious functions. The property was held collectively, and the head of the family (Karta) managed it for the benefit of all members.

---

## 2. Hindu Joint Family and Joint Family Property

### Hindu Joint Family
A Hindu Joint Family consists of:
- The common ancestor (father/grandfather).
- All male lineal descendants and their wives and unmarried daughters.
- After the 2005 Amendment, **daughters** are also coparceners by birth.

**Key Feature:** A joint family can consist of even a single male member with his wife. It is a wider body than a coparcenary.

### Joint Family Property
Joint family property (also called "ancestral property") is property inherited by a Hindu from his **father, father's father, or father's father's father.** It includes:
1. Property inherited from the three immediate paternal male ancestors.
2. Property acquired with the aid of joint family funds.
3. Property thrown into the common stock by a coparcener.
4. Accretions to joint family property.

### Self-Acquired Property
Property acquired by a member entirely with his own funds, labour, or skill, **without** the aid of joint family property. The acquirer is the absolute owner and can dispose of it by will or otherwise.

### Family Arrangement
A family arrangement is an agreement among family members to settle disputes or prevent litigation regarding family property. It is based on antecedent title and does not require registration if it merely records pre-existing rights.

---

## 3. Coparcenary

### Meaning
A coparcenary is a **narrower body** within the joint family. It consists of the common ancestor and his lineal descendants up to **four degrees** (under Mitakshara). Only coparceners have a right of **birth** in the joint family property.

### Mitakshara Coparcenary
- Prevails in all of India except Bengal.
- Right in property arises by **birth** (not by succession).
- Coparcenary extends to four generations: the propositus (common ancestor), his son, grandson, and great-grandson.
- **After 2005 Amendment (Sec 6):** Daughters are coparceners by birth, with the same rights and liabilities as sons. *(Landmark: Vineeta Sharma vs. Rakesh Sharma, 2020)*.

### Dayabhaga Coparcenary
- Prevails in Bengal and Assam.
- Right in property arises only on the **death** of the last owner (by succession), NOT by birth.
- Every coparcener holds a defined share and can alienate it.

### Rights and Powers of Coparceners
1. Right to joint possession and enjoyment.
2. Right to claim partition.
3. Right to restrain unauthorized acts by other coparceners.
4. Right of maintenance.
5. Right of survivorship (Mitakshara — abolished partially by 2005 Amendment for daughters).

### Sole Surviving Coparcener
When only one coparcener remains, they become the sole and absolute owner of the entire joint family property.

---

## 4. Karta — Position, Powers and Duties

### Who is the Karta?
The Karta is the **head and manager** of the Hindu Joint Family, typically the senior-most male member. After the 2005 Amendment, a senior female coparcener can also be a Karta.

### Powers of the Karta
1. **Management of Joint Family Property:** The Karta has the sole right to manage, control, and administer the affairs of the joint family property.
2. **Right to Represent the Family:** Can sue and be sued on behalf of the family.
3. **Power to Alienate for Legal Necessity:** Can alienate (sell, mortgage) joint family property for:
   - **Legal necessity** (e.g., payment of debts, medical expenses, marriages).
   - **Benefit of the estate** (e.g., improvements, defending family property in litigation).
4. **Power to Refer Disputes to Arbitration.**
5. **Power to Compromise Bona Fide Claims.**
6. **Power to Acknowledge Debts.**

### Duties of the Karta
1. To manage the property with reasonable care and prudence.
2. To maintain all members of the joint family.
3. To render accounts (if other coparceners demand it, though no strict duty).

### Father's Powers of Alienation
Under Mitakshara, the father (as Karta) has the power to alienate joint family property for **legal necessity** or **benefit of the estate**. If the alienation is without legal necessity, it is **voidable** at the instance of the sons/coparceners.

### Alienee's Rights and Remedies
If the alienee has acted in good faith and after due inquiry into the existence of legal necessity, the alienation is valid even if the necessity did not actually exist. The burden of proving legal necessity lies on the alienee.

## 5. Partition

### Meaning
Partition is the **severance of joint status** and the division of property among coparceners. It involves:
1. Severance of joint status (intention to separate), and
2. Division of property by metes and bounds.

### Under Mitakshara
- Any coparcener can demand partition.
- Partition severs the joint status and converts the joint tenancy into tenancy-in-common.
- A coparcener can sever their interest by a mere declaration of intention (unilateral act).

### Under Dayabhaga
- Since each coparcener already holds a defined share, partition is primarily a division by metes and bounds.

### Persons Entitled to Claim Partition
1. Every coparcener (including daughters after 2005).
2. A minor coparcener (through a guardian).
3. A son in the womb at the time of partition (posthumous son).

### Who Gets Share on Partition
1. Coparceners — equal shares.
2. Wife — equal share with her husband's share (right to be maintained, not to claim partition independently).
3. Mother — share equal to a son.

### Kinds of Partition
1. **Total Partition:** Entire joint family property is divided.
2. **Partial Partition:** Only some members separate, or only some property is divided.

### Reopening of Partition
A partition can be reopened on the following grounds:
1. Fraud, misrepresentation, or undue influence.
2. A son was in the womb at the time and was not given a share.
3. An absent coparcener was not given a share.
4. A minor was prejudiced.

### Reunion
After partition, coparceners may agree to reunite and form a joint family again. Reunion requires **mutual consent** of the reuniting members. A person who was never a member of the original family cannot be admitted by reunion.
