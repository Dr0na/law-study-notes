# Module 03: Testamentary Succession

**Key Statutes:** Indian Succession Act, 1925; Hindu Succession Act, 1956; Muslim Personal Law.

---

## 1. Wills under the Indian Succession Act, 1925

### Meaning and Definition (Sec 2(h))
A **Will** is the legal declaration of the intention of a testator with respect to his property which he desires to be carried into effect after his death. A **Codicil** is an instrument made in relation to a will, explaining, altering, or adding to its dispositions (Sec 2(b)).

### Competence of the Testator (Sec 59)
Every person of **sound mind** and **not a minor** may dispose of their property by will. A married woman can make a will of her own property. A person who is deaf, dumb, or blind can make a will if they understand its meaning.

### Execution of Unprivileged Wills (Sec 63)
An unprivileged will must be:
1. **Signed** by the testator or by some other person in his presence and by his direction.
2. The signature must be placed so as to show that it was intended to give effect to the will.
3. The will must be **attested** by two or more witnesses, each of whom must see the testator sign or affix his mark, or must receive personal acknowledgment from the testator.
4. Each witness must sign the will in the presence of the testator (but need not sign in each other's presence).

### Privileged Wills (Sec 65-66)
A privileged will may be made by soldiers engaged in warfare and mariners at sea. It can be made **orally** or in writing without attestation.

### Revocation of Wills (Sec 70)
A will can be revoked by:
1. Making another will or codicil.
2. A written declaration (executed like a will).
3. Burning, tearing, or destroying with the intention of revoking it.
- **Marriage** revokes a will (Sec 69 — applicable to Christians and Parsis).

### Construction of Wills (Sec 74-111)
Rules for interpreting ambiguous wills. The testator's intention is paramount. Words are given their ordinary meaning unless the context indicates otherwise.

## 2. Legacies under the Indian Succession Act, 1925

### Vesting of Legacies
A legacy vests in the legatee from the moment of death of the testator (unless made contingent or conditional).

### Types of Bequests
1. **Void Bequests:** A bequest to a person not in existence at the testator's death is void, unless it is to an unborn person who comes into existence during the lifetime of a named person (Sec 113-114).
2. **Onerous Bequests (Sec 127):** Where a legacy is attached with an obligation, the legatee must accept both the benefit and the burden, or reject both.
3. **Contingent Bequests (Sec 120):** A bequest dependent on a specified uncertain event.
4. **Conditional Bequests (Sec 121-137):** A bequest subject to a condition precedent or subsequent.
5. **Specific Legacies:** A bequest of a particular, identified thing.
6. **Demonstrative Legacies:** A bequest of a sum directed to be paid from a specific fund.

### Ademption
When the specific thing bequeathed is not part of the estate at death (e.g., testator sold the bequeathed house before dying), the legacy is adeemed (fails).

### Lapse of Legacies (Sec 105)
A legacy lapses if the legatee **dies before the testator**, unless it is a bequest to a class of persons or contains a substitution.

### Election (Sec 180-190)
Where a testator bequeaths property belonging to another person, the owner of that property must choose ("elect") either to:
- Accept the bequest made to them under the will, OR
- Retain their own property and reject the bequest.

### Gifts in Contemplation of Death (Donatio Mortis Causa) (Sec 191)
A gift made by a person who believes they are about to die. It is revocable, subject to the same formalities, and takes effect only upon the donor's death from the apprehended peril.

## 3. Testamentary Succession under Hindu Law

### Hindu Succession Act, 1956 (Sec 30)
Any Hindu may dispose of their property by will or other testamentary disposition. This includes:
- Self-acquired property — entirely disposable.
- Coparcenary interest — can be bequeathed after 2005 Amendment (a coparcener's undivided interest can be willed).

The will of a Hindu is governed by the **Indian Succession Act, 1925** (Sec 57 makes it applicable to Hindu wills).

## 4. Will under Muslim Law (Wasiyat)

### Nature
A Muslim can make a will (wasiyat) but with significant restrictions.

### Key Rules (3 Restrictions)
1. **One-Third Rule:** A Muslim can bequeath only up to **one-third (1/3rd)** of the net estate by will. Any bequest exceeding 1/3rd is invalid unless **consented to by all the heirs** after the testator's death.
2. **No Bequest to Heirs:** A Muslim cannot make a bequest in favor of a person who is already an heir by inheritance, unless the other heirs consent after the testator's death.
3. **No Registration Required:** A Muslim will can be oral or written. No special form or attestation is required.

### Revocation
A wasiyat is revocable at any time during the testator's lifetime.
