# Module 05: Gifts under Muslim Law (Hiba)

**Key Source:** Muslim Personal Law (uncodified).

---

## 1. Nature and Characteristics of Hiba

### Definition
**Hiba** means a transfer of property made immediately, without any consideration, by one person to another and accepted by or on behalf of the latter.

### Characteristics (5 Features)
1. Immediate and unconditional transfer.
2. Without consideration.
3. Inter vivos — takes effect during donor's lifetime.
4. Generally irrevocable once completed.
5. No specific form required — can be oral.

### Types of Hiba
1. **Hiba (Pure Gift):** Unconditional, without consideration.
2. **Hiba-bil-Iwaz:** Gift with return consideration (operates as sale, irrevocable).
3. **Hiba-ba-Shart-ul-Iwaz:** Gift with a stipulation for return (revocable until return is given).
4. **Sadaqah:** Charitable gift for religious merit (absolutely irrevocable).

### Donor and Donee
- **Donor:** Any Muslim of sound mind, who has attained majority, and owns the property.
- **Donee:** Any person including a minor or non-Muslim. Guardian accepts for minors.

---

## 2. Essentials of a Valid Gift (3 Essentials)

1. **Declaration (Ijab)** by the Donor.
2. **Acceptance (Qubul)** by the Donee.
3. **Delivery of Possession (Qabza)** — most critical requirement.

### Exceptions to Delivery Requirement
1. Gift by father to minor child (constructive delivery).
2. Gift between husband and wife of property in joint occupation.
3. Gift of property already in donee's possession.

### Mushaa (Undivided Share)
- **Sunni Law:** Gift of undivided share in divisible property is invalid without partition.
- **Shia Law:** Valid even without partition.

### Marz-ul-Maut (Death-bed Gift)
Subject to the **one-third rule**, takes effect only after death, cannot be made to an heir without others' consent.

---

## 3. Revocation of Gift

### General Rule: Irrevocable once completed.

### Absolutely Irrevocable (8 Cases)
1. Gift to a person within prohibited degrees (spouse, parent, child).
2. Sadaqah. 3. Hiba-bil-Iwaz (consideration given). 4. Property sold/consumed by donee.
5. Donor has died. 6. Donee has died. 7. Property substantially altered. 8. Relationship changed (e.g., marriage).

### Revocation by Court
Other gifts can be revoked only by a decree of the court.
