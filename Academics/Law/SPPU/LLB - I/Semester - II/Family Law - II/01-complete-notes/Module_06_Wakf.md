# Module 06: Wakf

**Key Statutes:** Muslim Personal Law; The Wakf Act, 1995.

---

## 1. Origin and Development
Wakf is an institution of Islamic law deeply rooted in the Quran and Hadith. The Prophet Muhammad is believed to have sanctioned the first wakf. Over centuries, it became a major instrument for endowing property for religious and charitable purposes in India.

## 2. Meaning, Definition, and Characteristics

### Definition
**Wakf** literally means "detention" or "tying up." Legally, it is the permanent dedication of property by a Muslim for any purpose recognized by Muslim law as religious, pious, or charitable.
**Section 3(r) of the Wakf Act, 1995** defines wakf similarly.

### Characteristics (5 Features)
1. **Permanent and irrevocable** dedication. Once created, it cannot be revoked.
2. The ownership vests in **God (Allah)** — the property is detained in the implied ownership of the Almighty.
3. The profit/usufruct is applied for the benefit of mankind.
4. It can be created for religious, pious, or charitable purposes.
5. Only a Muslim can create a wakf under Muslim law.

## 3. Essentials and Kinds

### Essentials (4 Essentials)
1. **The Wakif (Dedicator):** Must be a Muslim, of sound mind, major, and the owner of the property.
2. **Declaration (Wakfnama):** A clear declaration of intention to create the wakf (oral or written).
3. **Subject Matter:** Must be the property of the wakif — movable or immovable, tangible or intangible.
4. **Object/Purpose:** Must be recognized as religious, pious, or charitable under Muslim law.

### Kinds of Wakf
1. **Wakf-alal-aulad (Family Wakf / Private Wakf):** Created for the benefit of the wakif's own family/descendants. The ultimate benefit must be for a charitable or religious purpose.
2. **Public Wakf:** Created for the benefit of the general public (e.g., mosques, dargahs, graveyards, schools).

### Formalities for Creation
- No specific form is required — can be oral or written.
- Delivery of possession is essential (the property must be transferred to the mutawalli or dedicated to the purpose).
- Registration is advisable (and required for immovable property under the Registration Act).

## 4. Administration of Wakf

### Mutawalli (Manager/Trustee)
The person appointed to manage the wakf property is called the **Mutawalli**. They are NOT the owner — they are a superintendent or manager.

### Appointment
1. The wakif can appoint the mutawalli in the wakfnama.
2. If no appointment, the court or the Wakf Board can appoint one.
3. A non-Muslim can be a mutawalli (though not preferred).

### Powers of the Mutawalli
1. To manage and administer the wakf property.
2. To spend income on the objects of the wakf.
3. To carry out necessary repairs.
4. **Cannot alienate** wakf property (except with court permission for legal necessity).
5. To lease the property (short-term leases; long-term requires court sanction).

### Duties of the Mutawalli
1. To protect the wakf property.
2. To apply the income to the specified purposes.
3. To maintain accounts.
4. To comply with directions of the Wakf Board.

### Removal of Mutawalli
A mutawalli can be removed by the court or the Wakf Board for:
- Mismanagement, breach of trust, misappropriation, incompetence.

### Wakf Board (Under the Wakf Act, 1995)
Each state has a **Wakf Board** (Central Wakf Council at the national level) to:
- Survey and register wakf properties.
- Supervise and control mutawallis.
- Settle disputes regarding wakf.
- Ensure proper utilization of wakf funds.
