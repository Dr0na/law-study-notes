# Module 04: Right of Pre-emption

**Key Sources:** Muslim Personal Law; Hindu Law (customary).

---

## 1. Pre-emption under Muslim Law (Shufa)

### Meaning and Nature
**Shufa** (Pre-emption) is the right of a person to acquire, by purchase, immovable property in preference to other persons whenever it is sold. It is a right of substitution — the pre-emptor steps into the shoes of the buyer.

It arises from **neighborhood or co-ownership** and exists to prevent the introduction of strangers into a neighborhood or co-ownership.

### Who Can Claim the Right? (3 Classes — Order of Priority)
1. **Shafi-i-Sharik (Co-sharer):** A co-owner in the property itself. Has the highest priority.
2. **Shafi-i-Khalit (Participator in appendages):** A person who shares a common right of way, water supply, or easement.
3. **Shafi-i-Jar (Neighbor):** An owner of adjoining property.

### Subject Matter
The right can be exercised over **immovable property** only. It does not apply to movable property, gifts, inheritance, or wakf property.

### Formalities — Three Demands (Talab)
A pre-emptor must observe the following formalities to preserve the right:
1. **Talab-i-Mowasibat (First Demand / Demand on receipt of information):** Immediately upon hearing of the sale, the pre-emptor must declare their intention to exercise the right of pre-emption in the presence of witnesses.
2. **Talab-i-Ishhad (Second Demand / Demand of confirmation):** The pre-emptor must confirm their intention before witnesses, in the presence of the seller or the buyer, or on the property itself.
3. **Talab-i-Tamlik (Third Demand / Demand by suit):** If the buyer refuses to surrender, the pre-emptor must file a suit in court within a reasonable time.

### Loss of the Right
The right of pre-emption is lost if:
1. The pre-emptor fails to observe the three demands promptly.
2. The pre-emptor acquiesces in the sale (implied waiver).
3. The pre-emptor dies (the right is personal and non-heritable).
4. The pre-emptor releases the right expressly or impliedly.
5. The property ceases to exist.

### Legal Devices to Evade Pre-emption (Hiyal)
Sellers sometimes use devices to defeat the right:
- Claiming a higher consideration than actually paid.
- Making a "gift" instead of a sale.
- Selling to a co-sharer of the pre-emptor.
- Including movables with immovables to confuse the subject matter.

## 2. Pre-emption under Hindu Law

Pre-emption under Hindu law is **customary** in nature, not based on any specific statute. It exists in certain parts of India (Punjab, etc.) where local custom recognizes it.

The Punjab Pre-emption Act, 1913 was one such statutory recognition. The Supreme Court in **S. Bakhtawar Singh vs. S. Balbir Singh (2007)** upheld the right of pre-emption based on vicinage (neighborhood) but struck down certain provisions as violating the right to equality (Article 14).
