## 📜 Q2. Who is a Karta? Discuss the position, powers, and duties of a Karta in a Hindu Joint Family. When can the Karta alienate joint family property?

### 1. Introduction
The Karta is the head and manager of the Hindu Joint Family. The institution of Karta is unique to Hindu law and is central to the management of joint family property.

### 2. Who is the Karta?
The **senior-most male coparcener** is ordinarily the Karta. After the 2005 Amendment, a senior **female coparcener** (daughter) can also be a Karta.

### 3. Powers of the Karta
1. **Management:** Sole right to manage, control, and administer joint family property.
2. **Representation:** Can sue and be sued on behalf of the family.
3. **Alienation for Legal Necessity:** Can sell, mortgage, or pledge joint family property for legal necessity (debts, medical expenses, marriages, maintenance).
4. **Alienation for Benefit of Estate:** Can alienate if it benefits the estate (defending property in litigation, essential improvements).
5. **Compromise Claims:** Can compromise bona fide disputed claims.
6. **Refer to Arbitration:** Can refer disputes to arbitration.
7. **Acknowledge Debts:** Can acknowledge time-barred debts to revive them.

### 4. Duties of the Karta
1. Manage property with reasonable care and prudence.
2. Maintain all members of the joint family.
3. Arrange for the education and marriage of dependents.
4. Render accounts when demanded by coparceners (though no strict legal duty to maintain accounts regularly).

### 5. Father's Power of Alienation
Under Mitakshara, the father as Karta can alienate joint family property for:
- **Legal Necessity:** Genuine family needs.
- **Benefit of the Estate:** Protecting or improving property.
If the alienation is **without legal necessity**, it is voidable at the instance of sons/coparceners. *(Case: Pratap Singh vs. Union of India, 1985)*.

### 6. Alienee's Rights
An alienee who has acted in **good faith** and after **due inquiry** into the existence of legal necessity is protected — even if the necessity did not actually exist.

### 7. Conclusion
The Karta occupies a position of trust and authority. Their powers are extensive but circumscribed by the requirement of legal necessity and the duty to act for the benefit of the family.
