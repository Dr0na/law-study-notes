## 🏛️ Q1. Define Coparcenary. Distinguish between Mitakshara and Dayabhaga coparcenary. Discuss the effect of the 2005 Amendment on coparcenary rights.

### 1. Introduction
A coparcenary is the core institution of Hindu joint family property law. It determines who has a right of birth in ancestral property and how that right operates.

### 2. Definition
A **coparcenary** is a narrower body within the Hindu Joint Family, consisting of the common ancestor and his lineal descendants up to four degrees. Only coparceners have a right in the joint family property by birth.

### 3. Mitakshara vs Dayabhaga

| 📊 Basis | 🏛️ Mitakshara | 📜 Dayabhaga |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Prevalence** | All India except Bengal. | Bengal and Assam. |
| **Right in Property** | By **birth**. | By **succession** (on death of last owner). |
| **Share** | Fluctuating — changes with births/deaths. | Defined and ascertainable. |
| **Alienation** | Cannot freely alienate undivided interest. | Can alienate share freely. |
| **Survivorship** | Applies. | Does NOT apply. |

### 4. The 2005 Amendment (Sec 6, Hindu Succession Act)
The Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005 made a landmark change:
- **Daughters** are now coparceners **by birth** with the same rights and liabilities as sons.
- A daughter can demand partition, act as Karta, and inherit coparcenary property equally.
- *(Vineeta Sharma vs. Rakesh Sharma, 2020)*: The Supreme Court held the amendment is **retrospective** — applies even if the father died before 09.09.2005.

### 5. Conclusion
The 2005 Amendment transformed Hindu succession law by establishing gender equality in coparcenary rights, fulfilling the constitutional promise of equality under Article 14.
