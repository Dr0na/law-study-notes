## 📖 Q3. Discuss the rules of intestate succession to the property of a Hindu male dying intestate under the Hindu Succession Act, 1956. Who are Class I heirs?

### 1. Introduction
The Hindu Succession Act, 1956, governs the devolution of property of a Hindu dying without a will (intestate). Section 8 lays down the order of succession for a Hindu male's property.

### 2. Application (Sec 2)
Applies to Hindus (including Buddhists, Jains, Sikhs). Does NOT apply to Muslims, Christians, Parsis.

### 3. Order of Succession (Sec 8)
The property of a Hindu male dying intestate devolves in the following order:
1. **Class I heirs** (take simultaneously, excluding all others).
2. **Class II heirs** (if no Class I heir).
3. **Agnates** (related through males).
4. **Cognates** (related through females).
5. **Government** (Escheat — Sec 29).

### 4. Class I Heirs (Schedule to Sec 8) — 12 Heirs
1. Son 2. Daughter 3. Widow 4. Mother
5. Son of predeceased son 6. Daughter of predeceased son
7. Son of predeceased daughter 8. Daughter of predeceased daughter
9. Widow of predeceased son
10. Son of predeceased son of predeceased son
11. Daughter of predeceased son of predeceased son
12. Widow of predeceased son of predeceased son

### 5. Rules of Distribution Among Class I Heirs (Sec 10)
- The intestate's widow (or widows together) takes **one share**.
- The surviving sons and daughters take one share each.
- Heirs in the branch of each predeceased son/daughter take the share that the predeceased would have taken if alive, divided among them equally.
- Class I heirs take as **tenants-in-common** (not joint tenants).

### 6. Disqualifications (Sec 25-28)
- **Murderer (Sec 25):** Disqualified from inheriting the murdered person's property.
- **Convert's children (Sec 26):** Children born after conversion are disqualified.

### 7. Escheat (Sec 29)
If no qualified heir exists, property passes to the Government.

### 8. Conclusion
The Hindu Succession Act ensures orderly devolution of property with Class I heirs receiving priority. The 2005 Amendment further strengthened the Act by including daughters as coparceners and Class I heirs with equal rights.
