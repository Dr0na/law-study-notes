## 🕌 Q5. Discuss the general principles of inheritance under Muslim law. Distinguish between Sunni and Shia schools of inheritance.

### 1. General Principles
1. No distinction between movable and immovable property.
2. No joint family property concept — estate vests in heirs immediately on death.
3. A Muslim can dispose of only **1/3rd** by will; the rest goes by inheritance.
4. Heirs inherit in three classes: **Sharers → Residuaries → Distant Kindred.**

### 2. Sharers (Quranic Heirs)
Entitled to a **fixed share** prescribed by the Quran. Examples: Husband (1/2 or 1/4), Wife (1/4 or 1/8), Daughter (1/2 or 2/3), Mother (1/6 or 1/3), Father (1/6).

### 3. Residuaries
Take the **remainder** after sharers. Son is the primary residuary.

### 4. Sunni vs Shia Distinction

| 📊 Basis | ☪️ Sunni (Hanafi) | ☪️ Shia |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Representation** | No right of representation. | Recognized. |
| **Distant Kindred** | Inherit if no sharers/residuaries. | More elaborate classification. |
| **Exclusion** | Nearer in degree excludes remoter. | More flexible. |
| **Murderer** | Disqualified. | Not always disqualified. |

### 5. Disqualifications
1. Murderer (Sunni law). 2. Difference of religion. 3. Child must be born alive.

### 6. Conclusion
Muslim inheritance law ensures equitable distribution among family members with fixed shares for primary heirs and flexible residuary rules.
