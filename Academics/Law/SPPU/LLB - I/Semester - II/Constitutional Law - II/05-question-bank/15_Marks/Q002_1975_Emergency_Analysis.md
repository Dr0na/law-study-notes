### Introduction (1 mark)

The Emergency of **1975-77** was the darkest period in Indian democracy when Fundamental Rights were suspended, press censored, and opposition leaders imprisoned. Proclaimed on **June 25, 1975 (midnight)** by President Fakhruddin Ali Ahmed on the ground of "internal disturbance," it lasted till **March 21, 1977**. This 21-month period taught India valuable lessons about protecting democracy and led to significant constitutional reforms through the **44th Amendment (1978)**.

---

### ⚖️ Causes/Background of 1975 Emergency (3 marks)

### 4 Main Causes (COUNT = 4)

#### 1️⃣ Allahabad High Court Judgment (June 12, 1975) ⚖️
**The trigger:** Justice Jagmohan Lal Sinha of Allahabad HC found Prime Minister Indira Gandhi guilty of electoral malpractices in 1971 Lok Sabha elections.

- Found guilty of corrupt practices
- Election declared void
- Barred from holding office for 6 years
- Political crisis for PM

#### 2️⃣ Growing Opposition Movements 📢
**Political pressure:** Massive opposition movement led by Jayaprakash Narayan (JP) demanding PM's resignation.

- JP Movement gaining momentum
- "Total Revolution" call
- Railway strike (1974)
- Student protests widespread

#### 3️⃣ Political Crisis 🚨
**Loss of authority:** Government faced challenge to its authority with opposition demanding resignation.

- Pressure to resign mounting
- Political instability
- Opposition unity growing

#### 4️⃣ Fear of Losing Power 👤
**Real reason (widely believed):** PM wanted to suppress opposition and continue in power despite legal judgment against her.

- Political survival at stake
- Wanted to avoid stepping down
- Suppress dissent

### 📝 Key Point:
**Official reason:** "Internal disturbance"  
**Real reasons:** Political - to save government from crisis

---

### 😱 What Happened During Emergency (5-6 marks)

### 6 Major Actions/Consequences (COUNT = 6)

#### 1️⃣ Suspension of Fundamental Rights 🚫
**Most severe impact:** Fundamental Rights, including right to life and liberty, made unenforceable - people had no legal protection.

**Rights Suspended:**
- **Article 19** (6 freedoms) suspended
- **Article 21** (Life & Liberty) made unenforceable (Presidential order under Article 359)
- Right to move courts suspended
- Citizens helpless against government actions

#### 2️⃣ Press Censorship 📰
**Attack on freedom:** Pre-censorship imposed on newspapers - they could only publish what government allowed.

- Pre-censorship on all newspapers
- Electricity cut to newspaper offices
- Journalists arrested
- News controlled by government
- Press freedom completely destroyed

#### 3️⃣ Mass Arrests Without Trial 🔒
**MISA misuse:** Thousands arrested under Maintenance of Internal Security Act (MISA) and detained without trial.

- Opposition leaders imprisoned
- **George Fernandes, Atal Bihari Vajpayee, L.K. Advani** arrested
- Thousands of political workers detained
- No trials, no charges, indefinite detention
- Estimated 1 lakh+ arrests

#### 4️⃣ Parliament Became Rubber Stamp 🏛️
**Democracy suspended:** Parliament passed laws without debate, democratic functioning suspended.

- Laws passed without debate
- Opposition absent (in jail)
- **42nd Amendment** passed (most controversial)
- Democratic process defunct

#### 5️⃣ ADM Jabalpur Case (1976) - Judiciary's Dark Hour ⚖️
**Supreme Court's failure:** In **ADM Jabalpur v. Shivakant Shukla (1976)**, Supreme Court upheld government's position that even Article 21 could be suspended during emergency.

**What Supreme Court Held:**
- During emergency, right to move court for enforcement of FRs suspended
- Even **right to life (Article 21)** not enforceable
- Government could detain anyone without giving reasons
- Only **Justice H.R. Khanna dissented** (and was superseded - punishment for dissent)

**Why This is Called Darkest Hour:**
- Judiciary failed to protect people
- Most criticized judgment in Indian legal history
- Showed danger of unlimited emergency powers

#### 6️⃣ Forced Sterilization & Excesses 😱
**Administrative excesses:** Sanjay Gandhi's drive for forced sterilization and slum demolition caused widespread suffering.

- Forced vasectomy camps
- Slum demolitions without notice
- Police excesses unchecked
- Administrative arbitrariness

---

### 🎓 Lessons Learned (2 marks)

### 5 Key Lessons (COUNT = 5)

1️⃣ **Emergency powers dangerous** - Can destroy democracy if misused  
2️⃣ **Checks and balances essential** - No institution should have unlimited power  
3️⃣ **Fundamental Rights protection** - Some rights must never be suspended  
4️⃣ **Judicial independence crucial** - Courts must protect people even during crisis  
5️⃣ **People's vigilance necessary** - Democracy requires eternal vigilance  

### 📝 Dr. Ambedkar's Warning:
"However good a Constitution may be, it is sure to turn out bad if those implementing it are not good"

---

### 🛡️ Reforms - 44th Constitutional Amendment (1978) (4-5 marks)

### 7 Major Safeguards Introduced (COUNT = 7)

**Context:** After Emergency ended in March 1977, Janata Party came to power and enacted 44th Amendment to prevent future misuse.

#### 1️⃣ Ground Changed 📝
**From "internal disturbance" to "armed rebellion":**
- Made ground more specific
- Requires actual armed violence
- Ordinary disturbances not enough
- Prevents arbitrary proclamation

#### 2️⃣ Cabinet Recommendation Mandatory 📋
**Cannot proclaim without Cabinet advice:**
- President cannot act suo motu
- Written Cabinet recommendation required
- Prevents individual arbitrary action

#### 3️⃣ Parliamentary Approval Made Stricter 🏛️
**Special majority required:**
- **Before 1978**: Simple majority sufficient
- **After 44th Amendment**: Special majority needed
- 2/3rd of members present and voting + majority of total membership
- Makes approval more difficult

#### 4️⃣ Protection of Articles 20 & 21 🛡️
**Most important safeguard:**
- **Articles 20 and 21 cannot be suspended** even during emergency
- Right to life and personal liberty always protected
- Protection against ex-post facto laws maintained
- Directly responds to ADM Jabalpur failure

#### 5️⃣ Lok Sabha Can Revoke 🗳️
**Democratic check:**
- Lok Sabha can pass resolution to revoke emergency
- Only **simple majority** needed in Lok Sabha
- President **must revoke** if Lok Sabha so directs
- Empowers people's House

#### 6️⃣ Limited to Specific Area 🗺️
**Geographical limitation:**
- Emergency can be proclaimed for whole India OR specific part
- Need not apply to entire country
- Allows targeted response

#### 7️⃣ Judicial Review Strengthened ⚖️
**Courts can examine:**
- Whether grounds existed for proclamation
- Whether procedure followed
- Whether Cabinet recommendation genuine
- Cannot be completely immune from review

---

### Conclusion (1 mark)

The 1975-77 Emergency was a watershed moment in Indian constitutional history. It demonstrated how emergency provisions could be misused to destroy democracy. The **44th Amendment reforms** strengthened democratic safeguards ensuring such abuse doesn't recur. As Justice H.R. Khanna's dissent reminds us, "the Constitution is not suspended even during emergency." Today, these reforms stand as guardian against authoritarianism, proving democracy in India has learned from its darkest hour.
