### Introduction (1-2 marks)

**What is Federalism:**
Federalism is a system of government where constitutional powers are divided between a central authority (Union) and regional units (States). Both levels of government derive authority from the Constitution and operate independently in their respective spheres.

**India's Federal Character:**
India is described as **"quasi-federal"** - federal in structure but unitary in spirit (Article 1 calls it "Union of States" not "Federation"). The Constitution establishes a federal system with unique features distinguishing it from classical federations like the United States.

---

### ⚖️ Essential Characteristics of Indian Federalism (7-8 marks)

### 7 Essential Characteristics (COUNT = 7)

#### 1️⃣ Written Constitution 📜
**Explanation:** India has a comprehensive written Constitution that clearly defines and distributes powers between the Union and States. This provides legal certainty and forms the basis of federal structure.

- Detailed written document
- Defines Union-State powers explicitly
- Unlike Britain's unwritten Constitution
- Provides legal framework for federalism

#### 2️⃣ Division of Powers 🔀
**Explanation:** The Constitution distributes legislative powers through the Seventh Schedule containing three Lists, ensuring clear demarcation of Union and State subjects.

**Three Lists:**
- **Union List** (List I): 97 subjects (e.g., Defense, Foreign Affairs, Railways)
- **State List** (List II): 66 subjects (e.g., Police, Agriculture, Local Government)
- **Concurrent List** (List III): 47 subjects (e.g., Criminal Law, Education, Forests)
- **Residuary Powers**: With Parliament (Article 248)

#### 3️⃣ Supremacy of the Constitution 👑
**Explanation:** The Constitution is the supreme law of land. All laws must conform to it; any law violating the Constitution is void (Article 13).

- Constitution supreme over Union and State laws
- Judicial review ensures conformity
- Neither Union nor State can violate it

#### 4️⃣ Independent Judiciary ⚖️
**Explanation:** Supreme Court acts as the guardian of Constitution with power of judicial review, settling disputes between Union and States impartially.

- Supreme Court at apex
- Power of judicial review
- Settles Centre-State disputes
- Interprets Constitution authoritatively

#### 5️⃣ Bicameral Legislature 🏛️
**Explanation:** Parliament consists of two Houses - Lok Sabha representing people and Rajya Sabha representing States, ensuring States have voice in Union legislation.

- **Lok Sabha**: Represents people directly
- **Rajya Sabha**: Represents States
- Bicameralism ensures federal balance
- States participate in central law-making

#### 6️⃣ Dual Government 🏢
**Explanation:** Two levels of government - Union Government at Centre and State Governments in States - each with defined powers and functions.

- Union Government (Centre)
- State Governments (in each State)
- Both derive powers from Constitution
- Operate in respective spheres

#### 7️⃣ Rigid and Flexible Constitution ⚙️
**Explanation:** Constitution has provisions for amendment but procedure varies - some provisions easy to amend (flexible), others difficult (rigid), especially federal provisions.

**Three Types of Amendment (Article 368):**
- **Simple Majority**: Few provisions (e.g., creation of new States)
- **Special Majority**: Most provisions (2/3 present + majority of total)
- **Special Majority + State Ratification**: Federal structure provisions (50% States must ratify)

### 🔁 Indian Federalism Distinguished from American Federalism (5-6 marks)

### 10 Key Differences (COUNT = 10)

**Comparison Table:**

| S.No. | Feature | Indian Federalism 🇮🇳 | American Federalism 🇺🇸 |
|-------|---------|---------------------|----------------------|
| 1️⃣ | **Nature** | Quasi-federal (federal in structure, unitary in spirit) | True/Classical federation |
| 2️⃣ | **Creation** | Union created and reorganized States | States came together to form Union |
| 3️⃣ | **Term Used** | "Union of States" (Article 1) | "Federation" |
| 4️⃣ | **Distribution** | Three Lists (Union, State, Concurrent) | Two Lists (Federal and State) |
| 5️⃣ | **Residuary Powers** | Vested in Union Parliament (Article 248) | Vested in States |
| 6️⃣ | **Citizenship** | Single citizenship (only Indian) | Dual citizenship (Federal + State) |
| 7️⃣ | **Emergency** | Strong emergency powers; can become unitary | Limited/weak emergency provisions |
| 8️⃣ | **Governor/State Head** | Governor appointed by President | Governor elected by State people |
| 9️⃣ | **Constitution** | Single Constitution for entire country | Each State has separate Constitution |
| 🔟 | **Flexibility** | Can shift federal-unitary during emergency | Rigid federal structure maintained |

### 📝 Key Points to Elaborate:

**1. Nature:**
- India called quasi-federal by K.C. Wheare
- Federal features exist but strong unitary bias
- USA is classical federation with States having more autonomy

**2. Residuary Powers:**
- Most crucial difference
- In India: What's not mentioned goes to Union (centralizing)
- In USA: What's not given to federal goes to States (decentralizing)

**3. Emergency Provisions:**
- India's extensive emergency powers can convert it to unitary state
- Article 352, 356, 360 give Centre tremendous control
- USA has no such comprehensive emergency provisions

---

### Conclusion (1 mark)

Indian federalism is unique - it combines federal structure with unitary features to suit India's needs. While inspired by USA, it's significantly different due to India's diversity, size, and need for strong Centre. The **quasi-federal** character allows flexibility - federal in normal times, unitary when needed.

**Key Case:** **Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973)** held federalism is part of basic structure and cannot be destroyed by amendment, protecting India's federal character.
