### Introduction (1-2 marks)

**What is Defection:**
Defection means when an elected representative leaves the party on whose symbol they were elected and joins another party, or votes against party's direction. In the 1960s-80s, India witnessed rampant defections causing political instability - famously called **"Aaya Ram, Gaya Ram"** politics (named after a Haryana MLA who changed parties thrice in a day).

**Legislative Response:**
To curb this menace, the **52nd Constitutional Amendment (1985)** added the **Tenth Schedule** to the Constitution, providing for disqualification of members on grounds of defection. The law came into effect on **March 1, 1985** and applies to both Parliament and State Legislatures.

---

### ⚖️ Grounds for Disqualification (6-7 marks)

### 4 Main Grounds Under Paragraph 2 (COUNT = 4)

#### 1️⃣ Voluntarily Gives Up Membership of Party 🚪
**Explanation:** If a member voluntarily resigns from the political party on whose ticket they were elected, they are disqualified.

**What Constitutes "Voluntary":**
- Formal written resignation from party, OR
- Conduct showing resignation (joining another party, accepting position in another party)

**Example:** If a BJP member resigns from BJP and joins Congress, disqualified

**Key Point:** Need not be express resignation - can be inferred from conduct

#### 2️⃣ Votes or Abstains Contrary to Party Whip 🗳️
**Explanation:** If a member votes in the House contrary to any direction (whip) issued by their party, or abstains from voting when party directed to vote, they are disqualified.

**What is Whip:**
- Direction issued by political party
- Instructs members how to vote on important matters
- Binding on all party members

**Exception:** If party condones the conduct within **15 days**, no disqualification

**Example:** If party whip says "vote for bill" but member votes against or remains absent, disqualified (unless condoned)

**Types of Whips:**
- **One-line whip**: Informing about business
- **Two-line whip**: Expected to be present
- **Three-line whip**: Must vote as directed (strictly enforced)

#### 3️⃣ Nominated Member Joins Political Party (within 6 months) 🎫
**Explanation:** Nominated members are appointed for their expertise/eminence, not political affiliation. If they join a political party within 6 months, shows they were not independent.

**Application:**
- Applies only to **nominated members** (Rajya Sabha, Legislative Council)
- Must join within **6 months** of taking seat
- After 6 months, can join freely without disqualification

**Rationale:** Nominated members expected to be independent initially

**Example:** If President nominates a sports person to Rajya Sabha and they join BJP within 6 months, disqualified

#### 4️⃣ Independent Candidate Joins Political Party 🆓
**Explanation:** If a candidate elected as independent (without any party symbol) joins any political party after election, they are disqualified.

**Rationale:** 
- Voters elected them as independent
- Joining party betrays voters' trust
- Voter mandate was for independent, not party member

**Example:** If independent MLA wins and later joins a political party, disqualified

---

### ⚠️ Exceptions - When NOT Disqualified (2 marks)

### 2 Exceptions (COUNT = 2)

#### Exception 1: Merger of Party 🔀
**Explanation:** If 2/3rds of members of a legislature party decide to merge with another party, it's considered legitimate political realignment, not defection.

**Requirements:**
- **At least 2/3 members** of legislature party must agree
- Formal merger with another party
- Not individual defection but collective decision

**Rationale:** Collective decision of overwhelming majority represents genuine political change

**Example:** If 10 out of 12 party MLAs merge with another party, all 10 are safe. Only 2 who don't join are considered to have split.

#### Exception 2: Presiding Officer (Speaker/Chairman) 🪑
**Explanation:** When a member is elected as Speaker or Chairman, they must remain neutral. Hence, they can give up party membership temporarily. On ceasing to be presiding officer, can rejoin original party.

**Why Exception:**
- Presiding officer must be impartial
- Cannot belong to party while presiding
- Temporary resignation from party needed
- Can rejoin without penalty after office

### 📌 Kihoto Hollohan v. Zachillhu (1992) - Landmark Case (3-4 marks)

### Case Details

**Facts:**
- Kihoto Hollohan was member of Nagaland Legislative Assembly
- Belonged to Congress party
- Disqualified by Speaker under Tenth Schedule
- Challenged the constitutional validity of Tenth Schedule
- Also challenged Speaker's power to decide disqualification

**Issues Before Supreme Court (3 issues):**
1. Whether Tenth Schedule violates freedom of speech (Article 105/194)?
2. Whether it violates right to form associations (Article 19(1)(c))?
3. Whether Speaker's decision is final and beyond judicial review?

### Supreme Court's Decision (5-Judge Bench)

#### Held 1: Tenth Schedule is Valid ✅
**Reasoning:**
- Tenth Schedule does not violate basic structure
- Freedom of speech in Parliament not absolute
- Must be regulated by parliamentary rules
- Anti-defection law is reasonable restriction
- Promotes party discipline and political stability

#### Held 2: Does Not Violate Article 19(1)(c) ✅
**Reasoning:**
- Right to form associations doesn't include right to defect
- Tenth Schedule regulates membership consequences, not formation
- Reasonable restriction in public interest

#### Held 3: Speaker's Decision Subject to Judicial Review ⚖️
**Key Ruling:**
- Paragraph 7 of Tenth Schedule gave finality to Speaker's decision
- Supreme Court held: **Not completely final**
- **Judicial review possible** on grounds of:
  - Malafide (bad faith)
  - Perversity (completely unreasonable)
  - Violation of constitutional mandate
- However, limited review - courts won't interfere in merits

**Practical Effect:**
- Speaker decides but courts can review
- Review only on procedural/jurisdictional grounds
- Not on merits of whether defection occurred

### Significance of the Case:
✅ Upheld anti-defection law  
✅ Balanced parliamentary autonomy with judicial review  
✅ Protected Tenth Schedule from challenge  
✅ Ensured some judicial oversight  

---

### Conclusion (1 mark)

The Anti-Defection Law has succeeded in reducing political defections and providing stability to governments. While it has limitations (restricts freedom of conscience, gives power to Speaker who may be biased), it has overall strengthened party system and democracy. The **Kihoto Hollohan judgment** struck the right balance between parliamentary autonomy and judicial review, ensuring the law remains effective while being subject to constitutional checks.
