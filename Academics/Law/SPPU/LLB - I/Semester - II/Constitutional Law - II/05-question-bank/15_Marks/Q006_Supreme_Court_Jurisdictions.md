### Introduction (1 mark)

**Supreme Court - The Apex Court:**
The Supreme Court of India, established under **Article 124**, is the highest judicial authority in the country and the guardian of the Constitution. It exercises wide-ranging jurisdictions under Articles 131-143, including original, writ, appellate, advisory, and review jurisdictions. Being the apex court, its decisions are binding on all courts in India under **Article 141**.

---

### ⚖️ Five Jurisdictions of Supreme Court (12-13 marks)

### Jurisdiction Types (COUNT = 5)

#### 1️⃣ Original Jurisdiction (Article 131) 🏛️

**What it means:** Supreme Court has exclusive original jurisdiction in certain disputes - parties directly approach SC without going to lower courts.

**Disputes Under Article 131:**

**3 Types of Disputes (COUNT = 3):**

**A. Union vs One or More States:**
- Centre has dispute with State(s)
- Example: Border disputes, constitutional interpretation

**B. Union and One or More States vs Other State(s):**
- Centre and some States on one side
- Other State(s) on opposite side

**C. Two or More States:**
- Inter-State disputes
- Example: River water disputes, boundary disputes

**Exclusions (Cannot hear):**
- Disputes arising from pre-Constitution treaties/agreements
- Disputes where treaty provides different mechanism
- Election disputes (go to High Courts first)

**Important Cases:**
- **State of Kerala v. State of Tamil Nadu** (River water dispute)
- **State of Karnataka v. State of Maharashtra** (Border dispute)

**Significance:** Only Supreme Court can decide these disputes - ensures neutral federal umpire.

---

#### 2️⃣ Writ Jurisdiction (Article 32) 📜

**What it means:** Supreme Court can issue five types of writs for **enforcement of Fundamental Rights only**.

**Dr. Ambedkar:** Called Article 32 **"Heart and Soul of the Constitution"**

**5 Types of Writs (COUNT = 5):**

**A. Habeas Corpus** 🔓 ("Produce the body")
- To release person from illegal detention
- Example: Rudul Sah v. State of Bihar (1983)

**B. Mandamus** 📋 ("We command")
- To command public official to perform duty
- Cannot issue to private persons

**C. Prohibition** 🚫 ("Forbid")
- To prevent lower court from exceeding jurisdiction
- Issued **before** final judgment (preventive)

**D. Certiorari** ✔️ ("Be certified")
- To quash order of lower court/tribunal
- Issued **after** judgment (curative)

**E. Quo Warranto** ❓ ("By what authority")
- To check if person holding public office is qualified
- Prevents usurpation of office

🧠 **Mnemonic**: **H-M-P-C-Q** = "Help Me Please, Correct Quickly"

**Key Features:**
- Article 32 is itself a Fundamental Right
- Supreme Court **must entertain** such petitions (cannot refuse)
- Can be suspended during emergency (Article 359)
- **Limited scope**: Only for Fundamental Rights (not other legal rights)

**Article 32 vs Article 226 (High Court):**
- Article 32: Only Fundamental Rights
- Article 226: Fundamental Rights + Other legal rights (WIDER)

---

#### 3️⃣ Appellate Jurisdiction (Articles 132-136) 📤

**What it means:** Supreme Court hears appeals from High Courts on important questions of law.

**4 Types of Appeals (COUNT = 4):**

**A. Constitutional Cases (Article 132):**
**When:** If High Court certifies the case involves substantial question of constitutional interpretation.

- HC must certify
- Constitutional question must be substantial
- Automatic right of appeal

**B. Civil Cases (Article 133):**
**When:** If High Court certifies the case is fit for appeal to Supreme Court.

- Substantial question of law of general importance
- HC certifies fitness
- High value civil cases

**C. Criminal Cases (Article 134):**
**When:** Appeals in criminal matters in specific situations.

**3 Situations:**
1. HC has **reversed acquittal** and sentenced to **death**
2. HC **withdrew case** from subordinate court and convicted with sentence
3. HC **certifies** case is fit for appeal

**D. Special Leave Petition (Article 136) - MOST IMPORTANT:**
**What it is:** Supreme Court may grant special leave to appeal from any judgment/order of any court/tribunal in India.

**Key Features:**
- **SC's discretion** to grant leave or refuse
- From **any court or tribunal** (not just High Courts)
- **Most common route** to Supreme Court (90%+ cases)
- Can be on any matter (civil, criminal, constitutional, tax, service, etc.)
- Used when substantial injustice has occurred

**Limitations:**
- Discretionary (SC can refuse)
- Not as of right
- SC grants only in cases of substantial injustice

**Significance:** Makes Supreme Court accessible for justice even when regular appeal routes exhausted.

---

#### 4️⃣ Advisory Jurisdiction (Article 143) 💭

**What it means:** President can refer questions to Supreme Court for its opinion.

**When Used:**
President may refer questions:
- Of law or fact
- Of public importance
- Have arisen or likely to arise

**Supreme Court's Role:**
- Examines the question
- Hears arguments
- Gives opinion

**Nature of Opinion:**
- **Advisory only** (not binding on President)
- However, usually followed
- Not a judgment (so not binding under Article 141)

**Examples:**
- **Re: Berubari Union** (1960): Transfer of territory to Pakistan
- **Re: Special Courts Bill** (1979): Validity of special courts
- **In Re: Presidential Poll** (1974): Electoral college issues
- **Ayodhya Reference** (attempted but withdrawn)

**Significance:** Allows President to get SC's opinion on important questions before taking action.

---

#### 5️⃣ Review Jurisdiction (Article 137) 🔄

**What it means:** Supreme Court can review its own judgments and orders to correct errors.

**When Exercised:**
- To correct errors apparent on face of record
- When new evidence discovered
- Sufficient cause shown

**Limitations:**
- **Not an appeal** (not re-hearing on merits)
- **Limited scope** - only for apparent errors
- Cannot substitute judgment
- Review power used sparingly

**Same Bench or Larger:**
- Usually same bench reviews
- For important matters, larger bench may review

**Examples:**
- **Rupa Ashok Hurra v. Ashok Hurra (2002)**: Review in divorce case
- Many criminal cases reviewed when new evidence emerges

**Significance:** Acts as self-correcting mechanism, ensuring finality with justice.

### 📊 Summary Table - Five Jurisdictions

| Jurisdiction | Article | Type | Scope | Can Refuse? |
|--------------|---------|------|-------|-------------|
| **Original** | 131 | Exclusive | Centre-State disputes | No |
| **Writ** | 32 | Concurrent with HCs | Only Fundamental Rights | No |
| **Appellate** | 132-136 | From HCs | Constitutional, Civil, Criminal, SLP | Yes (SLP) |
| **Advisory** | 143 | Advisory | Questions by President | No |
| **Review** | 137 | Own judgments | Correct errors | Yes |

### Conclusion (1 mark)

The Supreme Court's multiple jurisdictions make it the guardian of Constitution, protector of Fundamental Rights, and final arbiter of law in India. Its **original jurisdiction** maintains federal balance, **writ jurisdiction** protects rights, **appellate jurisdiction** ensures uniform law, **advisory jurisdiction** guides executive, and **review jurisdiction** corrects errors. The **Article 141** provision making SC's decisions binding ensures uniformity of law throughout India, making it truly the apex court.
