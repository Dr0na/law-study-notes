### Introduction (1 mark)

**President - The Constitutional Head:**
Under Article 52, there shall be a President of India who is the Constitutional Head of State. The President is elected indirectly by an Electoral College (MPs + MLAs) for a term of 5 years. While Article 53 vests all executive power of the Union in the President, Article 74 makes it clear that the President must act on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers, making the President a **nominal executive** while real power rests with the Prime Minister and Cabinet.

---

### ⚖️ Powers and Functions of President (10-11 marks)

### 5 Categories of Powers (COUNT = 5)

#### 1️⃣ Executive Powers (Article 53) 🏛️

**What this covers:** All executive actions of Union Government taken in President's name, but decisions made by Cabinet.

**7 Key Executive Powers:**

**A. Appointment Powers:**
- **Prime Minister** and other Ministers (Article 75)
- **Attorney General of India** (Article 76)
- **Comptroller and Auditor General** (Article 148)
- **Chief Election Commissioner** and other Election Commissioners (Article 324)
- **Judges** of Supreme Court and High Courts (Articles 124, 217)
- **Governors** of States (Article 155)
- **Chairman and Members** of UPSC, Finance Commission
- All major Union appointments

**B. Administrative Powers:**
- All executive actions in President's name (Article 77)
- Makes rules for business allocation
- Allocates portfolios (on PM's advice)

**C. Military Powers:**
- **Supreme Commander** of Defense Forces
- Can declare war or conclude peace (on Cabinet's advice)
- Appoints Chiefs of Army, Navy, Air Force

**D. Diplomatic Powers:**
- Represents India internationally
- Appoints **Ambassadors and High Commissioners** to other countries
- Receives credentials of foreign ambassadors

**E. Treaty-Making Power:**
- International treaties and agreements made in President's name
- Requires Parliament's approval (Article 253)

#### 2️⃣ Legislative Powers 📜

**What this covers:** President is part of Parliament and has important legislative functions.

**7 Key Legislative Powers:**

**A. Summoning and Proroguing Parliament (Article 85):**
- Summons both Houses
- Prorogues sessions
- Can dissolve Lok Sabha

**B. Addressing Parliament (Article 87):**
- Addresses joint session after general elections
- First session of each year
- Outlines government's policy

**C. Assent to Bills (Article 111):**
- Bills need President's assent to become law
- Can give assent, withhold assent, or return for reconsideration (once, except money bills)
- **Pocket veto**: Can keep bill pending

**D. Ordinance-Making Power (Article 123):**
- When Parliament not in session
- If immediate action needed
- Ordinance has same force as law
- Must be approved by Parliament within **6 weeks** of reassembly
- Can be withdrawn anytime

**Limitations on Ordinance:**
- Cannot violate Fundamental Rights
- Not a substitute for legislation
- **D.C. Wadhwa case (1987)**: Excessive ordinances unconstitutional

**E. Nominating Members:**
- **12 members** to Rajya Sabha (Article 80) - experts in literature, science, art, social service
- 2 Anglo-Indians to Lok Sabha (Article 331) - *lapsed in 2020*

**F. Messages to Parliament (Article 86):**
- Can send messages to either House
- Can address either House

**G. Recommendations:**
- President's recommendation needed for Money Bills
- For bills affecting taxation
- For creation of new States

#### 3️⃣ Financial Powers 💰

**What this covers:** Financial matters require President's approval, ensuring executive control over finances.

**5 Key Financial Powers:**

**A. Budget (Article 112):**
- Annual Financial Statement (Budget) laid before Parliament in President's name
- No taxation without President's recommendation

**B. Appropriation:**
- No money can be withdrawn from Consolidated Fund without President's approval
- Through Appropriation Act

**C. Contingency Fund (Article 267):**
- President controls Contingency Fund of India
- For urgent unforeseen expenditures
- Later regularized by Parliament

**D. Finance Commission (Article 280):**
- President constitutes Finance Commission every 5 years
- Recommends financial distribution

**E. Grants:**
- Grants from Consolidated Fund on President's recommendation

#### 4️⃣ Judicial Powers ⚖️

**What this covers:** President has important judicial functions including mercy petitions.

**4 Key Judicial Powers:**

**A. Appointment of Judges:**
- Supreme Court judges (Article 124)
- High Court judges (Article 217)
- Through collegium system

**B. Pardon Power (Article 72) - MOST IMPORTANT:**

**Can Grant 5 Types:**

**1. Pardon** 🕊️ - Complete forgiveness, conviction + sentence removed  
**2. Commutation** ⚖️ - Reduces sentence (death → life imprisonment)  
**3. Remission** 📉 - Reduces duration of sentence  
**4. Respite** ⏸️ - Delays execution temporarily  
**5. Reprieve** ⏳ - Stays execution for some time  

**Extent of President's Pardon:**
- All offenses against Union laws
- **All death sentences** (even under State laws)
- **Court-martial cases** (military offenses)

**Comparison with Governor (Article 161):**
- Governor: Only State law offenses
- Governor: Cannot pardon death sentences
- Governor: Cannot pardon court-martial
- **President's power wider**

**Key Case:** **Kehar Singh v. Union of India (1989)**
- Mercy petition rejection challenged
- Held: Pardon power absolute but judicially reviewable for malafide

**C. Seeking Advice from Supreme Court (Article 143):**
- Can refer questions to SC for advisory opinion

**D. Appointments of Judicial Officers:**
- Can appoint ad-hoc judges (Article 127)

#### 5️⃣ Emergency Powers 🚨

**What this covers:** President can proclaim three types of emergencies in crisis situations.

**3 Types of Emergency:**

**A. National Emergency (Article 352):**
- Grounds: War, External Aggression, Armed Rebellion
- Union becomes supreme

**B. State Emergency/President's Rule (Article 356):**
- Ground: Constitutional machinery failure in State
- State under Union control

**C. Financial Emergency (Article 360):**
- Ground: Financial stability threatened
- Never used

**(Detailed in Module 8)**

### 📋 Position of President Under Article 74 (3-4 marks)

**Context:** Article 74 is crucial to understanding that President is nominal head, not real executive.

### Article 74 - Aid and Advice (COUNT = 4 aspects)

#### 1️⃣ Constitutional Provision 📜
**Article 74(1) states:** "There shall be a Council of Ministers with the Prime Minister at the head to aid and advise the President who **shall**, in the exercise of his functions, **act in accordance with such advice**."

**Key Word:** **"Shall act"** = Mandatory, not discretionary

#### 2️⃣ 42nd Amendment (1976) - Made Binding 🔒
**What changed:** Word **"shall"** inserted, removing all ambiguity.

**Before 42nd:** Could be interpreted that President may or may not accept advice  
**After 42nd:** President **must** act on advice (made explicitly binding)  
**Context:** During Emergency, made President completely subservient to PM

#### 3️⃣ 44th Amendment (1978) - Reconsideration Clause ⚖️
**What added:** Article 74(2) - President can ask Council to **reconsider** advice once.

**Provision:**
- President may ask Council to reconsider
- Council reconsiders (may modify or send same advice)
- President **must accept** reconsidered advice
- Only **one chance** to ask reconsideration

**Balance:** Prevents completely mechanical acceptance while keeping advice binding

#### 4️⃣ Nominal vs Real Executive 👤
**What this means:** President is Constitutional Head (de jure) but PM and Cabinet are Real Executive (de facto).

**President:**
- Signs orders, appointments
- Formal head
- Acts on advice

**PM & Cabinet:**
- Make actual decisions
- Run government
- Real power holders

**Very Limited Discretion:**
President can act independently only in:
- Appointing PM (no clear majority)
- Dismissing government (extreme case)
- Seeking information from PM (Article 78)
- Returning non-money bill once

### 📌 Samsher Singh v. State of Punjab (1974)
**Principle:** President has no independent powers; must act on aid and advice of Council of Ministers in all matters.

---

### Conclusion (1 mark)

The President of India, while possessing vast powers on paper, exercises them only on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister. Article 74 ensures India follows the true Parliamentary system where the executive is responsible to the legislature. The President's role is that of a Constitutional Head who maintains dignity of the office while the PM and Cabinet run the actual government, making India's system similar to Britain's Constitutional monarchy.
