# 📝 Question ID: CON2-DIST-D001

## Question
**Distinguish between Prohibition and Certiorari.**

**Marks**: 5  
**Pattern**: 🔁 Comparative  
**Topic**: Module 5 - Writs  
**Exam Frequency**: 🔥🔥🔥🔥🔥 (Most asked distinction)  
**Time**: 6-7 minutes  

---

## 📋 MODEL ANSWER

### Introduction (0.5 mark)

**Two Important Writs:**
Prohibition and Certiorari are both writs issued by Supreme Court (Article 32) and High Courts (Article 226) to prevent or correct jurisdictional errors by lower courts and tribunals. While both deal with excess/lack of jurisdiction, they differ in timing, nature, and effect.

---

### 🔁 Key Distinctions (4 marks)

### 10 Differences (COUNT = 10)

| # | Feature | Prohibition 🚫 | Certiorari ✔️ |
|---|---------|---------------|---------------|
| 1️⃣ | **Timing** | **Before** final judgment/order | **After** final judgment/order |
| 2️⃣ | **Nature** | **Preventive** (stops before) | **Curative** (corrects after) |
| 3️⃣ | **Purpose** | To **prohibit** lower court from proceeding | To **quash** order already passed |
| 4️⃣ | **Stage** | During trial/proceedings | After decision given |
| 5️⃣ | **Effect** | Stops further proceedings | Nullifies/quashes the order |
| 6️⃣ | **Latin Meaning** | "To forbid" | "To be certified" |
| 7️⃣ | **When Issued** | Before jurisdictional error becomes final | After jurisdictional error materialized |
| 8️⃣ | **Prevents/Cures** | Prevents harm | Cures harm done |
| 9️⃣ | **Scope** | Limited to proceedings pending | Limited to order passed |
| 🔟 | **Action** | "Don't proceed further" | "This order is void" |

---

### 📝 Examples (0.5 mark)

**Prohibition Example:**
A labor tribunal starts hearing a service matter that only CAT can hear. Party files Prohibition writ; High Court orders labor tribunal to stop proceedings immediately.

**Certiorari Example:**
Income Tax Tribunal passes order on matter outside its jurisdiction. Party files Certiorari writ after order is passed; High Court quashes the order as without jurisdiction.

---

### 🧠 Memory Trick (0.5 mark)

**Remember:**
- **PRO**-hibition = **PRE**-vents (comes **before**)
- **CERT**-iorari = **CERT**-ainly wrong (corrects **after**)

**Or:**

- Prohibition = Doctor stops you **before** surgery (preventive)
- Certiorari = Doctor fixes you **after** wrong surgery (curative)

---

### Conclusion (0.5 mark)

Both writs are essential remedies for correcting jurisdictional errors by lower courts and tribunals. **Prohibition** is preferred when proceedings are ongoing (stops damage before it occurs), while **Certiorari** is used when order has already been passed (undoes the damage). The key difference lies in **timing** - before vs after judgment.
