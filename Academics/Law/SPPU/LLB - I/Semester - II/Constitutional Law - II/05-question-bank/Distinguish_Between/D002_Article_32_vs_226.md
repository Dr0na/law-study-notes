# 📝 Question ID: CON2-DIST-D002

## Question
**Distinguish between Article 32 and Article 226 of the Constitution.**

**Marks**: 5-10  
**Pattern**: 🔁 Comparative  
**Topic**: Module 5 - Writ Jurisdiction  
**Exam Frequency**: 🔥🔥🔥🔥 (Very frequent)  
**Time**: 6-7 minutes (5M) | 13 minutes (10M)  

---

## 📋 MODEL ANSWER

### Introduction (0.5 mark)

**Two Writ Jurisdictions:**
Both Article 32 (Supreme Court) and Article 226 (High Courts) empower courts to issue writs for protection of rights. However, they differ significantly in scope, nature, and accessibility. While Article 32 is called **"Heart and Soul"** of the Constitution by Dr. Ambedkar, **Article 226 has wider scope** as it covers not just Fundamental Rights but also other legal rights.

---

### 🔁 Key Distinctions (For 5 marks: 8 differences | For 10 marks: 12 differences)

### 12 Major Differences (COUNT = 12)

| # | Feature | Article 32 (SC) 🏛️ | Article 226 (HC) 🏢 |
|---|---------|-------------------|---------------------|
| 1️⃣ | **Scope** | Only for **Fundamental Rights** | For FRs + **Other legal rights** |
| 2️⃣ | **Court** | Supreme Court | High Courts |
| 3️⃣ | **Nature** | Fundamental Right itself (Part III) | Constitutional power (not FR) |
| 4️⃣ | **Duty** | SC **must** entertain (mandatory) | HC **may** entertain (discretionary) |
| 5️⃣ | **Can Refuse?** | ❌ No - cannot refuse FR petitions | ✅ Yes - discretion to refuse |
| 6️⃣ | **Territory** | All India (any violation anywhere) | Only that State/UT |
| 7️⃣ | **Against Whom** | Any authority in India | Authorities within State |
| 8️⃣ | **Writs** | Same 5 writs (H-M-P-C-Q) | Same 5 writs |
| 9️⃣ | **Suspension** | Can be suspended (Article 359 during emergency) | Cannot be suspended |
| 🔟 | **Appeal** | Review under Article 137 | Appeal to SC under Article 136 (SLP) |
| 1️⃣1️⃣ | **Accessibility** | Less (only in Delhi) | More (in every State) |
| 1️⃣2️⃣ | **Dr. Ambedkar** | "Heart and Soul" of Constitution | Not specifically described |

---

### 📊 Scope Comparison - Most Important Difference

**Article 32 (Narrower):**
- ✅ Fundamental Rights violations only
- ❌ Not for contractual rights
- ❌ Not for property rights (after 44th Amendment)
- ❌ Not for statutory rights
- ❌ Not for other legal rights

**Article 226 (Wider):**
- ✅ Fundamental Rights violations
- ✅ Other legal rights (property, contract)
- ✅ Statutory rights
- ✅ Any legal right under law
- ✅ "For any other purpose" under law

**Practical Impact:**
- If only FR violated → Can go to SC (32) or HC (226)
- If non-FR legal right violated → Can only go to HC (226)
- HC's power wider and more accessible

---

### 🧠 Memory Trick (0.5 mark)

**Article Numbers:**
- **32** is smaller number → Smaller scope (Only FRs)
- **226** is bigger number → Bigger scope (FRs + Others)

**Or:**

**"32 for Three-Two Rights"** = Only FRs (limited)  
**"226 for Two-Too-Big"** = FRs + others (wide)  

---

### Conclusion (0.5 mark)

While both Articles provide writ jurisdiction, **Article 226 is significantly wider in scope** than Article 32. However, Article 32 has special status as a Fundamental Right itself and Supreme Court cannot refuse to entertain petitions. In practice, most petitions go to High Courts under Article 226 due to accessibility, wider scope, and lower costs, with Supreme Court being approached under Article 136 (SLP) if High Court denies relief.
