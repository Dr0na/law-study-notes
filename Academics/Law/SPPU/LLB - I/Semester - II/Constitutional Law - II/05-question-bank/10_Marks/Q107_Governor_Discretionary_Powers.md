### Introduction (1 mark)

**Governor - Constitutional Head of State:**
Under Article 153, there shall be a Governor for each State who acts as the Constitutional Head. Unlike the President who is elected, the Governor is **appointed by the President** under Article 155 and holds office **during the President's pleasure** (Article 156). While Governor normally acts on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers (Article 163), the Governor has **more discretionary powers** than the President, making the position more independent in certain matters.

---

### ⚖️ Discretionary Powers of Governor (5-6 marks)

**Context:** Article 163 provides that Governor "may act in his discretion" in certain matters - these are situations where Governor need not follow CM's advice.

### 5 Main Discretionary Powers (COUNT = 5)

#### 1️⃣ Appointment of Chief Minister 👔
**When Governor uses discretion:** When no party has clear majority or after elections result in hung Assembly.

**Situations:**
- No single party has majority (hung Assembly)
- Coalition government formation
- Must assess who can form stable government

**Discretion:**
- Governor assesses who commands majority
- Can call leader of largest party OR
- Can call coalition leader who shows numbers
- Can ask to prove majority on floor (floor test)

**Post-Bommai:** Floor test preferred over Governor's assessment

**Example:** Karnataka 2018, Maharashtra 2019 - Governors used discretion in government formation

#### 2️⃣ Dismissal of Chief Minister 🚪
**When Governor uses discretion:** When CM loses majority in Assembly.

**Situations:**
- Government loses confidence vote
- Coalition breaks, majority lost
- CM refuses to resign despite losing majority

**Discretion:**
- Can dismiss CM who lost majority
- Can ask to prove majority on floor
- Can explore alternative government

**Limitation:** Cannot dismiss arbitrarily (Nabam Rebia case, 2016)

#### 3️⃣ Dissolution of Legislative Assembly 💥
**When Governor uses discretion:** Deciding whether to dissolve Assembly or explore alternatives.

**Situations:**
- After government falls
- When CM recommends dissolution
- When no party can form government

**Discretion:**
- Can dissolve if no alternative
- Can refuse dissolution if alternative exists
- Can give time for alternative government formation

**Post-Bommai:** Must explore all alternatives before dissolution

#### 4️⃣ Reservation of Bills for President's Consideration 📜
**When Governor uses discretion:** Certain bills Governor must/can reserve for President's assent instead of giving assent themselves.

**Compulsory Reservation:**
- Bills affecting High Court powers
- Bills derogatory to President's powers
- Bills repugnant to Union law on Concurrent subject

**Discretionary Reservation:**
- Bills of grave importance
- Bills involving constitutional issues
- Governor feels President should decide

**Effect:** Bill doesn't become law until President gives assent

#### 5️⃣ Recommending President's Rule (Article 356) 🏴
**When Governor uses discretion:** Assessing whether constitutional machinery has failed and recommending President's Rule.

**When Recommended:**
- Government cannot be formed
- No party commands majority
- Complete breakdown of law and order
- Government acts unconstitutionally

**Discretion:**
- Governor assesses situation
- Sends report to President
- Recommends President's Rule
- Or recommends dissolution + elections

**Post-Bommai:** Must be based on objective assessment, not political considerations

### 📌 Special Provisions for Some States

**Additional Discretionary Powers:**
- **Nagaland** (Article 371A): Special provisions
- **Sikkim** (Article 371F): Tribal areas
- **Other North-Eastern States**: Various special provisions

### 🆚 Governor vs President - Key Differences (3-4 marks)

### 8 Major Differences (COUNT = 8)

| Feature | President | Governor |
|---------|-----------|----------|
| 1️⃣ **Selection** | **Elected** (Electoral College) | **Appointed** (by President) |
| 2️⃣ **Term Security** | 5 years (secure) | 5 years but "**President's pleasure**" (insecure) |
| 3️⃣ **Removal** | Impeachment (difficult) | President's will (easy) |
| 4️⃣ **Discretionary Powers** | Very limited (rare) | More extensive |
| 5️⃣ **Independence** | Relatively more | Less (Centre's appointee) |
| 6️⃣ **Pardon Power** | All offenses + death + court-martial (Art 72) | State offenses only, no death (Art 161) |
| 7️⃣ **Accountability** | To Constitution | To President |
| 8️⃣ **Federal Role** | Represents nation | Represents Centre in State |

---

### Conclusion (1 mark)

The Governor occupies a unique position - theoretically the Constitutional Head of State but practically an appointee of the Centre serving during President's pleasure. While having more discretionary powers than the President, the Governor's effectiveness depends on maintaining impartiality and respecting democratic norms. Post-Bommai judicial vigilance has ensured Governors cannot act arbitrarily, though controversies continue over appointment, removal, and exercise of discretion.
