### Introduction (1 mark)

**Article 356 - President's Rule:**
Article 356 empowers the President to impose President's Rule in a State when the constitutional machinery in that State has failed. This provision is also called **"State Emergency"** and has been the **most controversial and misused** provision of the Constitution. Before the landmark **S.R. Bommai case (1994)**, Article 356 was frequently misused to dismiss State governments for political reasons, undermining federal structure.

---

### ⚖️ Article 356 - Provisions (3-4 marks)

### 4 Key Aspects (COUNT = 4)

#### 1️⃣ Ground for Imposition 🎯
**When can be imposed:** If President is satisfied that a situation has arisen where the government of a State cannot be carried on in accordance with the Constitution.

**Situations:**
- Government loses majority in Assembly
- Hung Assembly, no party can form government
- Breakdown of constitutional machinery
- Complete lawlessness
- Usually based on **Governor's report**

#### 2️⃣ Procedure 📋
**How it's imposed:**

**Step 1:** President issues proclamation  
**Step 2:** Both Houses of Parliament must approve within **2 months**  
**Step 3:** Approval by **simple majority** (not special majority)  
**Step 4:** Valid for **6 months initially**, can be extended  

**Maximum Duration:** **3 years** (only if National Emergency in operation OR Election Commission certifies elections cannot be held)

#### 3️⃣ Effects 💥
**What happens when President's Rule imposed:**

**On State Executive:**
- State Council of Ministers **dismissed**
- Governor administers State on behalf of President
- President assumes State's executive functions

**On State Legislature:**
- Legislative Assembly **dissolved or suspended**
- If suspended, can be revived later
- Legislative Council (if any) prorogued

**On Legislative Powers:**
- **Parliament can legislate** for that State on State List subjects
- President can authorize Governor to promulgate ordinances
- State subjects come under Union control

#### 4️⃣ Misuse Before 1994 ⚠️
**How it was misused:**

**Examples of Misuse:**
- **Kerala (1959)**: First use - dismissed Communist government
- **1977-79**: 9 State governments dismissed after Janata Party came to power
- **1980**: 9 State governments dismissed after Congress returned
- Often used when opposition party ruled State and different party at Centre
- Political weapon to dismiss inconvenient governments

### 📌 S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994) - Landmark Case (4-5 marks)

### Case Background

**Facts:**
- After Babri Masjid demolition (December 6, 1992)
- BJP governments in several States dismissed using Article 356
- **President's Rule imposed** in: Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, Himachal Pradesh, later Uttar Pradesh
- Dismissed governments challenged the proclamations
- 9-judge bench heard the case

**Issue:**
Whether President's Rule can be challenged in court? Can courts review President's "satisfaction" under Article 356?

### Supreme Court's Historic Decision (9-Judge Bench)

#### Ruling 1️⃣: Judicial Review Possible ⚖️
**What SC held:** President's satisfaction under Article 356 is **not immune** from judicial review. Courts can examine whether there was material for President's satisfaction.

**Reasoning:**
- Article 356 is not absolute
- Subject to constitutional limitations
- Courts can review if action violates Constitution
- Cannot be arbitrary or mala fide

#### Ruling 2️⃣: Governor's Report Not Conclusive 📋
**What SC held:** Governor's report is just one input. President must independently apply mind, not act mechanically on Governor's report.

**Reasoning:**
- President must examine material personally
- Cannot rubber-stamp Governor's recommendation
- Must have objective satisfaction
- Governor cannot be partisan

#### Ruling 3️⃣: Floor Test Preferred 🗳️
**What SC held:** Before dismissing a government, **floor test** should be ordered in the Assembly to determine if government has majority.

**Reasoning:**
- Let Assembly decide who has majority
- Democratic way to resolve doubt
- Dismissal should be last resort
- Governor cannot pre-judge majority

#### Ruling 4️⃣: Federalism is Basic Structure 🏛️
**What SC held:** Federalism is part of the basic structure of Constitution. Arbitrary use of Article 356 violates federalism.

**Reasoning:**
- States' autonomy must be respected
- Federal structure is basic feature
- Cannot be destroyed by misuse of Article 356
- States' rights protected

#### Ruling 5️⃣: Assembly Dissolution Examined 🔍
**What SC held:** If Assembly is dissolved, courts can examine if dissolution was justified. If Article 356 misused, Assembly can be restored.

**Reasoning:**
- Dissolution must be for valid reasons
- Prevents permanent damage
- Courts can undo arbitrary dissolution
- State legislature can be revived

### Guidelines Laid Down:

**Bommai Guidelines (COUNT = 7):**

1. President's Rule should be used **sparingly** - as last resort
2. **Warning** should be given to State government before imposing
3. **Floor test** should be preferred over dismissal
4. Article 356 cannot be used for **political purposes**
5. **Material** must exist for President's satisfaction
6. President must **independently apply mind**, not just follow Governor
7. Proclamation is **subject to judicial review**

---

### 📊 Impact of S.R. Bommai Case (1 mark)

**Positive Impact:**
✅ **Reduced misuse** of Article 356 significantly  
✅ **Floor tests** became norm (Governors order floor test now)  
✅ **Judicial scrutiny** made governments careful  
✅ **Federal autonomy** strengthened  
✅ **States' rights** better protected  

**Statistics:**
- Before 1994: Article 356 used 100+ times
- After 1994: Use reduced by 80%+
- Floor tests routinely ordered

**Conclusion:**
S.R. Bommai case transformed Article 356 from a political weapon to a constitutional provision subject to judicial oversight, strengthening India's federalism.
