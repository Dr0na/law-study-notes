### Introduction (1 mark)

**Collective Responsibility - Heart of Parliamentary System:**
Collective responsibility is a fundamental principle of the Parliamentary system of government borrowed from Britain. Under **Article 75(3)**, the Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha, meaning all Ministers swim or sink together. This ensures accountability of the Executive to the Legislature and maintains democratic control over government.

---

### ⚖️ Article 75(3) - Provision (2 marks)

**Constitutional Text:**
"The Council of Ministers shall be collectively responsible to the House of the People."

### What This Means (COUNT = 3 aspects)

#### 1️⃣ Collective Accountability 🤝
**What it requires:** All Ministers are answerable together to Lok Sabha for government policies and actions.

- Not individual responsibility (that exists too)
- Entire Council accountable as a team
- All policies are Council's policies, not individual Minister's

#### 2️⃣ No-Confidence Motion 🎯
**How it operates:** If Lok Sabha passes a no-confidence motion, the entire Council must resign.

- Cannot say "remove only this Minister"
- All Ministers must go together
- Even Ministers who disagreed internally must resign

#### 3️⃣ Unity in Public 🗣️
**What it demands:** All Ministers must publicly support all Cabinet decisions.

- Cannot criticize Cabinet decisions publicly
- Must defend all policies
- Present united front
- If fundamental disagreement, must resign

---

### 🔹 How Collective Responsibility Works (5-6 marks)

### 6 Key Aspects (COUNT = 6)

#### 1️⃣ Cabinet Decision-Making 💼
**In Cabinet meetings:** Ministers may disagree, debate, argue during Cabinet meetings.

- Free discussion in Cabinet
- Ministers can voice concerns
- Healthy debate encouraged
- PM listens to all views

**After Decision:** Once Cabinet decides, all must support.

- Dissent in Cabinet ✅
- Dissent outside Cabinet ❌

#### 2️⃣ Public Solidarity 🤝
**What it requires:** All Ministers must speak with one voice publicly.

**Cannot Do:**
- Criticize Cabinet decision in public
- Distance themselves from policy
- Blame other Ministers
- Show internal disagreements

**Must Do:**
- Defend all Cabinet decisions
- Support government policy
- Maintain unity

**If Can't Support:** Must resign from Council

#### 3️⃣ Parliamentary Defense 🏛️
**In Parliament:** All Ministers must defend government policies when questioned.

- Even if personally disagreed
- Even if not their department
- Government's collective stand

**Example:** During no-confidence debate, all Ministers defend government, not just PM

#### 4️⃣ No-Confidence Motion Mechanism ⚖️
**How it works:** Any member can move no-confidence in Lok Sabha.

**Procedure:**
- At least **50 members** must support notice
- Speaker admits motion
- Debate held
- Vote taken by **simple majority**
- If motion passes, entire Council resigns

**Key Point:** Motion against "Council of Ministers" not individual Minister

#### 5️⃣ Resignation of Entire Council 🚪
**What happens:** If no-confidence passed or government loses crucial vote (like Budget).

**Effect:**
- Entire Council must resign
- PM and all Ministers
- Cannot continue in office
- Governor must find alternative OR
- President dissolves Lok Sabha (on PM's advice)

**Alternative:** PM can advise President to dissolve Lok Sabha and hold fresh elections

#### 6️⃣ Individual Responsibility Also Exists 👤
**Dual accountability:** While collectively responsible, each Minister also individually responsible for their department.

**Individual Responsibility Means:**
- Must answer questions on their ministry
- Accountable for department's performance
- Can be removed by PM for failures
- Doesn't affect collective responsibility

**Difference:**
- Individual: Only that Minister resigns/removed
- Collective: All Ministers must resign

---

### 📊 Why Collective Responsibility is Important

### 4 Reasons (COUNT = 4)

#### 1️⃣ Democratic Control ⚖️
**What it ensures:** Elected legislature controls appointed executive.

- Executive accountable to people (through Lok Sabha)
- Government cannot ignore Parliament
- Democratic principle upheld

#### 2️⃣ Government Stability 🏛️
**What it promotes:** Government remains stable as long as maintains majority.

- Clear majority = stable government
- Loss of majority = must resign
- No arbitrary changes

#### 3️⃣ Team Spirit 🤝
**What it creates:** Ministers work as team, not competing individuals.

- Collective decision-making
- Unified government
- Shared responsibility

#### 4️⃣ Accountability to People 👥
**Ultimate effect:** Government answerable to people through their representatives.

- Lok Sabha represents people
- Government must keep people's confidence
- Next election = people's verdict

---

### Conclusion (1 mark)

Collective responsibility is the cornerstone of Parliamentary democracy, distinguishing it from Presidential systems. It ensures the Executive remains accountable to the Legislature and ultimately to the people. While promoting government unity and stability, it also provides a clear mechanism for removing governments that lose people's confidence, making it a powerful tool for democratic accountability in India's Constitutional framework.
