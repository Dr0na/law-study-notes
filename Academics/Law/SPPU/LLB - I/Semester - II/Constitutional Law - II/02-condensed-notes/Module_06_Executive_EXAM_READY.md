## 📚 Topic 1: President of India

**What This Covers:** President is the Constitutional Head but real power lies with Prime Minister and Cabinet. Understanding this nominal vs real divide is crucial.

### ⚖️ 5 Key Aspects of President (COUNT = 5)

#### 1️⃣ Election (Articles 54-55) 🗳️
**How President is elected:** Not by direct vote of people but by an Electoral College consisting of elected MPs and MLAs, ensuring federal character.

**Electoral College consists of:**
- Elected MPs (Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha)
- Elected MLAs (all State Assemblies)

**Method:**
- Proportional representation
- Single transferable vote
- Secret ballot
- Must get 50% + 1 votes

**Vote Value Formula:**
- **MLA vote** = (State population / Total MLAs) ÷ 1000
- **MP vote** = (Total MLA votes / Total MPs)

#### 2️⃣ Qualifications (Article 58) 📋
**Who can become President:** Must meet these 4 basic requirements.

**4 Qualifications:**
1. Citizen of India
2. At least **35 years** old
3. Qualified to be Lok Sabha member
4. Not holding office of profit

#### 3️⃣ Term & Removal ⏰
**How long they serve:** Normal term is 5 years but can be removed earlier through impeachment (which is very difficult).

**Term:**
- **5 years** from assuming office
- Can be re-elected (no limit)
- Can resign

**Removal (Article 61):**
- Only by **impeachment**
- Ground: Violation of Constitution
- **Procedure**: 1/4 members initiate → 14 days notice → 2/3 majority in first House → Investigated by second House → 2/3 majority → Removed
- **Never done** in India

#### 4️⃣ Powers 💪
**What President can do:** President has wide powers on paper but exercises them only on Cabinet's advice (Article 74).

**5 Categories of Powers:**

**A. Executive:**
- Appoints PM, Ministers, Governors, Judges
- Supreme Commander of Armed Forces

**B. Legislative:**
- Summons/prorogues Parliament
- Assent to bills (Article 111)
- Ordinance power (Article 123)

**C. Financial:**
- Budget in President's name
- Controls Contingency Fund

**D. Judicial:**
- Appoints judges
- Pardon power (Article 72)

**E. Emergency:**
- Proclaims emergencies (Articles 352, 356, 360)

#### 5️⃣ Position - Nominal vs Real 👤
**The key distinction:** President is Constitutional head (nominal) while PM and Cabinet are real executives who actually govern.

**Article 74:**
- President **shall act** on aid and advice of Council of Ministers
- Advice is **binding**
- Can ask to reconsider once (44th Amendment)
- If same advice sent again, must accept

**When President Acts Independently:**
⚠️ Very Limited:
- Appointing PM (when no clear majority)
- Dissolving Lok Sabha (in exceptional cases)
- Seeking information from PM

### 📌 Samsher Singh v. State of Punjab (1974)
**What this established:** President has no independent powers; must act on Cabinet's advice in all matters.

## 📚 Topic 2: Vice-President

**What This Covers:** Vice-President has a dual role - Chairman of Rajya Sabha normally, and acts as President when President is unavailable.

### ⚖️ 4 Key Points (COUNT = 4)

#### 1️⃣ Election 🗳️
**How elected:** Only MPs (not MLAs) elect Vice-President, unlike President's election.

- Electoral College: MPs only (Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha)
- Elected + nominated both vote
- Proportional representation, STV

#### 2️⃣ Qualifications 📋
**Requirements:** Must be citizen, at least 35 years, qualified for Rajya Sabha membership, no office of profit.

#### 3️⃣ Dual Role 🎭
**What makes it unique:** Vice-President acts as Rajya Sabha Chairman (primary role) and also steps in as President when needed.

**Role 1:** Ex-officio Chairman of Rajya Sabha
- Conducts Rajya Sabha proceedings
- Gets salary as Chairman

**Role 2:** Acts as President
- When President unable to function
- When President's office vacant
- Temporary discharge of functions

#### 4️⃣ Term ⏰
**Duration:** 5 years, can be re-elected, simpler removal than President (resolution, not impeachment).

## 📚 Topic 3: Prime Minister & Council of Ministers

**What This Covers:** PM and Cabinet are the REAL executive - they actually run the government while President is nominal head.

### ⚖️ Prime Minister - 6 Key Powers (COUNT = 6)

**Context:** PM is most powerful person in Indian government - leads Cabinet, controls Parliament, and runs administration.

#### 1️⃣ Formation of Government 🏛️
**What it means:** PM selects all Ministers and recommends to President for appointment - PM decides who gets which ministry.

- Selects Ministers
- Recommends to President
- Allocates portfolios
- Can reshuffle

#### 2️⃣ Head of Council of Ministers 👔
**What it means:** PM presides over Cabinet meetings, coordinates work of all ministries, and makes final decisions.

- Chairs Cabinet meetings
- Coordinates ministries
- Final decision-maker

#### 3️⃣ Link Between President & Cabinet 🔗
**What it means:** PM communicates all Cabinet decisions to President and conveys President's messages to Cabinet - principal channel.

- Conveys decisions to President
- Gets President's messages
- Main communication channel

#### 4️⃣ Chief Advisor to President 💡
**What it means:** President acts on PM's advice on almost all matters - PM's recommendations carry weight.

- President depends on PM's advice
- Article 74: Aid and advice binding
- PM most powerful position

#### 5️⃣ Leader of Lok Sabha 🏛️
**What it means:** PM is leader of majority party in Lok Sabha, controlling House proceedings and legislative agenda.

- Leader of majority party
- Controls parliamentary business
- Party discipline

#### 6️⃣ Controls Key Appointments 📝
**What it means:** All major appointments (Governors, CJI, CAG, etc.) made on PM's recommendation to President.

- Governors
- Judges
- CAG, CEC
- Key officials

### ⚖️ Council of Ministers - 5 Essentials (COUNT = 5)

#### 1️⃣ Three Categories 📊
**How Ministers are ranked:** Cabinet has hierarchical structure based on importance and seniority.

**A. Cabinet Ministers** (Top tier):
- Head important ministries
- Attend Cabinet meetings
- Major policy decisions
- Usually 15-20

**B. Ministers of State** (Middle tier):
- Independent charge OR assist Cabinet Minister
- Don't attend Cabinet unless invited

**C. Deputy Ministers** (Junior tier):
- Assist other Ministers
- No Cabinet attendance

#### 2️⃣ Collective Responsibility (Article 75(3)) 🤝
**What it means:** All Ministers swim or sink together - if Lok Sabha passes no-confidence, entire Council must resign, not just one Minister.

- Collectively responsible to Lok Sabha
- No-confidence removes all
- Must support all Cabinet decisions publicly
- Cannot have individual dissent

#### 3️⃣ Individual Responsibility 👤
**What it means:** Each Minister also individually answerable for their department's work to Parliament.

- Answers questions on their ministry
- Responsible for department
- Can be removed by PM

#### 4️⃣ Size Limit (91st Amendment) 📏
**What it prevents:** To prevent jumbo Cabinets (too many Ministers), Constitution now limits Council size to 15% of Assembly strength.

- Maximum: **15% of Assembly**
- Minimum: 12 Ministers
- Prevents bloated Councils

#### 5️⃣ Must be MP/MLA 📋
**What it requires:** Ministers must be members of Parliament; if not, must get elected within 6 months.

- Must be MP
- If not, become MP within 6 months
- Ensures parliamentary accountability

## 📚 Topic 4: Governor of State

**What This Covers:** Governor is State's Constitutional head but with differences from President - appointed (not elected) and serves "during pleasure" (less secure).

### ⚖️ 5 Key Differences from President (COUNT = 5)

#### 1️⃣ Appointment 📌
**How selected:** Governor is APPOINTED by President (i.e., Central Government), not elected, raising questions about independence.

- Appointed by President
- Central government's choice
- Not elected
- Usually from outside State

#### 2️⃣ Term ⏰
**How secure:** While term is 5 years, Governor holds office "during President's pleasure" - can be removed anytime without reasons.

- 5 years nominal
- **During President's pleasure** (insecure)
- Can be removed anytime
- No impeachment needed

#### 3️⃣ Discretionary Powers ⚖️
**More independence:** Governor has MORE discretionary powers than President in certain matters.

**Can Act Independently:**
- Appointing CM (no clear majority)
- Dismissing CM (lost majority)
- Recommending President's Rule (Article 356)
- Reserving bills for President
- Special State provisions (Nagaland, Sikkim)

#### 4️⃣ Pardon Power (Article 161) 🕊️
**Limited compared to President:** Governor can pardon State law offenses but NOT death sentences or court-martial cases.

- Only State law offenses
- **Cannot pardon death** sentences
- Cannot pardon court-martial
- Narrower than President (Article 72)

#### 5️⃣ Ordinance Power (Article 213) 📜
**Similar to President:** Can promulgate ordinances when State Legislature not in session.

- When Legislature not in session
- Same force as law
- Must be approved within 6 weeks
- Can be withdrawn anytime

### 📌 Nabam Rebia v. Deputy Speaker (2016)
**What this established:** Governor cannot act arbitrarily or partisan - must follow constitutional norms, cannot aid horse-trading.

## 📚 Topic 5: Chief Minister

**What This Covers:** Chief Minister is the Real Executive of State (like PM at Centre) - actually runs the State government.

### ⚖️ CM's 5 Key Powers (COUNT = 5)

**Context:** Just as PM is most powerful at Centre, CM is most powerful in State - leads government, controls administration.

#### 1️⃣ Formation of State Government 🏛️
**What it means:** CM selects Ministers and recommends to Governor for appointment.

- Selects Ministers
- Recommends to Governor
- Allocates portfolios

#### 2️⃣ Head of State Council 👔
**What it means:** CM chairs Council meetings, coordinates departments, makes key decisions.

- Presides over meetings
- Coordinates ministries
- Final decision-maker

#### 3️⃣ Link with Governor 🔗
**What it means:** CM is the channel between Governor and Council, communicating decisions.

- Advises Governor
- Communicates decisions
- Main advisor

#### 4️⃣ Leader of Assembly 🏛️
**What it means:** CM leads majority party in Assembly, must maintain majority to survive.

- Leader of majority party
- Must have Assembly support
- Can be removed by no-confidence

#### 5️⃣ State Administration 📋
**What it means:** CM controls entire State administration, implements policies, runs day-to-day governance.

- Controls bureaucracy
- Implements policies
- Runs administration

**President:**
✅ Elected by MPs + MLAs, Electoral College, STV  
✅ 35 years age, 5 years term  
✅ Nominal executive, acts on Article 74 (aid & advice)

✅ Pardon: Article 72 (all offenses, death sentences)  

**Governor:**
✅ Appointed by President, 35 years, 5 years (President's pleasure)  
✅ More discretionary powers than President  
✅ Pardon: Article 161 (State offenses only, not death)  
✅ Can be removed anytime (no impeachment)  

**PM/CM:**
✅ Real executive, appointed by President/Governor  
✅ Leader of majority party  
✅ Collective responsibility: Article 75(3)  
✅ Council size: Maximum 15% (91st Amendment)  
✅ Must be MP/MLA within 6 months  

**Key Distinction:**
✅ President/Governor = Nominal (Constitutional head)  
✅ PM/CM = Real (Actually governs)  
✅ Article 74: Aid and advice binding
