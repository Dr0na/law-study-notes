## 📚 PART A: LEGISLATIVE POWERS

**What This Covers:** Legislative powers are distributed through the Seventh Schedule which contains three Lists. Each List assigns specific subjects to Union, States, or both. This prevents overlap and confusion.

### ⚖️ 3 Lists - Distribution Scheme (COUNT = 3)

#### 1️⃣ Union List (List I) - 97 Subjects 🏛️
**What it means:** These are subjects of national importance where only Parliament can make laws. States cannot legislate on these subjects at all.

**Article 246(1)**: Parliament exclusive power

**Top 10 Subjects to Remember:**
1. Defense
2. Foreign Affairs
3. Atomic Energy
4. Railways
5. Banking
6. Currency
7. Inter-state Trade
8. Citizenship
9. Elections
10. Audit

🧠 **Mnemonic**: **"DFAR-BCCEIA"**  
**D**efense, **F**oreign, **A**tomic, **R**ailways, **B**anking, **C**urrency, **C**itizenship, **E**lections, **I**nter-state, **A**udit

#### 2️⃣ State List (List II) - 66 Subjects 🏢
**What it means:** These are subjects of local/regional importance where only State Legislatures can make laws. Parliament cannot normally legislate on these subjects.

**Article 246(3)**: State Legislature exclusive power

**Top 10 Subjects to Remember:**
1. Police & Public Order
2. Public Health
3. Agriculture
4. Forests
5. Fisheries
6. Local Government
7. Land & Buildings
8. State Taxes
9. Betting & Gambling
10. State Public Services

🧠 **Mnemonic**: **"P-PAFF-LLBBS"**  
**P**olice, **P**ublic Health, **A**griculture, **F**orests, **F**isheries, **L**ocal Govt, **L**and, **B**etting, **B**uildings, **S**ervices

#### 3️⃣ Concurrent List (List III) - 47 Subjects ⚖️
**What it means:** These are subjects of common interest where BOTH Parliament and State Legislatures can make laws. However, if there's conflict, Union law prevails (Article 254).

**Article 246(2)**: Both can legislate

**Top 10 Subjects to Remember:**
1. Criminal Law & Procedure
2. Marriage & Divorce
3. Contracts
4. Civil Procedure
5. Education
6. Forests
7. Electricity
8. Newspapers & Books
9. Economic Planning
10. Population Control

🧠 **Mnemonic**: **"CC-MCCE-FNEP"**  
**C**riminal, **C**ivil, **M**arriage, **C**ontracts, **C**ontracts, **E**ducation, **F**orests, **N**ewspapers, **E**conomic, **P**opulation

## 🔹 Conflict Resolution Rule (Article 254)

### 3-Step Hierarchy (COUNT = 3)

#### 1️⃣ General Rule 📌
- On **Concurrent subjects**, if conflict between Union and State law
- **Union law prevails**
- State law void to extent of inconsistency

#### 2️⃣ Exception - Presidential Assent ⚠️
- If President has given assent to State law
- **State law prevails in that State**
- Union law prevails in other States

#### 3️⃣ Practical Effect 🔹
- Union law generally supreme
- States can seek Presidential assent for different provisions
- Allows State-specific needs

### 📌 **Key Case**: Deep Chand v. State of UP (1959)
**Principle**: Union law prevails on Concurrent subjects in case of repugnancy

## 📚 PART B: TERRITORIAL JURISDICTION

### ⚖️ 2 Territorial Rules (COUNT = 2)

#### 1️⃣ Parliament (Article 245) 🌍
**Scope:**
- Can legislate for **whole of India** or any part
- Can make **extra-territorial** laws (for Indians abroad)
- **Example**: IPC applies to Indians outside India

**Power:**
✅ Whole India  
✅ Part of India  
✅ Indians anywhere in world  

#### 2️⃣ State Legislature (Article 245) 🗺️
**Scope:**
- Can legislate only for **that State**
- Generally **no extra-territorial** operation
- **Exception**: If sufficient territorial nexus

**Limitation:**
❌ Cannot legislate outside State  
❌ Cannot bind other States  
⚠️ Exception: Territorial connection exists

## 📚 PART C: EXPANSION OF UNION POWERS

### 🔥 5 Ways Union Power Expands (COUNT = 5)

#### 1️⃣ National Emergency (Article 250) 🚨
- Parliament can legislate on **State List**
- During emergency period
- Laws valid for **6 months** after emergency ends

#### 2️⃣ Rajya Sabha Resolution (Article 249) 🏛️
- If **2/3rd majority** in Rajya Sabha declares subject "national importance"
- Parliament can legislate on State subject
- Valid for **1 year** (renewable)

#### 3️⃣ International Treaties (Article 253) 🌐
- To implement treaties/international agreements
- Parliament can legislate on **any subject** (even State List)
- Overrides normal distribution

#### 4️⃣ President's Rule (Article 356) 🏴
- When imposed in a State
- Parliament legislates for that State
- State Legislature suspended/dissolved

#### 5️⃣ State's Consent (Article 252) 🤝
- If **2 or more States** request by resolution
- Parliament can legislate on State subjects for them
- Other States can adopt later

### 🧠 Mnemonic: **"NRIPC"**
**N**ational Emergency, **R**ajya Sabha, **I**nternational, **P**resident's Rule, **C**onsent

### 📝 Exam Tip:
Always mention Article numbers with each expansion method!

## 📚 PART D: RESIDUARY POWERS

### ⚖️ Article 248 - 3 Key Points (COUNT = 3)

#### 1️⃣ Definition 📖
- Power to legislate on subjects **not in any List**
- Subjects not enumerated in three Lists

#### 2️⃣ Vested in Parliament 🏛️
- **Exclusive power** with Parliament
- Unlike USA (where States have residuary)
- Shows Union's dominance

#### 3️⃣ Examples 💡
- **Computers & Internet** (when first emerged)
- **Cyber crimes** (before specific laws)
- **New technologies** not contemplated earlier

## 📚 PART E: EXECUTIVE POWERS

### ⚖️ Basic Rule - Executive Follows Legislative (COUNT = 2)

#### 1️⃣ Union Executive Power (Article 73) 🏛️
**Extends to:**
- Union List subjects
- Concurrent List subjects
- Residuary subjects
- **Coextensive** with legislative power

#### 2️⃣ State Executive Power (Article 162) 🏢
**Extends to:**
- State List subjects
- Concurrent List subjects (within State)
- **Coextensive** with legislative power

### 📝 Golden Rule:
**"Whoever can MAKE law on a subject, can EXECUTE that law"**

## 📚 PART F: ALL INDIA SERVICES

### ⚖️ Article 312 - 3 Services (COUNT = 3)

#### 1️⃣ Indian Administrative Service (IAS) 🎖️
- Created by Constitution
- UPSC recruitment
- Serve in States but controlled by Centre

#### 2️⃣ Indian Police Service (IPS) 👮
- Created by Constitution
- UPSC recruitment
- State posting, Central control

#### 3️⃣ Indian Forest Service (IFS) 🌲
- Created by Parliament law
- UPSC recruitment
- Both Union and State cadres

### 🔹 4 Key Features (COUNT = 4)
1. **Central recruitment** (UPSC)
2. **State posting** (serve in States)
3. **Dual control** (day-to-day by State, overall by Centre)
4. **Cannot be dismissed by State** (protects from political pressure)

### 📝 Why Important?
✅ Ensures efficient administration  
✅ Uniform standards across India  
✅ Officers can serve anywhere  
✅ Acts as unifying force

## 📚 PART G: INTER-STATE COUNCIL

### ⚖️ Article 263 - 5 Key Points (COUNT = 5)

#### 1️⃣ Nature 🔹
- Constitutional provision (may be established)
- President's discretion
- **Established in 1990** (after Sarkaria Commission)

#### 2️⃣ Composition 👥
- **Chairman**: Prime Minister
- **Members**: All Chief Ministers, 6 Union Ministers, Lt. Governors

#### 3️⃣ Functions 📋
- Investigate subjects of common interest
- Discuss Centre-State issues
- Make recommendations
- Advise on disputes

#### 4️⃣ Nature of Decisions ⚠️
- **Advisory only** (not binding)
- Recommendations need implementation
- Depends on political will

#### 5️⃣ Importance 💡
- Forum for federal cooperation
- Reduces confrontation
- Promotes dialogue
- Strengthens federalism
