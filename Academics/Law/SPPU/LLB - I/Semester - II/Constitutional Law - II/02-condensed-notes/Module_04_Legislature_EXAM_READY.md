## 📚 PART A: PARLIAMENT - THE TWO HOUSES

**What This Covers:** India's Parliament consists of two Houses with different compositions, powers, and roles. Understanding their differences is crucial for exams.

### ⚖️ Lok Sabha vs Rajya Sabha - 10 Differences (COUNT = 10)

**Context:** Lok Sabha directly represents people while Rajya Sabha represents States. This creates a balance between popular will and federal interests.

| # | Feature | Lok Sabha 🏛️ | Rajya Sabha 🏢 |
|---|---------|--------------|---------------|
| 1️⃣ | **Name** | House of the People | Council of States |
| 2️⃣ | **Strength** | Maximum 552 (currently 543) | Maximum 250 (currently 245) |
| 3️⃣ | **Election** | Direct (by people) | Indirect (by State MLAs) |
| 4️⃣ | **Term** | 5 years (can dissolve) | Permanent (1/3 retire every 2 years) |
| 5️⃣ | **Age** | 25 years minimum | 30 years minimum |
| 6️⃣ | **Represents** | People | States |
| 7️⃣ | **Presiding Officer** | Speaker | Chairman (VP of India) |
| 8️⃣ | **Money Bills** | Can originate & pass | Can only recommend (14 days) |
| 9️⃣ | **No-Confidence** | Can be moved | Cannot be moved |
| 🔟 | **Power** | More powerful | Less powerful |

🧠 **Mnemonic for Lok Sabha**: **"543-5-25-SP-MP"**  
543 members, 5 years, 25 age, Speaker, More Powerful

🧠 **Mnemonic for Rajya Sabha**: **"245-P-30-CH-LP"**  
245 members, Permanent, 30 age, Chairman, Less Powerful

## 📚 PART B: QUALIFICATIONS & DISQUALIFICATIONS

**What This Covers:** To become an MP, certain qualifications must be met and certain disqualifying factors must be absent.

### ⚖️ Article 84 - 4 Qualifications (COUNT = 4)

**Context:** These are the basic eligibility criteria - what you MUST have to become an MP.

#### 1️⃣ Citizenship 🆔
**Mandatory requirement:** Must be Indian citizen - foreign citizens cannot become MPs even if living in India.

#### 2️⃣ Age 🎂
**Age requirement:** Must be at least 25 years (Lok Sabha) or 30 years (Rajya Sabha) - ensures maturity.

#### 3️⃣ Voter Registration 🗳️
**Must be registered:** Must be registered as voter in any parliamentary constituency in India.

#### 4️⃣ Oath 📜
**Constitutional requirement:** Must take oath as per Third Schedule, pledging to uphold Constitution.

### ⚠️ Article 102 - 5 Disqualifications (COUNT = 5)

**Context:** These are disqualifying factors - if any of these apply, the person CANNOT be an MP.

#### 1️⃣ Office of Profit 💼
**What it means:** Cannot hold any government job or position that gives profit/salary (except being a Minister).

- Government employees disqualified
- Ministers are exception

#### 2️⃣ Unsound Mind 🧠
**What it means:** If a court has declared the person of unsound mind, they cannot be an MP.

- Court declaration required
- Mental incapacity

#### 3️⃣ Insolvent 💸
**What it means:** If person has been declared bankrupt (insolvent) and not yet discharged, cannot be MP.

- Undischarged insolvent only
- After discharge, can contest

#### 4️⃣ Not a Citizen 🚫
**What it means:** If person stops being Indian citizen or voluntarily acquires foreign citizenship, disqualified.

- Loss of citizenship
- Acquiring foreign citizenship

#### 5️⃣ Disqualified by Law 📋
**What it means:** Parliament can make laws prescribing additional disqualifications (like Representation of People Act, 1951).

- Criminal convictions (6 years)
- Corrupt election practices
- Election expense violations

### 📌 Lily Thomas v. Union of India (2013)
**What this changed:** Earlier, convicted MPs remained in office till appeal. This case held they're disqualified IMMEDIATELY upon conviction (not after appeal).

## 📚 PART C: ANTI-DEFECTION LAW

**What This Covers:** The Tenth Schedule prevents MPs/MLAs from switching parties for personal gain, ensuring political stability.

### ⚖️ Tenth Schedule - 4 Grounds for Disqualification (COUNT = 4)

**Context (Why this law exists):** To stop "Aaya Ram, Gaya Ram" (party-hopping), the 52nd Amendment (1985) added the Tenth Schedule punishing defection with disqualification.

#### 1️⃣ Voluntary Resignation from Party 🚪
**What triggers disqualification:** If an elected member voluntarily gives up membership of the political party on whose ticket they were elected.

- Leaves party voluntarily
- Disqualified immediately
- Speaker/Chairman decides

#### 2️⃣ Voting Against Party Whip ❌
**What triggers disqualification:** If member votes in the House contrary to party's direction (whip) without permission, or abstains when party directed to vote.

- Votes against party direction
- Abstains against whip
- Must be condon within 15 days

#### 3️⃣ Nominated Member Joins Party 🎫
**What triggers disqualification:** If a nominated member joins a political party within 6 months of taking seat, disqualified.

- Only for nominated members
- Within 6 months of nomination
- After 6 months, can join

#### 4️⃣ Independent Member Joins Party 🆓
**What triggers disqualification:** If an independent candidate (who contested without party symbol) joins any political party after election.

- Independent candidate
- Joins any party after election
- Disqualified

### ⚠️ 2 Exceptions (COUNT = 2)

#### Exception 1: Merger 🔀
**When safe from disqualification:** If 2/3rds of party's legislature party agrees to merge with another party, no disqualification. This is considered legitimate merger, not defection.

- **2/3 members** must agree
- Legitimate party merger
- Not considered defection

#### Exception 2: Presiding Officer 🪑
**When safe from disqualification:** If member elected as Speaker/Chairman, can give up party membership temporarily. On ceasing to be Speaker, can rejoin original party without disqualification.

- For Speaker/Chairman only
- Temporary party resignation
- Can rejoin after office

### 📌 Kihoto Hollohan v. Zachillhu (1992)
**What this case held:** Supreme Court upheld the Tenth Schedule's validity, saying it doesn't violate basic structure. However, Speaker's decision is subject to judicial review (not completely final).

## 📚 PART D: PARLIAMENTARY PRIVILEGES

**What This Covers:** MPs have special rights and immunities (privileges) to function independently without fear of legal action for their parliamentary work.

### ⚖️ Article 105 - 3 Main Privileges (COUNT = 3)

**Context:** These privileges ensure MPs can debate freely, vote according to conscience, and perform duties without outside interference.

#### 1️⃣ Freedom of Speech in Parliament 🗣️
**What it protects:** MPs can say anything in Parliament without fear of prosecution or court case. This allows fearless debate on important issues.

- Absolute freedom during proceedings
- Cannot be prosecuted for speeches
- Courts cannot question
- Speaker can expunge remarks

#### 2️⃣ Immunity from Arrest 🛡️
**What it protects:** MPs cannot be arrested in civil cases during session and 40 days before/after session. This prevents misuse of arrest to disrupt Parliament.

- **Civil cases**: Cannot arrest
- **Criminal cases**: Can arrest (exception)
- 40 days before + during session + 40 days after

#### 3️⃣ Right to Regulate Internal Proceedings ⚙️
**What it protects:** Each House makes its own rules of procedure. Courts cannot question proceedings on grounds of procedural irregularity.

- Each House frames rules
- Courts cannot interfere
- Internal autonomy
- Self-regulation

## 📚 PART E: LAW-MAKING PROCEDURE

**What This Covers:** How a bill becomes a law in India - the journey from idea to Act through various stages in both Houses and Presidential assent.

### ⚖️ 6 Stages of Ordinary Bill (COUNT = 6)

**Context:** An ordinary bill (non-money bill) must pass through both Houses and get President's assent. Understanding this procedure is essential.

#### 1️⃣ First Reading - Introduction 📝
**What happens:** Member introduces the bill, explaining its purpose. House decides whether to consider it.

- Leave to introduce sought
- Bill introduced and published
- General discussion on objectives

#### 2️⃣ Second Reading - General Discussion 💬
**What happens:** Bill's general principles discussed. House may refer to Committee for detailed examination or take up immediately.

- **Stage A**: General principles debated
- Can refer to Committee or circulate for opinion
- **Stage B**: Clause-by-clause consideration
- Amendments moved and voted

#### 3️⃣ Third Reading - Final Debate 🎤
**What happens:** Final debate on the entire bill. Only minor verbal or consequential amendments allowed. Then voted upon.

- Final debate
- Limited amendments
- Vote taken
- If passed, goes to other House

#### 4️⃣ Other House Considers 🔄
**What happens:** Bill goes to second House which repeats all three stages. Can pass, reject, or amend the bill.

- Same 3 stages repeated
- Can pass as is
- Can amend
- Can reject

#### 5️⃣ Joint Sitting (if needed) (Article 108) 🤝
**What happens:** If both Houses disagree and deadlock continues, President can call a Joint Sitting where both Houses sit together and vote.

- Called by President
- Speaker presides
- Simple majority decides
- Lok Sabha's view usually prevails (more members)
- **Not for** Money Bills or Constitution amendments

#### 6️⃣ President's Assent (Article 111) ✅
**What happens:** After both Houses pass, bill goes to President who can give assent (becomes Act), withhold assent (doesn't become law), or return for reconsideration once.

- **Give assent**: Bill becomes Act
- **Withhold assent**: Bill dies
- **Return once**: Parliament reconsiders
- If passed again, President must give assent (convention)

## 📚 PART F: TYPES OF BILLS

**What This Covers:** Not all bills are equal - different types have different procedures and powers of Houses.

### ⚖️ 4 Types of Bills (COUNT = 4)

#### 1️⃣ Ordinary Bills 📄
**What they are:** Bills on subjects other than money matters. Both Houses have equal powers - both must agree for bill to pass.

- Can introduce in either House
- Both Houses equal powers
- Both must pass
- Needs President's assent

#### 2️⃣ Money Bills (Article 110) 💰
**What they are:** Bills dealing ONLY with taxation, borrowing, or government funds. Lok Sabha has supremacy because people pay taxes, so people's House decides.

**What qualifies as Money Bill (Article 110):**
- Taxation (impose, abolish, alter)
- Borrowing by Government
- Consolidated Fund matters
- Appropriation of money
- Government accounts audit

**Special Procedure:**
- **Only in Lok Sabha** (cannot originate in Rajya Sabha)
- Rajya Sabha can keep for **14 days only**
- Can only **recommend changes** (cannot reject)
- **Speaker certifies** if bill is Money Bill (final decision)
- If Rajya Sabha doesn't return in 14 days, deemed passed

**Why Lok Sabha Supreme on Money Bills:**
- Represents people directly
- People pay taxes
- Democratic control over finances

#### 3️⃣ Financial Bills (Article 117) 💵
**What they are:** Bills involving government expenditure but not qualifying as Money Bills. Both Houses have equal powers here.

**Two Sub-Types:**
- **Type I**: Contains Article 110 matters + other matters
- **Type II**: Involves Consolidated Fund expenditure

**Procedure:**
- Can introduce in either House
- Both Houses equal powers
- President's recommendation needed

#### 4️⃣ Constitutional Amendment Bills (Article 368) 📜
**What they are:** Bills to amend the Constitution. Need special majority and some need State ratification too.

**Procedure:**
- Can introduce in either House
- **Special majority** needed: 2/3 present + majority of total
- Both Houses must pass
- **Some amendments also need**: 50% States' ratification
- President's assent

**Which Need State Ratification:**
- Election of President
- Union-State power distribution
- Representation of States in Parliament
- High Courts provisions
- Article 368 itself

## 📚 PART G: STATE LEGISLATURE

**What This Covers:** Most States have unicameral legislature (only Assembly), but 6 States have bicameral (Assembly + Council).

### ⚖️ Bicameral States - 6 States (COUNT = 6)

**Context:** Only these States have Legislative Council (Upper House) along with Legislative Assembly (Lower House).

#### The 6 States with Vidhan Parishad:
1️⃣ **Andhra Pradesh**  
2️⃣ **Bihar**  
3️⃣ **Karnataka**  
4️⃣ **Maharashtra**  
5️⃣ **Telangana**  
6️⃣ **Uttar Pradesh**  

🧠 **Mnemonic**: **"AB-KMT-U"**  
**A**ndhra, **B**ihar, **K**arnataka, **M**aharashtra, **T**elangana, **U**ttar Pradesh

### ⚖️ Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha) - 5 Key Points (COUNT = 5)

#### 1️⃣ Composition 👥
**How members are chosen:** Directly elected by people of the State through universal adult franchise, just like Lok Sabha.

- Maximum: 500 members
- Minimum: 60 members
- Direct election
- Reservation for SC/ST

#### 2️⃣ Term ⏰
**How long it lasts:** Assembly continues for 5 years unless dissolved earlier by Governor (on CM's advice or during President's Rule).

- **5 years** from first sitting
- Can dissolve earlier
- Can extend during emergency

#### 3️⃣ Qualifications 📋
**Who can become MLA:** Must be citizen, at least 25 years old, registered voter in State, not disqualified.

- Article 173 specifies
- Similar to Lok Sabha (Article 84)
- Age: 25 years minimum

#### 4️⃣ Powers 💪
**What Assembly can do:** Makes laws on State and Concurrent subjects, controls State finances, can remove State Government through no-confidence.

- Legislates on State + Concurrent Lists
- Controls State budget
- Supreme on money matters
- Can pass no-confidence

#### 5️⃣ Presiding Officer 🪑
**Who leads:** Speaker elected by Assembly members, conducts proceedings, maintains order.

- **Speaker** elected by members
- **Deputy Speaker** also elected
- Similar powers to Lok Sabha Speaker

### ⚖️ Legislative Council (Vidhan Parishad) - 5 Key Points (COUNT = 5)

**Context:** Present only in 6 States, Council is like Rajya Sabha at State level - permanent body, indirectly elected, represents different segments.

#### 1️⃣ Composition (Article 171) 👥
**How members are chosen:** Unlike Assembly (directly elected), Council members come through 5 different methods representing various groups.

**Maximum**: 1/3 of Assembly strength  
**Minimum**: 40 members

**5 Categories of Members:**
1. **1/3rd**: Elected by local bodies (Municipalities, Panchayats)
2. **1/12th**: Elected by graduates (3 years standing)
3. **1/12th**: Elected by teachers (secondary & above, 3 years)
4. **1/3rd**: Elected by MLAs
5. **1/6th**: Nominated by Governor (eminent persons)

#### 2️⃣ Term ⏰
**How it works:** Like Rajya Sabha, Council is permanent - it never dissolves. 1/3 members retire every 2 years, each member serves 6 years.

- Permanent body
- 1/3 retire every 2 years
- Each member: 6-year term

#### 3️⃣ Age 📅
**Qualification:** Must be at least 30 years old (5 years more than Assembly requirement).

#### 4️⃣ Powers 💪
**Limited powers:** Council can only delay bills, not reject them. Assembly has final say on all matters, especially money matters.

- Can delay ordinary bills (4 months max)
- Cannot delay money bills (14 days)
- Cannot vote on confidence motion
- Can only recommend changes

#### 5️⃣ Creation/Abolition 🔧
**How Council is created/abolished:** Parliament can create or abolish Council for any State if that State's Assembly passes resolution by special majority.

- State Assembly requests by resolution
- **Special majority** in Assembly needed
- Parliament then passes law
- Can be created or abolished

## 📚 PART H: ANTI-DEFECTION - TENTH SCHEDULE

**What This Covers:** To stop MPs/MLAs from switching parties (defection) for personal gain, causing instability.

### ⚖️ Defection = 4 Situations (COUNT = 4)

**Context (Why anti-defection law was needed):** In the 1960s-80s, MPs/MLAs frequently switched parties causing government instability. This was called "Aaya Ram, Gaya Ram" politics. The 52nd Amendment (1985) added Tenth Schedule to curb this.

#### 1️⃣ Voluntarily Gives Up Party Membership 🚪
**What constitutes defection:** Member formally resigns from party or by conduct shows they've left the party.

- Written resignation from party
- OR by conduct (joining another party, acting against party)

#### 2️⃣ Votes/Abstains Against Whip ❌
**What constitutes defection:** Member votes contrary to party's direction or abstains when party directed to vote, without party's permission.

- Votes against party direction
- Abstains without permission
- Unless condoned within 15 days

#### 3️⃣ Nominated Member Joins Party (within 6 months) 🎫
**What constitutes defection:** Nominated members are independent experts. If they join a political party too soon (within 6 months), shows they weren't independent.

- Nominated member
- Joins party within 6 months
- After 6 months, can join freely

#### 4️⃣ Independent Joins Party 🆓
**What constitutes defection:** Independent candidates contest without party support. If they join a party after winning, they've betrayed voters' trust.

- Elected as independent
- Joins any party after election

### ⚠️ 2 Exceptions - When NOT Disqualified (COUNT = 2)

#### Exception 1: Party Merger 🔀
**Why this is allowed:** If 2/3rds of a party decides to merge with another party, it's a legitimate political realignment, not individual defection.

- **2/3 of legislature party** must agree
- Formal merger
- Not defection

#### Exception 2: Presiding Officer 🪑
**Why this is allowed:** Speaker/Chairman must be neutral, so they give up party temporarily. When they cease to be Speaker, they can rejoin without penalty.

- Speaker/Chairman can resign from party
- To maintain neutrality
- Can rejoin after ceasing office

### 📌 Kihoto Hollohan v. Zachillhu (1992)
**What this case held:** Supreme Court upheld Tenth Schedule's validity but said Speaker's decision on disqualification is subject to judicial review on grounds of malafide or perversity.

**Parliament = President + 2 Houses:**
- Lok Sabha: 543, 5 years, 25 age, Speaker, More powerful

- Only Lok Sabha (Article 110)
- Rajya Sabha: 14 days, recommend only
- Speaker certifies

- Citizen, Age (25/30), Voter, Oath

- Office of profit, Unsound mind, Insolvent, Non-citizen, By law

- 6 States bicameral: AB-KMT-U
- Assembly: Like Lok Sabha

- Article 108
