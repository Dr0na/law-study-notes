### 2. Raja Ram Pal v. Hon'ble Speaker, Lok Sabha (2007)

**Citation**: AIR 2007 SC 3184  
**Bench**: 5 judges  
**Topic**: Parliamentary privileges, Expulsion of MPs  

### Facts
- MPs caught on camera taking bribes ("cash-for-questions" scam)
- Sting operation by news channel showed MPs accepting money
- Lok Sabha expelled 11 MPs
- Expelled MPs challenged: Can Parliament expel members?

### Issue
- Does parliamentary privilege protect accepting bribes?
- Can courts review expulsion by Parliament?

### Held

**Main Rulings:**

#### Ruling 1: Privilege Not Absolute ⚖️
- Parliamentary privileges exist but have limits
- Don't protect criminal acts
- Privilege cannot be used to shield corruption

#### Ruling 2: Courts Can Review ✅
- Expulsion can be reviewed by courts
- If constitutional violation, courts can interfere
- Limited review - on procedural/jurisdictional grounds

#### Ruling 3: Expulsion Upheld 👍
- MPs taking bribes is misconduct
- Parliament justified in expelling them
- Protects parliamentary integrity

**Principle:** Parliamentary privilege cannot shield criminal conduct; subject to judicial review

### Significance
- Balanced parliamentary autonomy with accountability
- Privilege not a license for crime
- Courts can intervene in extreme cases

### Exam Usage
Cite for: Parliamentary privileges questions, Limits on privileges

### 3. Lily Thomas v. Union of India (2013)

**Citation**: (2013) 7 SCC 653  
**Topic**: Disqualification of convicted MPs/MLAs  

### Facts

**Earlier Position:**
- Section 8(4) of Representation of People Act, 1951
- Convicted MPs/MLAs could continue till appeal decided
- Could remain in office for years despite conviction

**Challenge:**
- Lily Thomas challenged this provision
- Argued it defeats purpose of disqualification

### Issue
Can convicted persons continue as MPs/MLAs till appeal decided?

### Held

**Decision:**
- Section 8(4) RPA **struck down**
- Convicted MPs/MLAs **disqualified immediately** upon conviction
- Cannot continue till appeal
- No protection period

**Effect:**
- Conviction = Immediate disqualification
- No waiting for appeal
- Several sitting MPs/MLAs disqualified
- Including some high-profile cases

**Significance:**
- Cleaned up Parliament/Legislatures
- Strengthened disqualification law
- People's representatives must be clean

### Exam Usage
Cite for: Disqualifications, Article 102, Electoral reforms

### 4. Keshav Singh v. Speaker, Lok Sabha (1965)

**Topic**: Freedom of speech in Parliament (Article 105)  

### Issue
Can courts question what is said in Parliament?

### Held
- Freedom of speech in Parliament **absolute** vis-à-vis courts
- Courts cannot question proceedings in Parliament
- Article 105(2) protects members
- No civil/criminal action for speeches in House

**Principle:** Parliamentary speech absolutely privileged

## MODULE 5: JUDICIARY CASES

### 5. First Judges Case - S.P. Gupta v. Union of India (1981)

**Citation**: AIR 1982 SC 149  
**Topic**: Judicial appointments  
**Also Known As**: Transfer of Judges Case  

### Issue
Meaning of "consultation" with CJI in judicial appointments?

### Held (7:2 majority)
- "Consultation" does **not** mean "concurrence"
- Government's view can prevail over CJI's
- CJI's opinion is advice, not binding
- President (i.e., government) has final say

**Effect:** Government dominated appointments

**Later Development:** Overruled by Second Judges Case (1993)

---

### 6. Second Judges Case - SC Advocates-on-Record Assn. v. UOI (1993)

**Citation**: AIR 1993 SC 2493  
**Bench**: 9 judges  
**Topic**: Birth of Collegium System  

### Issue
Reconsideration of First Judges Case interpretation

### Held - Revolutionary Change

#### Ruling 1: First Judges Case Overruled ✅
- First Judges Case wrongly decided
- Must be reconsidered

#### Ruling 2: "Consultation" Means "Concurrence" ⚖️
- Consultation with CJI means his opinion binding
- Not merely advice
- CJI's view has primacy

#### Ruling 3: Collegium System Born 👨‍⚖️
**New System:**
- **CJI + 2 senior-most judges** form collegium
- Plurality needed (not CJI alone)
- Collegium recommends
- President appoints

**Reasoning for Plurality:**
- Prevents individual bias
- Collective wisdom
- Checks within judiciary

**Effect:** Power shifted from Executive to Judiciary

---

### 7. Third Judges Case - Re: Special Reference No. 1 of 1998 (1998)

**Topic**: Clarifying collegium system  

### Issue
Questions on functioning of collegium system

### Held - Collegium Expanded

**Key Changes:**
- Collegium expanded to **5 judges** for Supreme Court
- **CJI + 4 senior-most judges**
- For High Courts: **CJI + 2 SC judges + HC Chief Justice**

**Consultation Process:**
- CJI must consult 4 judges
- Views must be seriously considered
- If 2 disagree, name generally not recommended
- Consensus preferred

**Current System:** Based on this case

### 8. Fourth Judges Case - NJAC Case (2015)

**Full Name**: Supreme Court Advocates-on-Record Association v. Union of India  
**Citation**: AIR 2015 SC 1571  
**Bench**: 5 judges  
**Decision**: 4:1 (Chelameswar J. dissented)  

### Facts
- 99th Amendment (2014) created NJAC
- Replaced collegium with NJAC
- **NJAC composition**: CJI + 2 judges + Law Minister + 2 eminent persons
- Challenged as violating independence

### Issue
Is NJAC constitutional? Does it violate independence of judiciary?

### Held - NJAC Struck Down

**Main Rulings:**

#### Ruling 1: NJAC Unconstitutional ❌
- 99th Amendment struck down
- NJAC cannot function
- Collegium system restored

#### Ruling 2: Independence is Basic Structure ⚖️
- Judicial independence part of basic structure
- Cannot be compromised by amendment
- Executive presence affects independence

#### Ruling 3: Law Minister's Presence Fatal 🚫
- Law Minister in NJAC gives Executive control
- Even minority role (1/6) problematic
- Potential for influence exists

#### Ruling 4: Veto Power Problematic ⚠️
- Any 2 members can veto
- Law Minister + 1 other can block
- Paralyzes system

**Dissent (Justice Chelameswar):**
- Favored NJAC
- Collegium has problems too (opacity, nepotism allegations)
- Some accountability better than none

**Effect:** Collegium continues despite criticism

### 9. L. Chandra Kumar v. Union of India (1997)

**Citation**: AIR 1997 SC 1125  
**Topic**: Tribunals, Article 226 jurisdiction  

### Facts
- Administrative Tribunals Act excluded High Court jurisdiction
- Question: Can tribunals completely bar Article 226?

### Issue
Can Tribunals provisions exclude High Courts' writ jurisdiction under Article 226?

### Held - Protection of Judicial Review

**Main Ruling:**
- Tribunals **cannot** completely exclude High Court jurisdiction
- **Article 226 is basic structure**
- Judicial review cannot be taken away
- All tribunal orders subject to HC review under Article 226

**Effect:**
- High Courts can review tribunal decisions
- Judicial review protected
- Tribunals cannot be final authority

**Principle:** Judicial review is inviolable - basic structure
