# 📊 Comparison Table: President vs Prime Minister

## 🎯 The Nominal vs Real Executive

**Context:** Understanding the difference between Constitutional Head (President) and Real Executive (PM) is crucial for grasping India's Parliamentary system.

---

## 📋 Complete Comparison (COUNT = 15 differences)

| # | Feature | President 🇮🇳 | Prime Minister 👔 |
|---|---------|--------------|------------------|
| 1️⃣ | **Position** | Constitutional/Nominal Head | Real Executive Head |
| 2️⃣ | **Nature** | De jure executive | De facto executive |
| 3️⃣ | **Selection** | Elected (indirect) by Electoral College | Appointed by President |
| 4️⃣ | **Electoral College** | MPs + MLAs of all States | Not applicable |
| 5️⃣ | **Term** | 5 years (fixed) | No fixed term (till majority) |
| 6️⃣ | **Removal** | Impeachment (very difficult) | No-confidence motion (easier) |
| 7️⃣ | **Powers** | Formal/nominal powers | Real/actual powers |
| 8️⃣ | **Decision Making** | Acts on Cabinet's advice | Makes decisions |
| 9️⃣ | **Article 74** | Bound by advice | Gives advice |
| 🔟 | **Accountability** | Not responsible to Parliament | Collectively responsible to Lok Sabha |
| 1️⃣1️⃣ | **Qualifications** | 35 years, citizen, not office of profit | Must be MP (or become within 6 months) |
| 1️⃣2️⃣ | **Cabinet Meetings** | Does not attend | Presides over Cabinet |
| 1️⃣3️⃣ | **Party Politics** | Above party politics | Leader of majority party |
| 1️⃣4️⃣ | **Discretion** | Very limited (exceptional cases) | Wide discretion |
| 1️⃣5️⃣ | **Real Power** | ❌ Minimal | ✅ Maximum |

---

## 🔹 Key Constitutional Provisions

### President:
- **Article 52**: There shall be President of India
- **Article 53**: Executive power vested in President
- **Article 74**: Shall act on aid and advice
- **Article 75**: PM appointed by President

### Prime Minister:
- **Article 74**: Heads Council of Ministers
- **Article 75**: Appointed by President, appoints other Ministers
- **Article 75(3)**: Collective responsibility to Lok Sabha

## 📝 When President Can Act Independently

**5 Rare Situations (COUNT = 5):**

1️⃣ **Appointing PM** when no clear majority  
2️⃣ **Dismissing PM** in exceptional circumstances  
3️⃣ **Dissolving Lok Sabha** when no alternative  
4️⃣ **Seeking information** from PM (Article 78)  
5️⃣ **Returning bill** for reconsideration (once)  

**All others:** Must follow Cabinet's advice

---

## 📌 Key Case Reference

**Samsher Singh v. State of Punjab (1974)**
- Held: President has no independent powers
- Must act on Cabinet's advice
- Real power with PM and Cabinet
