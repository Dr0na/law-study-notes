## 📚 Course Overview

Media is the 'fourth estate' of democracy. This course examines the legal framework governing media in India, including constitutional protections, regulatory mechanisms, and the intersection of media with various areas of law. It covers the historical evolution, present position, and future prospects of media freedom and regulation.

**Key Focus Areas:**
- Constitutional framework of media freedom (Article 19)
- Media laws and regulations
- Press theories and international perspectives
- Legal challenges facing media (defamation, contempt, sedition)
- Regulatory bodies and self-regulation

## 💡 Important Study Points

### **Key Concepts to Master:**

1. **Freedom of Press under Article 19(1)(a)**
   - Extent and scope
   - Includes freedom of circulation
   - Broadcasting and telecast rights
   - Not absolute - subject to Article 19(2)

2. **Press Theories:**
   | Theory | Key Concept | Example |
   |--------|-------------|---------|
   | Authoritarian | State control | Pre-independence India |
   | Libertarian | Free marketplace of ideas | USA model |
   | Social Responsibility | Freedom with responsibility | Modern democracies |
   | Communist | Party mouthpiece | China, USSR |
   | Development | Nation-building focus | Developing countries |
   | Democratic Participant | Community media | Local media |

3. **Reasonable Restrictions (Article 19(2)):**
   - Sovereignty and integrity
   - Security of State
   - Friendly relations with foreign States
   - Public order
   - Decency and morality
   - Contempt of court
   - Defamation
   - Incitement to offence

### **Essential Acts and Provisions:**

- **Article 19(1)(a)** - Freedom of speech and expression
- **Article 19(2)** - Reasonable restrictions
- **Contempt of Courts Act, 1971**
- **Defamation** - Sections 499-502 IPC / BNS
- **Sedition** - Section 124A IPC (under debate)
- **RTI Act, 2005** - Right to information
- **Press Council Act, 1978** - Press regulation
- **Cinematograph Act, 1952** - Film censorship
- **Cable TV Networks Act, 1995** - TV regulation

### **Landmark Cases:**

1. **Romesh Thappar v. State of Madras (1950)** - Freedom of press
2. **Brij Bhushan v. State of Delhi (1950)** - Pre-censorship
3. **Bennett Coleman v. Union of India (1972)** - Newsprint control
4. **Indian Express v. Union of India (1985)** - Import of newsprint
5. **Sahara India v. SEBI (2012)** - Media trial
6. **Common Cause v. Union of India** - Paid news
7. **Shreya Singhal v. Union of India (2015)** - Section 66A IT Act
8. **Anuradha Bhasin v. Union of India (2020)** - Internet shutdown

### **High-Weightage Topics:**

- Article 19 analysis (constitutional framework)
- Press theories (comparative study)
- Contempt of Court (media and judiciary)
- Defamation (criminal and civil)
- RTI Act and media
- Regulatory bodies (Press Council, TRAI, etc.)
- Contemporary issues (internet freedom, paid news)
