# ⚡ Doctrines and Maxims - Media Laws

## 📜 Key Legal Maxims

| Maxim | Meaning | Context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *Lex Iniusta Non Est Lex* | An unjust law is not a law. | Natural Law / Free Speech |
| *Sub Judice* | Under judicial consideration. | Contempt of Court / Media Trial |
| *Prima Facie* | On the face of it. | Defamation / Injunctions |
| *Ex Parte* | From one party. | Injunctions against media publication |
| *In Camera* | In private (judge's chambers). | Protecting privacy/victims during trials |

---

## 🏛️ Key Legal Doctrines & Tests

1.  **Doctrine of Prior Restraint (Pre-Censorship)**: The government stopping publication before it happens. Generally unconstitutional for print (*Brij Bhushan*), but valid for films (*K.A. Abbas*).
2.  **Hicklin Test**: An old test for obscenity—whether the material has a tendency to deprave and corrupt those whose minds are open to such immoral influences. (Replaced by the Community Standards Test).
3.  **Community Standards Test**: Modern test for obscenity—whether the material, taken as a whole, appeals to the prurient interest according to contemporary community standards.
4.  **Chilling Effect**: When a law or government action is so broad or severe that it discourages people from exercising their free speech rights (e.g., Section 66A of IT Act in *Shreya Singhal*).
5.  **Postponement Order (Gag Order)**: A temporary court order restraining the media from reporting on a trial to prevent prejudice (*Sahara Case*).
6.  **Public Figure Doctrine**: Public officials have a lower threshold of privacy. They cannot sue for defamation for acts related to their official duties unless they prove "reckless disregard for truth" (*Auto Shankar Case*).
