# 📊 Comparison Tables - Media Laws

## 1. Statutory Regulation vs Self-Regulation

| Basis | 🏛️ Statutory Regulation | 🤝 Self-Regulation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Origin** | Created by the Legislature (Acts of Parliament). | Created by the industry or media professionals themselves. |
| **Authority** | Backed by the coercive power of the State. | Backed by voluntary compliance and peer pressure. |
| **Examples** | CBFC (Films), TRAI (Telecom). | ASCI (Advertisements), NBSA (News Broadcasters). |
| **Punitive Power** | Can impose fines, revoke licenses, or imprison. | Usually limited to warnings, censures, or asking for apologies. |
| **Flexibility** | Rigid, requires legislative amendment to change. | Highly flexible, can adapt quickly to new trends. |

💡 **Exam Tip**: Use this table for questions on "Methods of Media Regulation."

## 2. Authoritarian vs Libertarian Theory of Press

| Basis | 👑 Authoritarian Theory | 🕊️ Libertarian Theory |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Core Belief** | Truth is the monopoly of the State/Ruler. | Truth emerges from a free marketplace of ideas. |
| **Role of Press** | To support and advance the policies of the government. | To act as a watchdog and inform the public. |
| **Control Mechanism** | Censorship, licensing, severe punishments. | Minimal state intervention; defamation/obscenity laws apply post-publication. |
| **Ownership** | Often private, but strictly controlled by the State. | Private ownership in a free market. |
| **Historical Era** | 16th-17th Century (Tudor England). | 18th-19th Century (Enlightenment). |

💡 **Exam Tip**: This is a classic 10-mark question. Contrast the "servant" role vs the "watchdog" role.

## 3. Civil Defamation vs Criminal Defamation

| Basis | 📜 Civil Defamation (Torts) | ⚖️ Criminal Defamation (IPC Sec 499) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nature of Wrong** | Private wrong against an individual. | Public wrong against society. |
| **Remedy/Punishment** | Unliquidated damages (monetary compensation). | Imprisonment up to 2 years, or fine, or both. |
| **Burden of Proof** | Balance of probabilities. | Beyond reasonable doubt. |
| **Intention** | Intention is not strictly necessary; negligence is enough. | Requires intention to harm, or knowledge that it will harm reputation. |
| **Action taken by** | The defamed individual files a civil suit. | The State prosecutes (usually on a complaint by the victim). |

💡 **Exam Tip**: Essential for questions in Module 04 on Media and Criminal/Civil Law.
