# ⚖️ Important Cases - Media Laws

## 📘 Module 02: History and Theories of Press
### **Romesh Thappar v. State of Madras (1950)**
- **Facts**: The entry and circulation of the English journal "Cross Roads" was banned by the Madras Government under the Madras Maintenance of Public Order Act.
- **Issue**: Does banning circulation violate Article 19(1)(a)?
- **Held**: Yes. The Supreme Court struck down the ban.
- **Principle**: Freedom of speech and expression includes the freedom of propagation of ideas, and that freedom is ensured by the freedom of circulation. "Public order" was not originally a ground for restriction in Art 19(2) (it was added by the 1st Amendment).

## 📘 Module 03: Constitutional Framework
### **Sakal Papers (P) Ltd. v. Union of India (1962)**
- **Facts**: The Newspaper (Price and Page) Act, 1956 empowered the government to regulate the number of pages according to the price charged.
- **Issue**: Can the state regulate the business aspects of a newspaper if it affects circulation?
- **Held**: The Act was struck down.
- **Principle**: The right to freedom of speech cannot be taken away with the object of placing restrictions on the business activities of a citizen.

### **Bennett Coleman & Co. v. Union of India (1972)**
- **Facts**: The Newsprint Control Order restricted the number of pages a newspaper could publish due to a shortage of newsprint.
- **Held**: The order was unconstitutional.
- **Principle**: Freedom of the press means freedom from interference from authority that would have the effect of interference with the content and circulation.

### **K.A. Abbas v. Union of India (1970)**
- **Facts**: A documentary film "A Tale of Four Cities" was denied a 'U' certificate. The petitioner challenged the constitutionality of prior censorship for films.
- **Held**: Prior censorship of films is valid.
- **Principle**: Motion pictures are able to stir up emotions more deeply than any other product of art. Therefore, pre-censorship under the Cinematograph Act is a reasonable restriction under Art 19(2).

## 📘 Module 04: Legal Dimensions
### **Kedar Nath Singh v. State of Bihar (1962)**
- **Facts**: A Forward Communist Party member gave a fiery speech against the ruling Congress party and was charged with sedition.
- **Issue**: Is Section 124A (Sedition) constitutional?
- **Held**: Yes, but its application is restricted.
- **Principle**: Strong words expressing disapprobation of government measures do not amount to sedition unless they have the tendency or intention to create public disorder or incite violence.

### **R. Rajagopal v. State of T.N. (Auto Shankar Case) (1994)**
- **Facts**: A convicted serial killer wrote an autobiography in jail implicating police officials. The officials tried to stop its publication.
- **Held**: The magazine had the right to publish it.
- **Principle**: The state cannot impose prior restraint on publication. Public officials cannot sue for defamation for acts related to their official duties unless they prove the publication was made with "reckless disregard for truth."

### **Sahara India Real Estate Corp. Ltd. v. SEBI (2012)**
- **Facts**: Details of a proposal made by Sahara to SEBI were leaked to the media during ongoing court proceedings.
- **Held**: The Supreme Court formulated guidelines for "postponement orders".
- **Principle**: Courts can temporarily gag the media from reporting on a trial if such reporting creates a real and substantial risk of prejudice to the proper administration of justice.

## 📘 Module 05: Regulatory Framework
### **Hamdard Dawakhana v. Union of India (1960)**
- **Facts**: The Drugs and Magic Remedies Act prohibited advertisements of certain drugs claiming magical cures.
- **Held**: The Act is valid.
- **Principle**: Commercial advertisements promoting self-medication are a part of trade and commerce, not freedom of speech. They do not propagate social, political, or economic ideas.

### **Shreya Singhal v. Union of India (2015)**
- **Facts**: Two girls were arrested under Section 66A of the IT Act for posting a Facebook comment questioning the shutdown of Mumbai after a politician's death.
- **Held**: Section 66A was struck down entirely.
- **Principle**: The section was overly broad, vague, and created a "chilling effect" on free speech on the internet.
