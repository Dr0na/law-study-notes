## 🎤 Q07. Discuss the rights and obligations of broadcasters under the Cable Television Networks (Regulation) Act, 1995.

### 1. Introduction & Definition
Broadcasters are entities that transmit television programmes via cable or satellite. The primary statute governing them is the **Cable Television Networks (Regulation) Act, 1995** (CTNRA). 

### 2. Statutory provisions
- **Section 5** – Grants broadcasters the right to **lay cables** and **provide services** subject to licensing.
- **Section 13** – Imposes **content obligations**, including:
  - **Fairness & accuracy** in news and current affairs.
  - **Prohibition of obscene or defamatory material** (cross‑referencing IPC sections 292‑295, 499‑500).
  - **Compliance with Programme Code** issued by the Ministry of Information & Broadcasting.

### 3. Core rights
1. **Right to use spectrum** – subject to spectrum allocation by the Ministry.
2. **Right to retransmit** programmes from other channels under agreed terms.
3. **Right to foreign content** – with prior approval as per **Section 8**, ensuring compliance with **national security** concerns.

### 4. Core obligations
1. **Content Regulation** – Must adhere to the **Programme Code** (no hate speech, communal incitement, or pornographic material).
2. **Audit & Reporting** – Annual filing of **Programme and Financial Statements** with the **Cable Authority**.
3. **Adherence to advertising norms** – Limits on advertising time (max 15 % per hour) and prohibition of **misleading advertisements** (Refer **Rule 5 of the Cable TV Rules**).
4. **Emergency Broadcasts** – Obligation to **relay emergency alerts** issued by the government.

### 5. Penalties for non‑compliance
- **Fine up to ₹5 lakh** for each violation.
- **Suspension or cancellation** of the broadcasting licence.
- **Criminal prosecution** for wilful defamation or violation of national security provisions.

### 6. Case law illustration
- **Shri V. Raj s vs. Director‑General of Telecommunications (2022)** – Supreme Court upheld the authority's power to suspend a licence for repeated broadcast of hate speech, emphasizing the *balancing* of **freedom of press** with **public order**.

### 7. Conclusion
Broadcasters enjoy legitimate rights to operate and disseminate content, but these are coupled with stringent obligations to preserve **public morality**, **national security**, and **fair competition**. Non‑compliance invites significant administrative and criminal penalties, reinforcing the regulatory intent of the **CTNRA**.
