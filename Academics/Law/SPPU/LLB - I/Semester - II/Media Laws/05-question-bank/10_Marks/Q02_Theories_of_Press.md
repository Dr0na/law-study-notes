# Q02: Theories of Press

## 📝 Question
Explain the Authoritarian and Libertarian theories of the press. (10 Marks)

---

## 🧾 MODEL ANSWER

### 1. 🧾 Answer Synopsis
- **Introduction**: Models explaining state-media relations.
- **Authoritarian**: State control, censorship, truth is a monopoly.
- **Libertarian**: Free market of ideas, watchdog role, minimal control.
- **Conclusion**: Shift from control to freedom.

### 2. 🧠 Introduction
Theories of the press, originally formulated by Siebert, Peterson, and Schramm, describe how media operates under different political philosophies. The Authoritarian and Libertarian theories represent two opposite ends of the spectrum regarding state control over media.

### 3. 📖 Body
#### A. Authoritarian Theory
- **Origin**: 16th and 17th century England (Tudor period).
- **Core Philosophy**: The state is the highest expression of human organization. The press exists to support and advance the policies of the government.
- **Concept of Truth**: Truth is the monopoly of the ruler or the state. The masses are considered incapable of understanding the truth independently.
- **Control Mechanism**: Strict censorship, licensing systems, and severe punishments for criticizing the monarch or state policies.
- **Ownership**: Can be private, but strictly controlled by the state.

#### B. Libertarian Theory
- **Origin**: 18th and 19th centuries, born out of the Enlightenment (John Milton, John Stuart Mill).
- **Core Philosophy**: Man is a rational being capable of distinguishing between truth and falsehood. The press should act as a "watchdog" over the government.
- **Concept of Truth**: Truth emerges from a "free marketplace of ideas." Let all voices be heard, and the truth will ultimately prevail.
- **Control Mechanism**: Minimal state intervention. No prior censorship. Abuses of freedom (like defamation or obscenity) are dealt with through courts *after* publication.
- **Ownership**: Primarily private, operating in a free market.

### 4. 📊 Diagram: Comparison
| Feature | Authoritarian | Libertarian |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Role** | Servant of the State | Watchdog of the State |
| **Control** | Censorship / Licensing | Free Market |
| **Truth** | Monopoly of the Ruler | Emerges from debate |

### 5. 🧾 Conclusion
While the Authoritarian model suppresses dissent to maintain order, the Libertarian model champions absolute freedom. Modern democracies usually adopt a middle ground, known as the Social Responsibility theory, which balances freedom with ethical obligations.
