## 📱 Q10. Analyse the impact of the Internet on traditional media forms (newspapers, television, radio) in India.

### 1. Introduction & Definition
The Internet, a global digital network, has transformed information dissemination, creating **new media** that competes with conventional press, TV, and radio.

### 2. Key Impacts (with statutory context)
1. **Circulation Decline** – Print newspaper circulation fell by ~30% post‑2015. The *Press and Registration of Books Act, 1867* does not address online publishing, leading to regulatory gaps.
2. **Advertising Shift** – Advertisers moved to digital platforms under the *Information Technology (Intermediary Guidelines) Rules, 2021*, reducing revenue streams for TV and radio.
3. **Content Convergence** – News channels stream live feeds on YouTube; broadcasters host podcasts, blurring boundaries. The *Cable TV Networks (Regulation) Act, 1995* restricts only cable, not OTT.
4. **Audience Fragmentation** – Younger audiences prefer smartphones; the *Right to Information Act, 2005* encourages e‑governance, further sidelining traditional outlets.
5. **Regulatory Challenges** – Existing rules lack provisions for online defamation, leading to *Shreya Singhal v. Union of India* (2015) clarifications on Section 66A.

### 3. Case Law Illustration
- **Shreya Singhal v. Union of India (2015)** – Supreme Court struck down Section 66A of the IT Act, emphasizing free speech online, indirectly affecting traditional media's legal exposure.

### 4. Conclusion
The Internet has **eroded** the financial and audience base of traditional media while **creating** new regulatory vacuums. Future reforms must harmonise the *Press Council Act, 1978* with the *IT Act, 2000* to ensure a level playing field.
