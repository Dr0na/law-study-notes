# Q06: Press Council of India

## 📝 Question
Discuss the powers and functions of the Press Council of India. Why is it often called a 'toothless tiger'? (10 Marks)

---

## 🧾 MODEL ANSWER

### 1. 🧾 Answer Synopsis
- **Introduction**: Statutory, quasi-judicial watchdog for print media.
- **Functions**: Maintain standards, protect press freedom.
- **Powers**: Warn, admonish, censure.
- **Criticism**: Lacks punitive powers ("Toothless Tiger").

### 2. 🧠 Introduction
The Press Council of India (PCI) is a statutory, quasi-judicial body established under the Press Council Act, 1978. It acts as a watchdog for the print media, functioning on the principle of "self-regulation."

### 3. 📖 Body
#### A. Functions of the PCI
1.  To help newspapers and news agencies maintain their independence.
2.  To build up a code of conduct for newspapers and journalists in accordance with high professional standards.
3.  To ensure that newspapers maintain high standards of public taste and foster a due sense of both the rights and responsibilities of citizenship.
4.  To encourage the growth of a sense of responsibility and public service among journalists.

#### B. Powers of the PCI
- It can hold inquiries into complaints against newspapers or journalists.
- It has the powers of a civil court while trying a suit (summoning witnesses, demanding documents).
- If it finds a newspaper guilty of violating journalistic ethics, it can **warn, admonish, or censure** the newspaper, the news agency, the editor, or the journalist.

#### C. The "Toothless Tiger" Criticism
Despite its noble objectives, the PCI is heavily criticized and often called a "toothless tiger" because:
1.  **No Punitive Power**: It cannot impose fines, send anyone to jail, or cancel the license of a publication. Its maximum punishment is a "censure" (a formal public reprimand).
2.  **Limited Jurisdiction**: It only covers print media. Television, radio, and digital media fall outside its purview.
3.  **No Contempt Power**: It cannot force compliance of its orders.

### 4. 🧾 Conclusion
While the PCI lacks the teeth to bite, its "bark" (censure) still carries moral weight. However, to effectively regulate modern media malpractices like "Paid News," there is a pressing need to grant the PCI more robust statutory powers and expand its jurisdiction to electronic media.
