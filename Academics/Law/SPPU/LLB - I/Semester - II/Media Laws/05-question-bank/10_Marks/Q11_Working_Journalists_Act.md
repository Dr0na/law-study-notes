# Q11: Working Journalists Act

## 📝 Question
Discuss the salient features of the Working Journalists (Conditions of Service) and Miscellaneous Provisions Act, 1955. (10 Marks)

---

## 🧾 MODEL ANSWER

### 1. 🧾 Answer Synopsis
- **Objective**: Protect journalists from exploitation.
- **Key Features**: Definition of working journalist, retrenchment compensation, working hours, Wage Boards.
- **Conclusion**: Ensures independence of the press by securing journalists' livelihoods.

### 2. 🧠 Introduction
Freedom of the press relies heavily on the independence of the journalists who gather and report the news. The Working Journalists Act, 1955 was enacted to regulate the conditions of service of journalists and protect them from arbitrary exploitation by media owners.

### 3. 📖 Body
#### A. Definition of Working Journalist
The Act defines a "working journalist" broadly to include editors, leader-writers, news editors, sub-editors, reporters, correspondents, cartoonists, news-photographers, and proof-readers. (It excludes those in purely managerial or administrative capacities).

#### B. Salient Features
1.  **Retrenchment Compensation**: A newspaper establishment cannot fire a journalist arbitrarily. They must give a notice period (6 months for an editor, 3 months for others) and pay retrenchment compensation under the Industrial Disputes Act.
2.  **Working Hours**: The Act limits the maximum working hours to 144 hours during any period of four consecutive weeks, ensuring journalists are not overworked.
3.  **Leave**: It provides statutory rights to earned leave, medical leave, and casual leave.
4.  **Wage Boards**: The Act empowers the Central Government to constitute Wage Boards to fix and revise the minimum rates of wages for working journalists, keeping in mind the cost of living and the capacity of the newspaper to pay.

### 4. ⚖️ Case Laws
- **Express Newspaper v. Union of India**: The Supreme Court upheld the constitutionality of the Act, stating that legislation aimed at ameliorating the conditions of workmen does not violate the freedom of the press under Article 19(1)(a).

### 5. 🧾 Conclusion
The Act recognizes that a journalist constantly worried about job security or unfair wages cannot report fearlessly. By securing their economic and service conditions, the Act indirectly fortifies the freedom of the press in India.
