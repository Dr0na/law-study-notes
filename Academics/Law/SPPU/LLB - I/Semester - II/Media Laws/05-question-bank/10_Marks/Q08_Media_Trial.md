# Q08: Media Trial

## 📝 Question
What is a 'Media Trial'? Discuss how it conflicts with the administration of justice and the Contempt of Courts Act. (10 Marks)

---

## 🧾 MODEL ANSWER

### 1. 🧾 Answer Synopsis
- **Meaning**: Media declaring an accused guilty before the court verdict.
- **Conflict**: Free Speech vs. Right to Fair Trial (Art 21).
- **Contempt of Court**: Prejudicing judicial proceedings.
- **Remedy**: Postponement orders (*Sahara case*).

### 2. 🧠 Introduction
A 'Media Trial' refers to the phenomenon where the media conducts its own parallel investigation and creates a widespread public perception of guilt or innocence about an accused, long before a court of law has pronounced its verdict.

### 3. 📖 Body
#### A. The Conflict of Rights
Media trials create a direct clash between two fundamental rights:
1.  **Article 19(1)(a)**: Freedom of the press to report on crimes and investigations.
2.  **Article 21**: The right of the accused to a fair trial and the presumption of innocence.

#### B. Impact on Administration of Justice
- **Prejudicing Witnesses**: Aggressive media coverage can intimidate witnesses or cause them to change their testimonies.
- **Influencing Judges**: While judges are trained to be objective, immense public pressure created by media campaigns can subconsciously influence judicial outcomes.
- **Character Assassination**: The accused's reputation is destroyed permanently, even if they are later acquitted by the court.

#### C. Contempt of Court
Under the Contempt of Courts Act, 1971, **Criminal Contempt** includes any publication that "prejudices, or interferes or tends to interfere with, the due course of any judicial proceeding."
- Once a person is arrested or a charge sheet is filed, the matter becomes *sub judice* (under judicial consideration).
- Media reporting that assumes guilt during this period amounts to criminal contempt.

### 4. ⚖️ Case Laws
- **State of Maharashtra v. Rajendra Jawanmal Gandhi**: The SC observed that a trial by press, electronic media, or public agitation is the very antithesis of the rule of law.
- **Sahara India Real Estate Corp. Ltd. v. SEBI**: To balance these rights, the SC held that courts can issue "postponement orders" (gag orders) temporarily restraining the media from reporting certain aspects of a trial to ensure fairness.

### 5. 🧾 Conclusion
While investigative journalism is crucial for exposing crimes, the media must not usurp the role of the judiciary. Self-restraint by the media is the best antidote to the menace of media trials.
