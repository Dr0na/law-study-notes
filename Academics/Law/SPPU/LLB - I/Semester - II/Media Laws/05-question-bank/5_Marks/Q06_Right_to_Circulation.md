# Q06: Right to Circulation

## 📝 Question
Explain the 'Right to Circulation' as a facet of Freedom of Press. (5 Marks)

---

## 🧾 MODEL ANSWER

### 1. 🧾 Answer Synopsis
- **Meaning**: Freedom to determine the volume of distribution.
- **Constitutional Basis**: Implicit in Article 19(1)(a).
- **Landmark Case**: *Sakal Papers* and *Romesh Thappar*.

### 2. 🧠 Introduction
Freedom of the press is meaningless if the printed material cannot reach the readers. Therefore, the Supreme Court has recognized the 'Right to Circulation' as an integral part of Article 19(1)(a).

### 3. 📖 Body
- **Scope**: It includes the right of the publisher to determine the number of copies to be printed and the geographical area of distribution.
- **State Interference**: Any state action that directly or indirectly limits circulation (e.g., by fixing page limits, controlling newsprint, or banning entry into a state) is unconstitutional unless justified under Article 19(2).

### 4. ⚖️ Case Laws
- **Romesh Thappar v. State of Madras**: The SC struck down a ban on the entry and circulation of a journal in Madras, stating that freedom of speech includes the freedom of propagation.
- **Sakal Papers v. UOI**: The state cannot restrict the number of pages a newspaper can publish based on its price, as it directly curtails circulation.

### 5. 🧾 Conclusion
The right to circulation ensures that ideas can freely flow in the marketplace, which is essential for educating the public in a democracy.
