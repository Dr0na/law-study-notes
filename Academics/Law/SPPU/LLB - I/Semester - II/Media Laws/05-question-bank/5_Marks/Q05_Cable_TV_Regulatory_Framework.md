### Q05. Outline the regulatory framework for Cable Television in India.

**📖 Statutory Provision:** Cable Television Networks (Regulation) Act, 1995 (CTNR Act) and the associated Rules, 1994.

**1. Meaning:** The CTNR Act governs the operation, licensing, and content regulation of cable TV networks across India.

**2. Core Elements:**
- **Licensing:** All cable operators must obtain a licence from the Ministry of Information & Broadcasting. The licence is renewable annually and subject to compliance with technical and content standards.
- **Program Code:** The Programme Code (Rule 7) specifies prohibited content (e.g., hate speech, obscenity, defamation) and mandates adherence to the Programme and Advertising Code.
- **Tariff Regulation:** The Cable Operators (Regulation of Tariff) Rules, 2005 empower the government to fix or cap subscription fees to protect consumers.
- **Consumer Protection:** The Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) may intervene in cases of illegal distribution, and consumers can file complaints with the Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI).

**3. Enforcement:**
- The Ministry, along with state governments, monitors compliance. Violations may lead to suspension or cancellation of the licence, fines, or criminal prosecution under the Indian Penal Code for obscene or defamatory content.

**🎯 Conclusion:** The CTNR Act establishes a comprehensive regulatory regime aiming to balance free transmission of information with safeguards against harmful content and exploitation of consumers.
