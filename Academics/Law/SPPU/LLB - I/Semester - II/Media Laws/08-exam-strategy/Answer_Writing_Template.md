# ✍️ Answer Writing Template - Media Laws

To score high in Media Laws, your answers must balance constitutional principles with landmark judgments.

## 1. 🎯 Structure for 15-Mark Questions (Long Answers)
1.  **Introduction**: Define the concept. Mention the relevant Constitutional Article (usually 19(1)(a) or 19(2)).
2.  **Core Principle**: Explain the rule of law.
3.  **Main Body**:
    - Use headings and sub-headings.
    - Explain the conflict (e.g., Free Speech vs Privacy).
    - Use a **Mermaid Diagram** or Flowchart if applicable.
4.  **Case Laws (Crucial)**: Cite at least 3 landmark cases. Use the format: *Case Name* → *Facts* → *Held* → *Principle*.
5.  **Critical Analysis**: Mention the current challenges (e.g., internet regulation, media trials).
6.  **Conclusion**: Summarize the balance achieved by the courts.

## 2. 🎯 Structure for 10-Mark Questions (Medium Answers)
1.  **Introduction**: Brief definition.
2.  **Key Points**: 4-6 major points with brief explanations.
3.  **Comparison Table**: If the question is "A vs B," always use a table.
4.  **Case Law**: At least 1-2 relevant cases.
5.  **Conclusion**: A short closing paragraph.

## 3. 🎯 Structure for 5-Mark Questions (Short Notes)
1.  **Definition**: 1-2 sentences.
2.  **Core Explanation**: 3-4 bullet points.
3.  **Mnemonic**: Mention the mnemonic for recall.
4.  **Example/Case**: 1 small example or case name.

---

## 💡 Pro-Tips for Media Laws
- **Always link back to the Constitution**: Even if the question is about the Press Council or Defamation, start by mentioning how it relates to Article 19(1)(a) and 19(2).
- **Use the exact 8 grounds**: When writing about restrictions, use the exact words from Art 19(2) (e.g., "Public Order", not "Public Peace").
- **Highlight the balance**: Media law is always a balancing act. Show the examiner that you understand both sides (e.g., the right of the press to report vs. the right of the accused to a fair trial).
