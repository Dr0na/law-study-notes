# 📘 Module 03: Constitutional Framework of Freedom of Media in India

🎚️ Difficulty: 🔴 Hard

---

### 🧠 Introduction

The Constitution of India does not explicitly mention "Freedom of Press." Instead, it is implicitly guaranteed under **Article 19(1)(a)** as "Freedom of Speech and Expression." This module explores the constitutional dimensions, the facets of this freedom, and the reasonable restrictions that can be imposed upon it.

---

### 📖 Main Content

#### 1. Free Speech and Constituent Assembly Debates
During the Constituent Assembly debates, Dr. B.R. Ambedkar clarified that the press has no special rights which are not to be given to an individual citizen. Therefore, a separate mention of freedom of the press was deemed unnecessary.

#### 2. Freedom of Speech and Expression in Indian Constitution
- **Article 19(1)(a)**: Guarantees to all citizens the right to freedom of speech and expression.
- **Scope**: It includes the right to express one's convictions and opinions freely by word of mouth, writing, printing, picture, or any other mode.

#### 3. Facets of Freedom of Speech and Expression
The Supreme Court has expanded the scope of Article 19(1)(a) to include several facets:
1.  **Freedom of Press**: The right to print and publish without prior restraint.
2.  **Right to Circulation**: Freedom to determine the volume of circulation (*Sakal Papers v. Union of India*).
3.  **Right to Receive Information**: The right of citizens to know and be informed (*State of UP v. Raj Narain*).
4.  **Right to Advertise**: Commercial speech is a part of freedom of speech (*Tata Press Ltd. v. MTNL*).
5.  **Right to Telecast / Broadcast**: The airwaves are public property and must be used for public good (*Ministry of I&B v. Cricket Association of Bengal*).
6.  **Censorship**: Prior restraint is generally unconstitutional, except in specific cases like films (*K.A. Abbas v. Union of India*).

🧠 **Mnemonic**: **P-C-R-A-T-C** (Press, Circulation, Receive info, Advertise, Telecast, Censorship).

#### 4. Reasonable Restrictions (Article 19(2))
Freedom of speech is not absolute. The State can impose "reasonable restrictions" on the following 8 grounds:
1.  Sovereignty and integrity of India
2.  Security of the State
3.  Friendly relations with foreign States
4.  Public order
5.  Decency or morality
6.  Contempt of court
7.  Defamation
8.  Incitement to an offence

🧠 **Mnemonic**: **S-S-F-P-D-C-D-I** (Sovereignty, Security, Friendly relations, Public order, Decency, Contempt, Defamation, Incitement).

#### 5. Legislative Privileges and Media
- Articles 105 and 194 grant privileges to Parliament and State Legislatures.
- Conflict arises when media reports on legislative proceedings. The legislature has the power to punish for breach of privilege or contempt of the house (e.g., *Searchlight Case*).

#### 6. Right to Privacy and Media
- Right to Privacy is a fundamental right under Article 21 (*K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India*).
- Media's right to report must be balanced against an individual's right to privacy.

#### 7. Freedom of Media during Emergency
- Under Article 358, Article 19 is automatically suspended during a National Emergency declared on grounds of war or external aggression.
- During the 1975 Emergency, severe censorship was imposed on the press.

---

### ⚖️ Case Laws

- **Brij Bhushan v. State of Delhi (1950)**: Pre-censorship on a journal is a restriction on the liberty of the press.
- **Sakal Papers (P) Ltd. v. Union of India (1962)**: The state cannot restrict the number of pages or fix the price of newspapers, as it directly affects the right to circulation.
- **Bennett Coleman & Co. v. Union of India (1972)**: The Newsprint Control Order was struck down. The court held that freedom of the press means freedom from interference from authority that would have the effect of interference with the content and circulation.
- **K.A. Abbas v. Union of India (1970)**: Censorship of films is constitutionally valid under Article 19(2) because motion pictures have a deeper impact on the audience than print media.

---

### 🧾 Conclusion

Article 19(1)(a) is the bedrock of media freedom in India. The judiciary has played a proactive role in expanding its horizons while ensuring that the reasonable restrictions under Article 19(2) are not misused by the State to stifle dissent.
