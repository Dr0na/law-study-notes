## 🚔 Q1. Critically evaluate the judicial approach to Police Reforms in India with special reference to the Prakash Singh case directives.

### 1. Introduction & Definition
The Indian police force continues to be governed by the colonial-era **Police Act of 1861**, which was designed to maintain state control rather than serve the public. Modern democratic policing requires a shift from a **"ruler-centric force"** to a **"citizen-centric service"**. 

Due to the executive's failure to implement various commission recommendations (such as the National Police Commission, 1977-81), the judiciary has stepped in to direct police reforms and protect fundamental rights.

---

### 2. The Seven Binding Directives in Prakash Singh v. Union of India (2006)
In the landmark case of **Prakash Singh v. Union of India (2006)**, the Supreme Court issued seven binding directives to all State Governments and the Union to insulate the police from political pressure and improve public accountability:

1.  **State Security Commission (SSC)**:
    *   *Directive*: Every state must establish a State Security Commission.
    *   *Purpose*: To insulate the police from illegitimate political interference and formulate broad policy guidelines for state policing.
2.  **Selection & Tenure of DGP**:
    *   *Directive*: The DGP must be selected from a panel recommended by the UPSC based on merit, and must have a guaranteed **minimum tenure of 2 years**, regardless of their date of superannuation.
    *   *Purpose*: To prevent arbitrary transfers and ensure administrative stability at the top.
3.  **Tenure of Operational Field Officers**:
    *   *Directive*: District Superintendents of Police (SPs) and Station House Officers (SHOs) must have a guaranteed **minimum tenure of 2 years**.
    *   *Purpose*: To prevent politically motivated transfers and ensure operational stability in local policing.
4.  **Separation of Functions**:
    *   *Directive*: Separate the **Crime Investigation** wing from the **Law & Order** wing in urban areas.
    *   *Purpose*: To allow for specialization, speed up investigations, and improve overall policing efficiency.
5.  **Police Establishment Board (PEB)**:
    *   *Directive*: Constitute a PEB in every state, comprising the DGP and senior officers.
    *   *Purpose*: To decide transfers, postings, and promotions for officers below the rank of Deputy SP, reducing political interference in personnel matters.
6.  **Police Complaints Authority (PCA)**:
    *   *Directive*: Establish PCAs at both the State and District levels, headed by retired judges.
    *   *Purpose*: To provide a forum for citizens to file complaints against police excesses, serious misconduct, or abuse of power.
7.  **National Security Commission (NSC)**:
    *   *Directive*: Establish an NSC at the Central level to recommend panels for selecting and placing heads of Central Armed Police Forces (CAPFs).

---

### 3. Conclusion
Through the *Prakash Singh* directives and other landmark rulings like *D.K. Basu* (arrest guidelines) and *Lalita Kumari* (mandatory FIR registration), the judiciary has worked to establish clear standards for police conduct. However, many states have been slow to implement these reforms fully, often introducing diluted versions through state legislation. Achieving comprehensive reform requires a strong political will and active public advocacy to transform the police force into an autonomous, professional, and citizen-friendly service.
