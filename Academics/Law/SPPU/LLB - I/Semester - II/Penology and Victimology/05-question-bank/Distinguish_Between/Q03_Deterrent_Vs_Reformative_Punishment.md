### Q3. Distinguish between Deterrent and Reformative Theories of Punishment.

**1. Concept Map:**  
Deterrent theory aims to prevent crime by creating fear through severe punishment, whereas reformative theory views crime as a social disease, treating and rehabilitating the offender through counseling and education.

**2. Comparison Table:**

| 📊 Basis | 🛡️ Deterrent Theory | 🤝 Reformative Theory |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Philosophy & Core Idea** | Create fear in the mind of the offender and prospective wrongdoers to show that crime is unprofitable. | View crime as a disease and the criminal as a patient who needs treatment and rehabilitation rather than physical pain. |
| **Slogan** | *"I do not punish you for stealing sheep, but so that sheep may not be stolen."* | *"Hate the crime, not the criminal."* |
| **Jurists** | Jeremy Bentham, John Austin. | Mahatma Gandhi, Justice V.R. Krishna Iyer. |
| **Target Focus** | Focuses on the **crime** and its consequences. | Focuses on the **offender** and their rehabilitation. |
| **Application** | Heavy penalties, long sentences, and harsh prison conditions to serve as a warning. | Probation, parole, open prisons, and vocational training. |
| **Primary Criticism** | Hardened criminals are rarely deterred by fear, and harsh punishments can brutalize society. | Lenient sentences can undermine public confidence, and habitual offenders may abuse the system. |

**3. Landmark Case/Statutory Rule:**  
In **Dr. Jacob George v. State of Kerala (1994)**, the Supreme Court held that sentencing policy must balance deterrent and reformative principles to achieve balanced justice.

**🎯 Conclusion:**  
Modern penology utilizes both theories: employing deterrent measures for grave, heinous crimes while expanding reformative opportunities for petty and first-time offenders.
