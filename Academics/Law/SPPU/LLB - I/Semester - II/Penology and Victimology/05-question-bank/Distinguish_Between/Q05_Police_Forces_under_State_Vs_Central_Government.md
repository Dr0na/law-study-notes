### Q5. Distinguish between Police Forces under State Government and Central Government.

**1. Concept Map:**  
State police forces maintain local law and order and investigate local crimes, whereas Central police forces manage national borders, secure critical infrastructure, and handle specialized investigative or intelligence duties.

**2. Comparison Table:**

| 📊 Basis | 🚔 State Police Forces | 🛡️ Central Police Forces |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Constitutional Status** | Falls under **Entry 2, List II (State List)** of the Seventh Schedule. | Falls under **Entry 2, List I (Union List)** of the Seventh Schedule. |
| **Primary Mandate** | Maintain local law and order, prevent crime, and investigate local offenses. | Manage borders (BSF), secure critical infrastructure (CISF), and assist in emergencies (CRPF). |
| **Operational Jurisdiction** | Limited to the physical boundaries of the respective state. | National jurisdiction across all states and Union Territories. |
| **Highest Authority** | Led by the **Director General of Police (DGP)** at the state level. | Administered by the **Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA)** at the Central level. |
| **Examples** | Maharashtra Police, Uttar Pradesh Police, Delhi Police (exception under Union control). | BSF, CRPF, CISF, CBI, NIA. |

**3. Landmark Case/Statutory Rule:**  
In **Prakash Singh v. Union of India (2006)**, the Supreme Court issued directives to reform state police administration while establishing a National Security Commission to oversee central police forces.

**🎯 Conclusion:**  
Both State and Central police forces are essential for national security, working together to maintain local order while protecting national borders and critical infrastructure.
