### Q4. Distinguish between Open Prison and Closed Prison.

**1. Concept Map:**  
Closed prisons are traditional high-security institutions designed for custodial holding, whereas open prisons are minimum-security, trust-based facilities focused on rehabilitation and social reintegration.

**2. Comparison Table:**

| 📊 Basis | 🔓 Open Prison | 🔒 Closed Prison |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Physical Security** | No high walls, barbed wire, locked cells, or armed guards. | High perimeter walls, iron bars, locked cells, and armed guards. |
| **Primary Philosophy** | Trust, self-discipline, and rehabilitation. | Custody, security, and strict discipline. |
| **Inmate Activities** | Inmates work in agriculture or local industries, earning normal wages. | Inmates work within the prison under strict supervision, earning minimal wages. |
| **Selection Criteria** | Selective transfer of long-term convicts based on good behavior. | Confines all classes of convicted and under-trial prisoners. |
| **Operational Cost** | Highly cost-effective; requires fewer security personnel and less physical infrastructure. | Expensive to construct, secure, and maintain. |
| **Family Co-habitation** | Allowed in models like the Sanganer Open Prison in Rajasthan. | Not permitted; visits are restricted to designated booth areas. |

**3. Landmark Case/Statutory Rule:**  
In **Sangat Singh v. State of Rajasthan (1984)**, the Supreme Court praised open prisons like the Sanganer model as highly effective tools for rehabilitation.

**🎯 Conclusion:**  
Open prisons represent a successful application of reformative penology, demonstrating that minimum-security, trust-based environments can be highly effective for rehabilitating well-behaved long-term prisoners.
