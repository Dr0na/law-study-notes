# 🏢 Q2. Discuss the role of prisons in modern penology and critically evaluate the various systemic problems in Indian prisons. Outline the major recommendations of the Mulla Committee on prison reforms.

## 1. Introduction
The **Prison System** is the primary institutional mechanism used by the state to execute sentences of imprisonment pronounced by courts. Under the Constitution of India, "Prisons, reformatories, borstal institutions, and other institutions of a like nature" fall under **Entry 4 of the State List** (Seventh Schedule).

Historically, prisons were purely punitive institutions designed to inflict hardship on inmates. However, modern penology views prisons as **correctional and rehabilitative centers**. The objective is to secure the offender, reform their character, and prepare them for a productive, law-abiding life after release.

---

## 2. Systemic Problems in Indian Prisons
Indian prisons face six severe systemic challenges:

1.  **Severe Overcrowding**: The average occupancy rate in Indian prisons regularly exceeds 120% of sanctioned capacity, with some facilities reaching over 200%.
2.  **Inadequate Basic Amenities**: Lack of proper sanitation, poor ventilation, and low-quality food remain common issues.
3.  **Neglected Prisoner Health**: Limited medical facilities, inadequate access to doctors, and a lack of mental health care, which is exacerbated by overcrowding.
4.  **High Ratio of Under-trial Prisoners**: Over 70% of all inmates in Indian prisons are under-trials who have not yet been convicted of any crime.
5.  **Criminality Within Prisons**: First-time offenders are often housed alongside hardened, habitual criminals, turning prisons into "schools of crime" rather than centers of reform.
6.  **Inconsistent Prison Discipline**: Abuse of authority by staff, inmate violence, and significant staff shortages, particularly in specialized medical and correctional roles.

---

## 3. Major Recommendations of the Mulla Committee (1980-83)
Chaired by Justice A.N. Mulla, the **All-India Committee on Prison Reforms** provided the most comprehensive blueprint for reform:

*   **National Prison Policy**: Formulate a national policy treating prisons as correctional institutions, aligning them with modern rehabilitation-focused penology.
*   **Separate Under-trials**: House under-trial prisoners completely separate from convicted prisoners to prevent criminal contamination.
*   **Modernize Recruitment**: Standardize the recruitment, training, and working conditions of prison staff, establishing specialized services.
*   **Expand Alternatives**: Expand the use of open prisons, parole, furlough, and probation to reduce overcrowding in traditional closed jails.
*   **Focus on Rehabilitation**: Introduce vocational training, educational programs, and psychological counseling to aid reintegration and reduce recidivism.
*   **Adequate Funding**: Direct state governments to allocate separate, adequate funds for prison development and inmate welfare.

---

## 4. Landmark Case Laws

*   **Sunil Batra v. Delhi Administration (1980)**: The Supreme Court held that **"prisons are not places to bury human rights"** and that Article 21 protects prisoners from cruel and degrading treatment, prohibiting solitary confinement and bar-fetters without judicial sanction.
*   **Hussainara Khatoon v. State of Bihar (1979)**: The Court held that the **right to a speedy trial** is a fundamental right implicit under Article 21, leading to the release of thousands of under-trials who had spent longer in prison awaiting trial than the maximum sentence for their alleged offenses.
*   **Ramamurthy v. State of Karnataka (1997)**: The Supreme Court synthesized the jurisprudence of prison reforms, identifying nine major issues (including overcrowding, lack of hygiene, and inadequate medical care) and directing all states to implement the Mulla Committee recommendations to humanize prison administration.

---

## 5. Conclusion
The problems of Indian prisons, particularly overcrowding and the plight of under-trial prisoners, represent a significant challenge for the criminal justice system. While the judiciary has established clear constitutional standards to protect prisoner rights, implementing these reforms requires systemic changes: streamlining trial processes, expanding legal aid, and shifting prison administration from a punitive approach to a modern, rehabilitative model.
