## 📘 Module 01: Penology - Introduction

### 1. Key Definitions & Nature
*   **Penology**: Derived from Latin ***Poena*** (punishment) and Greek ***Logia*** (study).
*   **Definition (Paranjpe)**: Study of principles and methods of punishment, prison policies, and rehabilitation.
*   **Nature**: Applied, interdisciplinary science (sociology, psychology, law, administration) transitioning from retributive to reformative.
*   **Mnemonic — P-A-P-E-R**: **P**hilosophical underpinnings, **A**lternatives (Parole/Probation), **P**rison administration, **E**volving policy, **R**ehabilitation.

### 2. Crime Control Mechanism (Five Organs)
*   **Mnemonic — P-C-P-J-O**: **P**olice, **C**ourt, **P**rosecutor, **J**ail, **O**pen Prison.
*   **1. Police**: Registers FIR under Sec 154 CrPC, conducts investigation under Chap XII CrPC.
*   **2. Prosecutor**: Officer of court, represents State (Sec 24 CrPC).
*   **3. Court**: Adjudicates trial, guarantees fair hearing under Art 21.
*   **4. Jail**: Custodial holding and security under the Prisons Act of 1894.
*   **5. Open Prison**: Corrective and trust-based rehabilitation.

### 3. Core Case Law
*   **D.K. Basu v. State of West Bengal (1997)**: Laid down 11 mandatory guidelines for arrest to prevent police excesses and custodial torture.

## 📘 Module 02: Punishment & Indian Penal Policy

### 1. Concept of Punishment
*   State-sanctioned infliction of pain or deprivation of rights for a legal breach, administered by an authorized court.

### 2. Four Theories of Punishment
*   **Mnemonic — D-R-P-R**: **D**eterrent, **R**etributive, **P**reventive, **R**eformative.
*   **A. Deterrent (Bentham)**: Fear and warning to deter others.
*   **B. Retributive (Kant)**: Desert and retaliation — *Lex Talionis* ("eye for an eye").
*   **C. Preventive (Paton)**: Disables the offender (incarceration).
*   **D. Reformative (Krishna Iyer)**: Rehabilitation — "Hate the crime, cure the criminal."

### 3. Forms of Punishment (Sec 53 IPC / BNS)
*   **Mnemonic — D-L-I-F-P-C**: **D**eath, **L**ife imprisonment, **I**mprisonment (Simple/Rigorous), **F**orfeiture of property, **P**ecuniary fine, **C**ommunity service (BNS).

### 4. Indian Penal Policy Evolvement
*   *Ancient*: Manu/Kautilya — *Danda* (Vakdanda, Prayaschitta, Arthadanda, Vadhadanda).
*   *Medieval*: Sharia (Qisas, Diyya, Tazir).
*   *Colonial*: Strict deterrence (Police Act 1861, transportation, whipping).
*   *Independent India*: Reformative balance, probation, open prisons, parole.

### 5. Core Case Laws
*   **Bachan Singh v. State of Punjab (1980)**: Death penalty constitutional but only in the **"rarest of rare cases"**.
*   **Mithu v. State of Punjab (1983)**: Struck down Section 303 IPC (mandatory death sentence) for violating Articles 14 and 21.
*   **Jacob George v. State of Kerala (1994)**: Sentencing policy must combine deterrent, preventive, reformative, and compensatory theories.

## 📘 Module 03: Police System in India & Reforms

### 1. Structure
*   **Constitutional Status**: State List (Entry 2, List II, 7th Schedule).
*   **State Hierarchy**: DGP $\rightarrow$ Range IG/DIG $\rightarrow$ District SP $\rightarrow$ SHO (Inspector/Sub-Inspector).
*   **Central Forces (CAPF)**: CRPF, BSF, CISF.
*   **Specialized Agencies**: CBI, NIA.

### 2. Principles of Policing (Sir Robert Peel)
*   Primary objective: Prevent crime. Depend on public approval. Use minimal force. **"The police are the public, the public are the police."**

### 3. Police Reforms & Prakash Singh Directives (2006)
*   **Mnemonic — S-T-A-B-L-E-S**:
    *   **S** = State Security Commission (SSC) to insulate police from political pressure.
    *   **T** = Tenure of 2 years for DGP and operational field officers.
    *   **A** = Authority for public complaints (PCA) at State & District levels.
    *   **B** = Board for police establishments (PEB) to decide postings/transfers.
    *   **L** = Law & order separated from crime investigation functions.
    *   **E** = Executive insulation.
    *   **S** = Selection of DGP via UPSC-recommended panel.

### 4. Core Case Laws
*   **Prakash Singh v. UOI (2006)**: Supreme Court issued 7 binding directives for police autonomy and accountability.
*   **Lalita Kumari v. Govt of U.P. (2014)**: Mandatory registration of FIR (Sec 154) if cognizable offense is disclosed.

## 📘 Module 04: Prison System in India & Reforms

### 1. Status & Types
*   **Constitutional Status**: State List (Entry 4, List II, 7th Schedule).
*   **Statute**: Prisons Act of 1894 (colonial focus on security and discipline).
*   **Types**: Central, District, Sub-Jails, Open Jails, Women Jails.

### 2. Six Systemic Problems of Prisons
*   **Mnemonic — O-B-D-H-C-U**:
    *   **O** = Overcrowding (Occupancy exceeds 120%).
    *   **B** = Basic amenities deficit (sanitation, hygiene).
    *   **D** = Discipline issues and staff abuse.
    *   **H** = Health challenges (mental and physical neglect).
    *   **C** = Criminality inside (contamination of first-timers by hardened convicts).
    *   **U** = Under-trial dominance (account for > 70% of prison population).

### 3. Prison Reforms & Mulla Committee (1980-83)
*   Recommended a National Prison Policy, completely separating under-trials from convicted inmates, standardizing staff training, and expanding open prisons/probation.

### 4. Core Case Laws
*   **Sunil Batra v. Delhi Admin (1980)**: Prisoners retain fundamental rights; solitary confinement and bar-fetters without judicial sanction violate Article 21.
*   **Hussainara Khatoon v. State of Bihar (1979)**: Right to a speedy trial is a fundamental right under Article 21; directed the release of thousands of under-trials who had spent longer in prison than the maximum sentence for their alleged offenses.
*   **Ramamurthy v. State of Karnataka (1997)**: Directed states to implement Mulla Committee reforms to address overcrowding, lack of hygiene, and medical neglect.
