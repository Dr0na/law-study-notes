## 📘 Module 05: Open Prisons Philosophy & Working

### 1. Concept & Origin
*   **UN Definition (1955)**: Minimum-security correctional institution characterized by the absence of material precautions against escape (no walls, bars, locks, or armed guards) and built on trust.
*   **Indian Origin**: First camp in 1953 at Chunar, UP. Landmark model: **Sanganer Open Prison in Rajasthan (1955)**, allowing inmates to live with families and work in the community.

### 2. Philosophy & Characteristics
*   Rehabilitation-focused: *"Hate the crime, cure the criminal."*
*   Rigorous selection criteria: Only long-term, well-behaved convicts.
*   **Mnemonic — F-R-E-E-D-O-M**:
    *   **F** = Family reintegration allowed (co-habitation in Sanganer model).
    *   **R** = Rigorous selection process for eligible inmates.
    *   **E** = Economic self-sustenance through vocational work.
    *   **E** = Erasing physical barriers (no walls, bars, or armed guards).
    *   **D** = Development of self-discipline and personal responsibility.
    *   **O** = Open to community-based, non-punitive supervision.
    *   **M** = Minimal escape rates due to valued privileges.

### 3. Advantages & Challenges
*   *Advantages*: Eases social reintegration, preserves family bonds, is highly cost-effective, and reduces overcrowding.
*   *Challenges*: Public concern regarding safety, underutilized capacity due to overly strict selection criteria, and restrictive state eligibility rules.

### 4. Core Case Law
*   **Sangat Singh v. State of Rajasthan (1984)**: Praised the Sanganer model, noting that allowing well-behaved prisoners to live with their families under minimal security is highly effective for rehabilitation.

## 📘 Module 06: Parole System in India

### 1. Concept & Distinctions
*   **Parole**: Conditional, discretionary release of a prisoner before completing their sentence, usually for good behavior or urgent personal reasons. Does not count as part of the sentence served.
*   **Furlough**: Regular home leave granted to long-term prisoners to maintain family connections. Considered an inmate right, and counts as part of the sentence served.
*   **Probation**: A judicial alternative to sentencing (pre-sentence), suspending imprisonment entirely under the Probation of Offenders Act, 1958.
*   **Mnemonic — P-A-R-O-L-E**: **P**ost-sentence, **A**dministrative, **R**egular reporting, **O**bjective criteria, **L**oss of privileges, **E**asing reintegration.

### 2. Parole Board & Conditions
*   *Parole Boards*: State-level boards chaired by the Home Secretary or District Magistrate that evaluate jail behavior, police risk assessments, and medical histories.
*   *Conditions*: Regular reporting to local police, residing within specified geographic limits, maintaining lawful conduct, and avoiding contact with victims or co-defendants.

### 3. Core Case Law
*   **Asfaq v. State of Haryana (2017)**: The landmark Supreme Court ruling defining the distinct roles of parole (discretionary, granted for specific emergencies) and furlough (routine leave, considered a right to prevent isolation).

## 📘 Module 07: Victimology - Victim and Victimization

### 1. Definition & Kinds
*   **Sec 2(wa) CrPC/BNSS**: Defines a "victim," including their guardians and legal heirs.
*   **UN Declaration (1985)**: Persons who suffer physical, emotional, or economic harm through acts violating criminal law.
*   **Mendelsohn’s Typology (Levels of Responsibility)**:
    1. Completely Innocent (sleeping train passenger).
    2. Minor Guilt (negligently leaving a car unlocked).
    3. Voluntary Victim (assisted suicide).
    4. More Guilty than Offender (provoking conflict).
    5. Most Guilty Solely Responsible (robber shot in self-defense).
    6. Simulating/Imaginary (false claims).

### 2. Victimization Impacts & Secondary Victimization
*   *Impacts*: Physical (injury/disability), Economic (financial loss/costs), and Psychological (PTSD/trauma).
*   *Secondary Victimization*: Additional trauma caused to victims by insensitive police questioning, invasive medical exams, aggressive cross-examination, and media intrusion during the justice process.

### 3. Four Theories of Victimology
*   **Mnemonic — P-L-D-R**:
    *   **P** = Precipitation Theory (Active or passive victim provocation).
    *   **L** = Lifestyle Theory (Daily habits and exposure).
    *   **D** = Deviant Place Theory (Confinement to high-crime locations).
    *   **R** = Routine Activity Theory (Convergence of a Motivated Offender, a Suitable Target, and the Absence of a Capable Guardian).

### 4. Core Case Laws
*   **Delhi Domestic Working Women's Forum v. UOI (1995)**: Highlighted secondary victimization and directed the establishment of state legal aid and counseling for rape victims.
*   **Laxmi v. UOI (2014)**: Regulated acid sales, established a minimum Rs. 3 Lakhs compensation, and mandated free medical care for acid attack victims.

## 📘 Module 08: Legal Perspectives of Victim Assistance

### 1. Global & Constitutional Framework
*   **UN Declaration (1985)**: Four pillars: Access to justice/fair treatment, Restitution, State Compensation, and Assistance programs.
*   **Constitution of India**: Fundamental rights read under Article 21 (right to dignity and speedy trial), Article 14 (equality), and Article 39A (free legal aid).

### 2. Statutory Provisions (Compensation & Trial Rights)
*   **Sec 357 CrPC / Sec 395 BNSS**: Compensation paid out of the offender's fines.
*   **Sec 357A CrPC / Sec 396 BNSS (Introduced in 2009)**: State-funded Victim Compensation Schemes administered by the DLSA/SLSA.
*   **Sec 372 CrPC Proviso (Introduced in 2009)**: Direct right of the victim to appeal against acquittals, lesser convictions, or inadequate compensation.
*   **Other Rights**: In-camera trials for sexual offenses (Sec 327 CrPC), notification and representation rights during bail hearings, and free legal aid.
*   **Mnemonic — C-O-M-P-E-N-S-A-T-E**: **C**ompensation schemes, **O**rdered UN principles, **M**andatory in-camera trials, **P**roviso to Section 372, **E**qual protection, **N**otification of bail, **S**tatutory legal aid, **A**ssistance programs, **T**rial rights, **E**asing financial burdens (Sec 5 Probation Act).

### 3. Core Case Laws
*   **Rudal Shah v. State of Bihar (1983)**: Landmark case establishing the state's liability to pay compensation for violating fundamental rights (Article 21).
*   **Ankush Shivaji Gaikwad v. State of Maharashtra (2013)**: Made it mandatory for courts to evaluate and record reasons regarding victim compensation in every criminal judgment.
*   **Mallikarjun Kodagali v. State of Karnataka (2018)**: Held that a victim's right to appeal under Section 372 is an unrestricted, substantive right that does not require prior permission from the court.
