# ⚖️ Case Law Digest: Important Cases in Penology & Victimology

This digest contains the essential landmark judgments of the Supreme Court of India and High Courts, structured for quick review and academic citation.

---

## 🏛️ Penology & Sentencing

### 1. Bachan Singh v. State of Punjab (1980)
*   **Facts**: The constitutional validity of the death penalty under Section 302 of the IPC was challenged on the grounds that it violates Articles 14, 19, and 21 of the Constitution.
*   **Issue**: Is the death penalty constitutional, and are there boundaries governing its imposition?
*   **Held**: The Supreme Court upheld the constitutionality of the death penalty by a 4:1 majority. However, the Court ruled that capital punishment must only be imposed in the **"rarest of rare cases"** where the alternative of life imprisonment is unquestionably foreclosed.
*   **Exam Relevance**: Cite in any question on the **Death Penalty** or **Theories of Punishment** (specifically Deterrent/Retributive vs. Reformative).

### 2. Mithu v. State of Punjab (1983)
*   **Facts**: Section 303 of the IPC mandated a compulsory death sentence for murder committed by a person undergoing life imprisonment. This provision was challenged as unconstitutional.
*   **Issue**: Does a mandatory death sentence violate the fundamental right to life and equal protection?
*   **Held**: The Supreme Court struck down Section 303 of the IPC as unconstitutional. The Court held that a mandatory death penalty violates Articles 14 and 21 because it strips courts of their sentencing discretion and prevents them from considering mitigating factors.
*   **Exam Relevance**: Cite in questions on the **Death Penalty**, **Judicial Discretion**, and **Penal Policy in India**.

### 3. Swamy Shraddananda v. State of Karnataka (2008)
*   **Facts**: The appellant was convicted of murder, and the court evaluated whether the death penalty was appropriate or if a severe form of life imprisonment was a viable alternative.
*   **Issue**: Can courts order a category of life imprisonment that excludes executive remission or parole as a substitute for the death penalty?
*   **Held**: The Supreme Court introduced a special category of life imprisonment. The Court ruled that as an alternative to the death penalty, a court can sentence a convict to life imprisonment for a fixed, extended term (e.g., 20 or 30 years) without the option of parole or executive remission.
*   **Exam Relevance**: Cite in questions on the **Death Penalty**, **Parole**, and **Sentencing Discretion**.

## 🚔 Police System & Reforms

### 4. Prakash Singh v. Union of India (2006)
*   **Facts**: A public interest litigation filed by retired police officer Prakash Singh sought the implementation of various National Police Commission recommendations to reform police administration.
*   **Issue**: Should the police force be insulated from political interference to ensure accountability and professional integrity?
*   **Held**: The Supreme Court issued seven binding directives to all states and the union. These included establishing a State Security Commission to prevent political interference, ensuring a fixed 2-year tenure for the DGP and operational field officers, separating investigation duties from law & order, and establishing Police Complaints Authorities.
*   **Exam Relevance**: **Mandatory citation** for any question on **Police Reforms** or **Police System in India**.

### 5. D.K. Basu v. State of West Bengal (1997)
*   **Facts**: A public interest petition brought before the court highlighted recurring incidents of custodial violence, torture, and deaths in police custody.
*   **Issue**: What safeguards are required to prevent custodial violence and protect the rights of arrested persons under Article 21?
*   **Held**: The Supreme Court laid down 11 mandatory guidelines governing arrests. These include requiring arresting officers to wear clear name tags, preparing a formal memo of arrest witnessed by a family member, and mandating a medical examination of the arrested person every 48 hours.
*   **Exam Relevance**: Cite in questions on **Police Administration**, **Custodial Violence**, and **Fundamental Rights of Accused Persons**.

### 6. Lalita Kumari v. Government of U.P. (2014)
*   **Facts**: A petition challenged police inaction and refusal to register an FIR in a cognizable offense, evaluating the scope of police discretion under Section 154 of the CrPC.
*   **Issue**: Is the registration of an FIR mandatory for police when they receive information disclosing a cognizable offense?
*   **Held**: The Supreme Court held that the registration of an FIR under Section 154 of the CrPC is **mandatory** if the information discloses a cognizable offense, leaving no discretion with the police to conduct a preliminary inquiry.
*   **Exam Relevance**: Cite in questions on the **Legal Functions of Police** and **Criminal Procedure**.

## 🏢 Prison Reforms & Parole

### 7. Sunil Batra v. Delhi Administration (1980)
*   **Facts**: A prisoner sent a letter to a Supreme Court judge detailing the brutal physical torture of a fellow inmate in Tihar Jail. The Court treated the letter as a habeas corpus petition.
*   **Issue**: Do prisoners retain their fundamental rights behind prison walls, and can the judiciary intervene in prison administration?
*   **Held**: The Supreme Court held that **"prisons are not places to bury human rights."** The Court ruled that prisoners retain all fundamental rights under Articles 14, 19, and 21, except those necessarily restricted by the fact of confinement. The Court prohibited the use of bar-fetters and solitary confinement without judicial approval.
*   **Exam Relevance**: **Mandatory citation** for questions on **Prison Reforms**, **Prisoners' Rights**, and the **Role of Prisons in Modern Penology**.

### 8. Hussainara Khatoon v. State of Bihar (1979)
*   **Facts**: A public interest litigation exposed the plight of thousands of under-trial prisoners in Bihar who were languishing in jail for periods exceeding the maximum sentence for their alleged offenses.
*   **Issue**: Does the right to a speedy trial constitute a fundamental right under Article 21?
*   **Held**: The Supreme Court held that the **right to a speedy trial** is a fundamental right implicit under Article 21. The Court directed the immediate release of all under-trials who had spent longer in prison awaiting trial than the maximum sentence for their alleged offenses.
*   **Exam Relevance**: Cite in questions on **Problems of Prisons**, **Under-trial Prisoners**, and **Access to Justice**.

### 9. Ramamurthy v. State of Karnataka (1997)
*   **Facts**: A prisoner wrote a letter to the Court highlighting the deplorable conditions, overcrowding, and lack of basic amenities in Bengaluru Central Jail.
*   **Issue**: What are the primary systemic problems in Indian prisons, and how can they be addressed?
*   **Held**: The Supreme Court synthesized the jurisprudence of prison reforms, identifying nine major issues (including overcrowding, lack of hygiene, and inadequate medical care) and directing all states to implement the Mulla Committee recommendations.
*   **Exam Relevance**: Cite in questions on **Prison Reforms**, **Problems of Jails**, and the **Jurisprudence of Corrections**.

### 10. Asfaq v. State of Haryana (2017)
*   **Facts**: A prisoner challenged the arbitrary rejection of his parole application by state administrative authorities.
*   **Issue**: What is the key distinction between parole and furlough, and what principles govern the grant of parole?
*   **Held**: The Supreme Court clarified the distinction. The Court held that parole is a discretionary privilege granted for specific emergencies, whereas furlough is a routine home leave right. The Court held that parole should not be denied arbitrarily based on vague police reports, emphasizing that rehabilitation requires a humane approach.
*   **Exam Relevance**: **Mandatory citation** for questions on **Parole**, **Furlough**, and **Alternatives to Incarceration**.

## 👥 Victimology & Assistance

### 11. Rudal Shah v. State of Bihar (1983)
*   **Facts**: Rudal Shah was kept in illegal confinement for 14 years after being formally acquitted by a competent court. He filed a habeas corpus petition seeking release and compensation.
*   **Issue**: Can the Supreme Court award monetary compensation under Article 32 for the violation of a citizen's fundamental rights?
*   **Held**: The Supreme Court introduced **compensatory jurisprudence for fundamental rights violations**. The Court directed the state government to pay Rudal Shah Rs. 35,000 in interim compensation, holding that Article 21 empowers courts to award financial relief for state excesses.
*   **Exam Relevance**: **Mandatory citation** for questions on **Victim Compensation**, **Access to Justice**, and **State Liability**.

### 12. Ankush Shivaji Gaikwad v. State of Maharashtra (2013)
*   **Facts**: The accused was convicted of homicide, and the court evaluated the application of victim compensation provisions under Section 357 of the CrPC.
*   **Issue**: Is the power of courts to award victim compensation under Section 357 discretionary or mandatory?
*   **Held**: The Supreme Court held that the power of courts to award compensation under Section 357 of the CrPC is **mandatory**, not discretionary. Courts must evaluate and record reasons in every criminal judgment explaining why they did or did not award compensation.
*   **Exam Relevance**: Cite in questions on **Victim Assistance**, **Section 357 CrPC**, and **Sentencing Policies**.

### 13. Mallikarjun Kodagali v. State of Karnataka (2018)
*   **Facts**: The victim challenged an acquittal order, and the court evaluated the procedural requirements for a victim to file an appeal.
*   **Issue**: Is a victim's right to appeal under Section 372 of the CrPC absolute, or does it require prior permission from the court?
*   **Held**: The Supreme Court held that the right of a victim to prefer an appeal under the proviso to Section 372 of the CrPC is a **real, substantive, and unrestricted right** that does not require prior permission from the High Court, ensuring direct access to justice.
*   **Exam Relevance**: Cite in questions on **Rights of Victims during Trial** and **Access to Justice**.

### 14. Laxmi v. Union of India (2014)
*   **Facts**: A public interest litigation was filed by an acid attack survivor seeking regulations on acid sales and rehabilitation for victims.
*   **Issue**: What measures are required to protect and compensate victims of acid attacks?
*   **Held**: The Supreme Court regulated the sale of acid, established a minimum compensation of Rs. 3 Lakhs for acid attack victims, and mandated that all public and private hospitals provide free medical treatment and reconstructive surgeries to survivors.
*   **Exam Relevance**: Cite in questions on **Victim Assistance Programs** and the **Physical and Economic Impact of Victimization**.

### 15. Delhi Domestic Working Women's Forum v. Union of India (1995)
*   **Facts**: A petition brought before the court highlighted the secondary trauma and lack of legal support faced by domestic workers who were victims of sexual assault.
*   **Issue**: How can courts mitigate the impact of secondary victimization and ensure support for rape victims during trials?
*   **Held**: The Supreme Court recognized the severe impact of secondary victimization during trials. The Court directed the establishment of a state-funded legal aid and counseling scheme to support victims through the criminal justice process.
*   **Exam Relevance**: Cite in questions on **Secondary Victimization** and **Victim Assistance Programs**.
