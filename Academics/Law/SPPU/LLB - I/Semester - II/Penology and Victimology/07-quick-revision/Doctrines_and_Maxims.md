# ⚡ Quick Revision: Doctrines, Maxims, & Section Checklist

This cheat sheet is designed for last-minute review, collecting key doctrines, statutory sections, and mnemonics in one place.

---

## 🏛️ Key Doctrines & Maxims

*   ***Lex Talionis***: The ancient doctrine of retaliation ("An eye for an eye, a tooth for a tooth"), forming the philosophical foundation of the **Retributive Theory of Punishment**.
*   ***Danda***: The ancient Indian penal philosophy (detailed by Manu and Kautilya) that views punishment as a divine tool to maintain *Dharma* (righteousness) and preserve social order.
*   **"Rarest of Rare Cases"**: The judicial doctrine established in *Bachan Singh v. State of Punjab (1980)*, restricting the death penalty to exceptional crimes where the alternative of life imprisonment is unquestionably foreclosed.
*   **Compensatory Jurisprudence**: The legal principle introduced in *Rudal Shah v. State of Bihar (1983)*, establishing that courts have the power to award financial compensation for the violation of fundamental rights under Article 21.
*   **Secondary / Double Victimization**: The additional trauma caused to victims of crime by insensitive police questioning, invasive medical exams, aggressive cross-examination, and media intrusion during the justice process.
*   **Separation of Investigation and Law & Order**: The key administrative reform directed in *Prakash Singh v. UOI (2006)* to improve investigation quality and reduce political interference.

## 📖 Key Statutory Sections Checklist

### 1. Indian Penal Code (IPC) / BNS
- [ ] **Section 53 IPC / BNS**: Prescribes the six forms of punishment in India (Death, Life Imprisonment, Imprisonment, Forfeiture, Fine, and Community Service).
- [ ] **Section 228A IPC / BNS**: Prohibits disclosing the identity of victims of certain offenses (such as sexual assault) to prevent social stigma.

### 2. Code of Criminal Procedure (CrPC) / BNSS
- [ ] **Section 24 CrPC**: Governs the appointment of Public Prosecutors to represent the State.
- [ ] **Section 41 CrPC**: Governs police powers to arrest suspects without a warrant, subject to *D.K. Basu* guidelines.
- [ ] **Section 154 CrPC**: Governs the registration of an FIR, made mandatory for cognizable offenses under *Lalita Kumari*.
- [ ] **Section 327 CrPC**: Mandates in-camera trials (closed to the public) for sexual offenses to protect victim privacy.
- [ ] **Section 357 CrPC**: Empowers courts to direct the offender to pay compensation to the victim out of any fine imposed.
- [ ] **Section 357A CrPC**: Mandates that states establish Victim Compensation Schemes administered by the DLSA.
- [ ] **Section 372 CrPC Proviso**: Grants the victim a direct right to appeal against acquittals, lesser convictions, or inadequate compensation.

### 3. Special Acts
- [ ] **Section 5 of the Probation of Offenders Act, 1958**: Empowers courts releasing an offender on probation to direct them to pay reasonable compensation and legal costs to the victim.
