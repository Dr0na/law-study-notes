# Module 6: Structure, Powers and Functions of Union and State Executive

## Introduction

India follows the **Parliamentary system** of government (also called Westminster model), where the Executive is responsible to the Legislature. The Executive implements laws, runs day-to-day administration, and formulates policies. This module covers both Union and State Executives.

---

## PART A: UNION EXECUTIVE

The Union Executive consists of:
1. **President** (Head of State - Nominal Executive)
2. **Vice-President**
3. **Prime Minister** (Head of Government - Real Executive)
4. **Council of Ministers** (Real Executive)

---

## 1. THE PRESIDENT OF INDIA

### A. Appointment/Election (Article 54-57)

**How is President Elected?**

**Indirect Election:**
- Not directly elected by people
- Elected by **Electoral College**

**Electoral College consists of (Article 54):**
1. **Elected members** of both Houses of Parliament (MPs)
2. **Elected members** of Legislative Assemblies of all States (MLAs)

**Why This System?**
- Represents both Union and States
- Ensures President is acceptable to entire nation
- MPs + MLAs = truly national figure

**Method of Election (Article 55):**
- **Proportional Representation**
- **Single Transferable Vote**
- **Secret Ballot**

**Value of Vote Formula:**
- **MLA's vote value** = (Total population of State / Total elected MLAs of State) ÷ 1000
- **MP's vote value** = (Total value of all MLAs' votes / Total elected MPs) ÷ 1
- Ensures balance between States and Parliament

**Example:**
- If a state has population 50,000,000 and 200 MLAs
- Each MLA's vote value = 50,000,000 ÷ 200 ÷ 1000 = 250

**To Win:**
- Candidate must secure **50% + 1** of valid votes
- If no one gets, lowest candidate eliminated
- Second preference votes counted
- Process continues till someone gets majority

### B. Qualifications (Article 58)

**To be President, a person must:**
1. Be **citizen of India**
2. Be **at least 35 years** old
3. Be **qualified to be elected** as Lok Sabha member
4. **Not hold any office of profit** under Union/State/Local authority

### C. Term of Office (Article 56)

**Term:**
- **5 years** from date of assuming office
- Can be re-elected (no limit on re-election)
- Can resign before 5 years (to Vice-President)
- Can be removed by impeachment

**Examples:**
- **Dr. Rajendra Prasad**: Served 2 full terms (1950-1962)
- Most Presidents serve one term
- Some complete less than 5 years due to death or resignation

### D. Removal - Impeachment (Article 61)

**Grounds:**
- **Violation of Constitution**

**Procedure:**
1. Impeachment motion initiated in **either House**
2. Signed by **1/4th members** of that House
3. **14 days' notice** given to President
4. Passed by **2/3rd majority** of total membership of that House
5. Goes to **other House** for investigation
6. Other House investigates (like a trial)
7. If charges proved, passed by **2/3rd majority**
8. President **removed** from office

**No President has been impeached so far**

### E. Oath (Article 60)
- Administered by Chief Justice of India
- Oath to preserve, protect, and defend Constitution

### F. Immunity (Article 361)
- **Criminal proceedings**: No criminal proceedings during term
- **Civil proceedings**: Not answerable for official acts
- **After term**: Can be prosecuted for personal acts (not official acts)

## 2. Powers and Functions of President

### A. EXECUTIVE POWERS (Article 53)

**1. Appointment Powers:**
- Prime Minister and Council of Ministers
- Attorney General of India
- Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG)
- Chief Election Commissioner and Election Commissioners
- Judges of Supreme Court and High Courts
- Governors of States
- Chairman and Members of UPSC
- Finance Commission
- All major appointments

**2. Administrative Powers:**
- All executive actions taken in President's name
- Makes rules for conduct of government business
- Allocates portfolios to Ministers
- Appoints commissions and committees

**3. Military Powers:**
- Supreme Commander of Defense Forces
- Can declare war or peace (in practice, Cabinet decides)

**4. Diplomatic Powers:**
- Represents India internationally
- Appoints ambassadors and diplomats
- Receives credentials of foreign ambassadors

### B. LEGISLATIVE POWERS

**1. Summoning and Proroguing (Article 85):**
- Summons Parliament
- Prorogues sessions
- Can dissolve Lok Sabha

**2. Address to Parliament (Article 87):**
- Addresses joint session at beginning of first session after elections
- Addresses first session of each year
- Outlines government's policy and programs

**3. Messages (Article 86):**
- Can send messages to either House
- Can address either House

**4. Assent to Bills (Article 111):**
- Bills need President's assent to become law
- Can give assent, withhold assent, or return for reconsideration
- Pocket veto possible

**5. Ordinance-Making Power (Article 123):**
- When Parliament is not in session
- If immediate action needed
- Can promulgate Ordinance
- Has same force as law
- Must be approved by Parliament within **6 weeks** of reassembly
- Can be withdrawn anytime

**Limitations on Ordinance Power:**
- Cannot violate Fundamental Rights
- Cannot be issued on State subjects (except during President's Rule)
- Not a substitute for legislation
- **D.C. Wadhwa case (1987)**: Excessive use of ordinances is unconstitutional

**6. Nominates Members (Article 80, 331):**
- 12 members to Rajya Sabha
- 2 Anglo-Indians to Lok Sabha (provision lapsed in 2020)

**7. Recommendations:**
- President's recommendation needed for:
  - Money Bills
  - Bills affecting taxation
  - Bills for creation of new States

### C. FINANCIAL POWERS

1. **Budget** (Article 112):
   - Annual Financial Statement (Budget) laid before Parliament in President's name
   - No tax without President's recommendation

2. **Contingency Fund** (Article 267):
   - President controls Contingency Fund of India
   - For urgent unforeseen expenses

3. **Finance Commission** (Article 280):
   - President constitutes every 5 years

### D. JUDICIAL POWERS

**1. Appointment of Judges:**
- Supreme Court judges
- High Court judges

**2. Pardon Powers (Article 72):**

Can grant:
- **Pardon**: Complete forgiveness (conviction + sentence removed)
- **Commutation**: Reduce sentence (death to life imprisonment)
- **Remission**: Reduce sentence period
- **Respite**: Delay execution (temporary)
- **Reprieve**: Stay execution (for some time)

**Extent:**
- All offenses against Union law
- Death sentences (even under State law)
- Court-martial cases

**Governor's Similar Power (Article 161):**
- For State law offenses
- Cannot pardon death sentences
- Cannot pardon court-martial cases

**President's Power is Wider**

**Case**: **Kehar Singh v. Union of India (1989)**
- Kehar Singh challenged rejection of his mercy petition
- Held: President's pardon power is absolute but can be judicially reviewed for malafide

**3. Seeks Advice from Supreme Court (Article 143)**

### E. EMERGENCY POWERS

**1. National Emergency (Article 352)**
**2. State Emergency/President's Rule (Article 356)**
**3. Financial Emergency (Article 360)**

(Detailed in Module 8)

## 3. Position of President

### Constitutional Head vs Real Executive

**President is:**
- **Constitutional/Nominal Head** of State
- **De jure** (by law) executive
- Formal head

**President is NOT:**
- Real executive
- **De facto** (in reality) executive is Prime Minister and Council of Ministers

**President acts on "Aid and Advice":**

**Article 74:**
- President shall act on **aid and advice** of Council of Ministers
- Advice is **binding** on President
- President cannot act independently

**42nd Amendment (1976):**
- Added: President **SHALL** act on advice (made binding)

**44th Amendment (1978):**
- President can ask Council to **reconsider** advice
- But if Council sends same advice again, President **must accept**

### When Can President Act Independently?

**Very Limited Situations:**

1. **Appointment of Prime Minister** (when no clear majority)
2. **Dismissal of Government** (in exceptional cases)
3. **Dissolution of Lok Sabha** (if no alternative government possible)
4. **Seeking information** from PM about administration
5. **Return of bill** for reconsideration (once)
6. **Seeking reconsideration** of advice (44th Amendment)

**Case**: **Samsher Singh v. State of Punjab (1974)**
- Held: President has no independent powers
- Must act on advice of Council of Ministers

## 4. VICE-PRESIDENT OF INDIA

### A. Election (Article 63-66)

**Electoral College:**
- Members of both Houses of Parliament (elected + nominated)
- Only MPs vote (not MLAs)

**Method:**
- Proportional representation
- Single transferable vote
- Secret ballot

### B. Qualifications (Article 66)

1. Be **citizen of India**
2. Be **at least 35 years** old
3. Be **qualified for Rajya Sabha**
4. Not hold office of profit

### C. Term
- **5 years**
- Can be re-elected
- Removal: Same as President (impeachment) - but simpler resolution

### D. Functions

**Dual Role:**

**1. Ex-officio Chairman of Rajya Sabha:**
- Presides over Rajya Sabha
- Conducts proceedings
- Gets salary as Chairman (not as Vice-President)

**2. Acts as President:**
- When President is unable to discharge functions
- When President's post is vacant
- Discharges President's functions temporarily
- Does not become President

### E. Vacancy
- If both President and Vice-President offices vacant
- Chief Justice of India (or senior-most SC judge) acts as President

## 5. PRIME MINISTER AND COUNCIL OF MINISTERS

### A. Prime Minister - Real Executive

**Appointment (Article 75):**
- Appointed by President
- Usually leader of majority party in Lok Sabha
- If no clear majority, President uses discretion

**Qualifications:**
- Must be member of Parliament (Lok Sabha or Rajya Sabha)
- If not already a member, must become within 6 months

**Term:**
- No fixed term
- Continues as long as he/she enjoys majority in Lok Sabha
- Or resigns
- Or removed by no-confidence motion

**Powers of Prime Minister:**

**1. Formation of Government:**
- Selects Ministers
- Recommends to President for appointment
- Allocates portfolios

**2. Head of Council of Ministers:**
- Presides over Cabinet meetings
- Coordinates work of ministries

**3. Link Between President and Cabinet:**
- Communicates Cabinet decisions to President
- Conveys President's messages to Cabinet
- Principal channel of communication

**4. Chief Advisor to President:**
- President acts on PM's advice
- PM's position most powerful

**5. Leader of Lok Sabha:**
- Leader of majority party
- Controls parliamentary proceedings (through party)

**6. Controls Appointments:**
- Key appointments made on PM's advice
- Ministers, Governors, CJI, etc.

**7. Controls Dissolution:**
- Advises President to dissolve Lok Sabha
- President usually accepts

### B. Council of Ministers (Article 74-75)

**Composition:**

**Three Categories:**

1. **Cabinet Ministers**:
   - Senior-most ministers
   - Head important ministries (Home, Finance, Defense, External Affairs)
   - Attend Cabinet meetings
   - Make major policy decisions
   - Usually 15-20 members

2. **Ministers of State (MoS)**:
   - Junior ministers
   - May have independent charge of ministry OR
   - Assist Cabinet Minister
   - Don't attend Cabinet unless invited

3. **Deputy Ministers**:
   - Assist Ministers of State or Cabinet Ministers
   - Do not attend Cabinet

**Collective Responsibility (Article 75(3)):**
- Council of Ministers **collectively responsible** to Lok Sabha
- If Lok Sabha passes no-confidence motion, entire Council must resign
- Cannot have individual opinions publicly against Cabinet decisions
- Must support all Cabinet decisions

**Individual Responsibility:**
- Each Minister responsible for his/her ministry
- Answers questions in Parliament
- Can be removed by PM

### C. Appointment and Term

**Appointment:**
- President appoints on PM's advice
- Must be members of Parliament
- If not, must become within **6 months**

**Term:**
- Holds office during President's pleasure
- In practice, during PM's pleasure
- PM can ask President to dismiss any Minister

**Oath (Article 75):**
- Oath of office
- Oath of secrecy
- Administered by President

**Salaries:**
- Fixed by Parliament
- Charged on Consolidated Fund

### D. Cabinet (Not mentioned in Constitution)

**Cabinet is a smaller body:**
- Consists of Cabinet Ministers only
- PM + senior Ministers
- Makes major policy decisions
- **Kitchen Cabinet**: Informal inner circle of PM

**Importance:**
- **Real decision-making body**
- Formulates policies
- Decides on legislation
- Handles crisis situations

## PART B: STATE EXECUTIVE

State Executive consists of:
1. **Governor** (Nominal Executive)
2. **Chief Minister** (Real Executive)
3. **Council of Ministers** (Real Executive)

---

## 2. GOVERNOR OF STATE

### A. Appointment (Article 155-156)

**Appointed by President:**
- Not elected
- President's discretion (in consultation with PM)
- **No specified qualifications** (in Constitution)

**Convention:**
- Should be from outside the State
- Should be eminent person
- Should be politically neutral
- Should not be active politician

**Recently:**
- Often retired judges, civil servants, or politicians appointed
- Same Governor for multiple States possible

### B. Qualifications (Article 157)

**Must be:**
1. **Citizen of India**
2. **At least 35 years** old
3. **Not a member** of Parliament or State Legislature
4. **Not hold office of profit**

### C. Term (Article 156)

**Normal Term:**
- **5 years** from date of assuming office
- Can be re-appointed

**Can be Removed:**
- Holds office during President's pleasure
- Can be removed anytime by President
- **No fixed tenure guarantee**
- **No impeachment required**

**Can Resign:**
- Can resign before 5 years (to President)

**Controversy:**
- Presidents have removed Governors arbitrarily
- When new government comes to power at Centre, Governors changed
- Raises questions about independence

**Case**: **B.P. Singhal v. Union of India (2010)**
- Governors cannot be removed arbitrarily
- Reasons should be communicated
- Opportunity of hearing should be given (in some cases)

### D. Powers and Functions of Governor

**Similar to President but at State level:**

#### **1. Executive Powers (Article 154):**
- Appoints Chief Minister
- Appoints Council of Ministers (on CM's advice)
- Appoints Advocate General
- Appoints State Public Service Commission members
- Appoints Vice-Chancellors of universities
- All executive actions in Governor's name

#### **2. Legislative Powers:**
- Summons and prorogues State Legislature
- Dissolves Legislative Assembly
- Addresses Legislature
- Assent to State Bills (Article 200):
  - Give assent
  - Withhold assent
  - Reserve for President (compulsory for some bills)
  - Return for reconsideration (once)
- **Ordinance power** (Article 213) - when Legislature not in session

#### **3. Financial Powers:**
- Budget presented in Governor's name
- Approves demands for grants
- Constitutes State Finance Commission
- Controls Contingency Fund

#### **4. Judicial Powers:**
- Appoints District Judges (in consultation with High Court)
- **Pardon power** (Article 161):
  - For State law offenses
  - Cannot pardon death sentence
  - Cannot pardon court-martial

#### **5. Discretionary Powers:**

**Governor can act independently (without CM's advice) in:**

1. **Appointment of CM** (if no clear majority)
2. **Dismissal of CM** (if lost majority)
3. **Dissolution of Assembly** (in exceptional cases)
4. **Reserving bills** for President's assent
5. **Recommending President's Rule** (Article 356)
6. **Special provisions** for certain States (e.g., Nagaland, Sikkim)

**Case**: **Nabam Rebia v. Deputy Speaker (2016)**
- Governor cannot dismiss government arbitrarily
- Must follow proper procedure
- Governor cannot act partisan

### E. Position of Governor

**Constitutional vs Real Position:**

**Article 163:**
- Governor shall act on aid and advice of Council of Ministers
- Except in matters where Governor can act in discretion

**Controversies:**
- Extent of discretionary powers debated
- Some Governors act partisan
- Centre uses Governors to control States (allegation)
- Appointment and removal create dependency on Centre

**Sarkaria Commission Recommendations:**
- Governor should be eminent person
- From outside State
- Non-political
- Fixed tenure
- Removal only for valid reasons

## 3. CHIEF MINISTER AND COUNCIL OF MINISTERS

### A. Chief Minister - Real Executive of State

**Appointment (Article 164):**
- Appointed by Governor
- Usually leader of majority party in Assembly
- If no clear majority, Governor's discretion

**Qualifications:**
- Must be member of State Legislature
- If not, must become within **6 months**

**Powers:**

**1. Formation of Government:**
- Selects Ministers
- Recommends to Governor

**2. Head of Council:**
- Presides over Council meetings
- Coordinates ministries

**3. Link Between Governor and Council:**
- Communicates decisions to Governor
- Chief advisor to Governor

**4. Leader of Assembly:**
- Controls House proceedings
- Must have majority support

**5. Policy Formulation:**
- Makes state policies
- Implements state programs

### B. Council of Ministers (Article 163)

**Composition:**
- Cabinet Ministers
- Ministers of State
- Deputy Ministers

**Collective Responsibility (Article 164(2)):**
- Collectively responsible to Legislative Assembly
- No-confidence motion can remove entire Council

**Individual Responsibility:**
- Each Minister responsible for his/her department

**Size:**
- **91st Amendment (2003)**: Council size cannot exceed **15% of Assembly strength**
- Minimum: 12 Ministers
- Aims to prevent large Councils

**Appointment and Term:**
- Appointed by Governor on CM's advice
- Hold office during Governor's pleasure (in practice, CM's pleasure)
- Must be members of Legislature (or become within 6 months)

## 4. Interaction Between Executive and Legislature

### How They Work Together:

### A. **Parliament Controls Executive**

**1. Responsible Government:**
- Executive responsible to Legislature
- Lok Sabha can remove government by no-confidence motion

**2. Question Hour:**
- Ministers answer questions
- Ensures accountability

**3. Debates:**
- Discussions on government policies
- Adjournment motions to discuss urgent matters

**4. Committees:**
- Parliamentary committees scrutinize executive actions
- PAC, Estimates Committee, etc.

**5. Budget Control:**
- Parliament approves all spending
- Can reduce/reject demands

**6. Legislative Control:**
- All executive rules subject to Parliament
- Delegated legislation can be scrutinized

### B. **Executive Controls Legislature**

**1. Majority in House:**
- Executive is formed by majority party
- Controls House proceedings

**2. Legislative Agenda:**
- Government decides what bills to introduce
- Controls parliamentary time

**3. Dissolution:**
- PM can advise President to dissolve Lok Sabha
- Can call fresh elections

**4. Party Whip:**
- Ministers are party leaders
- Control party members through whip

---

## 5. Conduct of Government Business

### Rules of Business (Article 77)

**Article 77(3):**
- President makes rules for conduct of business
- Allocates business among Ministers
- Procedures for disposing of business

### Cabinet System:

**Decision Making:**
1. **Cabinet meetings** held regularly
2. PM presides
3. Major decisions taken in Cabinet
4. Individual Ministers implement in their departments

**Cabinet Secretariat:**
- Coordinates between ministries
- Maintains records
- Cabinet Secretary is head

### Collective Responsibility:

**What it Means:**
- All Ministers must support Cabinet decisions
- Cannot criticize publicly
- Must resign if disagree fundamentally
- Speak with one voice

**If No-Confidence Passed:**
- Entire Council must resign
- OR
- PM can advise President to dissolve Lok Sabha and hold elections

## Comparison: President vs Governor

| Feature | President | Governor |
|---------|-----------|----------|
| **Method** | Elected (indirect) | Appointed by President |
| **Electoral College** | MPs + MLAs | None |
| **Term** | 5 years (secure) | 5 years (during President's pleasure) |
| **Removal** | Impeachment (difficult) | President's pleasure (easy) |
| **Discretion** | Very limited | More (in some matters) |
| **Independent** | Relatively | Less (Central appointee) |
| **Ordinance** | Article 123 | Article 213 |
| **Pardon** | Death + all offenses | State offenses (not death) |

---

## Comparison: Prime Minister vs Chief Minister

| Feature | Prime Minister | Chief Minister |
|---------|---------------|----------------|
| **Appointed by** | President | Governor |
| **Leader of** | Lok Sabha | Legislative Assembly |
| **Council responsible to** | Lok Sabha | Legislative Assembly |
| **Removal** | No-confidence in Lok Sabha | No-confidence in Assembly |
| **Powers** | Wider (national) | Limited (state) |
| **Ordinance** | President promulgates | Governor promulgates |
| **Discretion** | Very less (President bound) | Less (Governor has some) |

## Important Cases

### 1. **Ram Jawaya Kapur v. State of Punjab (1955)**
- Held: All executive actions must be taken in name of President/Governor
- This is mandatory requirement

### 2. **Shamsher Singh v. State of Punjab (1974)**
- Held: President has no independent powers
- Must act on aid and advice of Council of Ministers

### 3. **S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994)**
- Limits on Article 356 (President's Rule)
- Governor cannot act partisan
- President's Rule subject to judicial review

### 4. **Nabam Rebia v. Deputy Speaker (2016)**
- Governor cannot dismiss government arbitrarily
- Cannot aid horse-trading
- Must follow constitutional procedures

## Examination Tips

### For Executive Questions:

**15-Mark Question Pattern:**
1. Explain appointment procedure (3 marks)
2. Qualifications and term (2 marks)
3. Powers and functions (6 marks)
4. Position and discretionary powers (3 marks)
5. One case (1 mark)

**For PM/CM Questions:**
- Explain appointment
- Powers
- Relationship with President/Governor
- Collective responsibility
- One case

### Important Points:
- President elected, Governor appointed
- Article 74: Aid and advice binding
- Collective responsibility: Article 75(3)
- No-confidence: Only in Lok Sabha/Assembly
- Ordinance: Articles 123, 213
- Pardon: Articles 72, 161

## Quick Revision Points

✅ **President**: Elected, 5-year term, 35 years age, Electoral College = MPs + MLAs

✅ **Article 74**: President acts on aid and advice (binding)  
✅ **Collective Responsibility**: Article 75(3) - to Lok Sabha/Assembly  
✅ **Ordinance**: Article 123 (President), Article 213 (Governor)  
✅ **Pardon**: Article 72 (President), Article 161 (Governor)  
✅ **No-confidence**: Only against Council of Ministers
