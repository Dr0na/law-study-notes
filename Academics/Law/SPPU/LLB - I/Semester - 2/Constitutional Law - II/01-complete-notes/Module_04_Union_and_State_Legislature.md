# Module 4: Structure, Powers and Functions of Union and State Legislature

## Introduction

The Legislature is the law-making body in a democracy. India has a **Parliamentary system** of government (borrowed from Britain) where the Executive is responsible to the Legislature. This module covers the structure, powers, and functioning of both Union Parliament and State Legislatures.

---

## PART A: UNION LEGISLATURE (PARLIAMENT)

## 1. Bicameral Legislature

India has a **bicameral** (two-house) legislature at the Union level, called **Parliament**.

### Why Bicameral System?

**Advantages:**
1. **Second thought**: Rajya Sabha reviews laws passed by Lok Sabha
2. **Represents States**: Rajya Sabha gives states a voice
3. **Experts included**: Nominated members bring expertise
4. **Prevents hasty legislation**: Two houses mean more deliberation
5. **Continuous body**: Rajya Sabha never dissolves completely

### The Two Houses:

| Feature | Lok Sabha | Rajya Sabha |
|---------|-----------|-------------|
| **Meaning** | House of the People | Council of States |
| **Nature** | Lower House | Upper House |
| **Members** | Maximum 552 | Maximum 250 |
| **Representation** | Represents people directly | Represents States |
| **Term** | 5 years (can be dissolved earlier) | Permanent (1/3 retires every 2 years) |
| **Election** | Direct election by people | Indirect election by State MLAs |
| **Minimum Age** | 25 years | 30 years |
| **Presiding Officer** | Speaker | Chairman (Vice-President of India) |
| **Powers** | More powerful | Less powerful |
| **Money Bills** | Can originate | Cannot originate, can only recommend |

## 2. Composition of Union Legislature

### Article 79: Parliament Composition

Parliament consists of:
1. The President of India
2. Lok Sabha (House of the People)
3. Rajya Sabha (Council of States)

### A. LOK SABHA (House of the People)

#### Composition (Article 81):

**Maximum Strength: 552**
- **530 members**: Representing States (directly elected)
- **20 members**: Representing Union Territories
- **2 members**: Anglo-Indians (nominated by President) - *This provision lapsed in 2020*

**Current Strength (as of 2024): 543 elected members**

#### Allocation of Seats:
- Each State gets seats proportional to its population
- **Delimitation Commission** fixes constituencies
- **Reservation**: For Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes based on population

#### Term:
- **5 years** from date of first sitting
- Can be **dissolved earlier** by President (on PM's advice)
- Can be **extended during emergency** (by 1 year at a time, max extension not to exceed 6 months after emergency ends)

#### Qualifications for Membership (Article 84):
1. Must be **citizen of India**
2. Must be **at least 25 years** old
3. Must be **registered as voter** in any parliamentary constituency
4. Must take **oath** as per Third Schedule
5. Not disqualified under Article 102 or any law

#### Disqualifications (Article 102):
1. Holds **office of profit** under Union/State (except Ministers)
2. Is of **unsound mind** (declared by court)
3. **Undischarged insolvent**
4. Not a **citizen** or voluntarily acquired foreign citizenship
5. **Disqualified by Parliament law** (e.g., Representation of People Act)

**Additional Disqualifications (RPA 1951):**
- Convicted of certain offenses
- Corrupt practices in elections
- Failed to file election expenses
- Involvement in unlawful activities

#### Presiding Officer: **Speaker**
- Elected by Lok Sabha members
- Conducts proceedings
- Maintains order and discipline
- **Deputy Speaker** also elected

### B. RAJYA SABHA (Council of States)

#### Composition (Article 80):

**Maximum Strength: 250**
- **238 members**: Representatives of States and UTs (indirectly elected)
- **12 members**: Nominated by President (eminent persons in literature, science, art, social service)

**Current Strength: 245**

#### Allocation of Seats:
- Each State gets seats roughly proportional to population
- **Elected by elected members of State Legislative Assemblies**
- Election by **proportional representation** (single transferable vote)

**Union Territories with Representation:**
- Delhi and Puducherry have elected representatives
- Other UTs don't have representation (too small)

#### Term:
- **Permanent body** (never dissolves)
- **1/3rd members retire every 2 years**
- Each member has **6-year term**
- Can be re-elected

#### Qualifications (Article 84):
- Must be **citizen of India**
- Must be **at least 30 years** old
- Must be registered voter
- Must take oath
- Not disqualified

#### Presiding Officer: **Chairman**
- **Vice-President of India** is ex-officio Chairman
- When Vice-President acts as President, doesn't preside over Rajya Sabha
- **Deputy Chairman** elected by Rajya Sabha members

### C. Nominated Members (Article 80)

**President nominates 12 members** having special knowledge or practical experience in:
1. Literature
2. Science
3. Art
4. Social Service

**Examples of Nominated Members:**
- Sachin Tendulkar (Sports - debated as not originally intended)
- Mary Kom (Boxing)
- Swapan Dasgupta (Journalism)
- Dr. Subramanian Swamy (Economics)

## 3. Powers and Functions of Parliament

### A. LEGISLATIVE POWERS

**What Laws Can Parliament Make?**
1. Laws on **Union List** subjects
2. Laws on **Concurrent List** subjects
3. Laws on **Residuary subjects** (Article 248)
4. Laws on **State subjects** in special circumstances (Articles 249, 250, 252, 253, 356)

**Types of Bills:**

#### 1. **Ordinary Bills** (Non-financial)
- Can be introduced in either House
- Needs approval of both Houses
- President's assent required
- Becomes law after assent

#### 2. **Money Bills** (Article 110)
- Can ONLY be introduced in **Lok Sabha**
- Rajya Sabha can only make recommendations (within 14 days)
- Lok Sabha may accept or reject recommendations
- **Rajya Sabha cannot reject or amend**
- Speaker certifies if a bill is Money Bill

**What is a Money Bill?**
Contains ONLY provisions related to:
- Taxation
- Borrowing by Government
- Consolidated Fund or Contingency Fund
- Custody of these Funds
- Audit of government accounts

#### 3. **Financial Bills** (Article 117)
- Deals with money matters but NOT a Money Bill
- Can be introduced in either House
- Both Houses have equal powers
- Requires President's recommendation

#### 4. **Constitutional Amendment Bills** (Article 368)
- To amend the Constitution
- Can be introduced in either House
- Requires special majority (2/3rd present + majority of total)
- Some amendments also need State ratification

### B. EXECUTIVE CONTROL POWERS

**Parliament Controls the Executive (Council of Ministers):**

1. **Question Hour:**
   - Members ask questions
   - Ministers must answer
   - Types: Starred (oral), Unstarred (written), Short notice

2. **Zero Hour:**
   - After Question Hour
   - Members raise urgent matters
   - No prior notice needed

3. **Motions:**
   - **No-Confidence Motion**: Can remove government (only in Lok Sabha)
   - **Adjournment Motion**: Discuss urgent public matter
   - **Censure Motion**: Criticize government
   - **Cut Motions**: Reduce budget demands

4. **Debates and Discussions:**
   - Budget debate
   - Policy discussions
   - General debates

5. **Committees:**
   - **Public Accounts Committee**: Examines government spending
   - **Estimates Committee**: Scrutinizes budget estimates
   - **Standing Committees**: Subject-wise scrutiny

### C. FINANCIAL POWERS

1. **Money Bills** (Article 110):
   - Lok Sabha has final say
   - Rajya Sabha can only recommend

2. **Budget**:
   - Union Budget presented annually in Lok Sabha
   - Parliament must approve all government spending
   - No tax can be levied without Parliament's law

3. **Consolidated Fund** (Article 266):
   - Parliament controls withdrawals
   - No money can be spent without Parliament's approval

### D. CONSTITUENT POWERS

- Parliament can **amend the Constitution** (Article 368)
- Some amendments need State ratification

### E. JUDICIAL POWERS

1. **Impeachment**:
   - Can impeach President (Article 61)
   - Can impeach Judges of Supreme Court and High Courts (Article 124)
   - Can remove CAG, Election Commissioner

2. **Punish for Contempt**:
   - Can punish for its contempt
   - For breach of privilege

### F. ELECTORAL POWERS

- Elects **President** (along with State Legislatures)
- Elects **Vice-President**
- Parliament makes laws on elections

## 4. Qualifications, Disqualifications and Privileges of Members

### A. Qualifications (Article 84)

**To be a Member of Parliament, a person must:**
1. Be a **citizen of India**
2. Be **at least 25 years** old (for Lok Sabha) or **30 years** (for Rajya Sabha)
3. Be **registered as voter** in any parliamentary constituency
4. Take **oath or affirmation** as per Third Schedule
5. Possess other qualifications prescribed by Parliament

### B. Disqualifications (Article 102)

**A person is disqualified if he/she:**

1. **Holds Office of Profit**:
   - Under Union or State Government
   - Exception: Ministers are not considered office of profit
   - Parliament defines what constitutes "office of profit"

2. **Unsound Mind**:
   - Declared by competent court

3. **Undischarged Insolvent**:
   - Has been adjudged insolvent and not discharged

4. **Not a Citizen**:
   - Or voluntarily acquired foreign citizenship

5. **Disqualified by Law**:
   - Under Representation of People Act, 1951
   - For corrupt practices
   - For defection (Anti-Defection Law)

**Disqualifications Under RPA 1951:**
- Convicted of certain offenses (2 years imprisonment or more)
- Disqualified for **6 years** from date of conviction
- Corrupt practices in elections
- Excessive election expenditure
- Failed to file election returns

**Recent Development:**
- **Lily Thomas v. Union of India (2013)**: Convicted members disqualified immediately (not after appeal)

### C. Vacation of Seats (Article 101)

**Member's seat becomes vacant if:**
1. Resigns
2. Absent from House for **60 days** without permission
3. Becomes disqualified
4. Elected to both Houses (must resign from one within 14 days)
5. Disqualified under Anti-Defection Law

## 5. Anti-Defection Law (Tenth Schedule)

### What is Defection?

When an elected member:
- Leaves the party on whose symbol he/she was elected
- Votes against party's direction
- Acts against party's whip

### Constitutional Provision:

**Tenth Schedule** (added by 52nd Amendment, 1985)
- Provides for **disqualification on grounds of defection**

### Grounds for Disqualification:

**A member is disqualified if:**

1. **Voluntarily gives up membership** of political party
2. **Votes or abstains** contrary to party whip (without permission)
   - Unless condoned by party within 15 days
3. **Nominated member** joins a political party after 6 months
4. **Independent member** joins any political party after election

### Exceptions (Won't be Disqualified):

1. **Merger**: If 2/3rd of party members agree to merge with another party
2. **Presiding Officer**: If elected as Speaker/Chairman, can give up party temporarily
3. **Rejoining**: If presiding officer rejoins party after ceasing to hold office

### Decision on Disqualification:

- **Speaker/Chairman** of the House decides
- Decision is final (earlier)
- **After Kihoto Hollohan case**: Judicial review possible on grounds of malafide or perversity

### Important Cases:

**Kihoto Hollohan v. Zachillhu (1992)**
- **Issue**: Is Tenth Schedule valid? Is Speaker's decision final?
- **Held**: 
  - Tenth Schedule is valid and doesn't violate basic structure
  - Speaker's decision subject to judicial review (not completely final)
  - Review only on grounds of malafide, perversity, violation of constitutional mandate

**Advantages of Anti-Defection Law:**
✅ Provides stability to government  
✅ Curbs political defections  
✅ Strengthens party system  
✅ Reduces horse-trading of MLAs/MPs  

**Criticisms:**
❌ Limits freedom of conscience of members  
❌ Speaker's decision can be biased  
❌ Prevents genuine dissent  
❌ Strengthens party leadership too much

## 6. Privileges of Members (Article 105)

### What are Parliamentary Privileges?

Special rights and immunities enjoyed by Parliament and its members to function independently.

### Types of Privileges:

#### A. **Individual Privileges** (Article 105)

**1. Freedom of Speech (Article 105(1)):**
- Member has freedom of speech in Parliament
- Cannot be prosecuted for anything said in House
- **Not absolute**: Subject to rules of procedure
- Speaker can expunge unparliamentary remarks

**Case**: **Keshav Singh v. Speaker (1965)**
- Held: Freedom of speech in Parliament is absolute regarding courts
- No court can question what is said in Parliament

**2. Immunity from Court Proceedings:**
- Cannot be arrested in civil cases during session
- 40 days before session and 40 days after session
- **Exception**: Can be arrested in criminal cases

**3. Immunity from Jury Duty:**
- Member cannot be compelled to serve as witness or juror when Parliament is in session

#### B. **Collective Privileges**

**1. Right to Publish Debates:**
- Parliament can publish its debates
- Members and public cannot be prosecuted for true reports

**2. Right to Exclude Outsiders:**
- Can exclude strangers from the House
- Gallery can be cleared if needed

**3. Right to Regulate Internal Proceedings:**
- Each House makes its own rules of procedure
- Courts cannot question proceedings on grounds of irregularity

**4. Punish for Contempt and Breach of Privilege:**
- Can punish for contempt of House
- Can punish for breach of privilege
- Punishments: Warning, reprimand, imprisonment (till prorogation)

## 7. Law-Making Procedure

### How a Bill Becomes Law:

#### Stage 1: **Introduction (First Reading)**
- Member seeks leave to introduce bill
- If money bill, President's recommendation needed
- Bill introduced and published

#### Stage 2: **General Discussion (Second Reading)**
- **Stage A**: General principles discussed
- Bill may be:
  - Taken up immediately, OR
  - Referred to Standing Committee, OR
  - Referred to Select Committee, OR
  - Circulated for public opinion

- **Stage B**: Clause-by-clause consideration
- Amendments can be moved
- Voting on each clause

#### Stage 3: **Passing of Bill (Third Reading)**
- Final debate on entire bill
- Only verbal amendments or consequential amendments allowed
- Vote taken
- Bill passed

#### Stage 4: **Bill Goes to Other House**
- If bill originated in Lok Sabha, goes to Rajya Sabha (vice versa)
- Same three stages repeated in second House

#### Stage 5: **If Disagreement Between Houses (Article 108)**
- **Joint Sitting** can be called by President
- Only for ordinary bills (not money bills or Constitution amendment bills)
- Speaker of Lok Sabha presides
- Majority of total members present decides
- In practice, Lok Sabha's view prevails (more members)

#### Stage 6: **President's Assent (Article 111)**
- Bill presented to President
- President has three options:
  1. **Give assent** - Bill becomes Act
  2. **Withhold assent** - Bill doesn't become law
  3. **Return for reconsideration** (except money bills)
     - If Parliament passes again with/without amendments
     - President must give assent (convention)

**Pocket Veto:**
- If President doesn't take action, bill remains pending
- Keeps bill in "pocket"
- No time limit for President to decide (on ordinary bills)

## 8. Types of Bills

### A. **Ordinary Bills**
- Covers subjects other than financial matters
- Can be introduced in either House
- Both Houses have equal powers
- Needs approval of both Houses

### B. **Money Bills** (Article 110)

**Definition:**
Contains ONLY provisions dealing with:
1. Imposition, abolition, remission, alteration of taxes
2. Regulation of borrowing of money by Government
3. Consolidated Fund or Contingency Fund of India
4. Custody of these Funds
5. Appropriation of money from Consolidated Fund
6. Declaration/variation of amounts charged on Consolidated Fund
7. Receipt of money on account of Consolidated Fund
8. Audit of accounts of Union or States
9. Any matter incidental to the above

**Procedure:**
- Introduced ONLY in **Lok Sabha**
- Rajya Sabha can keep for **14 days**
- Can only make **recommendations** (no power to amend or reject)
- If recommendations not accepted, bill proceeds without them
- If Rajya Sabha doesn't return in 14 days, deemed to have passed
- **Speaker certifies** if a bill is Money Bill (decision final)

**Why Lok Sabha Has More Power?**
- Represents people directly
- People pay taxes, so people's House decides taxation
- Ensures democratic control over public finances

### C. **Financial Bills** (Article 117)

**Two Types:**

**Type I**: Contains provisions of Article 110 + other matters
- Needs President's recommendation to introduce
- Can be introduced in either House
- Both Houses have equal powers
- Example: Bill with taxation + other administrative provisions

**Type II**: Involves expenditure from Consolidated Fund
- Needs President's recommendation
- Can be introduced in either House
- Both Houses have equal powers

### D. **Constitution Amendment Bills** (Article 368)

**Procedure:**
- Can be introduced in either House
- Needs **special majority**: 2/3rd of members present and voting + majority of total membership
- Both Houses must pass with special majority
- **Some amendments also need** ratification by 50% of State Legislatures

**Amendments Requiring State Ratification:**
- Election of President
- Extent of executive power of Union and States
- Supreme Court and High Courts
- Distribution of legislative powers
- Representation of States in Parliament
- Article 368 itself

---

## 9. Private Member Bills vs Government Bills

| Feature | Government Bills | Private Member Bills |
|---------|-----------------|---------------------|
| **Introduced by** | Minister | Private Member (non-minister MP) |
| **Time** | Majority of session time | Limited time (Fridays) |
| **Passage Rate** | High (usually passed) | Very low (rarely passed) |
| **Priority** | High priority | Low priority |
| **Examples** | Budget, major laws | Social reforms, amendments |

**Importance of Private Member Bills:**
- Allow individual MPs to raise issues
- Can lead to government action
- Reflect concerns of members
- Democratic forum for ideas

## PART B: STATE LEGISLATURE

## 1. Composition of State Legislature (Article 168)

States can have:

### A. **Unicameral Legislature** (Only Vidhan Sabha)
- **Most states** have unicameral legislature
- Only Legislative Assembly
- Example: Gujarat, Tamil Nadu, Kerala

### B. **Bicameral Legislature** (Vidhan Sabha + Vidhan Parishad)
- **Few states** have bicameral legislature
- Legislative Assembly (Lower House)
- Legislative Council (Upper House)
- **States with Bicameral**: Andhra Pradesh, Bihar, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Telangana, Uttar Pradesh

### State Legislature Consists of:
1. The **Governor**
2. **Legislative Assembly** (Vidhan Sabha) - in all States
3. **Legislative Council** (Vidhan Parishad) - in some States

---

## 2. Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha)

### Composition (Article 170):

**Strength:**
- Maximum: **500 members**
- Minimum: **60 members**
- **Exception**: Smaller states/UTs may have less (e.g., Goa has 40, Sikkim has 32)

**Election:**
- Members **directly elected** by people
- Universal adult franchise
- Reservation for SC/ST

**Term:**
- **5 years** from first sitting
- Can be dissolved earlier by Governor
- Can be extended during emergency

### Qualifications (Article 173):
- Must be **citizen of India**
- Must be **at least 25 years** old
- Must be **registered voter** in State
- Not disqualified under Article 191

### Disqualifications (Article 191):
- Similar to Parliament (Article 102)
- Office of profit, unsound mind, insolvent, not citizen

### Presiding Officer: **Speaker**
- Elected by Assembly members
- Deputy Speaker also elected
- Powers similar to Lok Sabha Speaker

---

## 3. Legislative Council (Vidhan Parishad)

### Composition (Article 171):

**Strength:**
- Maximum: **1/3rd of Assembly strength**
- Minimum: **40 members**

**Composition Formula:**
1. **1/3rd elected** by members of Municipalities, District Boards, etc. (local bodies)
2. **1/12th elected** by graduates of 3 years standing in the State
3. **1/12th elected** by teachers of 3 years standing (secondary and above)
4. **1/3rd elected** by members of Legislative Assembly
5. **1/6th nominated** by Governor (persons with special knowledge in literature, science, art, cooperative movement, social service)

**Term:**
- **Permanent body** (like Rajya Sabha)
- 1/3rd members retire every 2 years
- Each member has **6-year term**

### Qualifications (Article 173):
- Must be **citizen of India**
- Must be **at least 30 years** old
- Must be registered voter
- Not disqualified

### Presiding Officer: **Chairman**
- Elected by Council members
- Deputy Chairman also elected

---

## 4. Powers and Functions of State Legislature

### A. **Legislative Powers**:
- Make laws on **State List** subjects
- Make laws on **Concurrent List** subjects
- Laws valid only within the State
- Cannot make laws on Union List

### B. **Executive Control**:
- Council of Ministers responsible to Assembly
- Can pass no-confidence motion
- Questions, debates, committees (similar to Parliament)

### C. **Financial Powers**:
- Controls State finances
- Approves State Budget
- Assembly has final say on money matters
- Council can only recommend (like Rajya Sabha)

### D. **Constituent Powers**:
- Some Constitutional amendments need State Legislature ratification
- Can pass resolution requesting Parliament to legislate on State subject (Article 252)
- Can create or abolish Legislative Council (needs Parliament's law)

### E. **Electoral Powers**:
- Participates in Presidential election
- Elects members to Rajya Sabha

---

## 5. Privileges of State Legislature Members (Article 194)

- Similar to Parliament (Article 105)
- Freedom of speech in Legislature
- Immunity from court proceedings during session
- Power to punish for contempt
- Power to regulate internal proceedings

---

## Differences Between Parliament and State Legislature

| Feature | Parliament | State Legislature |
|---------|------------|-------------------|
| **Subjects** | Union + Concurrent + Residuary | State + Concurrent (within state) |
| **Territory** | Whole India | Only that State |
| **Extra-territorial** | Yes | Limited |
| **Money Bills** | Lok Sabha has supremacy | Assembly has supremacy |
| **Emergency** | More powers during emergency | Powers reduced during emergency |
| **Supremacy** | Union laws prevail on Concurrent | State laws subordinate to Union |
| **Privileges** | Defined in Constitution | Defined in Constitution |

## Important Cases

### 1. **Raja Ram Pal v. Lok Sabha (2007)**
- **Issue**: Can courts interfere in expulsion of MP?
- **Held**: 
  - Parliamentary privileges exist
  - But not absolute
  - Courts can review in case of violation of Constitution
  - Speaker's decision on defection subject to judicial review

### 2. **Keshav Singh v. Speaker, Lok Sabha (1965)**
- **Issue**: Scope of privilege of freedom of speech
- **Held**: Freedom of speech in Parliament is absolute vis-à-vis courts

### 3. **P.V. Narasimha Rao v. State (1998)**
- **Issue**: Can MP be prosecuted for bribery to vote in House?
- **Held**: Privilege doesn't protect criminal acts like bribery
