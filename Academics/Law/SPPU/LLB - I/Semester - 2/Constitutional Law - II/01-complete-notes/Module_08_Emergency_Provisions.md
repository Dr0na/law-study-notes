# Module 8: Emergency Provisions

## Introduction

The Indian Constitution provides for **three types of emergencies** to deal with extraordinary situations when normal constitutional functioning becomes difficult. These provisions are contained in **Part XVIII (Articles 352-360)** of the Constitution.

**Purpose:**
- Protect national integrity
- Maintain constitutional order
- Deal with crisis situations
- Ensure continuity of governance

**Concern:**
- Emergency powers are extensive
- Can affect Fundamental Rights
- Federal structure can become unitary
- Past misuse (1975 Emergency)

---

## 1. NATIONAL EMERGENCY (Article 352)

### A. What is National Emergency?

When the security of India or any part of it is threatened, President can proclaim National Emergency.

### B. Grounds for Proclamation (Article 352)

**Three Grounds:**

1. **War**
   - Actual war with another country
   - Example: 1962 (China), 1965 and 1971 (Pakistan)

2. **External Aggression**
   - Threat of invasion from outside
   - Imminent danger from foreign country
   - Need not be actual war

3. **Armed Rebellion**
   - Internal armed revolt
   - Challenge to constitutional authority
   - Threat to nation's stability
   - Changed from "internal disturbance" by 44th Amendment

**Why "Armed Rebellion" Instead of "Internal Disturbance"?**
- 44th Amendment (1978) made it stricter
- "Internal disturbance" was too vague
- Was misused in 1975 Emergency
- "Armed rebellion" requires actual armed violence

### C. Proclamation Procedure

**Step 1: President Proclaims**
- President issues proclamation
- Must be based on **written recommendation** of Cabinet
- Cabinet recommendation required (44th Amendment)

**Step 2: Parliamentary Approval**
- Proclamation must be approved by **both Houses of Parliament**
- Within **one month** of proclamation
- Needs **special majority**: Majority of total membership + 2/3rd of members present and voting

**Step 3: Duration**
- Initially valid for **6 months**
- Can be extended indefinitely (every 6 months)
- Each extension needs Parliamentary approval
- After **one year**, needs approval only if:
  - National Emergency is in operation
  - Lok Sabha dissolved or dissolved during emergency

**Revocation:**
- Can be revoked by President anytime
- Must be revoked if Lok Sabha passes resolution by **simple majority**
- Cannot be challenged in court once Parliamentary approval obtained

### D. Types of Emergency Proclamation

**1. Emergency in entire India:**
- Applies to whole country

**2. Emergency in part of India:**
- Can be limited to a specific area
- 42nd Amendment allowed this
- Example: Punjab in 1980s (not formally declared)

### E. Effects of National Emergency

**On Union-State Relations:**

**1. Executive Relations (Article 353):**
- Union can give directions to States on any matter
- Union Government's power extends to State subjects
- States become subordinate to Union
- Federal structure suspended effectively

**2. Legislative Relations (Article 250):**
- Parliament can make laws on **State List subjects**
- Such laws remain in force for **6 months after emergency ends**
- Parliament becomes supreme

**3. Financial Relations (Article 354):**
- President can modify distribution of revenues
- Can direct States to reserve money bills for President's assent
- Financial autonomy of States reduced

**On Fundamental Rights:**

**1. Article 358 - Automatic Suspension:**
- **Only if emergency on grounds of "war" or "external aggression"**
- **Automatically suspends Article 19** (6 freedoms)
- No need for separate order
- All Article 19 rights suspended
- Applies only during emergency period
- Rights revive after emergency ends

**2. Article 359 - Suspension by Order:**
- President can suspend right to move courts for enforcement of Fundamental Rights
- **Except Articles 20 and 21** (cannot be suspended - 44th Amendment)
- Needs separate Presidential order
- Can suspend remedy, not the right itself

**44th Amendment Protection:**
- **Articles 20 and 21 cannot be suspended** even during emergency
- Right to life and protection against ex-post facto laws remain
- This corrects 1975 Emergency misuse

**On Lok Sabha:**
- Term can be **extended** by Parliament
- Maximum extension: **One year at a time**
- Cannot extend beyond **6 months after emergency ends**

### F. Emergency Proclamations in India

**Three Times National Emergency Declared:**

**1. 1962-1968:**
- **Reason**: Chinese aggression
- **Duration**: 6 years
- Continued even after war ended

**2. 1971-1977:**
- **Reason**: Pakistan war (initially)
- Continued after war for **"internal disturbance"**
- **Duration**: 6 years
- Most controversial (1975-77 period)

**3. 1975-1977 Emergency:**
- Most controversial
- Proclaimed on ground of **"internal disturbance"**
- Fundamental Rights suspended
- Press censorship
- Mass arrests without trial
- Opposition leaders imprisoned
- **Biggest black spot on Indian democracy**

**Post-1975 Reforms:**
- 44th Amendment made emergency provisions stricter
- Changed "internal disturbance" to "armed rebellion"
- Made Cabinet recommendation necessary
- Protected Articles 20 and 21 from suspension

## 2. STATE EMERGENCY / PRESIDENT'S RULE (Article 356)

### A. What is President's Rule?

When constitutional machinery in a State fails, President can take over governance of that State.

### B. Grounds (Article 356)

**President can proclaim if satisfied that:**
- Situation has arisen where **government of State cannot be carried on** in accordance with Constitution
- Constitutional machinery has failed

**When Can It Be Imposed?**

**1. Actual Failure:**
- Government has lost majority
- No party can form government
- Hung assembly with no coalition possible

**2. Governor's Report:**
- Governor sends report to President
- States that constitutional machinery failed
- President acts on this report

**3. From Other Sources:**
- President can act on information from any source
- Need not wait for Governor's report

**Controversial Uses:**
- Coalition governments dismissed arbitrarily
- Opposition-ruled States targeted
- Misused for political purposes
- **Most controversial provision**

### C. Procedure

**Step 1: Proclamation**
- President issues proclamation
- Can be based on Governor's report

**Step 2: Parliamentary Approval**
- Must be approved by **both Houses** within **2 months**
- Needs **simple majority** (not special majority)

**Step 3: Duration**
- Initially valid for **6 months**
- Can be extended up to **3 years** (maximum)
- Every 6 months, Parliamentary approval needed
- **After one year**: Extension only if:
  - National Emergency in operation, OR
  - Election Commission certifies elections cannot be held

**Revocation:**
- President can revoke anytime
- Does not need Parliamentary approval for revocation

### D. Effects of President's Rule

**1. On State Executive:**
- **State Council of Ministers dismissed**
- Governor administers State on behalf of President
- President can assume state functions
- All powers exercised through Governor

**2. On State Legislature:**
- **Legislative Assembly can be dissolved or suspended**
- If suspended, can be revived later
- If dissolved, elections must be held
- Legislative Council (if any) not dissolved but prorogued

**3. Legislative Powers:**
- **Parliament can legislate for that State**
- Parliament makes laws on State List subjects
- President can authorize Governor to issue ordinances
- State subjects under Union control

**4. Financial Powers:**
- President controls State finances
- State budget passed by Parliament
- Governor presents budget

**5. High Court:**
- High Court continues to function
- Jurisdiction not affected

### E. Judicial Review of President's Rule

**Earlier Position:**
- Courts couldn't question President's satisfaction
- **State of Rajasthan v. Union of India (1977)**: Emergency provisions subject to judicial review

**Landmark Case: S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994)**

**Facts:**
- President's Rule imposed in several States
- Dismissed BJP governments after Babri Masjid demolition

**Supreme Court's Historic Decision:**

**Held:**

1. **President's satisfaction subject to judicial review**
   - Courts can examine if there was material for satisfaction
   - Can't be subjective or arbitrary

2. **Governor's report not conclusive**
   - President must apply mind
   - Cannot act mechanically on Governor's report

3. **Floor test preferred**
   - Instead of dismissing government, floor test should be ordered
   - Let Assembly decide who has majority

4. **Federalism is basic structure**
   - Arbitrary use of Article 356 violates federalism
   - States' autonomy must be respected

5. **Judicial review possible**
   - Courts can restore dismissed governments if Article 356 misused
   - Assembly dissolution can be revoked

**Impact of S.R. Bommai:**
✅ Reduced misuse of Article 356  
✅ Made government more careful  
✅ Floor test becomes norm  
✅ Governor's reports examined carefully  
✅ Federalism protected  

### F. Controversies and Reforms

**Misuse Examples:**
- Kerala (1959): First use - dismissed Communist government
- Multiple dismissals in 1970s-80s
- Often used for political purposes

**Sarkaria Commission Recommendations (1988):**
- Article 356 should be used sparingly
- Only as last resort
- Alternatives must be exhausted
- Floor test preferred
- Warning to State Government before imposing

**Recent Trends:**
- After S.R. Bommai, use reduced
- Courts strictly scrutinize
- Floor test ordered
- Political consensus needed

## 3. FINANCIAL EMERGENCY (Article 360)

### A. What is Financial Emergency?

When financial stability or credit of India is threatened, President can proclaim Financial Emergency.

### B. Grounds

**President can proclaim if satisfied that:**
- Financial stability of India **threatened**
- Credit of India **in danger**

**What Could Trigger?**
- Economic crisis
- Inability to pay debts
- Severe financial mismanagement
- Loss of investor confidence
- Economic collapse threat

### C. Procedure

**Similar to National Emergency:**
- President proclaims
- Must be approved by both Houses within **2 months**
- Needs **simple majority**

**Duration:**
- No maximum limit specified
- Continues till revoked
- Must get Parliamentary approval periodically

### D. Effects of Financial Emergency

**1. Reduction of Salaries:**
- President can order reduction of salaries of:
  - All government servants (Union and State)
  - Judges of Supreme Court and High Courts
- Exception: President's salary cannot be reduced

**2. States' Financial Autonomy:**
- President can give directions to States on financial matters
- States must follow financial propriety
- Can direct States to reserve money bills for President

**3. Union Control:**
- Union gets control over State finances
- Can monitor State spending
- Can veto State financial decisions

**4. Constitutional Provisions:**
- Can direct reduction of salaries under Article 360(4)
- All money bills passed by State Legislature reserved for President

### E. Financial Emergency in Practice

**NEVER PROCLAIMED IN INDIA**

**Why Not Used?**
- India has faced financial crises (1991, 2008)
- But never proclaimed Financial Emergency
- Handled through economic reforms
- International assistance (IMF, World Bank)
- Political will and economic measures sufficient

**Why Kept as Reserve Power?**
- Deterrent effect
- Last resort measure
- Gives confidence to creditors
- Shows constitutional preparedness

## Comparison of Three Emergencies

| Feature | National Emergency | President's Rule | Financial Emergency |
|---------|-------------------|------------------|---------------------|
| **Article** | 352 | 356 | 360 |
| **Grounds** | War, External Aggression, Armed Rebellion | Failure of constitutional machinery | Financial instability |
| **Approval** | Special majority | Simple majority | Simple majority |
| **Duration** | 6 months (renewable) | 6 months (max 3 years) | No limit |
| **Effect on FRs** | Article 19 suspended (war/aggression) | No automatic effect | No effect |
| **Effect on Federalism** | Union supreme | State under Union | Financial control |
| **Used** | 3 times (1962, 1971, 1975) | Many times (100+) | Never |
| **Judicial Review** | Yes (limited) | Yes (after Bommai) | Yes |

## Safeguards Against Misuse of Emergency

### After 1975 Emergency Experience:

**44th Constitutional Amendment (1978) introduced safeguards:**

### For National Emergency:

1. **Cabinet Recommendation Mandatory:**
   - Earlier: Could be on President's satisfaction
   - Now: Written Cabinet recommendation required

2. **Changed Ground:**
   - "Internal disturbance" changed to **"Armed Rebellion"**
   - Made it more specific

3. **Parliamentary Approval Stricter:**
   - Special majority required
   - Must approve within one month

4. **Lok Sabha Can Revoke:**
   - If Lok Sabha passes resolution by simple majority
   - President must revoke

5. **Protection of Articles 20 and 21:**
   - Cannot be suspended even during emergency
   - Right to life protected

6. **Limited to Specific Area:**
   - Can be proclaimed for whole India or part of it

### For President's Rule:

1. **Judicial Review Possible:**
   - After S.R. Bommai case
   - Courts can examine grounds
   - Can restore government if misused

2. **Floor Test Preferred:**
   - Government should be tested on floor of House
   - Dismissal should be last resort

3. **Maximum Duration Limited:**
   - Cannot exceed 3 years
   - Election Commission must certify if elections can't be held

## Important Cases

### 1. **Minerva Mills v. Union of India (1980)**
- Emergency provisions don't give unlimited power
- Cannot destroy basic structure
- Articles 20-21 cannot be suspended (after 44th Amendment)

### 2. **A.D.M. Jabalpur v. Shivakant Shukla (1976)**
- Decided during 1975 Emergency
- Held: During emergency, even right to life can be suspended
- **Most criticized judgment** in Indian legal history
- Led to 44th Amendment protecting Articles 20-21

### 3. **S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994)**
- Landmark case on President's Rule
- Made Article 356 subject to judicial review
- Laid down guidelines
- Floor test must be ordered
- Cannot be used for political purposes

### 4. **State of Rajasthan v. Union of India (1977)**
- Emergency proclamation subject to judicial review
- Courts can examine if grounds existed
- Protects against arbitrary use

## 1975-77 Emergency - Lessons Learned

### What Happened?

**Proclaimed on:** June 25, 1975 (midnight)

**Official Reason:** Internal disturbance

**Real Reason (widely believed):**
- Political crisis for Prime Minister Indira Gandhi
- Allahabad High Court found her guilty of electoral malpractices
- Growing opposition movements
- Wanted to suppress dissent

### Actions During Emergency:

**1. Fundamental Rights Suspended:**
- Article 19 (6 freedoms) suspended
- Right to approach courts suspended (Article 359 order)
- Even Article 21 (life and liberty) couldn't be enforced

**2. Press Censorship:**
- Newspapers censored
- Pre-censorship imposed
- News controlled by government

**3. Mass Arrests:**
- Opposition leaders arrested
- Detentions without trial
- Under MISA (Maintenance of Internal Security Act)
- Thousands imprisoned

**4. Parliament Subservient:**
- Parliament passed laws without debate
- 42nd Amendment passed (most controversial)
- Democratic functioning suspended

**5. Judiciary's Dark Period:**
- ADM Jabalpur case (1976)
- Supreme Court upheld emergency
- Held even right to life can be suspended
- Only Justice H.R. Khanna dissented (and was superseded)

### End of Emergency:

- Emergency lifted in March 1977
- Elections held
- Congress party defeated
- Janata Party came to power

### Constitutional Reforms Post-Emergency:

**44th Amendment (1978) - Major Safeguards:**

1. Changed "internal disturbance" to **"armed rebellion"**
2. Made **Cabinet recommendation** mandatory
3. Required **Parliamentary approval with special majority**
4. Protected **Articles 20 and 21** from suspension
5. Allowed **Lok Sabha to revoke** emergency by simple majority
6. Limited emergency to **specific areas** possible
7. Made **judicial review** more explicit

**Why 1975 Emergency Important for Law Students:**
- Shows danger of unlimited powers
- Importance of checks and balances
- Led to constitutional reforms
- Strengthened democracy
- Shows importance of judicial independence

## Duration and Extensions

### National Emergency:
- 6 months initially
- Can be extended indefinitely (with approval)

### President's Rule:
- 6 months initially
- Can be extended
- **Maximum: 3 years** (under special conditions)

### Financial Emergency:
- No maximum duration specified
- Continues till revoked

---

## Parliamentary Control Over Emergencies

### How Parliament Controls:

**1. Approval Required:**
- All three emergencies need Parliamentary approval
- National Emergency: Special majority
- Others: Simple majority

**2. Time Limit for Approval:**
- Must be approved within specified time
- If not approved, lapses

**3. Resolution to Revoke:**
- Lok Sabha can pass resolution to revoke National Emergency
- Simple majority sufficient
- President must revoke

**4. Periodic Review:**
- Parliament must approve extensions
- Cannot be indefinite without Parliament

**5. Debate and Discussion:**
- Parliament can debate emergency
- Questions can be asked
- Ensures accountability

---

## Judicial Review of Emergency

### Can Courts Review Emergency?

**Yes (Post-1975 Reforms)**

**Courts Can Review:**
- Whether grounds existed for proclamation
- Whether procedure was followed
- Whether Cabinet recommendation was genuine
- Whether it's being misused

**Courts Cannot:**
- Substitute their judgment for President's
- Question "satisfaction" if material exists
- Interfere during emergency (limited scope)

**Case: State of Rajasthan v. Union of India (1977)**
- Held: Emergency proclamations subject to judicial review
- Courts can examine if President's satisfaction based on relevant material

## Criticisms and Concerns

### Why Emergency Provisions Criticized?

❌ **Too Extensive Powers**
- Union gets almost unlimited power
- Federal structure collapses
- States become powerless

❌ **Past Misuse**
- 1975 Emergency was political
- Used to suppress opposition
- Fundamental Rights violated

❌ **Suspends Democracy**
- Normal constitutional functioning stops
- Rights can be suspended
- Parliament's role reduced

❌ **Threat to Federalism**
- States lose autonomy
- Unitary structure temporarily

### Safeguards After 44th Amendment:

✅ **Stricter Conditions**
- "Armed rebellion" instead of "internal disturbance"
- Cabinet recommendation mandatory

✅ **Parliamentary Control**
- Special majority needed
- Lok Sabha can revoke

✅ **Judicial Review**
- Courts can examine grounds
- Can restore governments

✅ **Protection of Basic Rights**
- Articles 20-21 cannot be suspended
- Life and liberty protected

## Conclusion

Emergency provisions are necessary for dealing with extraordinary situations, but they come with great responsibility. The 1975 Emergency taught India valuable lessons about protecting democracy and rights even during crisis. The 44th Amendment and subsequent judicial pronouncements have balanced emergency powers with democratic safeguards.

**Key Takeaway:**
Emergency powers are necessary but must be:
- Used sparingly
- Subject to Parliamentary control
- Open to judicial review
- Protected by constitutional safeguards
- Never misused for political purposes
