# Module 7: Other Constitutional Institutions / Authorities

## Introduction

Besides the Legislature, Executive, and Judiciary, the Constitution creates several important institutions to ensure democratic governance, accountability, and decentralization. These institutions have constitutional status and specific functions that make India's democracy more robust.

---

## 1. UNION PUBLIC SERVICE COMMISSION (UPSC)

### Constitutional Provision: Articles 315-323

### A. Establishment (Article 315)

**Created by Constitution:**
- Permanent constitutional body
- For Union and each State

### B. Composition (Article 316)

**UPSC consists of:**
- **Chairman**
- **Maximum 10 other members** (or less)
- Appointed by President

**Qualifications:**
- Half members should have held office for at least 10 years under Union/State

**Term:**
- **6 years** or till age of **65** (whichever is earlier)
- Can resign
- Can be removed by President on grounds of:
  - Misbehavior (after Supreme Court inquiry)
  - Insolvency
  - Engaging in paid employment
  - Infirmity of mind or body

**Service Conditions:**
- Cannot hold office of profit
- After retirement, cannot hold office under Government of India (exception: as Chairman/member of State PSC)
- Salary and conditions cannot be changed to disadvantage

### C. Functions (Article 320)

**UPSC conducts examinations for:**
1. **Civil Services** (IAS, IPS, IFS)
2. **Central Services** (Group A and B)
3. **All India Services**

**UPSC advises on:**
- Methods of recruitment
- Principles of appointment
- Promotions and transfers
- Disciplinary matters
- Claims for pension

**Nature of Advice:**
- Advisory (not binding in all cases)
- But must be consulted
- Government usually follows advice

### D. Independence

**Safeguards:**
- Fixed tenure
- Removal only by President after Supreme Court inquiry
- Salary charged on Consolidated Fund
- Cannot practice after retirement

---

## 2. STATE PUBLIC SERVICE COMMISSION (Article 315-323)

**Similar to UPSC but at State Level:**

### Composition:
- **Chairman**
- **Members** (number as determined by Governor)

### Appointment:
- Governor appoints
- In consultation with UPSC (for Chairman)

### Functions:
- Recruitment to State civil services
- Advises on service matters
- Similar to UPSC's functions at State level

### Joint SPSC:
- Two or more States can have Joint SPSC
- If requested by State Legislatures

## 3. ATTORNEY GENERAL OF INDIA

### Constitutional Provision: Article 76

### A. Who is Attorney General?

**India's Top Law Officer:**
- Chief legal advisor to Government of India
- Represents Union in courts

### B. Appointment (Article 76)

**Appointed by:**
- President

**Qualifications:**
- Must be qualified to be judge of Supreme Court
- i.e., Distinguished jurist or Advocate for 10 years or High Court judge for 5 years

**Term:**
- Holds office during President's pleasure
- No fixed term
- Can resign

**Salary:**
- Not fixed by Constitution
- Determined by President
- Can practice privately (receives fees from clients)

### C. Duties (Article 76)

**Main Functions:**

1. **Legal Advisor to Government:**
   - Advises Government on legal matters
   - Gives opinions on questions referred by President

2. **Represents Government in Supreme Court:**
   - Appears in cases involving Government
   - Defends government actions

3. **Appears in High Courts:**
   - If required
   - Protects Government's interests

4. **Discharges functions assigned by President:**
   - Any other legal duty

### D. Rights and Limitations

**Rights:**
- Right to speak in Parliament (both Houses)
- Right to participate in proceedings
- Can attend committee meetings
- **Does NOT have right to vote**

**Limitations:**
- Cannot defend accused in criminal case without Government permission
- Must not appear against Government of India

### E. Advocate General of State (Article 165)

**Similar to Attorney General but for State:**
- Appointed by Governor
- Qualified to be High Court judge
- Chief legal advisor to State Government
- Represents State in courts
- Can speak in Legislature (no voting right)
- Can practice privately

## 4. ELECTION COMMISSION OF INDIA

### Constitutional Provision: Articles 324-329

### A. Establishment (Article 324)

**Constitutional Body:**
- Created to ensure free and fair elections
- Independent of government control

### B. Composition

**Consists of:**
- **Chief Election Commissioner (CEC)**
- **Two Election Commissioners**

**Originally:**
- Only had Chief Election Commissioner (1950-1989)

**1989 onwards:**
- Made multi-member body
- Currently 3 members

**Appointment:**
- President appoints
- **No qualifications prescribed** in Constitution

**Term:**
- **6 years** or till age of **65** (whichever is earlier)

### C. Removal

**Chief Election Commissioner:**
- Removed same way as **Supreme Court judge** (impeachment)
- Grounds: Proved misbehavior or incapacity
- Very difficult to remove
- Ensures independence

**Other Election Commissioners:**
- Can be removed by President
- BUT only on recommendation of CEC
- Cannot be removed without CEC's approval
- Ensures they cannot be pressured

**Why Such Protection?**
- Elections are foundation of democracy
- EC must be fearless
- Should not fear government
- Can take tough decisions

### D. Powers and Functions (Article 324)

**Article 324 gives "Superintendence, Direction and Control" of elections:**

**1. Conduct of Elections:**
- Parliamentary elections (Lok Sabha)
- Presidential and Vice-Presidential elections
- State Legislative Assembly elections
- State Legislative Council elections

**2. Powers:**
- Decides election schedule
- Announces dates
- Issues Model Code of Conduct
- Allots election symbols to parties
- Recognizes political parties
- Registers new parties

**3. Enforcement:**
- Can postpone/cancel elections
- Can remove polling officers
- Can order re-poll
- Can disqualify candidates
- Can recommend action against violators

**4. Electoral Rolls:**
- Preparation and revision of voter lists
- Issues voter ID cards
- Ensures no fake voting

**5. Campaign Regulation:**
- Enforces Model Code of Conduct
- Monitors election expenses
- Takes action on violations
- Seizes cash/gifts meant for bribing

**6. Dispute Resolution:**
- Decides disputes on party symbols
- Decides on party splits/mergers
- Recognition/de-recognition of parties

### E. Independence and Powers

**Independence Guaranteed:**
- Fixed tenure
- Difficult removal procedure
- Financial autonomy
- Cannot be removed by government

**Quasi-Judicial Powers:**
- Decides on disqualification
- Settles symbol disputes
- Orders are binding

**Limitations:**
- Cannot decide election disputes (goes to courts)
- No power over post-election period
- Dependent on government machinery for implementation

**Case**: **Mohinder Singh Gill v. Chief Election Commissioner (1978)**
- Election Commission's powers are wide
- Can ensure free and fair elections
- Orders subject to judicial review

## 5. COMPTROLLER AND AUDITOR GENERAL (CAG)

### Constitutional Provision: Articles 148-151

### A. Who is CAG?

**India's Chief Auditor:**
- Audits all government accounts
- Both Union and States
- Ensures financial accountability

### B. Appointment (Article 148)

**Appointed by:**
- President

**Qualifications:**
- No specific qualifications prescribed
- Usually experienced civil servant or auditor

**Term:**
- **6 years** or till age of **65** (whichever is earlier)

**Removal:**
- Same manner as Supreme Court judge (impeachment)
- Ensures independence

**Salary:**
- Charged on Consolidated Fund
- Cannot be varied to disadvantage
- Cannot hold office of profit after retirement

### C. Powers and Functions (Article 149-151)

**Main Function: AUDIT**

**What CAG Audits:**

1. **Union Government accounts**
2. **State Government accounts**
3. **Union Territory accounts**
4. **Government companies**
5. **Authorities substantially financed by Government**
6. **Grants/loans given by Government**

**Types of Audit:**

**1. Financial Audit:**
- Examines if money spent according to law
- Checks if proper authorization obtained
- Verifies accounts

**2. Regularity/Compliance Audit:**
- Checks if rules followed
- Verifies procedures
- Ensures regularity

**3. Performance/Efficiency Audit:**
- Examines if money spent efficiently
- Checks value for money
- Assesses performance

### D. CAG's Reports (Article 151)

**Submission:**
- CAG submits reports to President (for Union)
- To Governor (for States)
- President/Governor lays before Parliament/Legislature

**Scrutiny:**
- Public Accounts Committee examines reports
- Calls officials to explain
- Ensures accountability

**Famous CAG Reports:**
- 2G Spectrum scam
- Coal block allocation scam
- Commonwealth Games scam

### E. Importance

**Why CAG is Important:**
✅ Financial watchdog  
✅ Prevents misuse of public money  
✅ Ensures transparency  
✅ Promotes accountability  
✅ Detects irregularities

## 6. LOCAL SELF-GOVERNING BODIES

### A. PANCHAYATS (Articles 243-243O)

**Added by 73rd Amendment (1992)**

**What is Panchayati Raj?**
- Local self-government in rural areas
- Decentralization of power
- Grassroots democracy

### Three-Tier System:

1. **Gram Panchayat** (Village level)
2. **Panchayat Samiti** (Block/Taluka level)
3. **Zila Parishad** (District level)

**Exception:** States with population less than 20 lakhs may not have intermediate level

### Composition (Article 243C):

**Members:**
- Directly elected by people
- **Reservation**:
  - 1/3rd seats for women
  - Seats for SC/ST proportional to population
  - 1/3rd of Chairperson posts for women

**Term:**
- **5 years**
- Elections must be held before term expires
- **State Election Commission** conducts elections

### Powers and Functions (Article 243G):

**29 subjects** under **Eleventh Schedule:**
1. Agriculture
2. Land improvement
3. Animal husbandry
4. Fisheries
5. Social forestry
6. Minor forest produce
7. Small scale industries
8. Housing
9. Drinking water
10. Roads, bridges
11. Rural electrification
12. Education (primary and secondary)
13. Health and sanitation
14. Family welfare
15. Women and child development
... and 14 more

**Functions:**
- Economic development
- Social justice
- Implementation of schemes

**Finances:**
- State Finance Commission recommends grants
- State Government provides funds
- Can levy some local taxes

### B. MUNICIPALITIES (Articles 243P-243ZG)

**Added by 74th Amendment (1992)**

**For Urban Local Government:**

### Three Types:

1. **Nagar Panchayat** (Transitional area - village to town)
2. **Municipal Council** (Smaller urban area)
3. **Municipal Corporation** (Larger urban area)

### Composition (Article 243R):

**Members:**
- Directly elected
- **Reservation**:
  - 1/3rd for women
  - For SC/ST proportional to population
  - 1/3rd Chairperson posts for women

**Term:**
- **5 years**
- Elections by State Election Commission

### Powers (Article 243W):

**18 subjects** under **Twelfth Schedule:**
1. Urban planning
2. Regulation of land use
3. Roads and bridges
4. Water supply
5. Public health, sanitation
6. Fire services
7. Urban forestry
8. Slum improvement
9. Urban poverty alleviation
10. Parks and gardens
11. Cultural activities
12. Burial grounds
... and 6 more

**Functions:**
- Municipal services
- Urban development
- Implementation of schemes

### C. State Election Commission (Article 243K & 243ZA)

**For Panchayat and Municipal Elections:**

**Appointment:**
- Governor appoints State Election Commissioner

**Term:**
- Determined by State Legislature
- Removal: Same manner as High Court judge

**Functions:**
- Conducts Panchayat elections
- Conducts Municipal elections
- Prepares electoral rolls
- Independent of Election Commission of India

### D. State Finance Commission (Article 243I & 243Y)

**Function:**
- Reviews financial position of local bodies
- Recommends distribution of grants
- Every 5 years

## 7. CO-OPERATIVE SOCIETIES (Articles 243ZH-243ZT)

**Added by 97th Amendment (2011)**

**Provides Constitutional Status to Cooperatives:**

**Key Provisions:**
- Right to form cooperative societies (Article 19(1)(c) and 43B)
- Democratic functioning
- Elections within 6 months
- State to promote cooperative societies

**Importance:**
- Cooperative movement strengthened
- Democratic management ensured
- Audit and elections made mandatory

## 8. NATIONAL COMMISSIONS

### A. National Commission for Scheduled Castes (Article 338)

**Composition:**
- Chairman
- Vice-Chairman
- 3 other members
- Appointed by President

**Functions:**
- Investigate complaints of SC members
- Monitor safeguards provided
- Submit annual reports to President

### B. National Commission for Scheduled Tribes (Article 338A)

**Similar to SC Commission:**
- Chairman, Vice-Chairman, 3 members
- For Scheduled Tribes
- Monitors tribal welfare

### C. National Commission for Backward Classes (Article 338B)

**Added by 102nd Amendment (2018):**
- For Other Backward Classes (OBCs)
- Similar structure and functions
- Investigates complaints
- Monitors welfare measures

## Importance of These Institutions

### Why Multiple Constitutional Bodies?

**1. Decentralization of Power:**
- Power not concentrated in three organs
- Specialized bodies for specific functions
- Better expertise

**2. Checks and Balances:**
- CAG checks Executive's finances
- Election Commission ensures free elections
- PSCs ensure merit-based recruitment
- Commissions protect minorities

**3. Accountability:**
- CAG's audit reports
- Election Commission's actions
- PSC's transparent process

**4. Independence:**
- Constitutional status
- Fixed tenure
- Difficult removal
- Financial autonomy

**5. Democratic Participation:**
- Panchayats and Municipalities
- Grassroots democracy
- People's participation in governance

## Important Cases

### 1. **Lily Thomas v. Union of India (2013)**
- Convicted MPs/MLAs disqualified immediately
- Election Commission can disqualify

### 2. **T.N. Seshan Era Cases**
- Strengthened Election Commission
- Made Model Code of Conduct effective
- EC can take strong action

### 3. **People's Union for Civil Liberties (PUCL) v. Union of India (2003)**
- Right to know about candidates
- Criminal antecedents must be disclosed
- Assets and liabilities to be declared
- Election Commission can enforce

### 4. **Vineet Narain v. Union of India (1998)**
- CAG's role in detecting scams
- Importance of CAG's independence

## 73rd and 74th Amendments - Decentralization

### Why These Amendments?

**Before Amendments:**
- Local bodies were creatures of State law
- No constitutional status
- Could be suspended/dissolved easily
- No mandatory elections
- Weak and ineffective

**After Amendments:**
- Constitutional status guaranteed
- Mandatory elections every 5 years
- Reservation for women, SC/ST
- State Finance Commission mandatory
- Powers defined in Schedules

### Impact:

✅ **Strengthened local governance**  
✅ **Women's participation** increased (1/3 reservation)  
✅ **Grassroots democracy** empowered  
✅ **Development** more participatory  
✅ **Accountability** at local level  

**Challenges:**
❌ States reluctant to devolve powers  
❌ Inadequate funds  
❌ Lack of capacity building  
❌ Political interference
