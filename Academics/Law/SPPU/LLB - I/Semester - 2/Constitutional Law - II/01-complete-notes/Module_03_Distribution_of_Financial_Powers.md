# Module 3: Distribution of Financial Powers

## Introduction

Financial resources are essential for any government to function effectively. The Indian Constitution carefully distributes financial powers between the Union and States to ensure both have adequate resources while maintaining national economic unity. This distribution is covered in **Articles 264-293** (Part XII of the Constitution).

---

## 1. Allocation of Taxing Powers

Just like legislative powers, taxing powers are also distributed between Union and States through the Seventh Schedule.

### A. Union's Exclusive Taxing Powers (Union List)

**Parliament ALONE can levy these taxes:**

1. **Customs duties** (Import/Export)
2. **Corporation tax** (Tax on company profits)
3. **Taxes on capital value** of assets of individuals and companies
4. **Estate duty** (Inheritance tax - currently not levied)
5. **Duties of excise** on tobacco, petroleum, alcohol (now subsumed in GST)
6. **Income tax** (except agricultural income)
7. **Taxes on railway fares and freights**
8. **Taxes on inter-state trade and commerce**
9. **Taxes on transactions in stock exchanges**

**Why Union Has These Powers:**
- Require uniform policy across India
- Important sources of national revenue
- Affect inter-state trade and commerce
- Need centralized collection mechanism

### B. State's Exclusive Taxing Powers (State List)

**State Legislature ALONE can levy these taxes:**

1. **Land revenue** (Tax on agricultural land)
2. **Stamp duty** (except on certain documents)
3. **Tax on agricultural income**
4. **Excise duty on alcohol** for human consumption
5. **Tax on entry of goods** into local areas (Entry tax)
6. **Tax on vehicles**
7. **Tax on luxuries** (hotels, entertainment)
8. **Taxes on professions, trades, callings**
9. **Taxes on sale and purchase of goods** (now GST)

**Why States Have These Powers:**
- Subjects of local importance
- Revenue collected locally
- States can decide based on local needs
- Gives financial autonomy to States

### C. Concurrent Powers - Goods and Services Tax (GST)

**101st Constitutional Amendment (2016)** introduced:

- **Article 246A**: Both Parliament and State Legislatures can make laws on GST
- **Special provision** for concurrent taxation on goods and services
- GST Council coordinates Centre-State GST policies
- Replaced multiple Central and State taxes

## 2. Restrictions on State's Power to Levy Taxes

States cannot tax freely; they have constitutional restrictions:

### A. Restrictions on Taxing Power (Article 285)

**States CANNOT tax:**
- Property and income of Union Government
- Property used for Union purposes
- **Exception**: Parliament can authorize States to tax Union property

**Why?**
- Prevents States from interfering with Union's functioning
- Union shouldn't pay taxes to States for its own property
- Maintains Union's supremacy

### B. No Tax on Inter-State Trade (Article 286)

**States CANNOT impose tax on:**
- Sale or purchase of goods in inter-state trade or commerce
- Goods imported from or exported to outside India

**Why?**
- Ensures free flow of goods across India
- Prevents economic barriers between States
- Promotes national economic integration

### C. No Discrimination in Taxation (Article 304)

**Restrictions:**
- State can impose tax on goods from other States
- BUT President's sanction required
- State cannot discriminate between goods from other States and local goods
- Ensures equal treatment

### D. Freedom of Trade and Commerce (Articles 301-307)

**Article 301:**
- Trade, commerce, and intercourse throughout India shall be FREE
- States cannot create economic barriers

**Reasonable Restrictions:**
- Public interest considerations allowed
- Must not discriminate
- Must be reasonable

## 3. Distribution of Revenue Between Union and States

### Sharing of Tax Revenue

Not all taxes collected by Union remain with Union. Some must be shared with States:

### A. Taxes That Must Be Shared

According to the Constitution and Finance Commission recommendations:

**1. Income Tax** (Article 270)
- Levied by Union
- Distributed between Union and States
- Finance Commission decides sharing formula

**2. Goods and Services Tax (GST)** (Article 270)
- Both Centre and States levy GST (CGST + SGST)
- Integrated GST (IGST) for inter-state transactions
- Revenue shared as per GST Council recommendations

**3. Other Union Taxes** (Article 270)
- Except taxes mentioned in Article 268-269
- Shared between Union and all States
- Finance Commission determines distribution

---

## 4. Assignment of Union Revenue to States

Some taxes are collected by Union but **entirely assigned** to States:

### A. Stamp Duties on Certain Documents (Article 268)
- Levied by Union Government
- But **collected and appropriated by States**
- Applies to bills of exchange, cheques, promissory notes, etc.

**Why This Arrangement?**
- Collection is easier at state level
- States need this revenue
- Union levies to ensure uniformity

### B. Excise Duties on Medicinal & Toilet Preparations (Article 268)
- Levied by Union
- **Collected and appropriated by States**
- Containing alcohol, opium, narcotics

---

## 5. Compulsory Tax Sharing

### Taxes That MUST Be Distributed (Article 270)

**Constitutional Mandate:**
- Certain taxes levied by Union
- Must be shared with States
- Finance Commission recommends the formula

**Taxes Under Article 270:**

1. **Income Tax** (except agricultural income)
2. **Corporation Tax**
3. **Customs Duties**
4. **Central Excise Duties**
5. **GST** (CGST portion to be shared)
6. **Any other tax** levied by Union (with exceptions)

### How Distribution Works:

**Finance Commission's Role:**
- Constituted every 5 years (Article 280)
- Recommends sharing formula
- Considers:
  - State's needs
  - State's resources
  - Population
  - Area
  - Development needs
  - Special circumstances

**Current Formula (15th Finance Commission):**
- Tax devolution: 41% of divisible pool to States
- Distributed among States based on formula considering:
  - Population (15%)
  - Income distance (45%)
  - Area (15%)
  - Forest and ecology (10%)
  - Demographic performance (12.5%)
  - Tax effort (2.5%)

---

## 6. Permissive Sharing of Taxes

### Taxes That MAY Be Shared (Article 272 - Now omitted)

**Earlier Position:**
- Certain taxes could be shared at Union's discretion
- Example: Taxes on Union List subjects

**Current Position:**
- Article 272 omitted by 80th Amendment (2000)
- Now most taxes automatically shared under Article 270
- Simplified the system

---

## 7. Grants-in-Aid (Article 275)

### What are Grants-in-Aid?

Financial assistance given by Union to States from Consolidated Fund of India.

### Types of Grants:

#### A. **Statutory Grants (Article 275)**

**Mandatory Grants:**
- Parliament provides by law
- Finance Commission recommends
- For States in need of assistance

**Purpose:**
- Help financially weaker States
- Bridge revenue gaps
- Promote balanced development

**Special Grants:**
- For promoting welfare of Scheduled Tribes
- For specific development projects
- For States with special needs

#### B. **Discretionary Grants (Article 282)**

**Optional Grants:**
- Given at discretion of Union or State
- For any public purpose
- Even if not within Union/State's legislative competence

**Examples:**
- Centrally Sponsored Schemes
- Special development programs
- Disaster relief
- Infrastructure development

### C. **Plan and Non-Plan Grants**

**Earlier Classification:**
- Plan Grants: For development plans
- Non-Plan Grants: For revenue support

**Current Position:**
- Distinction abolished
- All grants now part of unified budget

---

## Difference Between Tax Sharing and Grants-in-Aid

| Feature | Tax Sharing | Grants-in-Aid |
|---------|-------------|---------------|
| **Nature** | States' constitutional right | Discretionary (mostly) |
| **Source** | Share of Union taxes | Consolidated Fund |
| **Purpose** | Revenue distribution | Specific purposes |
| **Formula** | Finance Commission decides | Government decides (mostly) |
| **Conditions** | Unconditional | Often with conditions |
| **Regularity** | Regular and assured | May vary year to year |

## Finance Commission (Article 280)

### Constitutional Body for Financial Distribution

**Establishment:**
- President constitutes every **5 years** or earlier if needed
- Not a permanent body

**Composition:**
- **Chairman**: Person with experience in public affairs
- **Four Members**: Appointed by President

**Functions (Article 280):**

1. **Distribution of Tax Revenue:**
   - Recommend sharing of Union taxes between Union and States
   - Distribution among States

2. **Grants-in-Aid:**
   - Recommend grants to States from Union's Consolidated Fund
   - Identify States in need of assistance

3. **Financial Relations:**
   - Review Centre-State financial relations
   - Make recommendations for healthy finances

4. **Other Matters:**
   - Any other matter referred by President
   - State finances and debt sustainability

### Finance Commissions So Far:
- **1st Finance Commission**: 1951 (K.C. Neogi - Chairman)
- **15th Finance Commission**: 2020-25 (N.K. Singh - Chairman)
- **16th Finance Commission**: To be constituted for 2026-31

### Importance:
- Ensures objective distribution of resources
- Reduces Centre-State conflicts
- Promotes fiscal federalism
- Constitutionally mandated (not discretionary)

## GST - A Revolution in Tax Structure

### 101st Constitutional Amendment (2016)

**What is GST?**
- **Goods and Services Tax**: One indirect tax replacing multiple taxes
- Unified tax system across India

**Constitutional Changes:**

**Article 246A Added:**
- Parliament can make laws on GST
- State Legislatures can make laws on GST
- Both have concurrent power (special case)

**Types of GST:**
1. **CGST**: Central GST (collected by Centre)
2. **SGST**: State GST (collected by State)
3. **IGST**: Integrated GST (for inter-state transactions)
4. **UTGST**: Union Territory GST

### GST Council (Article 279A)

**Composition:**
- **Chairman**: Union Finance Minister
- **Members**: Union Minister of State for Finance + Finance Ministers of all States

**Functions:**
- Recommend GST rates
- Decide on goods/services exempt
- Model GST laws
- Resolve disputes

**Voting:**
- Decisions by 3/4th majority
- Union has 1/3rd voting power
- States collectively have 2/3rd voting power

**Why GST is Important:**
- Promotes **One Nation, One Tax**
- Eliminates cascading effect of taxes
- Simplifies tax structure
- Enhances economic integration
- Example of **cooperative federalism**

## Borrowing Powers

### Union's Borrowing Power (Article 292)
- Union Government can borrow on security of Consolidated Fund
- Within limits fixed by Parliament
- Can borrow from domestic or foreign sources

### State's Borrowing Power (Article 293)
- State can borrow within India
- Within limits fixed by State Legislature
- **Restriction**: If State owes money to Union, needs Union's consent for new borrowing

**Why Restriction?**
- Prevents States from becoming over-indebted
- Ensures fiscal discipline
- Protects Union's financial interests

---

## Financial Emergency (Article 360)

If Union thinks financial stability of India is threatened:

**President Can Proclaim Financial Emergency:**

**Effects:**
- Union can give financial directions to States
- Can reduce salaries of State employees (including judges)
- Money bills passed by State Legislature reserved for President
- Union gets complete financial control

**So Far:**
- Financial Emergency has **never been proclaimed** in India
- Remains a reserve power

✅ **Finance Commission**: Every 5 years, recommends distribution  
✅ **GST**: Articles 246A and 279A - cooperative federalism example  
✅ **Borrowing**: Union (Article 292), State (Article 293) - State needs Union consent if indebted
