# 📝 Question ID: CON2-DIST-D003

## Question
**Distinguish between President and Governor.**

**Marks**: 5-10  
**Pattern**: 🔁 Comparative  
**Topic**: Module 6 - Executive  
**Exam Frequency**: 🔥🔥🔥🔥 (Very frequent)  
**Time**: 6-7 minutes (5M) | 13 minutes (10M)  

---

## 📋 MODEL ANSWER

### Introduction (0.5 mark)

**Two Constitutional Heads:**
President (Article 52) and Governor (Article 153) are the Constitutional Heads of Union and State respectively. While both are nominal executives acting on ministerial advice, there are significant differences in their selection, security of tenure, powers, and independence, reflecting India's federal structure with Union's supremacy.

---

### 🔁 Complete Comparison (4-4.5 marks for 5M | 8-9 marks for 10M)

### 10 Key Differences (COUNT = 10)

| # | Feature | President 🇮🇳 | Governor 🏛️ |
|---|---------|-------------|------------|
| 1️⃣ | **Method of Selection** | **Elected** (Electoral College: MPs + MLAs) | **Appointed** (by President/Central Govt) |
| 2️⃣ | **Term Security** | 5 years (relatively secure) | 5 years but "**President's pleasure**" (insecure) |
| 3️⃣ | **Removal** | **Impeachment** (Articles 61, very difficult) | **President's pleasure** (can be removed anytime, no impeachment) |
| 4️⃣ | **Grounds for Removal** | Only "violation of Constitution" | No specific grounds needed |
| 5️⃣ | **Discretionary Powers** | Very limited (exceptional cases only) | More extensive (5 specific powers) |
| 6️⃣ | **Independence** | More independent (elected, difficult removal) | Less independent (appointed by Centre, removable) |
| 7️⃣ | **Pardon Power** | All offenses, **death sentences**, court-martial (Art 72) | State offenses only, **no death**, no court-martial (Art 161) |
| 8️⃣ | **Ordinance** | Article 123 (Union subjects) | Article 213 (State subjects) |
| 9️⃣ | **Represents** | Nation (federal character) | Centre in State (agent of Centre) |
| 🔟 | **Salary** | Rs. 5 lakh per month | Varies by State |

### 📋 Detailed Comparison of Key Aspects (Optional for 10M)

#### Selection Process:

**President (Article 54-55):**
- Electoral College = All elected MPs + All elected MLAs
- Proportional representation, STV
- Vote value formula ensures balance
- Federal character - represents Union + States

**Governor (Article 155):**
- President appoints
- Central Government decides
- No electoral process
- Usually from outside the State

#### Term and Removal:

**President:**
- 5 years fixed term
- Can be re-elected unlimited times
- Removal only by impeachment (1/4 initiate, 2/3 pass in both Houses)
- Never been impeached

**Governor:**
- 5 years nominal but serves "during President's pleasure"
- Can be removed anytime without reasons
- No impeachment process
- Many removed when Central government changes

**Case:** B.P. Singhal v. Union of India (2010) - Governor removal should not be arbitrary

#### Pardon Power:

**President (Article 72) - WIDER:**
- All offenses under Union laws
- All death sentences (even State law offenses)
- All court-martial cases
- More extensive

**Governor (Article 161) - NARROWER:**
- Only State law offenses
- Cannot pardon death sentences
- Cannot pardon court-martial cases
- Limited scope

**Case:** Kehar Singh v. UOI (1989) - President's pardon power scope

#### Discretionary Powers:

**President - Very Limited:**
- Appointing PM (no clear majority)
- Dissolving Lok Sabha (extreme)
- Almost always bound by Article 74

**Governor - More Extensive (5 powers):**
- Appointing CM (hung Assembly)
- Dismissing CM (loses majority)
- Dissolving Assembly
- Reserving bills for President
- Recommending President's Rule (Article 356)

**Post-Bommai:** Even Governor's discretion is judicially reviewable

---

### Conclusion (0.5 mark)

While both are Constitutional Heads with similar formal positions, the **Governor's appointed status and removability** at President's pleasure makes them less independent than the President. The Governor effectively acts as **Centre's representative** in the State, whereas the President, being elected, has more independence. However, both remain **nominal executives** with real power resting with PM/CM and their Councils of Ministers.
