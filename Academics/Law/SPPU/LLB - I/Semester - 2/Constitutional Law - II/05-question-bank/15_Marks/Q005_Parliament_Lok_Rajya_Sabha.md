### Introduction (1 mark)

**Parliament - The Union Legislature:**
Under Article 79, Parliament consists of the President and two Houses - **Lok Sabha** (House of the People) and **Rajya Sabha** (Council of States). India follows a **bicameral system** at the Union level where Lok Sabha represents the people directly while Rajya Sabha represents the States. This ensures both popular representation and federal balance in national legislation.

---

### ⚖️ PART A: Composition of Two Houses (4-5 marks)

### Lok Sabha - House of the People (COUNT = 5 aspects)

#### 1️⃣ Strength 👥
**Current composition:** Maximum 552 members, currently 543 elected members.

**Breakdown:**
- **530 members**: From States (directly elected)
- **20 members**: From Union Territories  
- **2 members**: Anglo-Indians (nominated by President) - *Provision lapsed in 2020*

#### 2️⃣ Election Method 🗳️
**How elected:** Direct election by people through universal adult suffrage.

- One person, one vote
- People vote directly
- First-past-the-post system
- Secret ballot

#### 3️⃣ Allocation of Seats 📊
**Based on population:** Each State gets seats proportional to its population, ensuring fair representation.

- Larger states get more seats (UP: 80, Maharashtra: 48)
- **Reservation**: For SC/ST based on population
- **Delimitation Commission** fixes constituencies

#### 4️⃣ Term ⏰
**Duration:** 5 years from date of first sitting unless dissolved earlier.

- Can be **dissolved** earlier by President (on PM's advice)
- Can be **extended** during National Emergency (max 1 year at a time)

#### 5️⃣ Qualifications 📋
**To be member:** Must be Indian citizen, at least **25 years** old, registered voter, not disqualified.

**Presiding Officer:** **Speaker** elected by Lok Sabha members

### Rajya Sabha - Council of States (COUNT = 5 aspects)

#### 1️⃣ Strength 👥
**Current composition:** Maximum 250 members, currently 245 members.

**Breakdown:**
- **238 members**: Representatives of States and UTs (elected)
- **12 members**: Nominated by President (eminent persons in literature, science, art, social service)

#### 2️⃣ Election Method 🗳️
**How elected:** Indirect election by elected members of State Legislative Assemblies.

- **Proportional representation** system
- **Single transferable vote** method
- Elected by State MLAs
- Each State gets seats roughly proportional to population

#### 3️⃣ Permanent Body ♾️
**Never dissolves:** Rajya Sabha is a continuing body that never dissolves.

- **1/3rd members retire** every 2 years
- Each member has **6-year term**
- Always remains functional
- Ensures continuity

#### 4️⃣ Qualifications 📋
**To be member:** Must be Indian citizen, at least **30 years** old (5 years more than Lok Sabha), registered voter.

**Presiding Officer:** **Chairman** (Vice-President of India is ex-officio Chairman)

#### 5️⃣ Represents States 🏛️
**Federal character:** Ensures States have voice in Union legislation.

- Represents States' interests
- Federal balance maintained

### 🔁 Quick Comparison Table

| Feature | Lok Sabha | Rajya Sabha |
|---------|-----------|-------------|
| **Strength** | 543 (max 552) | 245 (max 250) |
| **Election** | Direct (by people) | Indirect (by MLAs) |
| **Term** | 5 years (dissolves) | Permanent (1/3 retire/2 yrs) |
| **Age** | 25 years | 30 years |
| **Represents** | People | States |
| **Officer** | Speaker | Chairman (VP) |

🧠 **Formulas**: Lok Sabha = **543-5-25-SP**, Rajya Sabha = **245-P-30-CH**

### ⚖️ PART B: Powers and Functions (4-5 marks)

### Powers Where Both Houses Are Equal (COUNT = 4)

#### 1️⃣ Ordinary Legislation 📜
**Equal power:** Both Houses have equal power in making ordinary laws (non-money bills).

- Bill can be introduced in either House
- Both must pass
- Disagreement → Joint Sitting (Article 108)

#### 2️⃣ Constitutional Amendment 📋
**Equal power:** Both Houses needed for amending Constitution with special majority.

- Both must pass with special majority
- Some amendments need State ratification too

#### 3️⃣ Impeachment ⚖️
**Equal power:** Both participate equally in impeaching President or judges.

- Either House can initiate
- Both must pass with special majority

#### 4️⃣ Control Over Executive 👔
**Questions and debates:** Both Houses can question Ministers, debate policies.

- Question Hour
- Discussions
- Committee membership

### Powers Where Lok Sabha is Superior (COUNT = 3)

#### 1️⃣ Money Bills (Article 110) 💰
**Lok Sabha supreme:** Can **originate only in Lok Sabha**, Rajya Sabha can only recommend (14 days).

- Rajya Sabha cannot reject or amend
- Only Lok Sabha's decision final

#### 2️⃣ No-Confidence Motion 🎯
**Only in Lok Sabha:** Council of Ministers collectively responsible only to Lok Sabha.

- Article 75(3): Collective responsibility to Lok Sabha only
- Rajya Sabha cannot move no-confidence
- Lok Sabha can remove government

#### 3️⃣ Control Over Budget 💵
**Final say on finances:** Lok Sabha has ultimate control over financial matters.

- Budget presented in Lok Sabha
- Financial Bills originate in Lok Sabha
- People's House controls finances

### Powers Where Rajya Sabha is Superior (COUNT = 2)

#### 1️⃣ Create New All India Service (Article 312) 🎖️
**Rajya Sabha exclusive:** Can pass resolution by 2/3rd majority to create new All India Service.

- Only Rajya Sabha can initiate
- Parliament then creates by law
- Federal character (States' House decides)

#### 2️⃣ Authorize Parliament to Legislate on State List (Article 249) 🏛️
**Rajya Sabha exclusive:** Can pass resolution declaring State subject as of national importance.

- 2/3rd majority needed
- Parliament can then legislate on State subject
- Valid for 1 year

### 💰 PART C: Money Bills - Procedure (4-5 marks)

**Context:** Money Bills deal with taxation and government finances. Since people pay taxes, people's House (Lok Sabha) has supremacy.

### Article 110 - What is a Money Bill? (COUNT = 9 matters)

**A Bill is a Money Bill if it contains ONLY provisions dealing with:**

1️⃣ Imposition, abolition, remission, alteration of taxes  
2️⃣ Regulation of borrowing by Government  
3️⃣ Consolidated Fund or Contingency Fund of India  
4️⃣ Custody of these Funds  
5️⃣ Appropriation of money from Consolidated Fund  
6️⃣ Declaration/variation of amounts charged on Consolidated Fund  
7️⃣ Receipt of money on Consolidated Fund  
8️⃣ Audit of accounts  
9️⃣ Any matter incidental to the above  

**Key Point:** Must contain **ONLY** these matters. If contains other matters too, not a Money Bill.

### Procedure for Money Bills (COUNT = 6 stages)

#### Stage 1️⃣: Introduction - Only in Lok Sabha 📝
**Exclusive right:** Money Bill can be introduced **only in Lok Sabha**, never in Rajya Sabha.

- Needs **President's recommendation** to introduce
- Usually introduced by Finance Minister
- Budget is prime example

#### Stage 2️⃣: Lok Sabha Passes 🏛️
**Normal procedure:** Lok Sabha debates and passes the Money Bill through three readings.

- First Reading: Introduction
- Second Reading: Clause-by-clause discussion
- Third Reading: Final vote

#### Stage 3️⃣: Transmitted to Rajya Sabha 📤
**Goes to upper House:** After Lok Sabha passes, bill transmitted to Rajya Sabha.

#### Stage 4️⃣: Rajya Sabha's Limited Role ⏰
**14 days maximum:** Rajya Sabha can keep the bill for maximum **14 days** only.

**Three Options for Rajya Sabha:**
1. **Return with recommendations** (within 14 days)
2. **Return without recommendations** (within 14 days)
3. **Not return** (if 14 days pass)

**Important:** Rajya Sabha can **only recommend** changes - cannot reject or amend

#### Stage 5️⃣: Lok Sabha's Final Decision ✅
**Ultimate authority:** Lok Sabha may accept or reject Rajya Sabha's recommendations.

- **If accepts**: Bill passes with changes
- **If rejects**: Bill passes in original form
- **If Rajya Sabha doesn't return in 14 days**: Deemed to have passed
- Lok Sabha's version final in all cases

#### Stage 6️⃣: President's Assent 🖊️
**Final step:** Bill presented to President for assent.

- President gives assent
- Bill becomes Act
- **President cannot return** Money Bill for reconsideration

### Who Decides if Bill is Money Bill?

**Speaker's Certificate (Article 110(3)):**
- **Speaker of Lok Sabha certifies** whether a bill is Money Bill or not
- Speaker's decision is **final**
- Cannot be questioned in any court
- Protects parliamentary sovereignty

### Why Lok Sabha Has Supremacy on Money Bills?

**3 Reasons:**
1. **Direct representation**: Elected directly by people
2. **Taxpayers' control**: People pay taxes, so people's House decides
3. **Democratic principle**: Financial matters decided by those directly accountable to people

---

### Conclusion (1 mark)

The bicameral Parliament ensures both popular representation (Lok Sabha) and federal balance (Rajya Sabha). While Lok Sabha is more powerful, especially on financial matters, Rajya Sabha provides valuable second opinion and represents States' interests. The **Money Bill procedure** reflects democratic principle that financial matters must be controlled by the House directly elected by taxpayers.
