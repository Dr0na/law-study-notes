# 📝 Question ID: CON2-2M-Q304

## Question
**What is Basic Structure Doctrine? Which case established it?**

**Marks**: 2  
**Pattern**: 🧠 Conceptual + 📚 Case-Law  
**Topic**: Module 1 - Basic Structure  
**Exam Frequency**: 🔥🔥🔥🔥 (Very common)  
**Time**: 2-3 minutes  

---

## 📋 MODEL ANSWER

**Basic Structure Doctrine:**
The Basic Structure Doctrine, established in **Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973)** by a 13-judge bench, holds that while Parliament can amend any part of the Constitution including Fundamental Rights, it **cannot alter or destroy the basic structure or fundamental features** of the Constitution, even with a 2/3rd special majority.

**Basic Features Include:**
Supremacy of Constitution, Federalism, Separation of Powers, Judicial Review, Democracy, Secularism, Parliamentary system, Rule of Law.

**Significance:** This doctrine protects the Constitution's core from being destroyed by amendments, balancing Parliamentary power with Constitutional integrity. **Federalism being part of basic structure** means States cannot be abolished by amendment.
