# 📝 Question ID: CON2-2M-Q301

## Question
**What is meant by "quasi-federal" nature of Indian Constitution?**

**Marks**: 2  
**Pattern**: 🧠 Conceptual  
**Topic**: Module 1 - Federalism  
**Exam Frequency**: 🔥🔥🔥🔥 (Common)  
**Time**: 2-3 minutes  

---

## 📋 MODEL ANSWER

**Quasi-Federal Meaning:**
The Indian Constitution is described as **"quasi-federal"** by K.C. Wheare, meaning it is **federal in form but unitary in spirit**. While it has federal features like division of powers and dual government, it also has strong unitary features that make the Centre very powerful.

**Federal Features:**
- Written Constitution
- Division of powers (Three Lists)
- Independent judiciary

**Unitary Features:**
- Strong Centre (residuary powers, emergency provisions)
- Single citizenship
- Governor appointed by President
- Article 356 (President's Rule)

**Article 1:** Constitution itself doesn't use the word "Federation" but states India is a **"Union of States"**, reflecting this quasi-federal character.
