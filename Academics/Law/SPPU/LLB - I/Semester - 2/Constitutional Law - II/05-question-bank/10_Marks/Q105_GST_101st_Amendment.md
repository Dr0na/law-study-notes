### Introduction (1 mark)

**GST - One Nation, One Tax:**
The **101st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2016** introduced the **Goods and Services Tax (GST)**, India's biggest tax reform since independence. It subsumed **17 Central and State taxes** into one unified tax, creating a common national market. The Amendment added **Articles 246A, 269A, and 279A** to the Constitution and inserted GST in various Lists of Seventh Schedule.

**Implementation:** GST launched on **July 1, 2017** (midnight session in Parliament) - called "India's tryst with tax destiny."

---

### ⚖️ Key Constitutional Provisions (4-5 marks)

### 3 New Articles Added (COUNT = 3)

#### 1️⃣ Article 246A - Power to Make GST Laws 📜
**What it creates:** Special concurrent power for both Parliament and State Legislatures to make GST laws.

**Provision:**
- **Parliament** can make laws on GST on inter-state trade (IGST)
- **State Legislatures** can make laws on GST within their State (SGST)
- **Both** can levy GST simultaneously

**Unique Feature:**
- Creates **dual taxation** power
- Both Centre and States tax same transaction
- But divided into CGST and SGST to maintain federal structure

**Significance:** Exception to normal rule where either Union or State can tax - here BOTH can tax together.

#### 2️⃣ Article 269A - Inter-State GST 🔀
**What it covers:** GST on inter-state supply of goods and services assigned to Union but collected and appropriated by Union and States.

**Provision:**
- **IGST** on inter-state trade levied by Union
- Revenue shared between Union and States
- As recommended by GST Council

#### 3️⃣ Article 279A - GST Council 🏛️
**What it establishes:** Creates GST Council as constitutional body for federal cooperation on GST matters.

### 4 Types of GST Created (COUNT = 4)

#### 1. **CGST** - Central GST 🏛️
**Levied by:** Union Government on intra-state supply

#### 2. **SGST** - State GST 🏢
**Levied by:** State Government on intra-state supply

#### 3. **IGST** - Integrated GST 🔀
**Levied by:** Union Government on inter-state supply  
**Shared with:** Destination State

#### 4. **UTGST** - Union Territory GST 🏝️
**Levied by:** Union on supply in UTs without legislature

---

### 🏛️ GST Council (Article 279A) (4-5 marks)

**Context:** GST Council is the federal body where Union and States jointly decide GST policies - true example of cooperative federalism.

### Composition (COUNT = 3 categories)

#### 1️⃣ Chairman 👤
**Who:** Union Finance Minister

**Role:**
- Heads the Council
- Conducts meetings
- No casting vote

#### 2️⃣ Union Representative 🏛️
**Who:** Union Minister of State in charge of Revenue or Finance

**Role:**
- Represents Union Government
- Participates in decisions

#### 3️⃣ State Representatives 🏢
**Who:** Finance Minister or Taxation Minister of each State and UT with Legislature

**Number:** Currently 33 (28 States + 3 UTs with legislature + 2 others)

**Role:**
- Represent State interests
- Participate in decisions

### Functions of GST Council (COUNT = 6)

#### Function 1️⃣: Recommend GST Rates 💰
**Key power:** Council recommends tax rates for goods and services.

- Different slabs: 0%, 5%, 12%, 18%, 28%
- Can change rates based on economic needs
- Reviews periodically

#### Function 2️⃣: Decide Exemptions 🆓
**What it covers:** Council decides which goods/services to exempt from GST.

- Essential items (food grains) often exempt
- Life-saving medicines may be exempted
- Based on social and economic considerations

#### Function 3️⃣: Threshold Limits 📊
**What it sets:** Minimum turnover for GST registration.

- Small businesses below threshold exempt
- Currently Rs. 20-40 lakhs depending on State

#### Function 4️⃣: Model GST Laws 📜
**Standardization:** Recommends model CGST, SGST, IGST laws ensuring uniformity.

#### Function 5️⃣: Dispute Resolution 🤝
**Mediator role:** Resolves disputes between Centre and States or between States on GST matters.

#### Function 6️⃣: Other Matters 📋
**Additional functions:** Any other matter referred by Centre or States related to GST.

### Decision-Making in GST Council

**Voting Pattern (Special Federal Formula):**

**Union's Voting Power:** 1/3 (33.33%)  
**States' Voting Power:** 2/3 (66.67%) collectively

**Decisions Taken By:** **3/4th majority** of weighted votes

**Why This Formula:**
- Protects States from Union's dominance
- Union cannot impose decisions alone
- States collectively have veto power
- True cooperative federalism

**Significance:** Even though Union chairs, States have collective majority - balances power.

---

### 💡 Significance of GST (1 mark)

### 4 Major Impacts (COUNT = 4)

#### 1️⃣ Unified Market 🇮🇳
**One Nation, One Tax:** Created single common market across India.

#### 2️⃣ Simplified Taxation 📉
**Replaced 17 taxes:** Central Excise, Service Tax, VAT, Entry Tax, Octroi, etc.

#### 3️⃣ Eliminated Cascading 🔄
**No tax on tax:** Input tax credit prevents cascading effect.

#### 4️⃣ Cooperative Federalism 🤝
**Best example:** Union and States work together through GST Council.

---

### Conclusion (1 mark)

GST represents a historic tax reform and exemplifies India's cooperative federalism. The **GST Council** model, where Union and States jointly decide policies with States having collective veto, demonstrates that federalism in India is evolving from competitive to cooperative. It shows that on matters of common interest, federal cooperation can produce solutions balancing national unity with regional diversity.
