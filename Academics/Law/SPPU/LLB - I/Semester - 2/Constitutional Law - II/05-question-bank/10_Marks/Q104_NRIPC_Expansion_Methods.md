## Question
**In what circumstances can the Parliament make laws on subjects enumerated in the State List? Explain with relevant Articles.**

### Introduction (1 mark)

**Normal Rule:**
Under Article 246, Parliament has exclusive power to legislate on Union List subjects, and State Legislatures have exclusive power on State List subjects. However, the Constitution provides for **5 exceptional circumstances** where Parliament can make laws even on State List subjects, showing Union's supremacy in India's quasi-federal structure.

---

### ⚖️ 5 Circumstances - NRIPC (8-9 marks)

### Expansion Methods (COUNT = 5)

#### 1️⃣ **N** - National Emergency (Article 250) 🚨
**When applicable:** During operation of National Emergency proclaimed under Article 352.

**Provision:**
- Parliament empowered to make laws on **any subject in State List**
- This power available for entire duration of emergency
- Laws continue to remain in force for **6 months after emergency ends**
- Then cease to operate unless extended by Parliament

**Rationale:**
- National crisis requires centralized control
- Uniform policy needed across country
- State governments may be unable to cope
- Emergency situation demands Union leadership

**Example:**
- During 1962 (Chinese aggression), 1971 (Pakistan war) emergencies
- Parliament made laws on subjects normally under State control

**Article 250:** Parliament's power to legislate during National Emergency

---

#### 2️⃣ **R** - Rajya Sabha Resolution (Article 249) 🏛️
**When applicable:** If Rajya Sabha passes resolution that a State List subject has become a matter of national importance.

**Procedure:**
- **Rajya Sabha** (Council of States) passes resolution
- Needs **2/3rd majority** of members present and voting
- Declares that it's necessary/expedient for Parliament to make laws on specified State subject

**Duration:**
- Resolution remains in force for **1 year**
- Can be renewed for another year by fresh resolution
- Can be revoked earlier

**Rationale:**
- Rajya Sabha represents States
- If States' House itself says subject has become national, legitimate for Parliament to legislate
- Federal consent implied

**Example:**
- If Rajya Sabha feels police reforms needed nationally
- Can pass resolution by 2/3rd majority
- Parliament can then make uniform police law for entire country

**Article 249:** Power of Parliament to legislate when Rajya Sabha passes resolution

---

#### 3️⃣ **I** - International Agreements/Treaties (Article 253) 🌐
**When applicable:** To implement international treaties, agreements, conventions India has signed.

**Provision:**
- Parliament can make laws on **any subject** (even State List)
- Purpose: To implement international obligations
- Overrides normal division of powers under Article 246

**Rationale:**
- India must honor international commitments
- Treaties may cover subjects in State List
- Cannot say "State subject, cannot implement"
- National obligation prevails

**Examples:**
- ILO (International Labour Organization) conventions implementation
- Environmental treaties (Climate Change, Biodiversity)
- Trade agreements (WTO obligations)
- Human Rights conventions

**Article 253:** Legislation to give effect to international agreements

---

#### 4️⃣ **P** - President's Rule (Article 356) 🏴
**When applicable:** When President's Rule imposed in a State due to constitutional machinery failure.

**Provision:**
- When Article 356 proclaimed in a State
- State Legislature suspended or dissolved
- **Parliament assumes State Legislature's functions**
- Can make laws on State List subjects for that State

**Duration:**
- For period of President's Rule
- Maximum 3 years
- Usually 6 months at a time

**Rationale:**
- State Legislature not functioning
- Someone must make laws for State
- Parliament fills the vacuum
- Temporary measure

**Example:**
- When President's Rule in Punjab, J&K, or other States
- Parliament made laws on State subjects for those States

**Article 356:** Provisions for President's Rule allowing Parliament to legislate

---

#### 5️⃣ **C** - Consent of States (Article 252) 🤝
**When applicable:** When two or more State Legislatures voluntarily request Parliament to legislate.

**Procedure:**
- **Two or more State Legislatures** pass resolutions
- Requesting Parliament to make law on a State List subject
- Parliament then makes law for those requesting States
- **Other States** can adopt later by passing resolutions

**Rationale:**
- Federal cooperation
- States themselves requesting
- Voluntary arrangement
- Maintains federal spirit

**Duration:**
- Continues till States request repeal
- Can be amended only by Parliament
- States cannot unilaterally repeal (once adopted)

**Examples:**
- Wildlife Protection Act, 1972 (many States requested)
- Water pollution control laws
- Some subjects where uniform law needed

**Article 252:** Power of Parliament to legislate for two or more States by consent

---

### 📊 Comparison Table

| Method | Article | Who Initiates | Duration | Extent |
|--------|---------|---------------|----------|--------|
| **National Emergency** | 250 | President | 6 months after emergency | All States |
| **Rajya Sabha** | 249 | Rajya Sabha (2/3) | 1 year (renewable) | All States |
| **International** | 253 | Parliament/Govt | Permanent | All States |
| **President's Rule** | 356 | President | Till Rule lasts | That State only |
| **Consent** | 252 | States (2+) | Till repealed | Consenting States |

---

### Conclusion (1 mark)

These five provisions demonstrate the flexibility of Indian federalism and the Union's supremacy. While they allow Parliament to legislate on State subjects, each has specific conditions and safeguards. The **NRIPC framework** balances national needs with federal structure, showing India's quasi-federal character where Centre can intervene when necessary while respecting States' autonomy normally.
