## Question
**Explain residuary powers under the Indian Constitution. How is it different from USA?**

**Marks**: 5  
**Pattern**: 🧠 Conceptual + 🔁 Comparative  
**Topic**: Module 2 - Residuary Powers  
**Exam Frequency**: 🔥🔥🔥 (Frequently asked)

---

## 📋 MODEL ANSWER

### 🔹 Residuary Powers - Definition (1 mark)

**What are Residuary Powers:**
Residuary powers refer to the power to make laws on subjects which are **not mentioned in any of the three Lists** (Union, State, or Concurrent) of the Seventh Schedule. These are subjects that were not contemplated when the Constitution was framed or emerged later due to technological/social developments.

---

### ⚖️ Article 248 - Constitutional Provision (2 marks)

**Article 248 - Residuary Powers of Legislation:**
- Parliament has **exclusive power** to make laws on residuary subjects
- No State Legislature can legislate on such subjects
- This power includes power to levy taxes on residuary subjects

**Why Article 248 is Important:**
- Prevents legislative vacuum
- Ensures new subjects can be regulated
- Maintains Union's supremacy
- Shows India's centralized federal structure

---

### 💡 Examples of Residuary Subjects (1 mark)

**When First Emerged:**
1. **Computer and Internet** (1990s) - Not in any List in 1950
2. **Cyber crimes** - Parliament made Information Technology Act, 2000
3. **E-commerce** - Online trade regulations
4. **GST** (before 101st Amendment added it to Lists)
5. **Space law** - Satellite communications, space programs

**Legislative Action:**
Parliament used residuary power to make laws on these subjects as they weren't specifically mentioned in any List.

---

### 🆚 India vs USA - Key Difference (1 mark)

**Comparison:**

| Feature | India 🇮🇳 | USA 🇺🇸 |
|---------|----------|---------|
| **Residuary Powers** | With **Union** (Parliament) | With **States** |
| **Article** | Article 248 | 10th Amendment |
| **Effect** | Centralizing | Decentralizing |
| **Philosophy** | Strong Centre needed | States' autonomy preserved |

**Why Different:**
- **India**: Adopted centralized model for national unity and development
- **USA**: Adopted decentralized model as States created the Union

**Significance:**
This is one of the most important differences between Indian and American federalism, showing India's unitary bias despite federal structure.
