## 1. ADM Jabalpur v. Shivakant Shukla (1976)

### 📋 Case Details

**Citation**: AIR 1976 SC 1207  
**Bench**: 5 judges  
**Decision**: 4:1 (Justice H.R. Khanna dissented)  
**Article**: Emergency, Article 21  
**Importance**: 🔥🔥🔥🔥🔥 (Most criticized judgment)  
**Also Known As**: **Habeas Corpus Case**  

### Facts

**Context - During 1975-77 Emergency:**
- National Emergency proclaimed on June 25, 1975
- Presidential order issued under Article 359 suspending right to move courts
- Thousands detained under **MISA** (Maintenance of Internal Security Act)
- Detentions without trial, no reasons given
- Families approached courts through Habeas Corpus writs

**Petitioners:**
- Several persons detained under MISA
- Including **Kanu Sanyal** (Naxalite leader)
- Sought release through Habeas Corpus
- Argued: Right to life cannot be suspended

### Issue

**Main Question:**
During Emergency, when Presidential order under Article 359 suspends enforcement of Fundamental Rights, can courts entertain Habeas Corpus petitions challenging illegal detention?

**Sub-Issues:**
1. Can Article 21 (right to life and personal liberty) be suspended?
2. Is there any remedy if FRs suspended during emergency?
3. Are emergency powers unlimited?

### Supreme Court's Decision - Dark Hour

**Majority View (4:1):**

#### Held 1: No Remedy During Emergency ❌
**What court said:** When Presidential order suspends right to move courts, even Habeas Corpus petitions cannot be entertained.

- Article 359 order completely bars court remedies
- No judicial review possible
- Courts powerless

#### Held 2: Even Article 21 Suspended ❌
**Shocking ruling:** During emergency with Article 359 order, even right to life and personal liberty suspended.

- Government can detain anyone
- No need to give reasons
- No court can question
- **Complete immunity to Executive**

#### Held 3: Rule of Law Suspended ❌
**Effect:** During emergency, rule of law doesn't apply.

- Executive power unlimited
- No judicial check
- Democracy suspended

**Majority Judges:**
- Chief Justice A.N. Ray
- Justice M.H. Beg
- Justice Y.V. Chandrachud
- Justice P.N. Bhagwati

### The Lone Dissent - Justice H.R. Khanna 🏛️

**What Justice Khanna Said:**

#### "Even during emergency, Article 21 cannot be suspended" ✅

**His Reasoning:**
1. Right to life is most fundamental
2. Cannot be taken away even by emergency
3. Rule of law must prevail
4. Constitution not suspended during emergency
5. Some rights are inalienable

**His Famous Words:**
"The Constitution and the laws of India do not authorize the detention of a person by the Executive without any effective remedy"

**His Sacrifice:**
- He was senior-most judge after CJI
- Expected to become next CJI
- **Superseded** because of his dissent
- Justice M.H. Beg (who agreed with government) made CJI instead
- **Punished for upholding Constitution**

**Legacy:** Justice Khanna's dissent later vindicated - became beacon of judicial courage

### Impact and Consequences

#### Immediate Impact (1976-77):
❌ Legitimized Emergency excesses  
❌ People had no legal remedy  
❌ Detentions continued unchecked  
❌ Democracy and rule of law suspended  
❌ Judiciary failed in its duty  

#### Long-Term Impact (Post-1977):
✅ **Most criticized judgment** in Indian legal history  
✅ Led to **44th Amendment** reforms  
✅ Articles 20 & 21 now cannot be suspended (directly because of this case)  
✅ Shown as example of what judiciary should NOT do  
✅ Justice Khanna's dissent celebrated  

### 44th Amendment Response (1978)

**Direct Response to ADM Jabalpur:**

**Article 359 Amended:**
- **Articles 20 and 21 cannot be suspended** even during emergency
- Right to life and personal liberty always enforceable
- Courts can always entertain Habeas Corpus for these rights

**Why:** ADM Jabalpur showed danger of allowing even Article 21 suspension

## 2. Minerva Mills Ltd. v. Union of India (1980)

### 📋 Case Details

**Citation**: AIR 1980 SC 1789  
**Bench**: 5 judges  
**Article**: Emergency powers, 42nd Amendment  
**Importance**: 🔥🔥🔥🔥 (Major constitutional case)  

### Facts

**Background:**
- **42nd Amendment (1976)** passed during Emergency
- Made sweeping changes to Constitution
- Clauses 4 and 5 of Article 368 added
- Gave Parliament unlimited power to amend
- Took away judicial review of amendments

**What 42nd Amendment Did:**
- Made Parliament supreme over Constitution
- Directive Principles given primacy over Fundamental Rights
- Judicial review of amendments barred
- Emergency powers expanded

**Challenge:**
- Minerva Mills (textile company) challenged land ceiling law
- Also challenged validity of 42nd Amendment provisions

### Issues

**Main Questions:**
1. Can Parliament give itself unlimited amendment power?
2. Can judicial review of amendments be taken away?
3. Can Directive Principles override Fundamental Rights?

### Held - Protecting Constitution

#### Ruling 1: Unlimited Amendment Power Struck Down ⚖️
**What SC held:** Clauses 4 & 5 of Article 368 (added by 42nd Amendment) struck down.

**Reasoning:**
- Parliament cannot destroy basic structure
- Unlimited amendment power would destroy Constitution
- Balance between powers necessary
- Basic structure doctrine reaffirmed

#### Ruling 2: Judicial Review Cannot Be Taken Away ✅
**What SC held:** Power of judicial review is part of basic structure.

**Reasoning:**
- Judicial review protects Constitution
- Courts are ultimate interpreters
- Cannot be barred even by Constitutional amendment
- Essential check on legislative power

**Principle:** **"The power to amend is not the power to destroy"**

#### Ruling 3: Balance Between Parts III and IV Necessary ⚖️
**What SC held:** Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles must be balanced; one cannot completely override other.

**Reasoning:**
- Both are essential
- Constitution requires harmony
- Cannot make one supreme over other
- Balance is basic structure

### Significance

**Impact on Emergency Powers:**
✅ Limited emergency provisions  
✅ Confirmed they don't give unlimited power  
✅ Emergency subject to basic structure  
✅ Judicial review maintained  

**Impact on Constitution:**
✅ Reaffirmed Kesavananda doctrine  
✅ Protected judicial review  
✅ Maintained constitutional balance  
✅ Prevented Parliament's supremacy  

**Famous Quote:**
"The Constitution is not an ephemeral legal document embodying a passing phase of public opinion"

### Exam Relevance

**When to Cite:**
- Emergency powers questions
- Basic structure questions
- Judicial review questions
- 42nd Amendment questions

**Quote:** "Emergency powers don't give unlimited authority - Minerva Mills (1980)"

## 3. State of Rajasthan v. Union of India (1977)

### 📋 Case Details

**Citation**: AIR 1977 SC 1361  
**Bench**: 7 judges  
**Topic**: Emergency provisions, Judicial review  

### Facts

**Post-Emergency Context:**
- Emergency lifted in March 1977
- Elections held, Congress defeated
- New Janata Party government formed
- Article 356 used to dismiss Congress governments in 9 States
- Rajasthan government challenged

### Issue

Can emergency proclamations (Articles 352, 356) be challenged in court? Is President's satisfaction under emergency articles subject to judicial review?

### Held

**Decision:**
- Emergency proclamations **subject to judicial review**
- President's satisfaction not absolute
- Courts can examine if:
  - Material existed for proclamation
  - Procedure followed properly
  - Power exercised mala fide or for irrelevant considerations

**Significance:**
- Opened door for judicial review of emergencies
- Limited President's discretion
- Federal structure protected
- Paved way for Bommai case (1994)
