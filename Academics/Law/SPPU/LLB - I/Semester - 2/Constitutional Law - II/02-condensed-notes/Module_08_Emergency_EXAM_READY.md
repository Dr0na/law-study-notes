## 📚 Overview: Three Types of Emergencies

**What This Covers:** Constitution provides for 3 types of emergencies to handle extraordinary situations when normal governance becomes difficult.

### ⚖️ 3 Emergencies - Quick Compare (COUNT = 3)

| Emergency | Article | Ground | Used? |
|-----------|---------|--------|-------|
| 1️⃣ **National** | 352 | War, External Aggression, Armed Rebellion | 3 times (1962, 1971, 1975) |
| 2️⃣ **State (President's Rule)** | 356 | Constitutional machinery failure | 100+ times |
| 3️⃣ **Financial** | 360 | Financial instability | NEVER |

## 📚 Topic 1: National Emergency (Article 352)

**What This Covers:** When security of India is threatened by war, external aggression, or armed rebellion, President can proclaim National Emergency giving Union extensive powers.

### ⚖️ 3 Grounds for Proclamation (COUNT = 3)

**Context (When can be proclaimed):** Only in grave situations threatening India's security - not for ordinary law and order problems.

#### 1️⃣ War ⚔️
**What it means:** Actual war with another country - armed forces engaged in combat.

- Real war situation
- Armed conflict
- Example: 1962 (China), 1965, 1971 (Pakistan)

#### 2️⃣ External Aggression 🛡️
**What it means:** Threat of invasion from outside even if war hasn't started yet - imminent danger.

- Threat from foreign country
- Invasion imminent
- Need not be actual war yet

#### 3️⃣ Armed Rebellion 🔫
**What it means:** Internal armed revolt against the nation - requires actual armed violence, not just protests or disturbances.

- Internal armed revolt
- Violent challenge to authority
- **Changed from "internal disturbance" by 44th Amendment**

**Why Changed:**
- "Internal disturbance" too vague
- Misused in 1975
- "Armed rebellion" more specific

🧠 **Mnemonic**: **"WEA"** = **War, External, Armed**

### ⚖️ Procedure - 3 Steps (COUNT = 3)

**Context (Safeguards after 1975):** 44th Amendment made procedure stricter to prevent misuse.

#### Step 1️⃣: Cabinet Recommendation 📝
**What's required:** President cannot proclaim on their own - needs written recommendation from Cabinet (44th Amendment safeguard).

- **Written Cabinet recommendation** mandatory
- President cannot act suo motu
- Prevents arbitrary proclamation

#### Step 2️⃣: Parliamentary Approval 🏛️
**What's required:** Within one month, both Houses must approve by special majority - ensures democratic check.

- Within **1 month** of proclamation
- Both Houses must approve
- **Special majority**: 2/3 present + majority of total

#### Step 3️⃣: Extension ⏰
**How it continues:** Initially valid for 6 months, then needs Parliamentary approval for every 6-month extension.

- Valid for **6 months** initially
- Extensions: Every 6 months
- Each extension needs Parliamentary approval
- Can continue indefinitely

**Revocation:**
- Lok Sabha can pass resolution by **simple majority** to revoke
- President must revoke immediately

### ⚖️ 3 Major Effects (COUNT = 3)

**Context (How India changes during emergency):** National Emergency transforms India from federal to unitary - Union becomes supreme.

#### Effect 1️⃣: On Union-State Relations 🔀
**What happens:** Federal structure suspended - Union can direct States on any matter, States become subordinate.

- Union supreme (Article 353)
- Can give directions on any subject
- States lose autonomy
- Becomes unitary temporarily

#### Effect 2️⃣: On Fundamental Rights 🚫
**What happens:** Some Fundamental Rights can be suspended - either automatically (Article 358) or by Presidential order (Article 359).

**Article 358** (Automatic):
- **Only if emergency due to "war" or "external aggression"**
- **Article 19** (6 freedoms) automatically suspended
- No separate order needed
- Revives after emergency

**Article 359** (By Order):
- President can suspend right to move courts
- For other FRs (except 20 and 21)
- **Articles 20 & 21 CANNOT be suspended** (44th Amendment)
- Needs separate Presidential order

#### Effect 3️⃣: On Parliament & States 🏛️
**What happens:** Parliament can legislate on State subjects, Lok Sabha term can be extended, State Legislatures' role reduced.

- Parliament can make laws on State List (Article 250)
- Such laws valid for 6 months after emergency
- Lok Sabha term can extend (max 1 year at a time)

## 📚 Topic 2: 1975 Emergency - Dark Chapter

**What This Covers:** The 1975-77 Emergency was the darkest period in Indian democracy - understanding this prevents repetition and shows importance of safeguards.

### ⚠️ 1975 Emergency - 5 Key Points (COUNT = 5)

#### 1️⃣ Why Proclaimed? 🚨
**The trigger:** On June 25, 1975 (midnight), Emergency proclaimed officially for "internal disturbance" but real reasons were political.

**Official Reason:** Internal disturbance  
**Real Reasons:**
- Allahabad HC found PM guilty of electoral malpractices
- Growing opposition movements (JP movement)
- Political crisis for government

#### 2️⃣ What Happened During Emergency? 😱
**The horror:** Fundamental Rights suspended, press censored, mass arrests without trial - democracy virtually suspended.

- **Fundamental Rights suspended** (even Article 21)
- **Press censorship** imposed
- **Mass arrests** without trial (MISA used)
- **Opposition leaders imprisoned**
- **Parliament became rubber stamp**
- **42nd Amendment passed** (most controversial)

#### 3️⃣ ADM Jabalpur Case (1976) 📌
**Judiciary's failure:** Supreme Court held that during emergency, even right to life and liberty can be suspended - most criticized judgment.

**What SC held:**
- During emergency, right to approach courts suspended
- Even Article 21 not enforceable
- Only Justice H.R. Khanna dissented (and was superseded)
- **Darkest hour of Indian judiciary**

#### 4️⃣ Emergency Ended 🎉
**How it ended:** PM Indira Gandhi lifted emergency in March 1977, called elections, and lost badly - people rejected emergency.

- Lifted: March 1977
- Elections held
- Congress defeated
- Janata Party won

#### 5️⃣ Lessons & Reforms 📚
**What we learned:** 44th Amendment (1978) added multiple safeguards to prevent future misuse.

**44th Amendment Safeguards:**
- Changed "internal disturbance" to **"armed rebellion"**
- Made **Cabinet recommendation** mandatory
- Required **special majority** in Parliament
- Protected **Articles 20 & 21** from suspension
- Allowed **Lok Sabha to revoke** by simple majority

## 📚 Topic 3: President's Rule (Article 356)

**What This Covers:** When State Government cannot function according to Constitution, President can impose President's Rule, taking over State's governance.

### ⚖️ 5 Key Aspects (COUNT = 5)

**Context (Most controversial provision):** Article 356 is called the "Dead Letter" by critics because of frequent misuse for political purposes to dismiss opposition governments.

#### 1️⃣ Ground 🎯
**When it can be imposed:** When constitutional machinery in State fails - government loses majority, no one can form government, or breakdown of law and order.

- Constitutional machinery failure
- Government cannot be carried on
- Based on Governor's report (usually)

#### 2️⃣ Procedure 📋
**How it's imposed:** President proclaims, Parliament must approve within 2 months by simple majority.

- President proclaims
- **Both Houses approve** within 2 months
- **Simple majority** (not special)
- Valid for 6 months, renewable

#### 3️⃣ Maximum Duration ⏰
**How long it can last:** Maximum 3 years but only in exceptional circumstances with EC's certification.

- Initially **6 months**
- Can extend up to **3 years maximum**
- After 1 year: Only if National Emergency OR EC certifies elections can't be held

#### 4️⃣ Effects 💥
**What happens to State:** State Government dismissed, Assembly dissolved/suspended, Parliament legislates for State, Governor administers on President's behalf.

- State Council of Ministers dismissed
- Assembly dissolved or suspended
- Parliament makes laws for State
- Governor administers

#### 5️⃣ Judicial Review 👨‍⚖️
**Post-Bommai protection:** After S.R. Bommai (1994), President's Rule subject to strict judicial review.

### 📌 S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994)
**What this changed:** Most important case on Article 356 - made it subject to judicial review, laid down guidelines.

**What SC Held (5 key points):**
1. President's satisfaction subject to judicial review
2. Governor's report not conclusive
3. **Floor test preferred** over dismissal
4. Federalism is basic structure
5. Courts can restore dismissed governments

**Impact:**
✅ Misuse reduced drastically  
✅ Floor tests ordered  
✅ States' autonomy protected  
✅ Governor cannot act partisan

## 📚 Topic 4: Financial Emergency (Article 360)

**What This Covers:** When India's financial stability or credit is threatened, President can proclaim Financial Emergency giving Union control over States' finances.

### ⚖️ 3 Quick Points (COUNT = 3)

**Context (Never used):** Despite provisions, Financial Emergency never proclaimed even during severe economic crises (1991, 2008).

#### 1️⃣ Ground 🎯
**When:** Financial stability or credit of India threatened.

- Economic crisis
- Inability to pay debts
- Severe financial mismanagement

#### 2️⃣ Effects 💥
**What happens:** Union gets control over State finances, can reduce salaries of all government employees including judges.

- Can reduce salaries (except President's)
- States' money bills reserved for President
- Union directs States on finances
- Complete financial control to Centre

#### 3️⃣ Never Used ❌
**Why never proclaimed:** India handled crises through economic reforms, IMF assistance, not emergency provisions.

- 1991 crisis: Economic reforms used
- 2008 crisis: Policy measures
- Kept as deterrent/reserve power

## ⚡ 60-SECOND RECALL - ALL EMERGENCIES

### National Emergency (352):
✅ **Grounds**: WEA (War, External, Armed)  
✅ **Approval**: Special majority, within 1 month  
✅ **Duration**: 6 months, renewable  
✅ **Effects**: Union supreme, Article 19 suspended (war/aggression), Parliament can legislate on State List  
✅ **Used**: 3 times (1962, 1971, 1975)  
✅ **Revoke**: Lok Sabha simple majority  

### President's Rule (356):
✅ **Ground**: Constitutional machinery failure  
✅ **Approval**: Simple majority, within 2 months  
✅ **Duration**: 6 months, max 3 years  
✅ **Effects**: State Govt dismissed, Assembly dissolved, Parliament legislates  
✅ **Used**: 100+ times  
✅ **Bommai (1994)**: Judicial review possible, floor test preferred  

### Financial Emergency (360):
✅ **Ground**: Financial stability threatened  
✅ **Approval**: Simple majority  
✅ **Effects**: Salaries reduced, Union controls State finances  
✅ **Used**: NEVER  

### 44th Amendment Safeguards (1978):
✅ "Armed rebellion" (not "internal disturbance")  
✅ Cabinet recommendation mandatory  
✅ Special majority required  
✅ Articles 20-21 cannot be suspended  
✅ Lok Sabha can revoke

## 📌 CASE LAW MUST-KNOW

### 1️⃣ ADM Jabalpur v. Shivakant Shukla (1976)
**The shame:** SC held even right to life can be suspended during emergency - led to 44th Amendment protecting Articles 20-21.

### 2️⃣ S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994)
**The savior:** Article 356 subject to judicial review, federalism is basic structure, floor test preferred, cannot be misused.

### 3️⃣ Minerva Mills v. Union of India (1980)
**The principle:** Emergency powers don't give unlimited authority, cannot destroy basic structure.
