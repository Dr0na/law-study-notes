## 📚 Course Overview

This course provides specialized understanding of penal policies, punishment theories, functioning of penal institutions, and protection of crime victims. It bridges criminal law with its practical implementation through the criminal justice system, focusing on both offenders (penology) and victims (victimology).

**Dual Focus:**
- **Penology**: Study of punishment, prisons, police, parole systems
- **Victimology**: Study of crime victims, their rights, and assistance

## 💡 Important Study Points

### **Key Concepts to Master:**

#### **1. Four Theories of Punishment:**

| Theory | Core Idea | Criticism | Example |
|--------|-----------|-----------|---------|
| **Deterrent** | Punish to deter others | May not deter hardened criminals | Public executions |
| **Retributive** | Eye for an eye | Doesn't consider reform | Death penalty |
| **Preventive** | Prevent future crime | Can be harsh | Life imprisonment |
| **Reformative** | Reform the criminal | May not work for all | Probation, parole |

#### **2. Four Theories of Victimology:**

| Theory | Key Concept | Application |
|--------|-------------|-------------|
| **Precipitation** | Victim's role in crime | Victim precipitation in assault |
| **Life-style** | Risky lifestyle | Night-time travel alone |
| **Deviant Place** | High-crime areas | Crime-prone neighborhoods |
| **Routine Activity** | Convergence of factors | Motivated offender + suitable target + absence of guardian |

#### **3. Important Distinctions:**

| Concept | Definition | Key Feature |
|---------|------------|-------------|
| **Parole** | Conditional early release | Supervision required |
| **Probation** | Alternative to imprisonment | First-time offenders |
| **Furlough** | Short-term release | Temporary, granted to all |
