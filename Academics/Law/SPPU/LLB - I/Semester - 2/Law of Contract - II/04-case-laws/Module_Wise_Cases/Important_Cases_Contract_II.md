# Important Case Laws Digest: Law of Contract II

This digest contains the landmark judgments categorized by module. Use these cases to enrich your 10/15 marks answers.

---

## Module 1: Contracts of Indemnity

### 1. Gajanan Moreshwar Parelkar vs. Moreshwar Madan Mantri (1942)
- **Facts:** Plaintiff transferred land to defendant and gave an indemnity to the municipal corporation for construction. Defendant failed to pay for materials. Plaintiff was sued and asked defendant to indemnify him even before he paid the amount.
- **Issue:** Can an indemnity holder claim compensation *before* actually suffering the loss?
- **Held:** Yes. Bombay HC held that the Indian Contract Act is not exhaustive. Following English equitable principles, an indemnity holder can ask the indemnifier to pay off the liability once it becomes absolute and certain. You do not have to be "damnified" before you are indemnified.
- **Exam Relevance:** Cite this for "Commencement of Liability of Indemnifier".

## Module 2: Contracts of Guarantee

### 2. State Bank of India vs. Mula Sahakari Sakhar Karkhana Ltd. (2006)
- **Facts:** A dispute arose over a bank guarantee whether it was conditional or absolute.
- **Principle:** Supreme Court held that a bank guarantee is an independent contract between the bank and the beneficiary. Courts will not generally interfere or grant injunctions against encashing a bank guarantee unless there is (1) egregious fraud, or (2) irretrievable injustice/harm.
- **Exam Relevance:** Crucial for questions on Bank Guarantees.

### 3. Amrit Lal Goverdhan Lalan vs. State Bank of India (1968)
- **Principle:** If the creditor loses or parts with the security without the consent of the surety, the surety is discharged to the extent of the value of the security (Section 141).

## Module 3: Contracts of Bailment

### 4. Ultzen vs. Nicols (1894)
- **Facts:** A customer went to a restaurant. The waiter took his coat and hung it on a hook. The coat was stolen.
- **Held:** It was a contract of bailment. The waiter took possession (constructive delivery) and was negligent in keeping it safe. The restaurant was held liable.
- **Exam Relevance:** Cite for "Delivery of Possession" and "Duties of Bailee".

### 5. Kaliaperumal Pillai vs. Visalakshmi (1938)
- **Facts:** A lady gave old jewels to a goldsmith to be melted and made into new ones. Every evening, she took the half-finished jewels, locked them in a box, and kept the key with herself. The jewels were stolen.
- **Held:** There was no bailment at the time of theft because the lady had retaken possession (by keeping the key). The goldsmith was not liable.
- **Exam Relevance:** Emphasizes that "Transfer of Possession" is essential for bailment.

## Module 4: Contracts of Pledge

### 6. Lallan Prasad vs. Rahmat Ali (1967)
- **Facts:** Defendant pledged aeroplanes to plaintiff for a loan. Plaintiff sued for recovery of the loan but was unable to produce the pledged goods (aeroplanes).
- **Held:** Supreme Court held that a pawnee cannot sue for the debt if they are not in a position to redeliver the pledged goods upon payment. The right to sue and the right to retain goods are concurrent.
- **Exam Relevance:** Important for "Rights of Pawnee" and "Suit for Debt".

## Module 5: Contracts of Agency

### 7. Keighley, Maxsted & Co. vs. Durant (1901)
- **Principle (Ratification):** An act cannot be ratified unless the person acting (agent) professed to act on behalf of the principal at the time of making the contract. An undisclosed principal cannot step in and ratify an act done by someone in their own name.
- **Exam Relevance:** Cite for "Agency by Ratification".

## Module 6: Sale of Goods Act

### 8. Grant vs. Australian Knitting Mills (1936)
- **Facts:** Buyer bought woolen underwear containing excess sulphite, which caused a severe skin disease. The defect was hidden.
- **Held:** There was a breach of implied condition as to fitness and merchantability. The buyer relied on the seller's skill (by buying from a reputed shop). The rule of *Caveat Emptor* did not apply here.
- **Exam Relevance:** Landmark case for Exceptions to Caveat Emptor.

### 9. Rowland vs. Divall (1923)
- **Facts:** Buyer bought a car from seller and used it for some months. It turned out the seller had stolen the car. The buyer had to return it to the true owner.
- **Held:** Total failure of consideration. The seller breached the implied condition as to title (Sec 14). Buyer entitled to recover the full price paid.
- **Exam Relevance:** Condition as to Title.

## Module 7: Partnership Act

### 10. Cox vs. Hickman (1860)
- **Principle:** established that sharing of profits is not a conclusive test of partnership. The true test is **Mutual Agency** (whether the business is carried on by persons acting on behalf of all).
- **Exam Relevance:** Absolute necessity for questions on "Definition and Tests of Partnership".
