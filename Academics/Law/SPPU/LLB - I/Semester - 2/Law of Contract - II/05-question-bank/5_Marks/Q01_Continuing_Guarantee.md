### Q1. Write a short note on: Continuing Guarantee and its revocation.
**📖 Statutory Provision:** Section 129, 130, and 131 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872.
**1. Meaning:** A guarantee which extends to a series of transactions is called a "continuing guarantee". It is not confined to a single, specific transaction but covers a running account or multiple future dealings.
**2. Example:** A guarantees payment to B, a tea-dealer, up to ₹10,000, for any tea he may from time to time supply to C.
**3. Revocation (How it ends):**
- 🛑 **By Notice (Sec 130):** The surety can revoke it at any time for *future transactions* by giving notice to the creditor. The surety remains liable for past transactions.
- ⚰️ **By Death (Sec 131):** The death of the surety automatically revokes a continuing guarantee for *future transactions*, unless there is a contract to the contrary.
**🎯 Conclusion:** Continuing guarantees are vital for ongoing business relationships but offer the surety an exit route for future liabilities.
