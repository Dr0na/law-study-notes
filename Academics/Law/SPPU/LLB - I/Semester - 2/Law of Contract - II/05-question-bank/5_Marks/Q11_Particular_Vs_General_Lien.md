### Q11. Distinguish between Particular Lien and General Lien.

**📖 Statutory Provision:** Sections 170 and 171 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872.

| 📊 Basis | 🔒 Particular Lien (Sec 170) | 🔐 General Lien (Sec 171) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Meaning** | Right to retain only those goods on which labour or skill has been exercised. | Right to retain any goods for any amount due — a general balance of account. |
| **Scope** | Narrow — limited to the specific goods. | Wide — extends to all goods in possession. |
| **Who can claim?** | Any bailee who has bestowed labour or skill on the goods (e.g., tailor, mechanic, printer). | Only specific categories: 🏦 Bankers, 📋 Factors, ⚓ Wharfingers, 👨‍⚖️ Attorneys, 📜 Policy brokers. |
| **Example** | A tailor can retain the stitched shirt until the stitching charges are paid, but not for old unpaid bills for different clothes. | A bank can retain all securities and valuables of a customer for any outstanding loan or balance. |

**🎯 Conclusion:** Particular lien is the default right of every bailee, while general lien is a privileged right granted only to specific professional bailees by virtue of trade custom or law.
