## 🛒 Q6. Distinguish between 'Condition' and 'Warranty'. Discuss the various implied conditions in a contract of sale.

### 1. Meaning of Condition and Warranty
Under **Section 12** of the Sale of Goods Act, 1930, a stipulation in a contract of sale with reference to goods which are the subject thereof may be a condition or a warranty.

- **Condition [Sec 12(2)]:** A condition is a stipulation *essential* to the main purpose of the contract. Its breach gives the aggrieved party a right to treat the contract as repudiated (cancelled), reject the goods, and claim damages.
- **Warranty [Sec 12(3)]:** A warranty is a stipulation *collateral* to the main purpose of the contract. Its breach gives rise to a claim for damages but NOT to a right to reject the goods and treat the contract as repudiated.

### 2. Implied Conditions
Implied conditions are presumed by law to be present in the contract, even if they have not been expressly agreed upon by the parties. The various implied conditions are:

1. **Condition as to Title (Sec 14(a)):**
   There is an implied condition that the seller has the right to sell the goods (in case of a sale) or will have the right to sell the goods when the property is to pass (in an agreement to sell). If the seller's title turns out to be defective, the buyer can reject the goods and recover the price.
   *(Case: Rowland vs. Divall)*.

2. **Sale by Description (Sec 15):**
   Where there is a contract for the sale of goods by description, there is an implied condition that the goods shall correspond with the description. If the buyer asks for a "copper vessel," a brass vessel will not suffice.

3. **Sale by Sample (Sec 17):**
   In the case of a contract for sale by sample, there are implied conditions that:
   - The bulk shall correspond with the sample in quality.
   - The buyer shall have a reasonable opportunity of comparing the bulk with the sample.
   - The goods shall be free from any defect rendering them unmerchantable, which would not be apparent on a reasonable examination of the sample.

4. **Sale by Sample as well as Description (Sec 15):**
   If the sale is by sample as well as by description, the goods must correspond with BOTH the sample and the description.

5. **Condition as to Quality or Fitness (Sec 16(1)):**
   Generally, there is no implied condition as to the quality or fitness for any particular purpose (Caveat Emptor). However, if the buyer makes known to the seller the particular purpose for which goods are required, and relies on the seller's skill/judgment, and the goods are of a description which it is in the course of the seller's business to supply, there is an implied condition that the goods shall be reasonably fit for that purpose.

6. **Condition as to Merchantability (Sec 16(2)):**
   Where goods are bought by description from a seller who deals in goods of that description, there is an implied condition that the goods shall be of merchantable quality (i.e., commercially saleable under that description).

### 3. Conclusion
Implied conditions form the bedrock of consumer protection in commercial transactions. They ensure that buyers receive goods that meet fundamental legal and commercial expectations without needing to explicitly negotiate every minute detail.
