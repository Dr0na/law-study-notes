## 🤝 Q3. Define Partnership. Explain how "Mutual Agency" is the true test of partnership.

### 1. Definition and Essentials
**Section 4** of the Indian Partnership Act, 1932: *"Partnership is the relation between persons who have agreed to share the profits of a business carried on by all or any of them acting for all."*

**5 Essentials:**
1. Association of two or more persons.
2. Agreement.
3. Carrying on a Business.
4. Sharing of profits.
5. **Mutual Agency.**

### 2. The True Test: Mutual Agency
Earlier, sharing of profits was considered the absolute test of a partnership. However, the landmark English case **Cox vs. Hickman (1860)** established that profit-sharing is only *prima facie* evidence. The conclusive true test is **Mutual Agency**.

### 3. What is Mutual Agency?
The phrase *"carried on by all or any of them acting for all"* signifies mutual agency. 
- Every partner is both an **Agent** and a **Principal**.
- **As an Agent:** A partner can bind the firm and all other partners by their acts done in the ordinary course of business.
- **As a Principal:** A partner is bound by the acts of other partners.

### 4. Why Profit Sharing is Not Conclusive (Exceptions)
Section 6 states that sharing profits does not automatically make someone a partner. Examples:
- A lender receiving a share of profits (as interest).
- A servant or agent receiving a share of profits as remuneration.
- A widow or child of a deceased partner receiving an annuity.
- A previous owner receiving a share of profits as consideration for selling goodwill.
*In none of these cases does the receiver have the right to bind the business (no agency).*

### 5. Conclusion
To determine if a partnership exists, the court looks at the real relation between the parties. If the element of mutual agency (the power to bind and be bound) is missing, there is no partnership, regardless of profit sharing.
