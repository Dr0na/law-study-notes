## 🛡️ Q6. What is a Contract of Guarantee? Discuss the rights of a surety against the principal debtor, the creditor, and the co-sureties.

### 1. Introduction and Definition
A contract of guarantee creates a tripartite relationship designed to provide security to a creditor.
**Section 126** of the Indian Contract Act, 1872: *"A contract to perform the promise, or discharge the liability, of a third person in case of his default, is called a contract of guarantee."*

**Parties:**
- 🏦 **Creditor** — the person to whom the guarantee is given.
- 💳 **Principal Debtor (PD)** — the person whose default is guaranteed.
- 🛡️ **Surety (Guarantor)** — the person who gives the guarantee.

### 2. Rights of Surety Against the Principal Debtor

#### I. Right of Subrogation (Sec 140)
Where a guaranteed debt has become due, or default of the principal debtor has taken place, the surety, upon payment or performance of all that they are liable for, is invested with **all the rights which the creditor had against the principal debtor.**
- The surety "steps into the shoes" of the creditor.
- *Example:* If A (surety) pays ₹1,00,000 to the bank (creditor) on behalf of B (PD), A can now sue B for ₹1,00,000 in exactly the same capacity as the bank could have.

#### II. Right to Indemnity (Sec 145)
In every contract of guarantee, there is an **implied promise** by the principal debtor to indemnify the surety.
- The surety is entitled to recover from the principal debtor whatever sum they have **rightfully paid** under the guarantee.
- This right exists even if there is no express contract of indemnity between the surety and PD.

### 3. Rights of Surety Against the Creditor

#### I. Right to Securities (Sec 141)
A surety is entitled to the benefit of **every security** which the creditor has against the principal debtor **at the time** when the contract of suretyship is entered into.
- If the creditor loses, or without the consent of the surety, parts with such security, the surety is **discharged** to the extent of the value of the security.
- *(Case: Amrit Lal Goverdhan Lalan vs. State Bank of India — surety discharged when bank lost the security).*

#### II. Right to Set-Off
If the principal debtor has any counterclaim or set-off against the creditor, the surety can also claim the benefit of such set-off.

#### III. Right to Share in Dividend
In case of insolvency of the principal debtor, the surety has a right to stand in the place of the creditor and prove the debt against the insolvent estate, and to receive any dividend declared on such debt.

### 4. Rights of Surety Against Co-Sureties

#### I. Right of Contribution (Sec 146)
When two or more persons are co-sureties for the same debt, they are **liable to contribute equally** to the extent of the default, subject to any contract to the contrary.
- *Example:* A, B, and C are co-sureties for D for ₹30,000. D defaults. Each of A, B, and C is liable to pay ₹10,000.

#### II. Contribution When Bound in Different Sums (Sec 147)
Co-sureties who are bound in different sums are liable to pay equally **as far as the limits of their respective obligations permit.**
- *Example:* A is surety for ₹10,000, B for ₹20,000, C for ₹40,000 for a debt of ₹30,000. A pays ₹10,000 (max limit), B pays ₹10,000, C pays ₹10,000.

#### III. Release of One Co-Surety (Sec 138)
If the creditor releases one of the co-sureties, the **other co-sureties are NOT discharged.** However, the released co-surety is also NOT freed from responsibility towards the other co-sureties (i.e., they must still contribute their share).

### 5. Conclusion
The law provides a comprehensive framework of rights to the surety, recognizing that the surety undertakes a significant risk out of goodwill. The rights of subrogation, indemnity, and contribution ensure that the surety is not left bearing the financial burden alone and can recover their outlay from the actual defaulter.
