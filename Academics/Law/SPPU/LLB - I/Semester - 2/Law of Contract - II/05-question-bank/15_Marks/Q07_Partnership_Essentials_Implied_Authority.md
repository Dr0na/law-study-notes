## 🏢 Q7. Define Partnership. Discuss its essential elements. Explain the implied authority of a partner and the acts which fall outside implied authority.

### 1. Introduction and Definition
Partnership is one of the oldest forms of business organization, governed by the **Indian Partnership Act, 1932.**
**Section 4:** *"Partnership is the relation between persons who have agreed to share the profits of a business carried on by all or any of them acting for all."*

Persons who have entered into partnership are called **"partners"** individually and a **"firm"** collectively. The name under which their business is carried on is the **"firm name."**

### 2. Essential Elements of Partnership (5 Essentials)

1. **Association of Two or More Persons:**
   - There must be at least two competent persons (persons capable of entering into a contract).
   - Maximum limit: 50 (as prescribed by the Companies Act).

2. **Agreement:**
   - Partnership arises from **contract, not from status.**
   - It may be oral or written. A Hindu Undivided Family (HUF) business is NOT a partnership because it arises from status.

3. **Business:**
   - The agreement must be to carry on some **business** — defined to include every trade, occupation, and profession.
   - Mere co-ownership of property does NOT constitute partnership.

4. **Sharing of Profits:**
   - The agreement must include sharing of profits.
   - ⚠️ However, sharing of profits is only *prima facie* evidence, NOT conclusive proof.

5. **Mutual Agency (The True Test — Sec 18):**
   - *"The business must be carried on by all or any of them acting for all."*
   - Every partner is simultaneously an **agent** (can bind the firm) and a **principal** (bound by other partners' acts).
   - *(Case: Cox vs. Hickman — profit sharing alone is insufficient; mutual agency is the true test.)*

### 3. Implied Authority of a Partner (Sec 19)

**Section 19** states: *"The act of a partner which is done to carry on, in the usual way, business of the kind carried on by the firm, binds the firm."*

**Acts within Implied Authority (a partner CAN do):**
1. Buying and selling goods on behalf of the firm (in a trading firm).
2. Receiving payments on behalf of the firm.
3. Hiring and firing employees.
4. Borrowing money on the credit of the firm (in a trading firm).
5. Drawing, accepting, and endorsing negotiable instruments.
6. Engaging a solicitor to defend actions against the firm.

### 4. Acts OUTSIDE Implied Authority (Sec 19(2))

A partner has **NO implied authority** to do the following acts **unless expressly authorized**:

1. 🏠 **Acquiring immovable property** on behalf of the firm.
2. ⚖️ **Submitting disputes to arbitration** relating to the business of the firm.
3. 🏦 **Opening a bank account** on behalf of the firm in the partner's own name.
4. 🤝 **Compromising or relinquishing** any claim or portion of a claim by the firm.
5. 📤 **Withdrawing a suit or proceeding** filed on behalf of the firm.
6. 📝 **Admitting any liability** in a suit or proceeding against the firm.
7. 📜 **Entering into a partnership** on behalf of the firm.
8. 🏗️ **Transferring immovable property** belonging to the firm.

### 5. Mode of Exercising Authority
The authority of a partner can be restricted by an agreement between the partners. However, any act done by a partner which falls within their apparent authority binds the firm to third parties who deal in good faith and without knowledge of the restriction (Sec 22).

### 6. Conclusion
Partnership is built on trust, agreement, and mutual agency. The implied authority enables smooth day-to-day operations, while the statutory restrictions on implied authority protect the firm from unauthorized actions by individual partners that could have far-reaching consequences.
