# Comparison: Indemnity vs. Guarantee

**Context:** Module 1 & Module 2 of Indian Contract Act, 1872. This is a very frequent 5-mark / "Distinguish Between" question.

| Basis of Difference | Contract of Indemnity (Sec 124) | Contract of Guarantee (Sec 126) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Meaning** | A contract to save the other party from loss caused by the promisor or any other person. | A contract to perform the promise or discharge the liability of a third person in case of his default. |
| **Number of Parties** | **Two Parties:** <br>1. Indemnifier<br>2. Indemnity Holder | **Three Parties:** <br>1. Creditor<br>2. Principal Debtor<br>3. Surety |
| **Number of Contracts**| **One Contract:** <br>Between the Indemnifier and the Indemnity Holder. | **Three Contracts:** <br>1. Creditor & Debtor<br>2. Creditor & Surety<br>3. Surety & Debtor |
| **Nature of Liability**| The liability of the indemnifier is **primary** and **independent**. | The liability of the surety is **secondary** and **conditional** (arises only if the principal debtor defaults). |
| **Commencement of Liability** | Arises when the contingent event occurs or liability becomes absolute (loss is suffered or imminent). | Arises as soon as the Principal Debtor makes a default. |
| **Object / Purpose** | The purpose is the **reimbursement** of a potential loss. | The purpose is to provide **security** to the creditor for a debt. |
| **Request of Debtor** | It is not necessary for the indemnifier to act at the request of the third party. | The surety generally gives the guarantee at the request of the Principal Debtor. |
| **Right to Sue Third Party** | The indemnifier cannot sue the third party in his own name (unless there is an assignment in his favor). | Once the surety pays the debt, he steps into the shoes of the creditor (Right of Subrogation) and can sue the principal debtor in his own name. |

---
**Exam Tip:** 
Always start your answer by defining both terms with their respective section numbers (Sec 124 and Sec 126) before drawing the table.
