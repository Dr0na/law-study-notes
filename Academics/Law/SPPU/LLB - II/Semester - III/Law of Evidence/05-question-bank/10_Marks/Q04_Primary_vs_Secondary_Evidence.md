# Q4. Distinguish between Primary and Secondary Evidence. Under what circumstances is Secondary Evidence admissible?

## 1. Definitions (BSS Sections 57 & 58)
- **Primary Evidence (Sec. 57)**: The original document produced for court inspection.
- **Secondary Evidence (Sec. 58)**: Copies, certified copies, or oral accounts of the document.

## 2. Best Evidence Rule
Evidence law is governed by the "best evidence rule," which dictates that primary evidence must be produced to prove a document's contents.

## 3. Conditions for Secondary Evidence Admissibility (Sec. 60 BSS)
Secondary evidence is admissible only under specific conditions:
1. When the original is in the possession of the opposite party who fails to produce it after notice.
2. When the original is lost or destroyed.
3. When the original is immovable (e.g., inscriptions on a wall).
4. When the original is a public document.

## 4. Conclusion
Secondary evidence is an exception that requires the proponent to prove the unavailability of the original document.
