# Q1. Explain the Doctrine of Res Gestae under BSS, 2023.

## 1. Introduction & Statutory Provision
Section 4 of BSS (formerly Section 6 IEA) codifies the Doctrine of Res Gestae:
> "Facts which, though not in issue, are so connected with a fact in issue as to form part of the same transaction, are relevant, whether they occurred at the same time and place or at different times and places."

Res Gestae is a Latin term meaning "things done." It is a major exception to the hearsay rule.

## 2. Essential Ingredients
For facts or statements to be admissible under Res Gestae:
1. They must form part of the **same transaction**.
2. The statement must be **spontaneous and contemporaneous** with the event, leaving no time for reflection or fabrication.
3. It must explain or illustrate the main transaction.

## 3. Landmark Case Laws
- *R v. Bedingfield (1879)*: The strict English view. A woman’s neck was cut and she ran out of the room crying "Oh dear, see what Bedingfield has done to me!" Held inadmissible as the transaction was completed.
- *R v. Foster (1834)*: A witness heard a groan and later received a statement from the deceased explaining the accident. Held admissible.
- *Sukhar v. State of UP (1999)*: The Supreme Court held that the statement must be spontaneous to exclude the possibility of concoction.

## 4. Conclusion
Res Gestae is a crucial rule that allows the court to reconstruct the crime scene through immediate reactions, bypassing the hearsay bar.
