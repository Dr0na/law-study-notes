# Q2. Explain the admissibility and evidentiary value of a Dying Declaration under BSS, 2023.

## 1. Introduction & Statutory Provision
Under Section 26(1) of BSS (formerly Section 32(1) IEA), a statement made by a person as to the cause of his death, or as to any of the circumstances of the transaction which resulted in his death, is relevant in cases in which the cause of that person's death comes into question.

## 2. Evidentiary Value & Sole Basis of Conviction
- **Nemo moriturus praesumitur mentiri**: A dying person is presumed not to lie.
- **Sole Basis of Conviction**: The Supreme Court in *Kaushal Rao v. State of Bombay* ruled that a dying declaration can form the sole basis of conviction if:
  1. It is voluntary and truthful.
  2. The declarant was in a fit mental condition (verified by medical certificate).
  3. It is free from tutoring or prompting.

## 3. Circumstances where Corroboration is Required
- If there are multiple conflicting dying declarations.
- If the declarant was in a semi-conscious state.
- If the statement is suspicious or prompted.

## 4. Conclusion
A dying declaration is highly valued, but courts must scrutinize it to ensure its voluntary nature.
