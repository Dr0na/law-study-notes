# Q1. Explain "May Presume", "Shall Presume", and "Conclusive Proof" under BSS, 2023.

## Statutory Definitions (Section 2 BSS)
- **May Presume (Sec. 2(h))**: The Court has discretion. It may either regard a fact as proved, unless and until it is disproved, or may call for proof of it. (Presumption of Fact).
- **Shall Presume (Sec. 2(l))**: The Court has no discretion. It *must* regard a fact as proved, unless and until it is disproved. (Presumption of Law).
- **Conclusive Proof (Sec. 2(b))**: When one fact is declared to be conclusive proof of another, the Court shall, on proof of the one fact, regard the other as proved, and shall not allow evidence to be given to disprove it.

## Key Differences & Rebuttal
1. May Presume is discretionary and rebuttable (e.g., presumption that a person in possession of stolen goods is the thief).
2. Shall Presume is mandatory but rebuttable (e.g., presumption of genuineness of certified copies).
3. Conclusive Proof is mandatory and irrebuttable (e.g., legitimacy of a child born during wedlock).
