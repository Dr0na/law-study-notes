# Q4. Explain the rules of Burden of Proof and Presumptions in criminal trials under BSS, 2023.

## 1. Burden of Proof vs. Onus of Proof (BSS Secs. 104-105)
- **Burden of Proof (Sec. 104)**: Legal burden to establish the guilt of the accused beyond reasonable doubt. It is constant on the prosecution and never shifts.
- **Onus of Proof (Sec. 105)**: Evidentiary burden to lead evidence. Shifts from party to party as the trial develops.

## 2. Facts especially within Knowledge (Section 107 BSS)
When a fact is especially within the knowledge of a person, the burden of proving it is on them (e.g., showing a valid train ticket).
- Case: *Shambhu Nath Mehra v. State of Ajmer*: Section 106 IEA (Sec 107 BSS) only applies to unique personal facts and cannot shift the prosecution's primary burden.

## 3. Statutory Presumptions shifting the Burden
To protect victims in gender-based crimes, BSS contains mandatory presumptions:
- **Abetment of Suicide (Sec. 119)**: Cruelty within 7 years of marriage allows the court to presume abetment.
- **Dowry Death (Sec. 120)**: Harassment for dowry soon before death requires the court to presume the husband caused it. This shifts the legal burden to the husband to prove his innocence.

## 4. Conclusion
Burden of proof rules maintain the presumption of innocence in criminal trials, while specific presumptions shift the burden in special categories to ensure justice.
