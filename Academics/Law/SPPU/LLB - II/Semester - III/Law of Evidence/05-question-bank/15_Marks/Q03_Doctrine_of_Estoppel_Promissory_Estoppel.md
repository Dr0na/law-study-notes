# Q3. Discuss the Doctrine of Estoppel and Promissory Estoppel with landmark cases.

## 1. Doctrine of Estoppel (Section 121 BSS)
Estoppel is a rule of equity and evidence. When one person has, by declaration, act, or omission, intentionally caused another to believe a thing to be true and act on it, they cannot later deny its truth.
- Case: *Pickard v. Sears (1837)*: Established that willful representation precluding the party from denying it is the foundation of Estoppel.

## 2. Promissory Estoppel
An equitable doctrine enforcing a promise made by one party to another, which the other acted upon to their detriment, even without a contract.
- Case: *Motilal Padampat Sugar Mills v. State of UP (1979)*: The Supreme Court held that the government was bound by its promise to exempt a mill from sales tax, as the owner set up the factory acting on that promise.

## 3. Limitations of Estoppel
1. **No Estoppel against a Statute**: Estoppel cannot be used to defeat a law enacted by Parliament (e.g., minor agreements are void, minor cannot be estopped from pleading minority).
2. **No Estoppel against the Constitution**: Fundamental rights cannot be waived.

## 4. Conclusion
Estoppel is a shield to prevent injustice and bad faith in transactions, but it is subordinate to statutory and constitutional law.
