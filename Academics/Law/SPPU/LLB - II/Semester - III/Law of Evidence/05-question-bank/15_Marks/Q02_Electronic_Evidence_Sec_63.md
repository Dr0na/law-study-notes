# Q2. Explain the certification and admissibility requirements of Electronic Evidence under Section 63 of BSS, 2023.

## 1. Introduction
With BSS 2023, electronic and digital records (emails, messages, logs, cloud records) are put on the same footing as physical documents. Section 63 BSS (formerly Sec. 65B IEA) governs their admissibility.

## 2. Conditions for Admissibility
The electronic record is admissible if the following conditions are satisfied:
1. The computer output was produced during a period when the computer was regularly used.
2. The computer was operating properly.
3. The data reproduces or is derived from information fed into the computer in the ordinary course.

## 3. Mandatory Section 63 Certificate
Any secondary electronic evidence (like printouts or copies on flash drives) must be accompanied by a signed Certificate of authenticity. The certificate must:
- Identify the electronic record.
- Describe the device and manner of production.
- Be signed by a person in charge of the device or system.

## 4. Landmark Judicial Rulings
- *Anvar P.V. v. P.K. Basheer (2014)*: The Supreme Court held that the certificate is mandatory for secondary electronic records. Oral evidence cannot bypass this requirement.
- *Arjun Panditrao Khotkar v. Kailash (2020)*: Confirmed *Anvar P.V.* and clarified that if the original device (e.g., the phone itself) is produced in court, no certificate is needed.

## 5. Conclusion
Section 63 is a necessary gatekeeper to ensure digital evidence is authentic and free from manipulation or deepfakes.
