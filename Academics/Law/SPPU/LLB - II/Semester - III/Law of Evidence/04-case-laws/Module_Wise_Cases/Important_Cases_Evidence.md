# Module Wise Important Case Digest

## Module 1: Introduction to Law of Evidence
1.  *Hanumant v. State of Madhya Pradesh* (AIR 1952 SC 343)
    - **Facts**: Accused was prosecuted for conspiracy and forgery. The case rested entirely on circumstantial evidence.
    - **Issue**: Under what conditions can a conviction be based on circumstantial evidence?
    - **Held**: The Supreme Court laid down the 5 golden principles of circumstantial evidence. Guilt must be proved beyond reasonable doubt, and the chain of circumstances must exclude all other hypotheses.
2.  *Sharad Birdhichand Sarda v. State of Maharashtra* (1984 4 SCC 116)
    - **Facts**: Wife died of potassium cyanide poisoning. Prosecution alleged murder by husband; defense argued suicide.
    - **Issue**: Appreciation of circumstantial evidence in a case of poisoning.
    - **Held**: Re-affirmed *Hanumant* principles. If two views are possible, the view pointing to innocence must be accepted.

## Module 2: Relevance & Admissibility
1.  *Sukhar v. State of UP* (1999)
    - **Facts**: Accused shot the victim. Immediately after, a witness reached the spot and the victim named the accused as the shooter.
    - **Issue**: Is the witness's statement admissible as Res Gestae?
    - **Held**: Yes, the statement was spontaneous, contemporaneous, and formed part of the same transaction under BSS Section 4.
2.  *Pulukuri Kottaya v. Emperor* (AIR 1947 PC 67)
    - **Facts**: Accused in police custody stated: "I hid the spear used in the murder in a pile of straw." He then led the police and produced it.
    - **Issue**: Scope of "Discovery of Fact" exception.
    - **Held**: Only the discovery of the physical object (the spear) and its location is admissible. The confession "used in the murder" is inadmissible.
