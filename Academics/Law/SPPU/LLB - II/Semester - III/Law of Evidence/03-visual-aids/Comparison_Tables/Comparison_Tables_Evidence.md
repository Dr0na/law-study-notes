# Comparison Tables: Law of Evidence

This document contains key comparative tables to help distinguish important concepts in the Law of Evidence for SPPU LLB examinations.

---

## 1. Physical Facts vs. Psychological Facts (Sec. 2(f))

| Basis | Physical Facts | Psychological Facts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Definition** | Facts capable of being perceived by the five senses. | Mental conditions of which a person is conscious. |
| **Nature** | Objective and externally visible. | Subjective and internal to a person's mind. |
| **Examples** | A sound heard, a signature on a document, goods in a room. | Fraudulent intention, good faith, malice, reputation. |
| **Proof** | Direct observation, photos, or physical inspection. | Inferred from surrounding conduct and circumstantial facts. |

---

## 2. Presumptions: May Presume vs. Shall Presume vs. Conclusive Proof

| Basis | May Presume (Sec. 2(h)) | Shall Presume (Sec. 2(l)) | Conclusive Proof (Sec. 2(b)) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Discretion** | Discretionary presumption. The court *may* choose to presume. | Mandatory presumption. The court *must* presume. | Mandatory and binding presumption. |
| **Rebuttal** | Rebuttable by showing contrary evidence. | Rebuttable, but must be presumed until disproved. | Irrebuttable; no evidence allowed to disprove. |
| **Evidence** | Court can request independent proof upfront. | Court cannot request proof until opponent disproves it. | Proof of one fact immediately proves the other. |
| **Example** | Accomplice untrustworthiness (Sec. 117). | Genuineness of certified copies (Sec. 79). | Birth during marriage proving legitimacy (Sec. 114). |

---

## 3. Admissions vs. Confessions

| Basis | Admissions (Secs. 15-21) | Confessions (Secs. 22-24) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Scope** | Broad term. Statement suggesting any inference of fact. | Narrow term. Specific statement admitting guilt of an offence. |
| **Proceedings** | Relevant in both civil and criminal proceedings. | Relevant only in criminal proceedings. |
| **Admissibility** | Admissible even if made to police in civil transactions. | Inadmissible if made to police or under custody (unless Magistrate present). |
| **Evidentiary Value** | Not conclusive proof; acts as estoppel. | Substantive evidence; can form the sole basis of conviction. |
| **Maker** | Can be made by agents, parties, or third parties. | Must be made by the accused person themselves. |

---

## 4. Primary Evidence vs. Secondary Evidence

| Basis | Primary Evidence (Sec. 57) | Secondary Evidence (Sec. 58) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Definition** | The original document itself produced in court. | Copies, certified copies, or oral accounts of the document. |
| **Priority** | Highest priority (best evidence rule). | Lower priority (admitted only under exceptions). |
| **Notice** | No prior notice required to produce. | Requires prior notice to produce to the opponent (Sec. 60). |
| **Examples** | Original signed contract, original letter, primary server logs. | Photocopy, certified copy, oral testimony of someone who saw it. |

---

## 5. Burden of Proof vs. Onus of Proof

| Basis | Burden of Proof (Sec. 104) | Onus of Proof (Sec. 105) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Meaning** | Legal burden to establish the case. | Evidentiary onus to introduce evidence. |
| **Shifting** | Never shifts; remains fixed throughout. | Shifts continuously from one party to the other. |
| **Constant** | Constant on the prosecution (criminal) or plaintiff (civil). | Shifts depending on who would fail if no more evidence is given. |
| **Statute** | Governed by BSS Section 104. | Governed by BSS Section 105. |
