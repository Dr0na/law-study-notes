# Module 8: Doctrine of Estoppel & Improper Admission

## Introduction
Module 8 covers the Doctrine of Estoppel under Sections 121 to 123 of the BSS, its comparison with Res Judicata and waiver, and the consequences of improper admission or rejection of evidence under Section 169 of BSS. 

Estoppel is an important rule of equity and civil procedure designed to prevent injustice by holding parties to their representations.

---

## 1. The Doctrine of Estoppel (BSS Sections 121 to 123)

*   **Estoppel — Sec. 121 BSS (formerly Sec. 115 IEA)**: When one person has, by his declaration, act or omission, intentionally caused or permitted another person to believe a thing to be true and to act upon such belief, neither he nor his representative shall be allowed, in any suit or proceeding between himself and such person or his representative, to deny the truth of that thing.
    - *Meaning*: It stops or debars a person from blowing hot and cold at the same time. It is a rule of pleading and evidence.
    - *Illustration*: A intentionally and falsely leads B to believe that certain land belongs to A, and thereby induces B to buy and pay for it. The land afterwards becomes the property of A, and A seeks to set aside the sale on the ground that, at the time of the sale, he had no title. He must not be allowed to prove his want of title.
*   **Tenant and Licensee Estoppel — Sec. 122 BSS**: No tenant of immovable property, or person claiming through such tenant, shall, during the continuance of the tenancy, be permitted to deny that the landlord of such tenant had, at the beginning of the tenancy, a title to such immovable property.
*   **Acceptor of Bill of Exchange Estoppel — Sec. 123 BSS**: No acceptor of a bill of exchange shall be permitted to deny that the drawer had authority to draw such bill or to endorse it.

---

## 2. Promissory Estoppel

Promissory Estoppel is an equitable doctrine developed by courts to enforce promises made by one party to another, which the other party acted upon to their detriment, even in the absence of a formal contract.
*   **Key Case**: *Motilal Padampat Sugar Mills v. State of UP (1979)*: The Supreme Court held that the government was bound by its promise to exempt the sugar mill from sales tax, as the mill owner had set up the factory acting on that promise. Promissory Estoppel applies against the government in its administrative capacity.
*   **Exception**: No estoppel lies against the law or the Constitution (e.g., the government cannot be estopped from enforcing a statute).

---

## 3. Comparison: Estoppel, Res Judicata, and Waiver

| Feature | Estoppel | Res Judicata | Waiver |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nature | Rule of Evidence / Equity | Rule of Public Policy / Civil Procedure | Voluntary relinquishment of a known right |
| Source | Party's representations (words/actions) | Court's final decision/judgment | Party's choice to abandon a right |
| Purpose | Prevents inconsistent conduct in court | Prevents multiplicity of litigation | Allows a party to give up a procedural benefit |
| Application | Applies to representations of fact | Applies to matters directly in issue | Applies to rights meant for party benefit |

## 4. Effect of Improper Admission and Rejection of Evidence — Sec. 169 BSS (formerly Sec. 167 IEA)

The improper admission or rejection of evidence shall not be ground of itself for a new trial or reversal of any decision in any case:
1. If it appears to the Court that, independently of the evidence objected to and admitted, there was sufficient evidence to justify the decision.
2. If, reject the evidence, the rejected evidence, if received, ought not to have varied the decision.

*   **Evidentiary Rule**: A trial decision will not be set aside on appeal simply because the trial judge admitted some inadmissible evidence or rejected some admissible evidence, provided the overall record contains enough valid evidence to support the judgment.

---

## 5. Landmark Judgments

1.  **Pickard v. Sears (1837 6 Ad & El 469)**
    - *Ratio*: The foundation case of Estoppel in English law. Held that where a party by their conduct willfully causes another to believe in a state of things, and induces them to act on it, the former is precluded from averring a different state of things.
2.  **Sharat Chunder Dey v. Gopal Chunder Laha (1892)**
    - *Ratio*: Clarified that representation under estoppel need not be fraudulent; even if made under mistake or innocently, if the other party acted on it and suffered detriment, estoppel applies.

## 6. Model Answers (10 Marks & 15 Marks)

### A. Model 10 Marks Answer: Explain Section 169 BSS regarding the effect of Improper Admission and Rejection of Evidence.
**Answer**:
1.  **Statutory Principle**: Section 169 of BSS establishes that procedural errors regarding admission or rejection of evidence are not fatal to a decision, provided substantial justice has been done.
2.  **Improper Admission**:
    - If some hearsay or irrelevant evidence was wrongly admitted by the trial court, the appellate court will not order a retrial if there is other sufficient, independent, and legally admissible evidence on record to justify the conviction or decree.
3.  **Improper Rejection**:
    - If some valid evidence was wrongly rejected by the trial court, the appellate court will not interfere if it finds that even if that rejected evidence had been admitted, it would not have changed the final outcome of the case.
4.  **Rationale**: To prevent unnecessary delays, endless litigation, and retrials over minor technical errors that do not affect the core merits of the case.
5.  **Conclusion**: Section 169 is a pragmatic rule that prioritizes substantive justice over technical procedural compliance.

### B. Model 15 Marks Answer: Critically analyze the Doctrine of Estoppel under BSS, 2023. Can Estoppel be pleaded against the State or against a Statute?
**Answer**:
1.  **Introduction**: Section 121 of BSS codifies the Doctrine of Estoppel, which prevents a person from denying a statement of fact they previously made, after another person has acted on it to their detriment.
2.  **Essentials of Estoppel**:
    - There must be a representation made by a person to another through a declaration, act, or omission.
    - The representation must be intentional, causing the other person to believe it to be true.
    - The other person must act upon such belief to their detriment.
3.  **Estoppel against the State (Promissory Estoppel)**:
    - *Motilal Padampat case*: Promissory Estoppel can be pleaded against the State when public authorities make promises (like tax holidays or land grants) acting in an administrative capacity, and citizens act on those promises.
4.  **No Estoppel against a Statute (No Estoppel against Law)**:
    - Estoppel cannot be used to defeat the law. If a representation is contrary to an Act of Parliament, the person making the representation is not bound by it.
    - *Example*: A minor represents himself as a major and enters a contract. He cannot be estopped from pleading minority later to declare the contract void (*Mohori Bibee v. Dharmodas Ghose*), because the law declares minor agreements void ab initio.
5.  **No Estoppel against the Constitution**:
    - Fundamental rights cannot be waived, and no estoppel can lie against constitutional provisions (*Olga Tellis v. Bombay Municipal Corporation*).
6.  **Conclusion**: Estoppel is a valuable shield of equity, but it must bow down to statutory and constitutional mandates.
