# Module 4: Documentary Evidence & Electronic Records

## Introduction
Documentary evidence is governed by Sections 56 to 103 of the Bharatiya Sakshya Sanhita, 2023 (BSS). Under BSS, documents are given precedence over oral evidence, and oral agreements cannot be admitted to contradict the terms of a written document (exclusion of oral by documentary evidence). 

Additionally, BSS has significantly modernized the legal framework by placing electronic and digital records on the same footing as physical documents.

---

## 1. Primary and Secondary Evidence (BSS Sections 57 to 60)

### A. Primary Evidence — Sec. 57 BSS
Primary evidence means the document itself produced for the inspection of the Court.
*   **Meaning**: The original source document.
*   **Explanation**: Where a document is executed in several parts, each part is primary evidence of the document. Where a document is executed in counterpart, each counterpart is primary evidence against the party executing it.

### B. Secondary Evidence — Sec. 58 BSS
Secondary evidence means and includes:
1. Certified copies.
2. Copies made from the original by mechanical processes which in themselves assure the accuracy of the copy.
3. Copies made from or compared with the original.
4. Counterparts of documents as against the parties who did not execute them.
5. Oral accounts of the contents of a document given by some person who has himself seen it.

### C. Rule of Admissibility — Sec. 60 BSS
Secondary evidence may be given of the existence, condition, or contents of a document only under specific circumstances, such as:
- When the original is in the possession of the adverse party who refuses to produce it.
- When the original has been destroyed or lost.
- When the original is of such a nature that it cannot be easily moved (e.g., an inscription on a wall).
- When the original is a public document.

---

## 2. Electronic and Digital Records — Secs. 61 to 63 BSS

BSS, 2023 has overhauled the admissibility of electronic evidence to simplify procedures while maintaining integrity:

- **Section 61 BSS**: Establishes that the contents of electronic records may be proved in accordance with the provisions of Section 62.
- **Section 62 BSS**: Places electronic records under the definition of primary evidence if they are original media or simultaneous outputs.
- **Section 63 BSS (formerly Sec. 65B of IEA)**: Sets out the conditions for admissibility of electronic records. An electronic record (emails, phone logs, CCTV, hard drives) is admissible as evidence without further proof or production of the original, provided it is accompanied by a **Certificate** signed by a person in charge of the device or system, declaring that the computer/system was operating properly and the data was not tampered with.

*   **Key Case**: *Anvar P.V. v. P.K. Basheer (2014)*: The Supreme Court held that the certificate under Section 65B IEA (now Sec. 63 BSS) is mandatory for admissibility of secondary electronic evidence.
*   **Key Case**: *Arjun Panditrao Khotkar v. Kailash Kushanrao Gorantyal (2020)*: Clarified that the certificate is a condition precedent to admissibility when the original device cannot be produced in court.

## 3. Public and Private Documents — Secs. 74 to 77 BSS

- **Public Documents (Sec. 74 BSS)**: Documents forming the acts or records of the acts of sovereign authorities, official bodies, tribunals, and public officers (e.g., acts, birth certificates, police reports, records of court).
- **Private Documents (Sec. 75 BSS)**: All other documents that are not public documents (e.g., contracts, wills, private letters).
- **Evidentiary Value**: Public documents can be proved by producing certified copies (Sec. 77 BSS). Private documents must be proved by producing the original and proving execution (attestation).

---

## 4. Exclusion of Oral by Documentary Evidence — Secs. 94 to 103 BSS

- **Section 94 BSS (formerly Sec. 91 IEA)**: When the terms of a contract, grant, or disposition of property have been reduced to the form of a document, no evidence shall be given in proof of such terms except the document itself or secondary evidence of its contents where admissible.
- **Section 95 BSS (formerly Sec. 92 IEA)**: When the terms of any such contract, etc. have been proved by the document, no evidence of any oral agreement or statement shall be admitted as between the parties for the purpose of contradicting, varying, adding to, or subtracting from its terms.
- **Exceptions**: Oral evidence can be given to show fraud, intimidation, illegality, want of due execution, or separate oral agreements where the document is silent (collateral agreements).

## 6. Model Answers (10 Marks & 15 Marks)

### A. Model 10 Marks Answer: Distinguish between Primary and Secondary Evidence. Under what conditions is Secondary Evidence admissible?
**Answer**:
1.  **Definitions**:
    - **Primary Evidence (Sec. 57 BSS)**: The original document itself produced for court inspection.
    - **Secondary Evidence (Sec. 58 BSS)**: Copies, certified duplicates, or oral accounts of the document.
2.  **Evidentiary Weight**: Primary evidence is the best evidence and is highly preferred. Secondary evidence is of lower priority and is only admitted under exceptions.
3.  **Conditions for Admissibility of Secondary Evidence (Sec. 60 BSS)**:
    - **Lost or Destroyed**: When the original has been lost or destroyed.
    - **Possession of Opponent**: When the original is with the opposite party who has been given notice to produce it but fails to do so.
    - **Immovable Original**: When the original cannot be easily moved (e.g., inscription on a rock).
    - **Public Document**: When the original is a public document.
4.  **Conclusion**: Secondary evidence is admissible only when the absence of the primary document is satisfactorily explained to the court.

### B. Model 15 Marks Answer: Critically examine the legal framework governing the admissibility of Electronic and Digital Records under Section 63 of BSS, 2023.
**Answer**:
1.  **Introduction**: Electronic records are now the most common form of evidence. BSS Section 61 to 63 provides a specialized statutory code for their admissibility, recognizing the risk of digital tampering.
2.  **Admissibility Conditions under Section 63 BSS**:
    - The computer output containing the information was produced by the computer during the period over which the computer was used regularly to store or process information.
    - The computer was operating properly.
    - The information contained in the electronic record reproduces or is derived from the information fed into the computer in the ordinary course of activities.
3.  **The Certificate Requirement**:
    - Any secondary electronic record (like a printout of an email or copy of a WhatsApp chat on a CD) must be accompanied by a **Section 63 Certificate**.
    - The certificate must identify the electronic record, describe the manner of its production, give details of the device, and be signed by a person in charge of the device.
4.  **Landmark Judicial Trends**:
    - *Anvar P.V. v. P.K. Basheer (2014)*: The Supreme Court ruled that Section 65B of IEA (equivalent to Section 63 BSS) is a complete code, and oral evidence cannot bypass it.
    - *Arjun Panditrao Khotkar v. Kailash Kushanrao Gorantyal (2020)*: The court ruled that the certificate is mandatory for secondary electronic records, but if the original device is brought to court, no certificate is needed.
5.  **BSS, 2023 Improvements**: BSS clarifies that if electronic records are stored on primary servers or clouds in the ordinary course, they can be treated as primary evidence directly.
6.  **Conclusion**: The certification requirement is a necessary safeguard to prevent deepfakes and manipulated digital evidence from polluting the judicial record.
