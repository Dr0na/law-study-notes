### ⚖️ Q4. Distinguish between 'Acquittal' and 'Discharge'.

**(Refer to Sections 250, 258, 262 & 272 of the BNSS, 2023)**

| 📊 Basis | 🔓 Discharge | 🏆 Acquittal |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Stage of Trial** | Occurs before the charges are formally framed against the accused. | Occurs after the charges are framed, evidence is taken, and trial is fully conducted. |
| **Prima Facie Check** | Accused is discharged because there is no *prima facie* case to proceed against them. | Accused is acquitted because the prosecution failed to prove guilt beyond reasonable doubt. |
| **Effect on Future Trial**| The accused **can be re-prosecuted** for the same offence if new or further evidence is found. | The accused **cannot be tried again** for the same offence (*Autrefois Acquit* / Art. 20(2)). |
| **Status of Guilt** | Does not imply innocence; it only indicates the lack of sufficient material to start a trial. | Clear declaration of innocence (or failure of prosecution to prove guilt) under the law. |
| **Revisional Intervention**| Sessions Court/High Court can order a inquiry into a discharged case (Sec. 438). | An acquittal can only be challenged via an Appeal (Sec. 419), not through revision. |
