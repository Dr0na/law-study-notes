### Q4. What is 'Anticipatory Bail'? Under what circumstances can it be granted?

**📖 Statutory Provision: Section 482 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023**

**1. Meaning**: Anticipatory bail is a direction issued by the High Court or Court of Session to release a person on bail *in the event of their arrest* for a non-bailable offence.

**2. Key Conditions for Grant**:
- The applicant must show a reasonable **apprehension of arrest** on accusations of committing a non-bailable offence.
- The court evaluates the nature and gravity of the accusation, the applicant's antecedents (prior record), and the likelihood of fleeing justice.

**3. Typical Restrictive Conditions**:
- The applicant must make themselves available for police interrogation as required.
- The applicant must not directly or indirectly make any inducement, threat, or promise to witnesses.
- The applicant cannot leave India without prior permission of the court.
- **Landmark Case**: *Gurbaksh Singh Sibbia v. State of Punjab* (1980) — Court discretion is wide but must protect personal liberty under Article 21.

**🎯 Conclusion**: Anticipatory bail prevents the harassment of individuals through motivated arrests and acts as a shield for personal liberty until guilt is established.
