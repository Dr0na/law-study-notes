## 🧒 Q5. Explain the constitution, powers, and functions of the Child Welfare Committee (CWC) under the Juvenile Justice Act, 2015.

### 1. Introduction & Definition
The Child Welfare Committee (CWC) is a statutory, quasi-judicial body established under **Section 27** of the Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015. It operates as a bench of Magistrates and has final authority over matters concerning the care, protection, rehabilitation, and social reintegration of **Children in Need of Care and Protection (CNCP)**.

### 2. Constitution, Powers and Functions (3 Core Areas)

#### I. Constitution of the Committee (Sec. 27)
- **Establishment**: The State Government must establish one or more CWCs in every district by notification.
- **Composition (5 Members)**:
  - **Chairperson**: An eminent person with experience in child welfare.
  - **Four Members**: Professionals, including at least one woman, with at least 7 years of active experience in child welfare (education, health, or psychology).
- **Disqualification**: Persons running Child Care Institutions, or having any conflict of interest (e.g., employment in a commercial adoption agency), cannot be members.

#### II. Powers of the CWC (Sec. 29)
- The CWC has exclusive authority to deal with all proceedings under the JJ Act relating to children in need of care and protection.
- It is vested with the powers of a **Judicial Magistrate First Class (JMFC)** or Metropolitan Magistrate under the CrPC/BNSS when conducting inquiries.
- Power to direct the Special Juvenile Police Unit (SJPU), Childline (1098), or NGOs to locate and produce children in distress.
- Power to order the temporary or long-term placement of a child in a Children's Home, Open Shelter, or Foster Care.
- Power to declare an orphaned, abandoned, or surrendered child legally free for adoption.

#### III. Functions and Responsibilities (Sec. 30)
The CWC performs several critical administrative and welfare functions:
1. **Taking Cognizance**: Receiving children produced before it by police, public servants, Childline, or social workers (under Sec. 31).
2. **Inquiry and Verification**: Conducting inquiries to verify the child's circumstances, family background, and protection needs.
3. **Restoration**: Restoring the child to their parents, guardians, or relatives after proper evaluation.
4. **Adoption Processing**: Declaring children legally free for adoption under CARA guidelines after ensuring no parents/guardians can be traced.
5. **Rehabilitation Plans**: Approving Individual Care Plans (ICPs) for children in Child Care Institutions (CCIs).
6. **CCI Monitoring**: Conducting inspection visits to Children's Homes and suggesting improvements in care standards.

### 3. Case Laws
- ***Sampurna Behrua v. Union of India* (2018) SC**: The Supreme Court noted that the CWC is the "backbone" of the JJ Act. It directed all states to immediately fill vacancies in CWCs, ensure members receive training, and conduct regular social audits of Children's Homes.
- ***Exploitation of Children in Orphanages in State of Tamil Nadu v. UOI* (2017)**: Highlighting that the CWC must monitor all CCIs in its district to prevent child exploitation and abuse.

### 4. Conclusion
The CWC represents a welfare-oriented, non-penal approach to child protection. By acting as a protective guardian, it ensures that neglected and abused children are rehabilitated through family restoration, institutional care, or secular adoption.
