## 🔍 Q3. Detail the statutory procedures for search and seizure under the BNSS, highlighting the new requirement of video recording.

### 1. Introduction & Definition
Search and Seizure are essential powers granted to investigating agencies to collect physical and digital evidence. 
- **Search**: The inspection of a person, place, or vehicle.
- **Seizure**: The taking into custody of property, documents, or devices.
These powers are regulated under **Sections 96 to 110** (general search warrants) and **Sections 185 to 186** (search by police officers during investigation) of the BNSS, 2023.

### 2. Procedures and Safegards (5 Key Steps)

#### I. Requisites of a Search Warrant (Sec. 96)
A search warrant is issued by a court when:
- The court believes a person will not produce documents called for.
- Such documents are not known to be in the possession of any person.
- A general inspection or search is necessary for the purposes of any inquiry, trial, or proceeding.

#### II. Search by Police Officer (Sec. 185)
- An investigating officer (IO) may conduct a search without a warrant if they have reasonable grounds to believe that something necessary for the investigation cannot be obtained without delay.
- The IO must record the grounds of belief in writing before conducting the search.

#### III. Presence of Witnesses (Sec. 103)
- Before making a search, the officer must call upon **two or more independent and respectable inhabitants** of the locality to attend and witness the search.
- The search must be conducted in their presence, and the list of all things seized must be prepared and signed by these witnesses.
- The occupant of the place searched must be permitted to attend and must be given a copy of the seizure list.

#### IV. Mandatory Video Recording (Section 105 — New Feature)
- Under the BNSS, 2023, the process of search and seizure (including the preparation of the seizure list) **must** be recorded through **audio-video electronic means** (e.g., mobile phones, cameras).
- The video recording must be forwarded to the Magistrate without delay to prevent tampering or plantation of evidence.

#### V. Personal Search of Women (Sec. 49 & 103)
- When a woman is to be searched, the search must be made by another **woman officer** with strict regard to decency.

### 3. Case Laws
- ***State of Maharashtra v. Natwarlal Damodardas Soni* (1980)**: The Supreme Court held that even if a search is illegal, the evidence collected during the search does not automatically become inadmissible if it is relevant. However, the court will inspect such evidence with greater caution.
- ***D.K. Basu v. State of West Bengal* (1997)**: Guidelines emphasize that search and seizure must respect the dignity and privacy of citizens, preventing police high-handedness.

### 4. Conclusion
The BNSS retains the rigorous witness safeguards of the old CrPC while introducing Section 105 video recording. This technological update increases police accountability, protects citizens from false implications, and strengthens the evidentiary value of seized goods in court.
