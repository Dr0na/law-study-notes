## ⚖️ Q1. Compare and contrast the Inquisitorial and Adversarial criminal justice systems. Discuss which system is followed in India.

### 1. Introduction & Definition
The criminal justice delivery mechanism is globally structured around two primary models: the **Adversarial (Accusatorial) System** and the **Inquisitorial System**. 
- **Adversarial System**: A common-law system where two opposing parties (Prosecution and Defence) present their cases before a neutral judge.
- **Inquisitorial System**: A civil-law system where the court/judge is actively involved in investigating the facts and determining the truth.

### 2. Distinguishing Features (5 Core Areas)

#### I. Role of the Judge
- **Adversarial**: The judge is a neutral referee or "passive umpire." They do not investigate independently. They evaluate only the evidence and arguments presented by the advocates.
- **Inquisitorial**: The judge is an active participant. They direct the police investigation, question witnesses, examine the accused, and lead the fact-finding process.

#### II. Burden of Proof and Presumption of Innocence
- **Adversarial**: The accused is presumed innocent until proven guilty. The burden of proof lies entirely on the prosecution to prove guilt **beyond reasonable doubt**.
- **Inquisitorial**: There is no strict presumption of innocence. The burden of proof is shared, and the accused is expected to cooperate with the court's inquiries.

#### III. Rights of the Accused
- **Adversarial**: Extensive procedural safeguards protect the accused, including the right to remain silent (Article 20(3) of the Indian Constitution) and the right to cross-examine prosecution witnesses.
- **Inquisitorial**: The accused has a more active role in participating in the inquiry and can be questioned extensively by the judge. The right to remain silent is limited.

#### IV. Role of Advocates
- **Adversarial**: Advocates play a dominant role. They collect evidence, summon witnesses, and conduct the questioning and cross-examination.
- **Inquisitorial**: Advocates play a secondary role. They suggest questions to the judge and submit legal briefs, but the judge conducts the actual examination of witnesses.

#### V. Focus and Outcome
- **Adversarial**: Focuses on whether the prosecution has met its legal burden of proof. It is party-driven.
- **Inquisitorial**: Focuses on discovering the objective truth of the event. It is court-driven.

---

### 3. The Indian Position
India follows the **Adversarial System** inherited from British common law. However, it incorporates several inquisitorial elements to ensure justice:
- **Section 165 of the BSA, 2023**: Empowers the judge to ask any question they please, in any form, to any witness or party, to discover relevant facts.
- **Section 349 of the BNSS, 2023**: Empowers the court to summon any person as a witness, or examine any person in attendance, at any stage of inquiry or trial.
- **Section 351 of the BNSS, 2023**: Allows the court to examine the accused to explain circumstances appearing in evidence against them.
- **Malimath Committee Report (2003)**: Recommended that the Indian system should adopt positive elements of the inquisitorial system (such as giving judges more power to search for truth) to address delays and low conviction rates.

### 4. Conclusion
While India is primarily adversarial, it is not a blind referee model. The Indian judiciary plays an active, corrective role to prevent miscarriage of justice, making it a modified adversarial system where truth-seeking is aided by magisterial powers.
