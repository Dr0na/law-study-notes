## 📜 Q2. Explain the procedure to be followed by a Magistrate on receiving a private complaint under the BNSS.

### 1. Introduction & Definition
A private complaint is one of the modes of initiating criminal proceedings under the BNSS. Under **Section 2(1)(h)**, a "Complaint" is an allegation made orally or in writing to a Magistrate, with a view to taking action, that some person, whether known or unknown, has committed an offence. It does not include a police report. The procedure upon receipt of a complaint is covered under **Sections 223 to 226 of the BNSS, 2023**.

### 2. Step-by-Step Procedure (4 Key Steps)

#### I. Examination of the Complainant (Sec. 223)
- **Oath**: Upon receiving a complaint, the Magistrate must examine the complainant and any present witnesses on oath.
- **Record**: The substance of the examination must be reduced to writing and signed by the complainant, the witnesses, and the Magistrate.
- **Exceptions (Where examination is not required)**:
  - If the complaint is made in writing by a public servant acting in the discharge of their official duties.
  - If the Magistrate makes over the case for inquiry or trial to another Magistrate under Section 212.

#### II. Postponement of Issue of Process (Sec. 223(2))
- If the accused resides at a place beyond the area in which the Magistrate exercises jurisdiction, the Magistrate **must** postpone the issue of process (summons/warrant).
- During this postponement, the Magistrate must:
  - Inquire into the case himself, or
  - Direct an investigation to be made by a police officer or other fit person to decide if there are sufficient grounds for proceeding.

#### III. Dismissal of Complaint (Sec. 224)
- After examining the complainant and witnesses, and considering the result of any inquiry/investigation, if the Magistrate finds **no sufficient ground** for proceeding, they shall dismiss the complaint.
- The Magistrate must briefly record the reasons for doing so.

#### IV. Commencement of Proceedings / Issue of Process (Sec. 225)
- If the Magistrate is of the opinion that there is **sufficient ground** for proceeding:
  - In a **Summons Case**: They will issue a summons for the attendance of the accused.
  - In a **Warrant Case**: They may issue a warrant or a summons for the accused to be produced or appear at a certain time.
- No summons or warrant shall be issued until a list of the prosecution witnesses has been filed.

### 3. Case Laws
- ***Lalita Kumari v. Govt. of UP* (2014)**: Clarified that if the police refuse to register an FIR under Section 154 (Sec. 173 BNSS), the aggrieved party has the right to approach the Magistrate under Section 156(3) (Sec. 175 BNSS) or file a private complaint under Section 200 (Sec. 223 BNSS).
- ***Chandra Deo Singh v. Prokash Chandra Bose* (1963)**: The Supreme Court held that the object of an inquiry under this stage is only to ascertain whether there is a prima facie case to call upon the accused to appear, not to conduct a full trial.

### 4. Conclusion
The private complaint mechanism provides a vital check against police inaction, allowing citizens direct access to the judiciary, while Sections 223-226 ensure that frivolous complaints are weeded out before the accused is summoned.
