## 🔒 Q4. Discuss the provisions relating to maintenance of wives, children, and parents under the BNSS, highlighting its secular nature.

### 1. Introduction & Definition
The provision for maintenance is a social justice measure designed to prevent vagrancy and destitution. Under **Section 144 of the BNSS, 2023** (formerly Section 125 of the CrPC, 1973), a summary procedure is provided to enable discarded wives, neglected children, and dependent parents to obtain a monthly allowance for their maintenance.

### 2. Entitlement and Enforcement (4 Key Elements)

#### I. Persons Entitled to Maintenance (Sec. 144(1))
A Judicial Magistrate First Class may order a person to pay monthly maintenance if that person, having **sufficient means**, neglects or refuses to maintain:
- Their **wife**, who is unable to maintain herself. (Wife includes a divorced wife who has not remarried).
- Their **legitimate or illegitimate minor child** (whether married or not), who is unable to maintain itself.
- Their **legitimate or illegitimate child** (not being a married daughter) who has attained majority, but is unable to maintain itself due to physical or mental abnormality.
- Their **father or mother**, who is unable to maintain themselves.

#### II. Essential Requisites for a Claim
1. **Sufficient Means**: The person against whom the claim is made must have the financial capacity or earning potential.
2. **Neglect or Refusal**: The person must have failed to fulfill their moral and legal obligation to support the claimant.
3. **Inability to Maintain**: The claimant must prove they do not have independent sources of income sufficient to support themselves.

#### III. Grounds for Denial of Maintenance to Wife (Sec. 144(4))
A wife is not entitled to receive maintenance if:
- She is living in adultery.
- She refuses to live with her husband without any sufficient reason.
- She and her husband are living separately by mutual consent.

#### IV. Enforcement of Order (Sec. 145 & 147)
- If a person fails without sufficient cause to comply with the maintenance order, the Magistrate can issue a warrant for levying the amount.
- The Magistrate may sentence such person to imprisonment for up to **1 month** or until payment is made, for every breach of the order.
- Application for execution must be made within **1 year** from the date it became due.

### 3. Secular Nature of the Provision
Section 144 is a **secular provision** that applies to all Indian citizens irrespective of their personal, religious, or customary laws.
- **Landmark Case**: *Mohd. Ahmed Khan v. Shah Bano Begum* (1985) — The Supreme Court held that Section 125 CrPC (Sec. 144 BNSS) overrides personal laws. A Muslim husband's obligation to maintain his divorced wife does not end with the *Iddat* period if she is unable to maintain herself.
- **Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Divorce) Act, 1986**: Enacted to limit the *Shah Bano* ruling, but the Supreme Court in *Daniel Latifi v. Union of India* (2001) interpreted it to mean that the husband must make "reasonable and fair provision" for the wife's entire life within the *Iddat* period, preserving her welfare.

### 4. Conclusion
Section 144 BNSS serves a humanitarian purpose. It provides a swift, inexpensive, and secular remedy to protect vulnerable family members from poverty, enforcing familial responsibility through magisterial powers.
