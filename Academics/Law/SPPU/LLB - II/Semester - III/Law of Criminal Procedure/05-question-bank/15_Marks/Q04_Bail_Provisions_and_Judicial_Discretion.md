## 🔒 Q4. Discuss the law relating to bail under the BNSS, 2023. Analyze the distinction between bail in bailable and non-bailable offences, and explain the guidelines for exercising judicial discretion.

### 1. Introduction
Bail is the temporary release of an accused person awaiting trial, conditional upon them lodging a sum of money or bond to guarantee their appearance in court. The concept of bail balances two competing interests: the **personal liberty of the individual** (presumed innocent until proven guilty under Article 21) and the **interest of society** in ensuring that the accused stands trial and does not tamper with evidence.

### 2. Definition (with Section number)
- **Section 2(1)(b) of the BNSS, 2023** provides a new statutory definition: "Bail" means release of a person from the custody of police or court on the execution of a bond or bail bond.
- **Section 2(1)(c) of the BNSS** defines "Bailable Offence" (bail is a right).
- **Section 478 of the BNSS** (formerly Section 436 CrPC) governs bail in bailable offences.
- **Section 480 of the BNSS** (formerly Section 437 CrPC) governs bail in non-bailable offences.

### 3. Detailed Analysis of Bail Provisions

#### I. Bail in Bailable Offences (Section 478)
- **Mandatory Right**: When a person accused of a bailable offence is arrested or detained without a warrant, they **shall** be released on bail.
- **Police or Court Power**: Both the officer-in-charge of the police station and the Magistrate are bound to grant bail.
- **Bond without Sureties**: If the person is indigent (unable to furnish sureties), the officer or court must release them on their personal bond without sureties. A person is deemed indigent if they cannot give bail within a week of arrest.

#### II. Bail in Non-Bailable Offences (Section 480)
- **Judicial Discretion**: Bail in non-bailable cases is not a right but a matter of judicial discretion.
- **General Restriction**: The court/magistrate shall **not** release the accused on bail if:
  - There appear reasonable grounds for believing they are guilty of an offence punishable with **death** or **life imprisonment**.
  - The offence is cognizable, and they have been previously convicted of a life/death offence, or previously convicted twice of a 3-7 year offence.
- **Exceptions (Special Concessions)**: The court may grant bail even in the restricted categories above if the accused is:
  - A **child** under the age of 16 years.
  - A **woman**.
  - A **sick** or **infirm** person.

#### III. Guidelines for Exercising Judicial Discretion
While exercising discretion to grant bail in non-bailable cases, courts must follow the guidelines established by the Supreme Court:
1. **Gravity of the Offence**: The nature and seriousness of the crime.
2. **Severity of Punishment**: The sentence carryable (e.g., life vs 5 years).
3. **Danger of Fleeing**: The likelihood of the accused absconding.
4. **Tampering with Evidence**: The risk of the accused threatening witnesses or destroying proof.
5. **Character and Status**: The social ties, antecedents, and character of the accused.
6. **Health and Age**: Whether the accused is vulnerable, sick, or elderly.

#### IV. Bail Bonds and Sureties (Secs. 484 & 488)
- Under the BNSS, a distinction is maintained between a **personal bond** (individual undertaking) and a **bail bond** (with sureties).
- The court cannot impose excessive or unconscionable bail amounts.

### 4. Case Laws
- ***State of Rajasthan v. Balchand* (1977) SC**: Justice Krishna Iyer laid down the classic rule of bail jurisprudence: **"Bail is the rule, jail is the exception."**
- ***Gurcharan Singh v. State (Delhi Admn.)* (1978) SC**: Established the factors courts must consider when granting bail: the nature of accusations, security of witnesses, and the likelihood of the accused fleeing.
- ***Sanjay Chandra v. CBI* (2012) SC**: The Supreme Court held that bail cannot be denied as a form of pre-trial punishment, as detaining an accused indefinitely during a long trial violates Article 21.
- ***Kalyan Chandra Sarkar v. Rajesh Ranjan* (2004) SC**: The court must record reasons when granting bail in heinous cases, balancing individual liberty with societal safety.

### 5. Conclusion
Bail is a vital tool to protect the presumption of innocence and prevent unnecessary pre-trial detention. Under the BNSS, the law remains structured to guarantee bail as a right for bailable offences, while directing the judiciary to exercise caution and balanced discretion for non-bailable crimes, protecting the integrity of the investigation while respecting human liberty.
