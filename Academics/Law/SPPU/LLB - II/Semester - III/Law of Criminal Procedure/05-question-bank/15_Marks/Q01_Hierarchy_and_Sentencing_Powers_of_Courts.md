## 🏛️ Q1. Detail the hierarchy and sentencing powers of the criminal courts in India under the BNSS, 2023. Discuss the newly introduced concept of 'Community Service'.

### 1. Introduction
The administration of criminal justice in India requires a structured judicial hierarchy to determine which court has the competence to try specific offences and impose appropriate sentences. The **Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS)** establishes this hierarchy, replacing the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973. It defines the classes of courts, their territorial divisions, and their maximum sentencing powers.

### 2. Definition (with Section number)
- **Section 6 of the BNSS, 2023** defines the classes of criminal courts in every State, besides the High Court and courts constituted under other laws.
- **Section 23 of the BNSS, 2023** defines the sentencing powers of Judicial Magistrates.
- **Section 2(1)(b) of the BNSS, 2023** (implied) defines "community service" as a non-custodial punishment.

### 3. Judicial Hierarchy and Sentencing Powers

#### I. Classes of Criminal Courts (Section 6)
In every State, there shall be the following classes of criminal courts:
1. **Courts of Session** (headed by a Sessions Judge).
2. **Judicial Magistrates of the First Class** (in districts) or Metropolitan Magistrates (in metropolitan areas).
3. **Judicial Magistrates of the Second Class**.
4. **Executive Magistrates** (headed by the District Magistrate).

#### II. High Courts and Sessions Courts (Section 22)
- **High Court (Sec. 22(1))**: Can pass **any sentence** authorized by law.
- **Sessions Judge or Additional Sessions Judge (Sec. 22(2))**: May pass any sentence authorized by law. However, any sentence of death passed by them **must be confirmed** by the High Court under Section 407.
- **Assistant Sessions Judge**: (Note: The category of Assistant Sessions Judge present in CrPC has been **abolished** in BNSS to streamline the hierarchy).

#### III. Judicial Magistrates (Section 23)
- **Chief Judicial Magistrate (Sec. 23(1))**: May pass any sentence authorized by law except death, life imprisonment, or imprisonment exceeding **7 years**.
- **Judicial Magistrate First Class (Sec. 23(2))**: May pass a sentence of imprisonment up to **3 years**, or fine up to **₹50,000**, or both, or **community service**.
- **Judicial Magistrate Second Class (Sec. 23(3))**: May pass a sentence of imprisonment up to **1 year**, or fine up to **₹10,000**, or both, or **community service**.

```
High Court (Any sentence)
      ↓
Sessions Court (Any sentence; death needs HC confirmation)
      ↓
Chief Judicial Magistrate (Up to 7 years)
      ↓
Judicial Magistrate First Class (Up to 3 years / ₹50,000 fine / Community Service)
      ↓
Judicial Magistrate Second Class (Up to 1 year / ₹10,000 fine / Community Service)
```

#### IV. The Concept of 'Community Service' (Sections 23 & 472)
- **Definition**: Under the BNSS, community service is introduced as a new form of punishment for minor/petty offences to reduce prison overcrowding and promote reformation.
- **Form**: It is defined as work ordered by the court which benefits the community, for which the offender is not entitled to any remuneration.
- **Applicability**: The court can award community service for specific minor offences, such as:
  - Petty theft (value < ₹5,000) upon first conviction where the stolen property is restored.
  - Attempt to commit suicide to obstruct a public servant.
  - Public misconduct by a drunken person.
  - Defamation.

#### V. Executive Magistrates (Sections 14 to 17)
- **Constitution**: Appointed by the State Government. The **District Magistrate (DM)** heads them.
- **Powers**: They do not try criminal offences. Their powers are administrative and preventive (e.g., maintaining public order, security for keeping peace, dispersing unlawful assemblies).

---

### 4. Case Laws
- ***Bachan Singh v. State of Punjab* (1980) SC**: Upholding the constitutionality of the death penalty (which Sessions Judges can award), but restricting it to the "rarest of rare cases" where life imprisonment is foreclosed.
- ***State of Karnataka v. Krishnappa* (2000) SC**: The Supreme Court held that sentencing must be proportional to the gravity of the offence, reflecting the conscience of society.
- ***Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India* (1978) SC**: Procedural rules governing the subordination and jurisdiction of magistrates must always be fair and non-arbitrary.

### 5. Conclusion
The BNSS, 2023 retains the structural court hierarchy of the old CrPC but streamlines it by abolishing the Assistant Sessions Judge category. More importantly, it modernizes penal philosophy by introducing **community service** as a rehabilitative alternative to imprisonment for minor offences, balancing deterrence with reform.
