## 🔒 Module 4: Bail, Bond, Maintenance & Public Order

### A. Bail & Bond (Secs. 478-496)
- **Bail in Bailable Offence (Sec. 478)**: Mandatory right. Police/Court *must* release on bond.
- **Bail in Non-Bailable Offence (Sec. 480)**: Discretionary. Vested in Court/Magistrate. Bail denied if reasonable grounds exist for offences punishable with death/life. Special concessions for women, minors, sick, or infirm.
- **Anticipatory Bail (Sec. 482)**: Direction to release a person on bail *in anticipation* of arrest for a non-bailable offence. Exclusive power of Sessions Court and High Court.
- **Landmark Case**: *Gurbaksh Singh Sibbia v. State of Punjab* (1980) — AB is a device to protect personal liberty (Art. 21); conditions cannot be overly restrictive.
- **Landmark Case**: *Sushila Aggarwal v. State of Delhi* (2020) — Anticipatory bail duration is not limited to a specific time.

### B. Maintenance (Secs. 144-147)
- **Claimants (Sec. 144)**: Wife (unable to maintain herself), minor children (legitimate/illegitimate), parents (father/mother).
- **Alteration and Enforcement (Sec. 146 & 147)**: On proof of change in circumstances, Magistrate can alter monthly allowance. Failure to comply can result in a warrant for levying the amount and imprisonment up to **1 month** per breach.
- **Landmark Case**: *Shah Bano Begum* (1985) — Sec. 125 CrPC (now Sec. 144 BNSS) is a secular provision overriding personal laws.

### C. Public Order & Tranquility (Secs. 148-167)
- **Unlawful Assembly (Sec. 148-151)**: Magistrate or Officer-in-charge of police station can command assembly to disperse. Civil force or military force may be used if dispersal refused.
- **Public Nuisance (Sec. 152-163)**: Conditional order by Executive Magistrate to remove public nuisance (e.g., obstructing pathways, dangerous structures).
- **Disputes as to Immovable Property (Sec. 164-167)**: Executive Magistrate can intervene in land/water disputes likely to cause a breach of peace.

## 📝 Module 5: Charge, Trials and Investigation Procedures

### A. Cognizance & Commencement (Secs. 210-222)
- **Cognizance by Magistrate (Sec. 210)**: Magistrate can take cognizance of any offence upon: (a) receiving a complaint, (b) a police report, (c) info from other sources.
- **Limitations to Cognizance (Sec. 513-519)**:
  - 6 months for offences punishable with fine only.
  - 1 year for offences punishable with imprisonment up to 1 year.
  - 3 years for offences punishable with imprisonment between 1 to 3 years.

### B. The Charge (Secs. 234-247)
- **Contents (Sec. 234)**: Charge must state the offence, section of law, time, place, and person against whom offence was committed.
- **Separate Charges (Sec. 241)**: General rule is one separate charge and separate trial for every distinct offence.
- **Exceptions (Sec. 242-246)**:
  - 3 offences of the same kind within **12 months** can be tried together (Sec. 242).
  - Offences committed in the course of the **same transaction** (Sec. 243).
  - Alternative charges (e.g., theft vs receiving stolen property) (Sec. 246).

### C. Types of Criminal Trials (4 Types)
1. **Trial Before Court of Session (Secs. 248-260)**:
   - Opened by Public Prosecutor. Discharge (Sec. 250) if no prima facie case. Framing of charge (Sec. 251). Prosecution evidence (Sec. 254). Statement of accused (Sec. 351). Acquittal or Conviction.
2. **Warrant Cases by Magistrates (Secs. 261-273)**:
   - Tried on Police Report (Secs. 261-266) or on Complaint (Secs. 267-270). Charge framed after checking prosecution witnesses.
3. **Summons Cases by Magistrates (Secs. 274-282)**:
   - No formal charge framed (substance of accusation stated). If accused pleads guilty, convicted (Sec. 275). If not, Magistrate hears prosecution & defence, then passes order.
4. **Summary Trials (Secs. 283-288)**:
   - Conducted by CJM, JMFC. For minor offences (theft up to ₹20,000, trespass, etc.). Maximum sentence is **3 months** imprisonment.

### D. Plea Bargaining (Secs. 289-300)
- **Scope**: Applicable to offences punishable with imprisonment **up to 7 years**. Excludes offences affecting socio-economic conditions of the country, or offences against women/children under 14.
- **Process**: Mutual satisfactory disposition of case. Sentence can be reduced to one-fourth (for minimum sentences) or half (others).

## ⚖️ Module 6: Judgment, Appeals, Revision & Execution

### A. Judgment (Secs. 392-412)
- **Timelines**: Pronounced in open court within **45 days** of trial conclusion. Uploaded to portal within **7 days** (BNSS update).
- **Victim Remedies**: Sec. 396 (State Victim Compensation Scheme); Sec. 397 (mandatory free first-aid for sexual assault victims).
- **Death Penalty Confirmation (Secs. 407-412)**: Death sentence passed by Sessions Court must be confirmed by the High Court (requires signatures of at least **2 Judges**).

### B. Probation & Parole (Probation of Offenders Act, 1958)
- **Release on Probation (Sec. 401)**: Vetted for offenders under 21 (unless life/death offence) or women. Released on good conduct bond up to **3 years**.
- **Admonition (Sec. 401(3))**: Release after warning for minor offences (theft, cheating, punishable with ≤ 2 years).
- **Parole**: Executive privilege. Governed by State Prison Rules, not BNSS.

### C. Appeals, Reference & Revision
- **Victim's Right to Appeal (Sec. 413 Proviso)**: Right to appeal against acquittal, lesser offence conviction, or inadequate compensation.
- **Reference (Sec. 436)**: Made by subordinate court to High Court on questions of validity of any Act or Ordinance.
- **Revision (Secs. 438-445)**: Vested in HC & Sessions Court to check correctness, legality, or propriety of any finding/sentence. Revisional court **cannot** convert an acquittal into a conviction.

## 🧒 Module 7: Juvenile Justice Act, 2015

### A. Core Distinctions
- **Child in Conflict with Law (CCL) [Sec. 2(13)]**: Accused of committing an offence. Subject to **Juvenile Justice Board (JJB) [Sec. 4]** (principal magistrate + 2 social workers).
- **Child in Need of Care and Protection (CNCP) [Sec. 2(14)]**: Neglected, abandoned, or abused child. Subject to **Child Welfare Committee (CWC) [Sec. 27]** (chairperson + 4 members).

### B. Preliminary Assessment (Sec. 15)
- Conducted by JJB for children in the **16-18 age group** accused of **heinous offences**.
- If mature, case is transferred to the **Children's Court** to be tried as an adult (no death/life without release).

### C. Child Care Institutions (CCIs)
- **Observation Home (Sec. 47)**: Custody during inquiry.
- **Special Home (Sec. 48)**: Reform home for CCLs post-inquiry.
- **Children's Home (Sec. 50)**: Long-term home for CNCPs.
- **Place of Safety (Sec. 49)**: Security home for heinous CCLs (16-18 years).
- **Fit Facility (Sec. 51)**: Recognized NGO/private care.

### D. secular Adoption (Secs. 56-61)
- Secular system overseen by **CARA** (Central Adoption Resource Authority).
- Vetted by the **District Magistrate (DM)** post-2021 amendment.
- Inter-country adoption is a last resort (60-day domestic window).
- Mnemonic for CCIs: **O-S-C-F-P**: *"Officially Safe Children Find Protection"*.
  - **O**bservation, **S**pecial, **C**hildren, **F**it Facility, **P**lace of Safety.
