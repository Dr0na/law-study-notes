# 📊 Comparison Tables - Law of Criminal Procedure (LCC 0701)

These tables are designed to maximize marks in "Distinguish Between" questions (5 Marks) and to provide clear conceptual clarity for descriptive answers.

---

## 1. Adversarial vs Inquisitorial Criminal Justice System

| Basis | ⚖️ Adversarial System (India, UK, US) | 🔍 Inquisitorial System (France, Germany) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Role of Judge** | Acts as a neutral umpire or passive referee. Adjudicates based on evidence presented. | Acts as an active investigator. Directs proceedings and gathers evidence. |
| **Burden of Proof** | Entirely on the Prosecution to prove guilt beyond reasonable doubt. | Shared between the court and the parties to discover the absolute truth. |
| **Accused's Status** | Presumed innocent until proven guilty. Has the right to remain silent. | Presumed to cooperate. The court can question the accused directly. |
| **Evidence Gathering**| Gathered and presented independently by the prosecution and defence. | Gathered by an investigating judge who compiles a dossier (*dossier de l'instruction*). |
| **Advantage** | Protects individual rights and ensures neutral, unbiased adjudication. | Highly efficient in fact-finding and avoids technical legal tactics. |

💡 **Exam Tip**: Essential for answers under Module 01 on the introduction to the criminal justice system.

## 2. Bailable vs Non-Bailable Offence

**(Refer to Section 2(1)(c) of the BNSS, 2023)**

| Basis | 🔓 Bailable Offence | 🔒 Non-Bailable Offence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | An offence shown as bailable in the First Schedule, or made bailable by any other law. | Any other offence which is not a bailable offence. |
| **Right to Bail** | Bail is a matter of right. Police/Court *must* release the accused on bond. | Bail is a matter of judicial discretion. The accused cannot claim it as a right. |
| **Gravity of Offence** | Less serious or minor crimes (e.g., simple hurt, trespass, defamation). | Grave and serious crimes (e.g., murder, rape, dacoity, kidnapping). |
| **Authority to Release**| Both the Police Officer in charge and the Court can grant bail (Sec. 478). | Only the Court has the discretion to grant bail (Sec. 480). |
| **Refusal Criteria** | Cannot be refused unless the accused fails to comply with bail conditions. | Denied if there are reasonable grounds of guilt in life/death offences, unless minor/woman. |

💡 **Exam Tip**: Combine this table with a discussion on Section 12 of the JJ Act when writing about juvenile bail.

## 3. Summons Case vs Warrant Case

**(Refer to Sections 2(1)(x) and 2(1)(z) of the BNSS, 2023)**

| Basis | ✉️ Summons Case | 📜 Warrant Case |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | A case relating to an offence, and not being a warrant case. | A case relating to an offence punishable with death, life, or imprisonment > 2 years. |
| **Maximum Punishment**| Imprisonment up to **2 years** or fine. | Imprisonment **exceeding 2 years**, life, or death. |
| **Trial Procedure** | Simplified trial (Secs. 274-282). No formal charge needs to be framed. | Elaborate trial (Secs. 261-273). Formal charge must be framed. |
| **Withdrawal** | Complainant can withdraw the complaint at any time with Magistrate's permission (Sec. 280). | Cannot be withdrawn easily; requires public prosecutor's withdrawal (Sec. 360). |
| **Conversion** | A summons case can be converted into a warrant case by the Magistrate in interest of justice. | A warrant case cannot be converted into a summons case. |

💡 **Exam Tip**: Highlight this distinction in Module 05 questions concerning trial procedures.

## 4. Compoundable vs Non-Compoundable Offence

**(Refer to Section 359 of the BNSS, 2023)**

| Basis | 🤝 Compoundable Offence | ⚖️ Non-Compoundable Offence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nature** | Private wrongs against individuals that can be settled amicably. | Public wrongs against society that cannot be settled privately. |
| **Settlement/Compromise**| The victim can compromise/settle with the accused, leading to a drop in charges. | No private compromise allowed. Trial must proceed to judgment. |
| **Effect of Compounding**| Has the effect of an **acquittal** of the accused (Sec. 359(8)). | No effect of acquittal; settlement does not automatically stop the trial. |
| **Permission of Court** | Some offences require court permission (Sec. 359 Table 2); others do not (Table 1). | Court cannot permit compounding at all. Only HC under Sec. 528 can quash charges. |
| **Examples** | Simple hurt, adultery, criminal trespass, defamation. | Murder, rape, kidnapping, dacoity. |

💡 **Exam Tip**: Cite Section 359 table formats when describing compounding.

## 5. Parole vs Probation

**(Refer to the Probation of Offenders Act, 1958 & State Prison Manuals)**

| Basis | ⛓️ Parole | 🕊️ Probation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Stage of Application** | Post-conviction. Applied after the offender has served a part of the sentence. | Pre-sentencing. Granted by the court instead of sentencing the offender to prison. |
| **Nature** | Temporary suspension of the sentence. A conditional release from prison. | Suspension of the sentence itself. The offender does not enter prison. |
| **Authority** | Granted by the Executive/State Government (Prison authorities). | Granted by the Judiciary (Trial Court). |
| **Consequence of Breach**| Sent back to prison to serve the remaining sentence. | Arrested and sentenced for the original offence. |
| **Primary Goal** | Reintegration into society after showing good conduct in prison. | Reformative treatment for first-time, youthful, or minor offenders. |

💡 **Exam Tip**: Classic distinction tested in Module 06 under sentencing and probation.

## 6. Child in Conflict with Law (CCL) vs Child in Need of Care and Protection (CNCP)

**(Refer to Sections 2(13) and 2(14) of the Juvenile Justice Act, 2015)**

| Basis | 🧑‍⚖️ Child in Conflict with Law (CCL) | 🤝 Child in Need of Care & Protection (CNCP) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | Child alleged or found to have committed an offence and is under 18 years. | Child who lacks a home, is abandoned, abused, exploited, or destitute. |
| **Governing Authority** | **Juvenile Justice Board (JJB)** under Sec. 4. | **Child Welfare Committee (CWC)** under Sec. 27. |
| **Placement Facility** | Observation Home (during inquiry) or Special Home/Place of Safety (after inquiry). | Children's Home, Open Shelter, or placement with a Fit Person/Foster Family. |
| **Proceeding Nature** | Quasi-criminal inquiry (determining offence details and reform measures). | Administrative/Welfare inquiry (determining safety, care, and shelter needs). |
| **Outcome Goal** | Rehabilitation, behavioral correction, and social reintegration. | Care, protection, maintenance, education, and permanent adoption. |

💡 **Exam Tip**: Mandatory table for any question on Module 07.
