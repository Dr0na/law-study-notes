# Module 3: Information to the Police and their Powers to Investigate

## Introduction

Investigation is the backbone of the criminal justice system. It is the process by which the police gather evidence to determine whether an offence has been committed and who committed it. The BNSS prescribes a detailed framework for initiation of investigation (FIR), interrogation, arrest, search and seizure, confessions, police diary, police report, and inquest proceedings. This module covers the most practically important provisions of the BNSS.

---

## 1. Initiation of Investigation Proceedings (Secs. 173-178)

### Sec. 173 — Information in Cognizable Cases (FIR)

The First Information Report (FIR) is the starting point of investigation in cognizable offences.

#### Key Provisions:

**Sec. 173(1)** — Every information relating to the commission of a cognizable offence shall be given to an officer in charge of a police station:
- **(i)** Orally or in writing
- **(ii)** By electronic communication — **New in BNSS (E-FIR)**

**Sec. 173(1) Proviso** — FIR can be registered at **any police station irrespective of jurisdiction**. The FIR shall then be transferred to the police station having jurisdiction. This is called **Zero FIR**.

> **Key Change from CrPC**: Under CrPC Sec. 154, FIR was registered only at the police station having jurisdiction. BNSS now mandates registration irrespective of jurisdiction.

**Sec. 173(2)** — If information is given orally:
- It shall be reduced to writing by the police officer
- Read over to the informant
- Signed by the informant
- A copy of the information shall be given free of cost to the informant

**Sec. 173(1)(ii) — E-FIR**: FIR may be registered through electronic means, but the informant must sign it within **3 days**.

**Sec. 173(3) — Preliminary Inquiry**: In offences punishable with imprisonment between **3 to 7 years**:
- The officer in charge may, with permission of **Deputy Superintendent of Police**
- Conduct a preliminary inquiry to ascertain whether a prima facie case exists
- Must be completed within **14 days**

> **New in BNSS**: CrPC had no such preliminary inquiry provision.

**Sec. 173(4)** — If information does not disclose a cognizable offence:
- The informant shall be directed to the Magistrate
- The officer shall not investigate but must enter the substance in the General Diary

#### Evidentiary Value of FIR
- FIR is **not substantive evidence** — it can only be used to:
  - Corroborate the maker (Sec. 157 BSA)
  - Contradict the maker (Sec. 145 BSA)
  - Refresh memory of the maker (Sec. 159 BSA)
- FIR is relevant under Sec. 8 BSA as forming part of the same transaction
- **Delay in FIR** is a relevant factor but does not by itself make it unreliable

**Landmark Case**: *Lalita Kumari v. Govt. of UP* (2014) — Registration of FIR is **mandatory** when information discloses a cognizable offence. The police officer cannot conduct a preliminary inquiry before registering the FIR (except in certain categories as outlined by the Supreme Court).

### Sec. 174 — Information in Non-Cognizable Cases
- Officer in charge shall enter the substance in the prescribed form (General Diary/Daily Diary)
- Shall forward daily diary reports to Magistrate **once in 14 days** (new timeline in BNSS)
- No investigation without order of Magistrate having jurisdiction

### Sec. 175 — Power of Police to Investigate
- SP may require DySP to investigate based on nature and gravity of offence
- Magistrate may consider police officer's submission before ordering investigation in complaint cases

### Sec. 176 — Procedure for Investigation
- Officer investigating must proceed to the spot and ascertain facts
- **Sec. 176(3) — Forensic Mandate**: For offences punishable with **7 years or more**, forensic evidence shall be collected at the crime scene by a forensic expert. **New in BNSS** — mandatory forensic involvement.

### Sec. 177 — Procedure When Investigation Cannot Be Completed in 24 Hours
- When investigation cannot be completed within 24 hours, the officer shall forward a diary to the Magistrate

### Sec. 178 — Power to Require Attendance of Witnesses
- Police officer making investigation may require attendance of any person acquainted with the facts

## 3. Interrogation Powers of Police Officer (Secs. 179-193)

### Sec. 179 — Examination of Witnesses by Police
- Police officer may examine orally any person acquainted with facts
- Such person is bound to answer all questions truthfully, except questions that expose them to a criminal charge
- **No male person under 15 years or above 60 years, no woman, no mentally/physically disabled person, no person with acute illness** shall be required to attend at the police station

> **New in BNSS**: Explicit protection for women, elderly, minors, and disabled persons from being summoned to police station.

### Sec. 180 — Recording Statements of Women Victims
- Statement of a woman victim of sexual assault shall be recorded by a **woman police officer** or any woman officer
- May be recorded by **audio-video electronic means**

### Sec. 183 — Recording of Confessions and Statements by Magistrate
- A Magistrate may record confessions and statements during investigation
- **Sec. 183(6)(a)**: Statement of rape victim shall be recorded only by a **woman Judicial Magistrate**; in her absence, by a male JM in the presence of a woman

> **New in BNSS**: Mandate for woman Judicial Magistrate for rape victim statements.

### Sec. 183(6) — Witness Statements for Serious Offences
- In cases punishable with **10 years or more, life, or death**, the Judicial Magistrate shall record statements of witnesses brought by the police

## 4. Arrest With and Without Warrant (Secs. 35-46, 62, 72-93)

### A. Arrest Without Warrant (Sec. 35)

A police officer may arrest without warrant any person who:
1. Has been concerned in any cognizable offence
2. Against whom a reasonable complaint or credible information exists
3. Who is in possession of stolen property
4. Who obstructs a police officer in duty
5. Who is a deserter from the armed forces
6. Who is under any law liable to be apprehended
7. Who is a released convict and commits breach of conditions
8. Who is reasonably suspected of being a proclaimed offender

**Sec. 35 — Restrictions on Arrest (New in BNSS)**:
- For offences punishable with **less than 3 years** imprisonment, no arrest of:
  - Person **above 60 years of age**, or
  - **Infirm person**
  - Without prior permission of **DySP**

> **Landmark Case**: *Arnesh Kumar v. State of Bihar* (2014) — Supreme Court laid down guidelines to prevent automatic arrests in cases under Sec. 498A IPC. Police must satisfy themselves of necessity of arrest.

## 5. Police to Report Apprehension (Secs. 59-61)

### Sec. 59 — Discharge of Person Apprehended
When a person is taken into custody without warrant for a non-cognizable offence, and a Magistrate is not available within 24 hours, the person shall be released on executing a bond.

### Sec. 60 — Pursuit of Offenders
A police officer may pursue and retake any person whom he is authorized to arrest into any place in India.

### Sec. 61 — Arrested Person to Be Taken Before Magistrate or Officer in Charge
Every police officer arresting a person without a warrant shall, without unnecessary delay, take the person to the nearest police station or Magistrate.

## 6. Rights of Arrested Person (Secs. 47-58, Art. 22)

### Statutory Rights Under BNSS

| Section | Right |
|---------|-------|
| **Sec. 36** | Right to be informed of grounds of arrest and right to bail |
| **Sec. 47** | Right to have a relative/friend informed of arrest |
| **Sec. 48** | Obligation to inform **designated police officer** about arrest (New) |
| **Sec. 49** | Right to be searched by officer of the same sex |
| **Sec. 50** | Right to be examined by medical practitioner |
| **Sec. 51-53** | Right to medical examination |
| **Sec. 55** | Protection from unauthorized detention |
| **Sec. 56** | Right to consult legal practitioner (Sec. 340-341) |
| **Sec. 44** | Right to be produced before Magistrate within 24 hours |

### Constitutional Rights (Art. 22)
1. **Right to be informed of grounds of arrest** — Art. 22(1)
2. **Right to consult and be defended by a legal practitioner** — Art. 22(1)
3. **Right to be produced before Magistrate within 24 hours** — Art. 22(2)

### D.K. Basu Guidelines (1997)
The Supreme Court laid down 11 mandatory guidelines for arrest:
1. Police officer carrying out arrest must bear accurate, visible identification
2. Memo of arrest to be prepared at the time of arrest
3. Arrestee has right to have a relative informed
4. Time, place of arrest, and venue of custody to be notified to next friend
5. Arrest memo to be countersigned by the arrestee
6. Arrestee to be medically examined at the time of arrest
7. Inspection of interrogation by senior police officer
8. Copies of all documents to be sent to Magistrate
9. Arrestee to be permitted to meet lawyer during interrogation
10. Police control room to be informed
11. Entry in case diary

> Many of these guidelines are now codified in BNSS (Secs. 37, 47, 48, 50).

## 8. Search and Seizure (Secs. 49, 96-110, 185-186)

### A. Search of Arrested Person (Sec. 49)
- Officer may search the arrested person
- Search to be conducted by officer of the **same sex**
- List of all articles seized to be prepared

### B. General Search Provisions (Secs. 96-110)

#### Sec. 96 — When Search Warrant May Be Issued
Magistrate may issue search warrant if satisfied that:
- A person to whom summons has been issued will not produce the document/thing
- The purpose of justice would be served

#### Sec. 103 — Search Before Independent Witnesses
- Search must be conducted in presence of **two or more independent and respectable inhabitants** of the locality (Sec. 103(4))
- **Refusal to be a witness is an offence** under Sec. 222 BNS (Sec. 103(8)) — New

#### Sec. 105 — Audio-Video Recording of Search
- The police officer shall record the whole process of search and seizure by **audio-video electronic means, preferably by mobile phone** — **New in BNSS**
- The recording shall be forwarded to DM, SDM, or JMIC without delay

#### Sec. 107 — Attachment, Forfeiture, or Restoration of Property (New)
- IO may apply (with SP/Commissioner approval) for **attachment of proceeds of crime**
- Magistrate issues show cause notice (14 days)
- May order attachment; may pass **ex-parte interim order**
- **Proceeds of crime to be distributed among victims** rateably by DM within 60 days

> **Entirely new provision in BNSS** — addresses property derived from criminal activity.

### C. Investigation-Related Search (Secs. 185-186)

#### Sec. 185 — Procedure for Investigation
- IO must record reasons for search in **case diary** (Sec. 185(1))
- IO should conduct search **in person** if practicable (Sec. 185(2))
- If unable, may depute subordinate officer after recording reasons (Sec. 185(3))
- **Audio-video recording mandatory** (Proviso to Sec. 185(2))
- IO must send search records to Magistrate within **48 hours** (Sec. 185(5))

## 9. Recording of Confessions and Statements (Sec. 183)

### Confession Before Magistrate
- No confession made to a **police officer** is admissible (Sec. 25 BSA)
- Confession must be recorded by a **Judicial Magistrate** (Sec. 183)
- Magistrate must satisfy that the confession is **voluntary**
- Magistrate must warn the accused that he is not bound to make a confession
- Must allow reflection time before recording

### Statements by Witnesses
- Sec. 183 provides for recording of statements of witnesses by Magistrate
- For offences punishable with 10+ years, life, or death — Magistrate must record statements of prosecution witnesses

## 10. Special Remand Order (Sec. 187)

### Sec. 187 — Remand of Accused
**Key Provisions:**
- Magistrate may authorize detention (remand) if investigation cannot be completed within 24 hours
- **Police custody remand**: Up to 15 days at a time
- **Judicial custody remand**: Up to the following periods:

**New in BNSS — Extended Police Remand**:
- For offences punishable with **less than 10 years**: Police remand may be sought within first **40 days** of arrest
- For offences punishable with **10 years or more**: Police remand may be sought within first **60 days** of arrest

> **Key Change**: Under CrPC Sec. 167, police remand could only be sought during the initial 15 days. BNSS allows police remand to be sought in parts within the 40/60-day window.

- **Maximum period**: 60 days (for offences with less than 10 years) or 90 days (for offences with 10+ years, life, or death)
- **Default bail**: If investigation not completed within the prescribed period, the accused is entitled to bail

---

## 11. Police Diary (Sec. 192)

- IO shall maintain a **case diary** (also called police diary or investigation diary)
- It shall contain: day-to-day proceedings, time of investigation, places visited, statements recorded
- Police diary is **not evidence** but the court may use it to aid investigation
- Neither the accused nor his agents are entitled to call for the diary, nor use it as evidence
- The court may use it for the purpose of contradiction

---

## 12. Police Report (Sec. 193)

### Sec. 193 — Report of Police Officer (Charge Sheet)

**Sec. 193(3)** — After completion of investigation, the IO shall forward to the Magistrate a report containing:
- Names of parties
- Nature of information
- Names of persons who appear to be acquainted with the circumstances
- Whether any offence appears to have been committed
- Whether the accused has been arrested
- Whether released on bond and if so, with or without sureties
- Whether forwarded in custody

**Sec. 193(3)(ii) — Victim Information** (New):
- IO shall inform the victim about the **progress of investigation within 90 days** by any means including electronic communication

**Sec. 193(8) — Electronic Copies** (New):
- Copies of police report and documents may be supplied through **electronic communication** to the Magistrate, accused, and victim

**Sec. 193(9) — Further Investigation** (New):
- Further investigation during trial requires **court permission**
- Must be completed within **90 days** (extendable with court permission)

**Sexual Offence Investigations**: Investigation in offences under BNS Secs. 64-71 and POCSO Secs. 4, 6, 8 must be completed within **2 months** from the date of recording information.

## 13. Inquest Proceedings (Secs. 194-196)

### Sec. 194 — Inquest
When a police officer receives information that a person has:
- Committed suicide, or
- Been killed by another, or
- Been killed by an animal, machinery, or accident, or
- Died under circumstances raising reasonable suspicion of commission of an offence

The officer shall proceed to the place, draw up a report of the apparent cause of death, describing injuries, and forward it to the **DM or SDM within 24 hours** (New timeline).

### Sec. 195 — Persons Required to Attend for Inquest
- Same protections apply — no minor under 15, person above 60, woman, disabled person, or acutely ill person shall be required to attend at any place other than their residence

### Sec. 196 — Exhumation
In cases where body has been buried, the Magistrate may cause exhumation and examination.

## Landmark Judgments

### 1. Lalita Kumari v. Govt. of UP (2014)
- **Held**: FIR registration is **mandatory** for cognizable offences; police cannot refuse to register FIR
- 7 categories identified where preliminary inquiry may precede FIR

### 2. D.K. Basu v. State of West Bengal (1997)
- **Held**: 11 guidelines for arrest; arrest memo, medical examination, right to inform relatives
- Many guidelines now codified in BNSS Secs. 37, 47, 48, 50

### 3. Arnesh Kumar v. State of Bihar (2014)
- **Held**: Arrest not necessary in every cognizable case; police must record reasons for arrest; checklist to be maintained
- Particularly relevant for offences punishable up to 7 years

### 4. Joginder Kumar v. State of UP (1994)
- **Held**: Arrest cannot be made as a matter of course; must be necessary in the circumstances; right to have a friend/relative informed

### 5. State of UP v. Deoman Upadhyaya (1960)
- **Held**: Investigation under Sec. 154 CrPC (now Sec. 173 BNSS) can only be by a police officer; private persons cannot investigate

### 6. Nandini Satpathy v. P.L. Dani (1978)
- **Held**: Right against self-incrimination extends to police interrogation; accused cannot be compelled to answer incriminating questions
