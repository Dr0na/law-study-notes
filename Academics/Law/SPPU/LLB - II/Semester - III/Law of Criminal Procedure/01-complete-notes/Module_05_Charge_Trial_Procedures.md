# Module 5: Charge, Trial and Investigation Procedures

## Introduction

The trial stage is the centrepiece of the criminal justice system. It is through the trial process that the guilt or innocence of the accused is determined. The BNSS, 2023 prescribes distinct procedures for different categories of cases — sessions trial, warrant cases, summons cases, and summary trials. This module covers the concept of fair trial, the process of taking cognizance, framing of charges, the various trial procedures, plea bargaining, and important procedural safeguards.

---

## 1. Concept of Fair Trial

### Constitutional Foundation

The right to a fair trial is an integral part of Article 21 of the Constitution. It includes:

1. **Presumption of Innocence** — Every person is presumed innocent until proved guilty. The burden of proof lies on the prosecution.

2. **Right of Silence** — The accused has the right to remain silent and cannot be compelled to be a witness against himself (Art. 20(3)).

3. **Right to Speedy Trial** — Recognized by the Supreme Court as a fundamental right under Art. 21.

> **Landmark Case**: *Hussainara Khatoon v. Home Secretary, State of Bihar* (1980) — Supreme Court held that the right to a speedy trial is a fundamental right implicit in Art. 21. Thousands of undertrial prisoners were ordered to be released.

> **Landmark Case**: *Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India* (1978) — Supreme Court held that the procedure established by law must be just, fair, and reasonable.

### Key Principles

| Principle | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Presumption of Innocence** | Accused is innocent until proven guilty beyond reasonable doubt |
| **Right of Silence** | Art. 20(3) — protection against self-incrimination |
| **Right to Speedy Trial** | Art. 21 — trial within reasonable time |
| **Right to Legal Aid** | Art. 39A read with Art. 21 — free legal aid for the poor |
| **Open Trial** | Trials to be held in open court as a general rule |
| **Right to Examine Witnesses** | Accused has right to cross-examine prosecution witnesses |
| **Double Jeopardy** | Art. 20(2) — no person shall be prosecuted and punished for the same offence more than once |

## 2. Taking Cognizance (Secs. 210-222)

### Sec. 210 — Cognizance of Offences by Magistrates

A Magistrate may take cognizance of any offence:
- **(a)** Upon receiving a **complaint** of facts constituting such offence
- **(b)** Upon a **police report** of such facts
- **(c)** Upon **information** received from any person other than a police officer, or upon his own knowledge, that such offence has been committed

> **Key Point**: "Taking cognizance" does not mean merely applying the judicial mind to the facts but includes the intention to proceed further on those facts.

### Limitations on Taking Cognizance (Secs. 213-222)

#### Sec. 213 — Prosecution by Public Servants
- No court shall take cognizance of offences punishable under specified BNS sections (e.g., giving false evidence) except on complaint by the court itself or a public servant acting in official capacity.

#### Sec. 215 — Prosecution for Contempt of Lawful Authority
- Complaint by the public servant concerned or with their authorisation.

#### Sec. 217 — Prosecution for Offences Against the State
- No court shall take cognizance except with **previous sanction** of Central or State Government.

#### Sec. 218 — Prosecution of Judges and Public Servants
- No court shall take cognizance of offences by Judges/public servants acting in official capacity except with **previous sanction** of Central/State Government.
- **Proviso**: Government must decide within **120 days**; failure → sanction **deemed granted** (New in BNSS).
- **Exception**: No sanction required for offences of rape, sexual assault, etc.

#### Sec. 219 — Prosecution for Offences Against Marriage
- Cognizance only on complaint by **aggrieved person**.

#### Sec. 222 — Prosecution for Defamation
- Cognizance only on complaint by **person aggrieved**.

## 3. Commencement of Proceedings Before Magistrate (Secs. 227-233)

### Sec. 223 — Examination of Complainant
- Magistrate shall **examine on oath** the complainant and witnesses present.
- **Proviso (New in BNSS)**: No cognizance shall be taken without giving the accused an **opportunity of being heard**.

### Sec. 225 — Postponement of Issue of Process
- Magistrate may, before issuing process, **inquire** into the case himself or **direct police investigation** to decide whether sufficient ground exists.

### Sec. 226 — Dismissal of Complaint
- If no sufficient ground for proceeding → Magistrate shall **dismiss** the complaint and record reasons.

### Sec. 227 — Issue of Process
- If sufficient ground → issue **summons** (summons case) or **warrant** (warrant case).
- **Sec. 227(1) Proviso (New)**: Summons or warrants may be issued through **electronic means**.
- **Sec. 227(2) (New)**: No summons/warrant until **prosecution witness list** is filed.

### Sec. 230 — Supply of Documents to Accused
- In police report cases, Magistrate shall furnish **within 14 days** (New timeline):
  - Police report, FIR, witness statements, confessions, other documents
  - May be furnished through **electronic means** (New)

### Sec. 232 — Commitment to Court of Session
- Offences triable exclusively by Sessions Court → Magistrate shall commit the case.
- **Proviso (New)**: Commitment proceedings shall be completed within **90 days** (extendable to **180 days**).

## 4. Charge: Content and Form (Secs. 234-240, 247)

### A. Form of Charges (Secs. 234-240)

#### Sec. 234 — Contents of Charge
- State the **offence** with which the accused is charged.
- If law gives the offence a **specific name** → use that name only.
- Mention the **law and section** under which the offence is punishable.
- Charge shall be written in the **language of the Court**.
- Previous conviction, if relevant to enhanced punishment → state fact, date, and place.

#### Sec. 235 — Particulars as to Time, Place, and Person
- Charge shall contain such particulars as are **reasonably sufficient** to give the accused notice.
- For CBT/misappropriation → sufficient to specify **gross sum** and **dates** (within 1 year).

#### Sec. 236 — When Manner Must Be Stated
- If the particulars in Secs. 234-235 are insufficient → charge must include **manner** of offence.

#### Sec. 237 — Words Used in Sense of the Law
- Words in the charge bear the meaning attached by the law under which the offence is punishable.

#### Sec. 238 — Effect of Errors
- Error or omission shall not be **material** unless the accused was **actually misled** and it caused **failure of justice**.

#### Sec. 239 — Court May Alter Charge
- Court may **alter or add** to charge at any time **before judgment**.
- Altered charge to be read and explained to accused.
- If prejudice likely → court may direct **new trial** or **adjournment**.

#### Sec. 240 — Recall of Witnesses When Charge Altered
- Prosecution and accused may **recall witnesses** after alteration.

### B. Joinder of Charges (Secs. 241-246)

#### Sec. 241 — Separate Charges for Distinct Offences (General Rule)
- **General Rule**: For every distinct offence → **separate charge** and **separate trial**.
- **Proviso (New in BNSS)**: Where accused desires in writing and Magistrate finds no prejudice → may try all charges **together**.

#### Sec. 242 — Same Kind Offences Within 12 Months
- Offences of the **same kind** committed within 12 months → may be charged together, **not exceeding 5**.

#### Sec. 243 — Offences in Same Transaction
- Offences committed in **one series of acts forming the same transaction** → may be tried together.
- Also covers: falsification of accounts to conceal CBT (Sec. 243(2)).
- **Sec. 243(3)**: Acts falling within **two or more definitions** → person may be charged with each.
- **Sec. 243(4)**: Several acts constituting different offences when combined → may be tried together.

#### Sec. 244 — When Doubtful Which Offence Committed
- If doubtful → may charge with **all or any** of such offences, or charge **in the alternative**.

#### Sec. 245 — When Minor Offence Proved
- If combination of some particulars constitutes a **minor offence** → may be convicted of minor offence.
- May be convicted of an **attempt** even though attempt is not separately charged.

#### Sec. 246 — Persons Who May Be Charged Jointly
- Accused of **same offence** in same transaction
- Accused of offence + accused of **abetment/attempt**
- Accused of **same kind offences** jointly within 12 months
- Accused of **different offences** in same transaction

#### Sec. 247 — Withdrawal of Remaining Charges on Conviction
- On conviction on one charge → remaining charges may be withdrawn with court's consent.
- Withdrawal has effect of **acquittal** (unless conviction set aside).

## 5. Trial Before a Court of Session (Secs. 248-260)

### Key Features of Sessions Trial

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Prosecution** | Conducted by **Public Prosecutor** (Sec. 248) |
| **Commencement** | Accused appears pursuant to commitment; prosecutor opens case (Sec. 249) |
| **Discharge** | Application within **60 days** of commitment (Sec. 250) — New timeline |
| **Charge Framing** | Within **60 days** from first hearing on charge (Sec. 251(1)(b)) — New timeline |
| **Plea** | Charge read, explained; asked to plead guilty or claim trial (Sec. 251(2)) |
| **Conviction on Plea** | If accused pleads guilty → Judge may convict (Sec. 252) |
| **Evidence** | Prosecution evidence first; may be recorded by **audio-video** means (Sec. 254) |
| **Acquittal** | If no evidence → record order of acquittal (Sec. 255) |
| **Defence** | Accused called upon to enter defence and adduce evidence (Sec. 256) |
| **Arguments** | Prosecutor sums up; accused entitled to **reply** (Sec. 257) |
| **Judgment** | Within **30 days** of completion of arguments (extendable to **45 days**) (Sec. 258) — New timeline |

### New Provisions in BNSS

1. **Sec. 250(1)**: Application for discharge must be filed within **60 days** of commitment.
2. **Sec. 251(1)(b)**: Charge must be framed within **60 days** from first hearing on charge.
3. **Sec. 254(1) Proviso**: Evidence may be recorded by **audio-video electronic means**.
4. **Sec. 254(2)**: Public servant's deposition may be taken through **audio-video means**.
5. **Sec. 258(1)**: Judgment within **30 days** (extendable to **45 days**) — ensures speedy trial.

### Stages of Sessions Trial (Sequential Flow)

```
Commitment (Sec. 232)
    ↓
Opening of Case by PP (Sec. 249)
    ↓
Application for Discharge (Sec. 250) — within 60 days
    ↓
Framing of Charge (Sec. 251) — within 60 days of first hearing
    ↓
Plea of Accused (Sec. 251(2))
    ↓
    ├── Guilty → Conviction (Sec. 252)
    └── Not Guilty / Claims Trial
            ↓
        Prosecution Evidence (Sec. 254)
            ↓
        Acquittal if no evidence (Sec. 255)
            ↓
        Defence Evidence (Sec. 256)
            ↓
        Arguments (Sec. 257)
            ↓
        Judgment (Sec. 258) — within 30/45 days
```

## 6. Trial of Warrant Cases by Magistrates

### A. Warrant Cases on Police Report (Secs. 261-266)

| Stage | Section | Provision |
|-------|---------|-----------|
| Commencement | Sec. 261 | Compliance with Sec. 230 (supply of documents) |
| Discharge | Sec. 262 | Application within **60 days** (New); discharge if charge groundless |
| Charge | Sec. 263 | Framing within **60 days** from first hearing (New) |
| Plea | Sec. 264 | Guilty → conviction |
| Prosecution Evidence | Sec. 265 | Witnesses; may be by **audio-video** means |
| Defence Evidence | Sec. 266 | Accused called upon to enter defence |

### B. Warrant Cases Otherwise Than Police Report (Secs. 267-270)

| Stage | Section | Provision |
|-------|---------|-----------|
| Evidence | Sec. 267 | Prosecution evidence heard first |
| Discharge | Sec. 268 | If no case made out → discharge |
| Charge & Plea | Sec. 269 | Charge framed; accused asked to plead |
| Cross-Examination | Sec. 269(4)-(6) | Accused may recall prosecution witnesses for cross-examination |
| Witness Unavailability | Sec. 269(7) (New) | If prosecution witness unavailable for cross-examination despite reasonable measures → deemed not examined; Magistrate may close prosecution evidence |
| Defence | Sec. 270 | Defence evidence |

### C. Conclusion of Warrant Trial (Secs. 271-273)

- **Sec. 271**: Acquittal or conviction.
- **Sec. 272**: Absence of complainant → give **30 days** time (New timeline); then discharge.
- **Sec. 273**: Compensation for accusation without reasonable cause.

## 7. Trial of Summons Cases (Secs. 274-282)

### Key Features

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **No formal charge** | Only substance of accusation stated (Sec. 274) |
| **Groundless accusation** | If groundless → release (with effect of discharge) — Sec. 274 Proviso |
| **Plea** | Guilty → conviction (Sec. 275) |
| **Petty offences** | Guilty plea by post/messenger; fine up to Rs. 5,000 (Sec. 276) |
| **Procedure** | Prosecution evidence → defence evidence → acquittal/conviction (Secs. 277-278) |
| **Absence of complainant** | 30 days' time (New); then acquittal (Sec. 279) |
| **Withdrawal** | Complainant may withdraw → acquittal (Sec. 280) |
| **Stop proceedings** | Magistrate may stop and acquit/release (Sec. 281) |
| **Conversion** | May convert to warrant-case procedure for offences > 6 months (Sec. 282) |

### Petty Offences (Sec. 229)

- Offence punishable **only with fine not exceeding Rs. 5,000** (not motor vehicle offences)
- Accused may plead guilty **without appearing** — by post or messenger
- Guilty plea through **authorised advocate** also permitted

## 8. Summary Trials (Secs. 283-288)

### Sec. 283 — Power to Try Summarily

**Who can try**: Chief Judicial Magistrate and Magistrate of the First Class.

**Offences triable summarily** (Sec. 283(1)):
- Theft where property value ≤ **Rs. 20,000**
- Receiving/retaining stolen property where value ≤ Rs. 20,000
- House-breaking
- Insult with intent to provoke breach of peace
- Criminal intimidation
- Abetment/attempt of the foregoing
- Cattle trespass

**Sec. 283(2) (New in BNSS)**: Magistrate may, **after hearing the accused**, try summarily any offence **not punishable with death, life imprisonment, or imprisonment exceeding 3 years**.

> **New in BNSS**: The expansion of summary trial jurisdiction to all offences punishable up to 3 years is a significant change to promote speedy disposal.

**Sec. 283(3)**: If during summary trial, it appears undesirable to continue summarily → Magistrate shall recall witnesses and **re-hear** the case under regular procedure.

### Sec. 284 — Procedure for Summary Trials
- Procedure for summons cases applies, but record of trial must contain:
  - Serial number and date of the case
  - Name of the complainant
  - Name, parentage, and residence of accused
  - Offence complained of or proved
  - Plea of the accused and examination
  - Finding
  - Sentence or order
  - Date of order

### Sec. 286 — Language of Record
- Record shall be in the language of the Court.

### Sec. 287 — Sentence in Summary Trials
- No sentence of imprisonment exceeding **3 months** shall be passed (New — previously 1 year for First Class Magistrate).

### Sec. 288 — Appeal
- In every case tried summarily in which the accused does not plead guilty → brief statement of reasons for finding must be recorded.

## 9. Evidence in Inquiries and Trials (Secs. 307-318, 319-336)

### Key Provisions

| Section | Provision |
|---------|-----------|
| **Sec. 307** | Court shall record evidence in **open court** |
| **Sec. 308** | Evidence to be taken in presence of accused or advocate |
| **Sec. 309** | Examination of complainant |
| **Sec. 310** | Record of evidence in summons cases |
| **Sec. 311** | Record of evidence in other cases |
| **Sec. 312** | Interpretation of evidence |
| **Sec. 316** | Power to examine witness — court may summon any witness at any stage |
| **Sec. 317** | Examination of accused — court may put questions to accused at any stage (Sec. 313 CrPC equivalent) |

### Sec. 317 — Examination of Accused (Sec. 313 CrPC Equivalent)

- Court shall question accused on circumstances appearing in evidence **against him** to enable him to explain.
- Accused may file **written statement** in addition to oral examination.
- **No oath** administered to the accused.
- Accused may refuse to answer.
- Answers may be taken into consideration but silence or refusal **cannot** lead to adverse inference.

### Commission for Examination of Witnesses (Secs. 319-336)

- Court may issue **commission** for examination of witnesses who cannot attend.
- May also be examined through **audio-video electronic means** (New).

## 10. Plea Bargaining (Secs. 289-300)

### Sec. 289 — Application of Chapter

Plea bargaining applies to offences:
- Punishable with imprisonment up to **7 years**
- **Not applicable** to:
  - Offences affecting **socio-economic conditions** of the country
  - Offences committed against **woman** or **child below 14 years**

### Sec. 290 — Application for Plea Bargaining

- Accused may file application in the court where trial is pending.
- Application must contain:
  - Brief description of the case
  - Offence to which the case relates
  - **Voluntary** statement of the accused

### Procedure

| Step | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Filing** | Accused files application; court issues notice to PP and complainant |
| **Verification** | Court ensures application is **voluntary** and not under threat/coercion — in camera proceedings |
| **Mutually Satisfactory Disposition** | Court provides time to parties to work out arrangements including compensation to victim |
| **Guidelines** | Court facilitates; cannot participate in negotiations |
| **Disposition** | Court disposes of case as per mutually agreed terms |

### Sec. 293 — Disposal of Case

- Court may pass judgment based on mutually satisfactory disposition:
  - Award **compensation** to victim
  - Sentence accused to **one-half** of the minimum punishment (if minimum prescribed)
  - Sentence accused to **one-fourth** of the punishment (if no minimum prescribed)

### Sec. 296 — Statement of Accused

- Statement of accused recorded during plea bargaining shall **not be used** for any other purpose except for plea bargaining proceedings.

### Sec. 300 — Limitation

- No appeal against judgment under plea bargaining (except on jurisdictional grounds or coercion).

## 11. Principle of Autrefois Acquit and Autrefois Convict (Sec. 337 & Art. 20)

### Sec. 337 — Person Once Convicted or Acquitted Not to Be Tried for Same Offence

**Sec. 337(1)** — A person who has once been tried by a court of competent jurisdiction for an offence and **convicted or acquitted** of such offence shall not be tried again for the **same offence** or on the **same facts** for any other offence for which a different charge could have been made.

**Sec. 337(2)** — A person acquitted or convicted of any offence may afterwards be tried for any **distinct offence** for which a separate charge might have been made.

### Constitutional Protection — Art. 20(2)

"No person shall be prosecuted and punished for the same offence more than once."

> **Key Distinction**: Art. 20(2) protects against prosecution and punishment, while Sec. 337 protects against being tried again (whether convicted or acquitted).

## 12. Compounding of Offences and Withdrawal (Secs. 359-360)

### Sec. 359 — Compounding of Offences

- Certain offences may be **compounded** (settled between parties) — with or without court permission.
- Two categories:
  - **Compoundable without permission**: Minor offences (e.g., simple hurt, defamation)
  - **Compoundable with permission of court**: More serious offences
- Effect: Acquittal of the accused.

### Sec. 360 — Withdrawal from Prosecution

- **Public Prosecutor** or **Assistant PP** may, with consent of the court, **withdraw** from prosecution at any time before judgment.
- If withdrawal is before charge → **discharge** (accused may be re-prosecuted).
- If withdrawal is after charge → **acquittal** (no re-prosecution for the same offence).

## 13. Irregular Proceedings and Inherent Powers (Secs. 506-528)

### Sec. 506-512 — Irregular Proceedings

| Section | Issue |
|---------|-------|
| **Sec. 506** | Irregularity in arrest or personal search → does not vitiate proceedings |
| **Sec. 507** | Irregularity in attaching property → does not vitiate |
| **Sec. 508** | Proceeding in wrong place → does not vitiate |
| **Sec. 509** | Non-compliance with Sec. 230 → may vitiate if prejudice caused |
| **Sec. 510** | Error in charge → does not vitiate unless prejudice and failure of justice |
| **Sec. 511** | Finding/sentence by court of competent jurisdiction → not reversible on ground of irregularity |
| **Sec. 512** | Defect/error in complaint/warrant/summons → does not vitiate |

> **Key Principle**: Mere irregularity does not vitiate proceedings unless it has caused **prejudice** and **failure of justice**.

### Sec. 520 — Limitation on Taking Cognizance

- Period of limitation for various categories of offences (3 years for offences punishable with fine only, etc.)

### Sec. 525-527 — General Provisions

- **Sec. 525**: Trial before High Court → same procedure as Sessions trial.
- **Sec. 526**: Tender of pardon to accomplice during trial.
- **Sec. 527**: Trial to be conducted in Hindi, English, or language of the Court.

### Sec. 528 — Inherent Powers of High Court

**Nothing in this Sanhita shall be deemed to limit or affect the inherent powers of the High Court to make such orders as may be necessary:**
- To give effect to any order under this Sanhita
- To prevent **abuse of process** of any court
- To otherwise secure the **ends of justice**

> **Landmark Case**: *R.P. Kapur v. State of Punjab* (1960) — Supreme Court identified three categories where the High Court should exercise its inherent powers to quash proceedings: (i) no legal evidence, (ii) allegations even if taken at face value do not constitute offence, (iii) express legal bar.

> **Landmark Case**: *State of Haryana v. Bhajan Lal* (1992) — Supreme Court laid down 7 categories where FIR/criminal proceedings may be quashed under Sec. 482 CrPC (now Sec. 528 BNSS).

## Landmark Judgments

### 1. Hussainara Khatoon v. Home Secretary, State of Bihar (1980)
- **Held**: Right to speedy trial is a fundamental right under Art. 21.
- **Significance**: Led to release of thousands of undertrial prisoners.

### 2. Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India (1978)
- **Held**: Procedure under Art. 21 must be just, fair, and reasonable.

### 3. R.P. Kapur v. State of Punjab (1960)
- **Held**: Three categories for quashing proceedings under inherent powers.

### 4. State of Haryana v. Bhajan Lal (1992)
- **Held**: Seven categories where criminal proceedings may be quashed by High Court.

### 5. Sheonandan Paswan v. State of Bihar (1987)
- **Held**: Withdrawal from prosecution is an executive function subject to judicial control; court must exercise judicial mind.

### 6. K. Veeraswami v. Union of India (1991)
- **Held**: Previous sanction is mandatory for prosecution of a Judge; acts must be connected with official duty.
