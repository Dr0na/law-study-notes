# Module 6: Judgment, Probation and Parole, Appeals, Reference, Revision and Execution

## Introduction

This module covers the culmination of the criminal trial process — the judgment — and the post-trial mechanisms available under the BNSS, 2023. It addresses the form and content of judgments, sentencing principles including probation and release on admonition, the system of appeals, reference to the High Court, revision jurisdiction, confirmation of death sentences, and the execution of sentences. These provisions ensure that the criminal justice system provides safeguards against erroneous convictions and disproportionate sentences.

### A. Pronouncement of Judgment (Sec. 392)

**Sec. 392(1)** — Judgment in every trial in any Criminal Court of original jurisdiction shall be **pronounced in open court** by the presiding officer:

| Mode | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **(a)** Delivering | Delivering the whole of the judgment |
| **(b)** Reading out | Reading out the whole of the judgment |
| **(c)** Reading operative part | Reading out the operative part and explaining the substance in a language understood by the accused or advocate |

**Timeline**: Judgment shall be pronounced **immediately after the termination of trial** or at some subsequent time **not later than 45 days**.

**Sec. 392(4) — New in BNSS**: The Court shall, as far as practicable, **upload the copy of the judgment on its portal within 7 days** from the date of judgment.

**Sec. 392(5)**: If accused is in custody → brought up to hear judgment in person or **through audio-video electronic means** (New).

**Sec. 392(7)**: Judgment shall not be invalid merely due to absence of any party on the day of delivery.

### B. Language and Contents of Judgment (Sec. 393)

**Sec. 393(1)** — Every judgment shall:
- **(a)** Be written in the **language of the Court**
- **(b)** Contain **points for determination**, the **decision** thereon, and **reasons** for the decision
- **(c)** Specify the **offence** and **section** under which convicted, and the **punishment**
- **(d)** If acquittal → state the offence and direct that accused be **set at liberty**

**Sec. 393(3)** — In cases of death or life imprisonment → must state **reasons for the sentence** awarded; for death → **special reasons** must be recorded.

> **Landmark Case**: *Bachan Singh v. State of Punjab* (1980) — Supreme Court laid down the **"rarest of rare"** doctrine for imposition of death penalty. Special reasons must be recorded.

**Sec. 393(4)** — When conviction is for an offence punishable with 1 year or more but sentence is less than 3 months → Court must record **reasons** for awarding such sentence.

**Sec. 393(5)** — Death sentence → shall direct that he be **"hanged by the neck till he is dead."**

### C. Other Judgment Provisions

| Section | Provision |
|---------|-----------|
| **Sec. 403** | Court shall **not alter or review** judgment after signing, except to correct **clerical or arithmetical error** |
| **Sec. 404** | Copy of judgment to be given to accused **free of cost** immediately after pronouncement if sentenced to imprisonment; copy of death sentence → **immediately, free of cost** |
| **Sec. 404(4)** | If death sentence → Court shall **inform accused** of the period within which appeal should be preferred |
| **Sec. 405** | Original judgment filed with record; translation to Court language if required |
| **Sec. 406** | Court of Session/CJM to forward copy of finding and sentence to **District Magistrate** |

### D. Compensation (Secs. 395-396)

#### Sec. 395 — Order to Pay Compensation

- When fine imposed or sentence of fine is part → Court may order whole or part of fine to be applied for:
  - **(a)** Defraying **prosecution expenses**
  - **(b)** Compensation for **loss or injury** caused by the offence
  - **(c)** Compensation in **fatal accident** cases
  - **(d)** Compensation to **bona fide purchaser** of stolen property

**Sec. 395(3)** — Even where fine does not form part of sentence → Court may order **compensation** to the victim.

#### Sec. 396 — Victim Compensation Scheme (New in BNSS)

- Every **State Government** in coordination with Central Government shall prepare a **Victim Compensation Scheme**.
- **District/State Legal Services Authority** decides the quantum.
- If trial court compensation is **inadequate** → may recommend further compensation.
- Where offender is **not traced** but victim is identified → victim/dependents may apply directly.
- **Immediate first-aid/medical benefits** to be provided free of cost (Sec. 396(6)).
- Compensation is **in addition to** fines under BNS provisions for rape, etc. (Sec. 396(7)).

#### Sec. 397 — Treatment of Victims (New)

All hospitals (public/private) shall **immediately provide first-aid or medical treatment free of cost** to victims of sexual offences and acid attacks.

#### Sec. 398 — Witness Protection Scheme (New)

Every State Government shall prepare and notify a **Witness Protection Scheme**.

## 2. Probation and Release on Admonition (Sec. 401)

### Sec. 401 — Release on Probation of Good Conduct or After Admonition

#### Who Is Eligible?

| Category | Eligible For |
|----------|-------------|
| Person **not under 21 years** | Offence punishable with fine only OR imprisonment up to **7 years** |
| Person **under 21 years** OR **woman** | Any offence not punishable with **death or life imprisonment** |

**Conditions**:
- No **previous conviction** proved against the offender
- Court considers it expedient having regard to **age, character, antecedents**, and **circumstances** of offence

#### Probation Order (Sec. 401(1))

Instead of sentencing → Court may direct release on **bond or bail bond**:
- To appear and receive sentence when called upon during such period (**not exceeding 3 years**)
- To **keep the peace** and be of **good behavior**

#### Release After Admonition (Sec. 401(3))

For offences of:
- Theft, theft in building, dishonest misappropriation, cheating
- Any offence under BNS punishable with **not more than 2 years**
- Any offence punishable with **fine only**

→ Court may release **after due admonition** (without any bond), considering:
- Age, character, antecedents
- Physical or mental condition of offender
- Trivial nature of offence or extenuating circumstances

#### Sec. 401(7) — Fixed Abode Requirement
Before directing release → Court must be satisfied that offender or surety has **fixed place of abode** or **regular occupation** in the area.

#### Sec. 401(8)-(9) — Breach of Conditions
- If offender fails to observe conditions → Court may issue **warrant for apprehension**
- On apprehension → may remand in custody or admit to bail → then **pass sentence**

#### Sec. 402 — Special Reasons
Where Court could have dealt with accused under Sec. 401 or Probation of Offenders Act but **did not** → must record **special reasons** in judgment.

> **Landmark Case**: *Ishwar Singh v. State of MP* (2008) — Supreme Court emphasised that when conditions for probation are met, the court should not refuse without recording special reasons.

## 3. Confirmation of Death Sentences (Secs. 407-412)

### Procedure

| Step | Section | Description |
|------|---------|-------------|
| Submission | **Sec. 407** | Sessions Court must **forthwith submit** proceedings to High Court; sentence **not executed** unless confirmed |
| Further inquiry | **Sec. 408** | HC may make further inquiry or take additional evidence itself or through Sessions Court |
| HC Powers | **Sec. 409** | HC may: (a) confirm sentence or pass other sentence; (b) annul conviction and order retrial; (c) acquit |
| Signing | **Sec. 410** | Confirmation must be signed by **at least 2 Judges** |
| Division | **Sec. 411** | If HC Judges equally divided → decided as per Sec. 433 (laid before another Judge) |
| Communication | **Sec. 412** | Copy of HC order sent **without delay** — physically or through **electronic means** (New) |

> **Important**: No order of confirmation can be made until period for appeal has expired or appeal is disposed of (Sec. 409 Proviso).

## 4. Appeals (Secs. 413-435)

### A. General Principle (Sec. 413)

- No appeal except as provided by BNSS or other law.
- **Proviso (New in BNSS)**: **Victim** has right to appeal against:
  - Acquittal of accused
  - Conviction for a **lesser offence**
  - **Inadequate compensation**

### B. Appeals from Convictions (Sec. 415)

| From | To | Details |
|------|----|---------|
| Trial by **High Court** (extraordinary jurisdiction) | **Supreme Court** | Sec. 415(1) |
| Trial by **Sessions/Additional Sessions Judge** | **High Court** | Sec. 415(2); also when sentence > 7 years by any court |
| Trial by **Magistrate** (1st or 2nd class) | **Court of Session** | Sec. 415(3) |

**Sec. 415(4) (New)**: Appeal against conviction for sexual offences (Secs. 64-71 BNS) shall be disposed of within **6 months**.

### C. No Appeal When Guilty Plea (Sec. 416)

Where accused has **pleaded guilty**:
- Conviction by HC → **no appeal**
- Conviction by Sessions/Magistrate → appeal only as to **extent or legality of sentence**

### D. No Appeal in Petty Cases (Sec. 417)

No appeal where:
- HC passes ≤ 3 months imprisonment or ≤ Rs. 1,000 fine
- Sessions passes ≤ 3 months or ≤ Rs. 200 fine
- Magistrate I Class passes ≤ Rs. 100 fine
- Summary trial → ≤ Rs. 200 fine

### E. Appeal Against Sentence (Sec. 418)

- **State/Central Government** may direct PP to appeal against sentence on ground of **inadequacy**.
- **Sec. 418(3)**: Sentence shall not be enhanced unless accused has had **opportunity of showing cause**; accused may plead for **acquittal or reduction**.
- **Sec. 418(4) (New)**: Appeal against sentence for sexual offences → disposed of within **6 months**.

### F. Appeal Against Acquittal (Sec. 419)

| By Whom | To | Details |
|---------|----|---------|
| **District Magistrate** (may direct PP) | Court of Session | From Magistrate's acquittal in cognizable/non-bailable case |
| **State Government** (may direct PP) | High Court | From any court (other than HC) |
| **Central Government** | High Court | Where offence investigated by central agency |
| **Complainant** | High Court | With **special leave** (Sec. 419(4)) |

**Sec. 419(3)**: No appeal to HC (under sub-secs 1 or 2) without **leave of the High Court**.

**Sec. 419(5)**: Application for special leave → **6 months** (public servant) / **60 days** (others) from date of acquittal.

### G. Suspension of Sentence and Bail Pending Appeal (Sec. 430)

- Appellate Court may **suspend execution** of sentence and release on bail.
- For death/life/10+ years → must give **PP opportunity** to show cause against release.
- Convicted and on bail + sentence ≤ 3 years → shall be released on bail unless **special reasons** for refusal.
- Time on bail pending appeal → **excluded** from computing sentence.

### H. Abatement of Appeals (Sec. 435)

- Appeal by State/complainant → **abates on death of accused**.
- Appeal by convicted person → **abates on death of appellant** (except: near relative may apply within 30 days for leave to continue).

## 5. Reference to High Court (Secs. 436-437)

### Sec. 436 — When Reference is Made

**Sec. 436(1)** — When any court is satisfied that a case involves a question as to the **validity of any Act/Ordinance/Regulation** and the court considers it invalid/inoperative (but HC/SC has not so declared) → shall **state a case** and **refer** to the High Court.

**Sec. 436(2)** — Court of Session may refer **any question of law** arising in a pending case.

**Sec. 436(3)** — Pending reference → Court may commit accused to jail or release on bail.

### Sec. 437 — Disposal

- High Court passes such order as it thinks fit.
- Referring court shall dispose of the case **conformably** to the HC order.
- HC may direct **costs**.

## 6. Revision (Secs. 438-442)

### Sec. 438 — Power to Call for Records (Revisional Jurisdiction)

**Sec. 438(1)** — The **High Court** or any **Sessions Judge** may call for and examine the record of any proceeding before any inferior Criminal Court to satisfy itself/himself as to:
- **Correctness** of finding
- **Legality** of sentence or order
- **Propriety** of sentence or order
- **Regularity** of proceedings

May direct **suspension of sentence** and **release on bail** pending revision.

**Sec. 438(2)** — No application for revision by a person convicted at a trial held by High Court.

**Sec. 438(3)** — If application for revision made by person convicted on trial by Sessions Court → application shall first be made to **Sessions Judge**; only if rejected → to High Court.

**Sec. 438(4)** — Where any proceeding is pending before sessions court in revision → **no application** to HC unless Sessions Court declines to exercise jurisdiction.

**Sec. 438(5)** — Where revision application made by **any person**, High Court or Sessions Judge may **direct stay** of proceedings.

### Sec. 439 — Powers of Revisional Court

The High Court or Sessions Judge may:
- **(a)** Call for further information
- **(b)** Direct the Magistrate to make inquiry as directed
- **(c)** In case of Sessions Judge → **cannot convert acquittal to conviction** (but may order retrial)

### Sec. 440 — Power of High Court

In revision, the High Court may exercise any power conferred on a Court of Appeal by Secs. 427-432 (appellate powers), and may:
- **Enhance sentence**
- But **cannot** enhance sentence in revision where it could have been done in appeal under Sec. 418
- **Cannot** convert acquittal to conviction

**Sec. 440(5)** — Where accused is called upon to show cause against enhancement → entitled to plead for **acquittal or reduction of sentence**.

### Sec. 441 — Sessions Judge's Power

Sessions Judge may exercise all powers of a Magistrate, including ordering further investigation or additional evidence.

### Sec. 442 — Finality of Orders in Revision

High Court's order in revision is **final**.

### Key Distinctions: Appeal vs. Revision

| Aspect | Appeal | Revision |
|--------|--------|----------|
| **Nature** | Matter of **right** (where provided) | Matter of **discretion** |
| **Scope** | Full review — facts and law | Limited — correctness, legality, propriety |
| **Who initiates** | Aggrieved party | Court (suo motu) or aggrieved party |
| **Power over acquittal** | May convert acquittal to conviction | **Cannot** convert acquittal to conviction |
| **Power over sentence** | May enhance/reduce | May enhance (HC only), but limited |
| **Limitation** | Specific time limits | No specific time limit |

## 7. Execution of Sentences (Secs. 443-477)

### A. Death Sentence (Secs. 443-455)

| Section | Provision |
|---------|-----------|
| **Sec. 443** | Death sentence → when confirmed by HC, **warrant** shall issue for execution |
| **Sec. 444** | Pregnant woman sentenced to death → HC may commute to **life imprisonment** |
| **Sec. 445** | State Government power to **suspend execution** or **commute** death sentence |
| **Sec. 445(4)** | Appropriate Government may commute death sentence even without consent of offender |

### B. Imprisonment (Secs. 447-448)

| Section | Provision |
|---------|-----------|
| **Sec. 447** | Warrant for execution of sentence of imprisonment → Sec. 447(1) |
| **Sec. 448** | Where accused is **already sentenced** to life imprisonment → death sentence overrides |

### C. Fines (Secs. 449-456)

| Section | Provision |
|---------|-----------|
| **Sec. 449** | Time for payment of fine — if sentence does not include imprisonment and fine not paid → Court may order detention for **up to 30 days** |
| **Sec. 450** | Warrant for levy of fine → may direct fine to be levied by distress and sale of movable property |
| **Sec. 451** | Suspension of execution of fine pending appeal |

### D. Suspension, Remission and Commutation (Secs. 466-477)

#### Sec. 473 — Power to Suspend or Remit Sentences

Appropriate Government may:
- **Suspend** execution of any sentence
- **Remit** the whole or any part of the punishment

#### Sec. 474 — Power to Commute Sentence

Appropriate Government may commute:
- Death sentence → any other sentence
- Life imprisonment → imprisonment for up to **14 years** or fine
- Rigorous imprisonment → simple imprisonment or fine
- Simple imprisonment → fine

> **"Appropriate Government"** means:
> - Cases under Central Act investigated by central agency → **Central Government**
> - Other cases → **State Government**

#### Sec. 475 — Concurrent Power of Central Government

Powers of the State Government under Secs. 473-474 may also be exercised by the **Central Government**.

#### Sec. 476 — Restriction on Powers

Powers under Secs. 473-474 cannot be exercised by State Government where sentence relates to offence under a **Central Act** or where the accused is sentenced by a Court established under that Act, unless such Act expressly provides otherwise.

## 8. Parole

### Nature and Definition

**Parole** is a system of releasing a prisoner before the completion of his sentence on certain conditions. It is not a right but a **privilege** granted by the State Government. Parole is governed by **prison rules** of the respective State, not by the BNSS directly.

### Types

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Regular Parole** | For a longer duration (21-30 days); for events like death/marriage in family, serious illness of near relative |
| **Emergency Parole (Furlough)** | Short duration; granted as a right after serving a specified minimum period |

### Distinction: Bail vs. Parole

| Aspect | Bail | Parole |
|--------|------|--------|
| **Stage** | Before/during trial | After conviction and during imprisonment |
| **Source** | BNSS | State prison rules |
| **Nature** | Right (bailable) / Discretion (non-bailable) | Privilege |
| **Duration** | Until trial/appeal concludes | Specific period (21-30 days) |
| **Authority** | Court | State Government / Prison authorities |

> **Landmark Case**: *State of Haryana v. Mohinder Singh* (2000) — Supreme Court held that parole and furlough are reformative aspects of criminal justice; they allow the prisoner to maintain family ties and social contacts.

## Landmark Judgments

### 1. Bachan Singh v. State of Punjab (1980)
- **Held**: Death penalty valid but to be imposed only in the **"rarest of rare"** cases. Court must state special reasons.
- **BNSS Relevance**: Sec. 393(3)

### 2. Machhi Singh v. State of Punjab (1983)
- **Held**: Laid down categories for "rarest of rare" — manner of commission, motive, anti-social nature of crime, magnitude of crime, and personality of victim.

### 3. Hussainara Khatoon v. Home Secretary (1980)
- **Held**: Speedy trial is a fundamental right under Art. 21.
- **BNSS Relevance**: Sec. 392 (judgment within 45 days)

### 4. Ishwar Singh v. State of MP (2008)
- **Held**: Courts must consider probation; failure to grant probation when conditions exist requires special reasons.
- **BNSS Relevance**: Sec. 401, Sec. 402

### 5. K.M. Nanavati v. State of Maharashtra (1962)
- **Held**: Governor's power to suspend sentence pending petition for pardon/commutation. Powers under Art. 72/161 are distinct from appellate court powers.
- **BNSS Relevance**: Sec. 473-474 (suspension, remission, commutation)

### 6. Sharad Birdhichand Sarda v. State of Maharashtra (1984)
- **Held**: Five "golden threads" of circumstantial evidence — circumstances proved must be consistent only with the guilt of the accused and must exclude every reasonable hypothesis of innocence.

### 7. State of Maharashtra v. Prabhakar Pandurang Sanzgiri (1966)
- **Held**: Revisional court should not ordinarily interfere with findings of fact by the trial court unless there is manifest error of law or procedure.
- **BNSS Relevance**: Sec. 438-440

## Quick Revision Points

✅ **Sec. 392**: Judgment in **open court**; within **45 days**; upload to portal within **7 days** (New); audio-video appearance for accused in custody (New)

✅ **Sec. 393**: Judgment must contain points for determination, decision, and reasons; **special reasons** for death sentence

✅ **Sec. 393(5)**: Death sentence — **"hanged by the neck till he is dead"**

✅ **Sec. 395**: Compensation from fine; **Sec. 395(3)** — compensation even without fine

✅ **Sec. 396 (New)**: State Victim Compensation Scheme; District/State Legal Services Authority decides quantum

✅ **Sec. 397 (New)**: All hospitals must provide **free first-aid** to victims of sexual offences

✅ **Sec. 398 (New)**: Every State to notify **Witness Protection Scheme**

✅ **Sec. 401**: Probation — up to 7 years offences (for persons ≥21); any non-death/non-life offence (for <21 or women); bond for **max 3 years**

✅ **Sec. 401(3)**: Release after **admonition** — offences up to 2 years or fine only

✅ **Sec. 402**: **Special reasons** must be recorded if probation not granted

✅ **Sec. 403**: No **alteration** of judgment after signing (except clerical/arithmetical error)

✅ **Secs. 407-412**: Death sentence **must be confirmed** by HC; signed by **at least 2 Judges**; not executed until confirmation

✅ **Sec. 413 Proviso (New)**: **Victim's right** to appeal acquittal/lesser offence/inadequate compensation

✅ **Sec. 415**: Appeal hierarchy — Magistrate → Session; Session → HC; HC → SC

✅ **Sec. 416**: No appeal on guilty plea (except legality/extent of sentence)

✅ **Sec. 419**: Appeal against acquittal — by State/DM (with PP); complainant with **special leave**; time limit: 6 months (public servant), 60 days (others)

✅ **Sec. 430**: Suspension of sentence + bail pending appeal; convicted + on bail + ≤3 years → **shall release** (unless special reasons)

✅ **Sec. 435**: Appeal abates on death of accused (State appeal) or appellant (others), but near relative may apply within **30 days**

✅ **Sec. 436**: **Reference** to HC — question of validity of Act/Ordinance/Regulation

✅ **Sec. 438**: **Revision** — HC or Sessions Judge; check correctness, legality, propriety, regularity

✅ **Revision cannot** convert acquittal to conviction; **appeal can**

✅ **Sec. 474**: Commutation — death → any other; life → up to **14 years** or fine

✅ *Bachan Singh* (1980) — **rarest of rare** doctrine for death penalty

✅ *Nanavati* (1962) — Governor's power to suspend pending pardon/commutation

✅ **Parole** is a **privilege**, not a right; governed by State prison rules, not BNSS directly
