# Answer Writing Template — Interpretation of Statutes

## 15-mark doctrinal answer

1. **Define the rule or issue.** State the interpretive problem, not just a maxim.
2. **Set out the legal framework.** Identify statutory text, relevant definition and constitutional context.
3. **Explain the rule.** Describe purpose, prerequisites and limits.
4. **Apply with a short example.** Show how the words operate in context.
5. **Use authority.** Give the case name, citation if known, and the precise holding.
6. **Compare or qualify.** Distinguish related rules, such as ejusdem generis/noscitur or literal/golden.
7. **Conclude.** State the likely construction and why it fits text and scheme.

## Problem question

Use: **Issue → Text → Context → Rule → Application → Counter-reading → Conclusion.** Address both plausible interpretations before selecting one.

## Common mistakes

- stating that purpose overrides clear statutory language;
- treating “shall” as always mandatory or “may” as always directory;
- using ejusdem generis without identifying a genus;
- treating non-obstante as overriding all laws automatically;
- extending a legal fiction beyond its purpose;
- using Parliamentary debates to contradict enacted text;
- treating all external aids as equally authoritative;
- confusing expiry with repeal;
- assuming every retrospective law is invalid;
- quoting a case for a proposition it did not decide.
