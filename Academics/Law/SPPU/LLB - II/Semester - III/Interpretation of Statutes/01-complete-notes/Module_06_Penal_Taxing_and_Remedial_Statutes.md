# Module 6: Penal, Taxing and Remedial Statutes

## 1. Strict construction: meaning and limits

Strict construction means applying the enacted language carefully and refusing to extend a burden, offence or tax beyond what the legislature has clearly imposed. It does not mean choosing an artificially narrow meaning regardless of text, context or purpose. Modern interpretation reads the provision as a whole and gives effect to its language and scheme.

## 2. Taxing statutes

Tax liability must have clear statutory authority. A person cannot be taxed by implication or equitable considerations. The charge, person liable, taxable event, rate and measure must be found in the statute. Where the charging provision is genuinely ambiguous, the ambiguity is generally resolved in favour of the taxpayer; exemptions and deductions are construed under their own statutory language and governing precedent.

**Cases:** *A.V. Fernandez v. State of Kerala*, AIR 1957 SC 657 (tax liability must be supported by the charging words); *Commissioner of Customs v. Dilip Kumar & Co.*, (2018) 9 SCC 1 (ambiguity in an exemption notification resolved in favour of Revenue, after the claimant establishes eligibility for exemption under the governing framework). Distinguish uncertainty about the charge from uncertainty about an exemption.

## 3. Penal statutes

Penal provisions are construed with fair notice and legality in mind. Courts do not create offences by analogy or impose punishment beyond the statutory language. If, after ordinary interpretive tools, genuine ambiguity remains about the reach of a penal provision, the accused may receive the benefit of the narrower construction. This does not permit courts to ignore clear wording or legislative purpose.

The legality principle is reflected in Article 20(1): no person may be convicted for an act that was not an offence when done, nor receive a greater penalty than then prescribed.

## 4. Mens rea in statutory offences and BNS, 2023

Mens rea is the mental element such as intention, knowledge, dishonesty or negligence required for an offence. The presumption is that mens rea is required unless the statute expressly or by necessary implication excludes it. The wording, subject matter, penalty, regulatory purpose and feasibility of proving fault inform the inquiry.

**Cases:** *Nathulal v. State of Madhya Pradesh*, AIR 1966 SC 43 (mens rea presumed unless excluded); *State of Maharashtra v. Mayer Hans George*, AIR 1965 SC 722 (statutory context may exclude mens rea in a regulatory offence).

The Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (BNS) came into force on 1 July 2024 and defines the general criminal law framework and punishments. Many special statutes create their own offences and mental elements. Do not assume BNS definitions or general principles replace the express ingredients of a special enactment. Check commencement, repeal/savings and transitional provisions for conduct spanning the changeover.

## 5. Vicarious liability in statutory offences

Vicarious criminal liability attributes an offence to a person because of another person's conduct or a specified relationship. It is not presumed merely from being a director, employer or officer. Parliament must create the liability by clear statutory language, often with conditions such as the person being “in charge of and responsible for” the business, consent, connivance or neglect.

**Cases:** *State of Karnataka v. Pratap Chand*, (1981) 2 SCC 335; *S.M.S. Pharmaceuticals Ltd. v. Neeta Bhalla*, (2005) 8 SCC 89 (statutory requirements for impleading company officers must be pleaded and satisfied). The offence-specific statute controls.

## 6. Remedial and beneficial statutes

Remedial statutes provide a remedy, protection or benefit to address a legal or social problem. Where the text supports more than one reasonable construction, courts may favour a beneficial reading that advances the remedy. Liberal construction cannot eliminate statutory conditions or extend a benefit to a class the legislature excluded.

**Cases:** *B. Shah v. Presiding Officer, Labour Court, Coimbatore*, (1977) 4 SCC 384 (beneficial construction of maternity benefit legislation); *Workmen of American Express International Banking Corp. v. Management*, (1985) 4 SCC 71.

## 7. Directory and mandatory provisions

Whether a procedural requirement is mandatory or directory depends on legislative intent, language, purpose, consequences of non-compliance, prejudice and whether invalidation would defeat the scheme. “Shall” is relevant but not conclusive; “may” is likewise contextual. Ask whether the requirement protects a substantive right, whether the statute specifies consequences, and whether substantial compliance is possible.

**Case:** *Sharif-ud-Din v. Abdul Gani Lone*, (1980) 1 SCC 403 discusses the distinction by reference to purpose and consequences.

## Conclusion

Strictness is a method of respecting statutory limits, not a mechanical rule of narrowness. The governing approach depends on the kind of provision, the precise words and the consequences the legislature intended.
