# Self-Assessment Test — Interpretation of Statutes

**Suggested duration:** 3 hours. The structure is for practice; confirm the current university question-paper pattern.

## Part A — Long answers (attempt any three; 15 marks each)

1. Explain the nature, purpose and principal rules of statutory interpretation. Distinguish interpretation from construction.
2. Explain the General Clauses Act, 1897, its scope and its rules on definitions, commencement and repeal.
3. Discuss the literal, golden and mischief rules, with cases and limits.
4. Explain internal and external aids to interpretation and the limits on using legislative history.
5. Discuss the modes of constitutional interpretation, including pith and substance, repugnancy, basic structure and precedent.
6. Explain strict construction of taxing and penal statutes, mens rea, vicarious liability and liberal construction of remedial statutes.
7. Explain commencement, retrospective operation, expiry and repeal, including the effect of section 6 of the General Clauses Act.

## Part B — Short answers (attempt any four; 10 marks each)

1. Mimansa principles as interpretive aids.
2. Ejusdem generis and noscitur a sociis.
3. Legal fiction and the limits on its extension.
4. Judicial restraint, judicial activism and the anti-majoritarian objection.
5. Presumptions concerning exclusion of court jurisdiction.
6. Mandatory and directory provisions.

## Part C — Short notes (attempt any five; 5 marks each)

1. Non-obstante clause.
2. Rule of last antecedent.
3. Reddendo singula singulis.
4. Words of rank.
5. Prospective overruling.
6. Section 21 of the General Clauses Act.
7. Presumption against retrospectivity.
8. Difference between repeal and expiry.

## Problem questions

1. A statute prohibits “cars, vans, trucks and other vehicles” from entering a protected zone. A party argues “other vehicles” includes aircraft. Identify and apply the relevant contextual rules; state what further statutory context is needed.
2. A new procedural amendment takes effect during a pending case and changes the method of filing evidence. Explain the factors that determine whether it applies to the pending proceeding.
3. A tax statute imposes duty on “steel, copper and other metals.” Determine which interpretive questions arise if the dispute concerns gold, and explain why no single canon decides the case without context.

## Self-review checklist

- Did I begin from enacted text and context?
- Did I state the prerequisites and limits of the rule?
- Did I apply a case to the precise issue?
- Did I distinguish similar doctrines?
- Did I consider an alternative construction before concluding?
