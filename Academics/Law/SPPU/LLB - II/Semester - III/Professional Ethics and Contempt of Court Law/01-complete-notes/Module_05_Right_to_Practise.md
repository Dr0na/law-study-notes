# Module 5: Right to Practise and Its Limits

## 1. Advocates' statutory right — ss.29–34

Section 29 recognises advocates as the only class of persons entitled to practise the profession of law, subject to the Act. Section 30 gives an advocate whose name is entered on a State roll a right to practise throughout India in all courts, including the Supreme Court, before tribunals or persons legally authorised to take evidence, and before other authorities/bodies where the law permits. The right is subject to the Act, court rules and other applicable law.

Section 32 preserves the power of a court, authority or person to permit a non-advocate to appear in a particular case. Section 33 generally bars persons other than advocates from practising before courts/authorities except as otherwise provided. Section 34 empowers High Courts to make rules laying down conditions subject to which an advocate may practise in the High Court and subordinate courts.

## 2. Limits and professional regulation

The right to practise is not absolute immunity from regulation. It is subject to enrolment conditions, BCI and State Bar Council rules, professional discipline, court procedure, designation restrictions for Senior Advocates, and lawful courtroom controls. Courts control audience and appearances; Bar Councils principally regulate professional enrolment and discipline.

## 3. Qualities of effective practice

Honesty, courage, industry, preparation, judgment, clear communication, sound legal research, empathy, discretion, collegiality and independence are professional capacities. Eloquence is useful only when supported by accuracy and relevance. A lawyer should give candid advice, manage expectations and protect client confidentiality.

## 4. Strikes and boycotts

In *Ex-Capt. Harish Uppal v. Union of India*, (2003) 2 SCC 45; AIR 2003 SC 739, the Supreme Court held that lawyers have no right to strike or call for a boycott of courts. A protest may be permissible in the rarest of rare cases involving the dignity, integrity or independence of the Bar or Bench, for no more than one day, after giving prior notice and ensuring no court work is disrupted; the judgment emphasises the exception is extremely narrow. Courts should not adjourn matters merely because of a strike call. An individual lawyer may abstain only in exceptional circumstances and must not abandon clients or obstruct justice.

## 5. Role in justice and legal reform

Advocates assist courts through accurate submissions, fair testing of evidence and respect for procedure. They protect clients' rights, promote legal aid, identify unjust laws, contribute to reform and strengthen public confidence. The advocate's duty to the client is strong but does not authorise deception, suppression or abuse of process.
