# Module 3: The Advocates Act, 1961 and Bar Council Authorities

## 1. Object and structure

The Advocates Act, 1961 consolidates the law relating to legal practitioners and provides for State Bar Councils and an All-India Bar. It covers Bar Council composition and functions, admission/enrolment, right to practise, professional conduct and discipline, rule-making and related matters.

Important defined terms appear in section 2. Read definitions with the context and later amendments.

## 2. State Bar Councils

### Establishment and composition

Section 3 provides for State Bar Councils for States and specified combined territories. Composition varies according to the Act and the State's advocate roll. Members are principally elected by advocates on the State roll through proportional representation by single transferable vote; certain law officers are ex officio members as specified by section 3. The current roll size and any transitional or special-committee arrangement should be checked against the Act and current official rules.

### Term, disqualification and vacancies

Section 8 generally provides a five-year term, subject to the statutory extension mechanism where an election cannot be held; if election is not completed within the prescribed extension, the Council ceases and a Special Committee may perform functions under section 8A. Section 10B addresses disqualification of members. Apply its current clauses and any amendments rather than relying on old summaries.

### Functions — section 6

State Bar Council functions include admitting persons as advocates on its roll, preparing and maintaining the roll, entertaining and determining misconduct cases, safeguarding advocates' rights and interests, promoting law reform, organising legal aid, managing funds and elections, and performing other prescribed functions.

## 3. Bar Council of India

### Composition — section 4

The BCI includes the Attorney-General of India and Solicitor-General of India as ex officio members, and one member elected by each State Bar Council from among its members, subject to the Act. It elects its Chairperson and Vice-Chairperson in accordance with the Act and Rules.

### Functions — section 7

BCI functions include laying down professional conduct and etiquette; prescribing disciplinary procedure; safeguarding advocates' rights; promoting legal education and laying down standards in consultation with universities and State Bar Councils; recognising law degrees and universities for enrolment; conducting legal aid and law reform work; recognising foreign qualifications on reciprocity; managing funds; and supervising State Bar Councils within statutory powers.

### International membership

Section 7(1)(g) authorises the BCI to recognise on a reciprocal basis foreign qualifications for admission as an advocate in India. International professional association membership is not a separate statutory entitlement; any such affiliation is subject to the Act, BCI decisions and applicable law.

## 4. Provisions common to the Councils

- **Body corporate — s.5:** each Bar Council is a body corporate with perpetual succession and a common seal.
- **Committees — ss.9 and 10:** Councils constitute disciplinary and other committees as provided.
- **Business and staff — ss.10A–10C and related provisions:** meeting procedure, staff and service conditions are governed by the Act and rules.
- **Accounts and audit — s.12:** Bar Council accounts are maintained and audited in the manner prescribed.
- **Rule-making — ss.15, 28 and 49:** State Bar Councils and BCI have separate rule-making powers for specified subject matters, subject to statutory approval/publication requirements.

## 5. Rule-making and hierarchy

Parliament sets the statutory framework in the Advocates Act. The BCI may make rules under section 49, including standards of conduct and etiquette. State Bar Councils make rules under section 15 and section 28 within their assigned fields. Court rules regulate appearance and procedure before the court. A rule cannot override the Act or Constitution; distinguish statutory power, valid rules, and administrative resolutions.
