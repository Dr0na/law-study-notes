# Module 6: Professional Misconduct and Disciplinary Process

## 1. Meaning of professional or other misconduct

The Advocates Act does not provide a closed list of misconduct. Section 35 applies when a State Bar Council, on complaint or otherwise, has reason to believe an advocate on its roll is guilty of professional or other misconduct. The conduct must be established through fair procedure and judged in light of professional obligations. Mere error of judgment or negligence is not automatically misconduct; dishonesty, breach of trust, unauthorised compromise, conflict, abandonment, solicitation or conduct undermining the profession may attract discipline depending on evidence and context.

## 2. State Bar Council process — s.35

1. Complaint is received, or the Council acts on its own information.
2. If there is reason to believe misconduct occurred, the matter is referred to a disciplinary committee.
3. The committee gives notice and opportunity to the advocate and complainant, holds an inquiry and may receive evidence.
4. After inquiry, the committee may dismiss the complaint, reprimand the advocate, suspend the advocate from practice for a period, or remove the advocate's name from the State roll.
5. The committee communicates its order as required. The procedure is governed by the Act and BCI Rules.

## 3. BCI disciplinary jurisdiction — s.36

The BCI may withdraw a proceeding pending before a State Bar Council disciplinary committee and inquire itself or refer it to its disciplinary committee, as permitted by the Act. It also has original disciplinary jurisdiction over advocates whose names are not entered on a State roll, subject to statutory provisions.

## 4. One-year transfer rule — s.36B

Disciplinary proceedings not concluded by the State Bar Council disciplinary committee within one year from receipt of the complaint are transferred to the BCI under section 36B, where they are to be disposed of as provided by that section. Do not confuse this transfer period with a limitation period for filing a complaint.

## 5. Appeals and review

- **Appeal to BCI — s.37:** person aggrieved by a State disciplinary committee order may appeal within 60 days from communication, subject to the Act and limitation rules.
- **Appeal to Supreme Court — s.38:** person aggrieved by a BCI disciplinary committee order may appeal within 60 days from communication.
- **Stay — s.40:** appeal does not automatically stay the order; the relevant committee/court may grant stay as the Act allows.
- **Review — s.44:** a disciplinary committee may review its own order within the statutory 60-day period, subject to the statutory procedure and approval requirements where applicable.
- **Alteration of roll — s.41:** the State Bar Council alters the roll to reflect orders of removal, suspension or restoration.

Section 48AA separately concerns review by the BCI or its non-disciplinary committees and was interpreted in *D. Saibaba v. Bar Council of India*, (2003) 6 SCC 186; AIR 2003 SC 2502.

## 6. Important prescribed decisions

- *Chandra Shekhar Soni v. Bar Council of Rajasthan*, (1983) 4 SCC 255; AIR 1983 SC 1012: switching sides and obtaining a brief through improper assurances violate professional standards; disciplinary findings and public confidence are central.
- *Noratanmal Chouraria v. M.R. Murli*, (2004) 5 SCC 689: explains misconduct and proportionality; allegations against a lawyer acting as litigant must be evaluated carefully.
- *Narain Pandey v. Pannalal Pandey*, (2013) 11 SCC 435: unauthorised vakalatnamas and fictitious compromises are grave misconduct; punishment serves deterrence and correction.
- *John D'Souza v. Edward Ani*, (1994) 2 SCC 64; AIR 1994 SC 975: concerns an advocate’s alleged retention and refusal to return a client’s will entrusted for safe custody; use it for client trust and proof in disciplinary proceedings.
