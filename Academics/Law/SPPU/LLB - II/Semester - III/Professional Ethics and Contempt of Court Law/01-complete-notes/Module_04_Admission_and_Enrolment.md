# Module 4: Admission and Enrolment of Advocates

## 1. Senior and other advocates — s.16

The Act recognises two classes: Senior Advocates and other advocates. The Supreme Court or a High Court may designate an advocate as Senior Advocate if, by ability, standing at the Bar or special knowledge/experience in law, the advocate is deserving of distinction. The advocate's consent is required. A Senior Advocate is subject to restrictions on accepting instructions directly from a client and must act through an advocate-on-record or other advocate as prescribed by BCI rules and court rules.

## 2. State roll and seniority — ss.17–22

Each State Bar Council prepares and maintains a roll of advocates. Section 17 requires the roll to be divided into parts as specified. Section 20 gives certain pre-Act practitioners a right to have their names entered. Section 21 provides a procedure for resolving disputes about seniority. Section 18 provides for transfer of an advocate's name from one State roll to another, subject to application and statutory conditions. Seniority is determined according to section 17 and applicable rules, not simply date of current practice in a locality.

## 3. Right of pre-audience — s.23

Section 23 sets the order of pre-audience: Attorney-General of India; Solicitor-General; Additional Solicitor-General(s), including the second Additional Solicitor-General; Advocate-General(s) of the State in inter se seniority; Senior Advocates; and other advocates according to seniority. The precise statutory order is subject to the Act and relevant court rules.

## 4. Qualification — s.24

Subject to the Act and rules, a person is qualified for admission on a State roll if the person is a citizen of India (with a reciprocity provision for foreign nationals), has completed the prescribed age, holds a recognised law degree, fulfils other rules made under the Act, and pays the prescribed enrolment fee and stamp duty. Section 24(1)(c) contains categories and educational conditions; rely on the current consolidated section and BCI rules, not a simplified checklist alone.

## 5. Disqualification — s.24A

Section 24A disqualifies a person from enrolment if convicted of an offence involving moral turpitude, convicted under specified untouchability legislation, or dismissed/removed from employment or office under the State on a charge involving moral turpitude, subject to the section's provisos. The disqualification ceases after the statutory period following release/dismissal as specified. The section contains exceptions, including certain convictions where the Probation of Offenders Act applies. Always read the provisos.

## 6. Application and disposal — ss.25–28

Application is made to the State Bar Council within whose jurisdiction the applicant proposes to practise. The enrolment committee considers the application and may refer doubts to the BCI. Section 26 permits refusal for failure to meet conditions and provides procedural safeguards. Section 26A allows removal of a name on request or where the advocate is deceased. A refusal and later application are governed by sections 26 and 27. The State Council makes rules under section 28, subject to the Act and required approval.

## 7. Case references

- *D. Saibaba v. Bar Council of India*, (2003) 6 SCC 186; AIR 2003 SC 2502: “date of the order” for the 60-day review period under section 48AA means the date the order is communicated or comes to the party's knowledge, as applicable; the review provision concerns the order and limitation context. The course lists this under ethics but it is principally about Bar Council review and limitation.
- *Noratanmal Chouraria v. M.R. Murli*, (2004) 5 SCC 689: professional or other misconduct is assessed in relation to the profession and proportionality; allegations against an advocate acting as litigant must be assessed on evidence and context.
