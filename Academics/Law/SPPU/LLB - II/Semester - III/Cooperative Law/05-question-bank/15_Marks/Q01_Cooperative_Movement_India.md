# Q1. Trace the development of the cooperative movement in India. Explain the role of the State and cooperative institutions.

## Introduction

Cooperation is collective self-help through a jointly owned and democratically controlled enterprise. In India, the movement developed to address rural indebtedness and later expanded into agriculture, marketing, processing, housing, consumer supply and credit.

## Historical development

1. **Co-operative Credit Societies Act, 1904:** created a legal framework for cooperative credit societies, chiefly to provide rural credit and reduce dependence on moneylenders.
2. **Co-operative Societies Act, 1912:** widened the framework beyond credit and enabled central/federal structures.
3. **Provincial responsibility:** the Government of India Act, 1919 transferred cooperation to the provincial field. Provincial legislation developed, including Bombay Presidency cooperative laws.
4. **Maharashtra framework:** the Maharashtra Co-operative Societies Act, 1960 consolidated and amended State law; the Maharashtra Co-operative Societies Rules, 1961 provide procedural detail.
5. **Planned development:** after Independence, Five-Year Plans used cooperatives to channel institutional credit, inputs, storage, marketing and services to rural communities. Expansion varied across regions and sectors.

## Role of the State

The State promotes cooperatives through enabling law, registration, classification and supervision. It may provide capital participation, loans, guarantees, subsidies and technical support under the relevant Act and schemes. The MCS Act's State partnership provisions are principally in Chapter VI, sections 50 onwards. The State can also support education and cooperative development through institutions.

State intervention has two sides. Assistance can build capacity and extend services, while excessive administrative or political control can weaken autonomy and member accountability. Sustainable cooperatives therefore require support accompanied by democratic control and transparent accounts.

## National institutions

- **NCDC:** established by the NCDC Act, 1962, it plans, promotes and finances cooperative development programmes in production, processing, marketing, storage and related sectors.
- **NABARD:** established under the NABARD Act, 1981 and operational from 1982, it supports rural development and provides refinance/institutional assistance to eligible cooperative credit structures.
- **Apex and federal societies:** coordinate affiliated societies, provide common services and facilitate finance or marketing at a larger scale, subject to their registered objects and applicable law.

## Assessment

The movement has widened access to credit and collective services and created institutions for producers and consumers. It has also faced weak participation, poor recovery, politicisation, inadequate professional management and dependence on public aid. Remedies include member education, timely elections, accurate accounts, audit rectification and responsible management.

## Conclusion

Indian cooperation has moved from a narrow rural-credit remedy to a broad institutional form of economic organisation. Its future depends on combining State support with genuine member ownership, professional management and legal accountability.
