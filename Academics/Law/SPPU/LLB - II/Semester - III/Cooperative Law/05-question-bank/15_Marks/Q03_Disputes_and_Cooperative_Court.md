# Q3. Explain the settlement of disputes under the Maharashtra Co-operative Societies Act, 1960.

## Introduction

The MCS Act creates a specialised mechanism for internal cooperative disputes. The Co-operative Court's jurisdiction is statutory and depends on both the subject matter and the parties.

## Reference to Co-operative Court — s.91

A dispute touching the constitution, elections of the committee or its officers, conduct of general meetings, management or business of a society may be referred to the Co-operative Court where the parties fall within the classes listed in section 91. These include societies, committees, specified past or present officers, members or past members and persons claiming through them, subject to the section's precise wording.

## Procedure and decision

The State constitutes Co-operative Courts under section 91A. Section 94 provides for hearing, evidence, witnesses and production of documents using procedures akin to those of a civil court, subject to the Act and Rules. The Court may make appropriate interlocutory orders. Under section 96, it decides the dispute after giving parties a reasonable opportunity to be heard.

## Limitation and remedies

Section 92 contains distinct limitation periods. Election disputes must generally be brought within two months from declaration of results; specified disputes involving acts or omissions of officers or members have a six-year period; other disputes follow the Limitation Act, 1963 subject to the MCS Act. Sufficient cause may permit a delayed reference in cases covered by the section.

Section 97 provides an appeal to the Co-operative Appellate Court against specified decisions/orders, ordinarily within two months. Review and revision are available only in the circumstances specified by the Act. Identify the exact order and remedy rather than assume a general appeal.

## Recovery and civil court jurisdiction

Sections 98, 101 and 156 create statutory recovery routes. Section 101 applies only to the society classes and dues specified by the provision, after the prescribed process. Section 163 bars civil or revenue courts from matters assigned to statutory cooperative authorities, but it does not remove civil jurisdiction over every matter involving a society.

## Conclusion

The specialised forum promotes efficient resolution of disputes connected with cooperative governance while preserving procedural safeguards and appellate review. Correct classification, limitation and forum are decisive.

**Authorities:** MCS Act, 1960, ss.91–98, 101, 149–154, 156, 163.
