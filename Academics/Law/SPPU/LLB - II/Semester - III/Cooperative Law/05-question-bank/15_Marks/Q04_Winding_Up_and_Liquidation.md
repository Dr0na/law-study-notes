# Q4. Describe the procedure for winding up and liquidation of a cooperative society under the MCS Act, 1960.

## Introduction

Winding up is the statutory process by which a society's operations are brought to a close, assets are realised, liabilities settled and any surplus dealt with. Chapter X of the MCS Act, sections 102–110, governs the process.

## Grounds and interim order — s.102

The Registrar may consider winding up after an inquiry or inspection or an auditor's report; on an application supported by a resolution of three-fourths of members present at a special general meeting called for the purpose; or on the Registrar's own motion on the statutory grounds. These grounds include failure to commence work, cessation of work, very low share/member deposits as stated in the Act, or failure to comply with registration or management conditions.

The Registrar issues an interim order and communicates it to the society, calling for an explanation within the statutory period. The society must receive an opportunity of being heard. The Registrar then vacates or confirms the order by a final order.

## Appointment and powers of liquidator — ss.103–105

The Registrar may appoint a liquidator and fix remuneration. Following an interim order, officers hand over the society's property, effects, claims, books and records. On confirmation, officers vacate and the general body does not exercise its powers while the winding-up order remains in force. The society's assets vest in the liquidator.

Subject to Registrar control and the Act/Rules, the liquidator may institute or defend proceedings, carry on business where necessary for beneficial winding up, sell assets, investigate and determine claims, pay creditors according to priority, determine member/officer contributions, and take other necessary steps.

## Effect, accounts and closure — ss.106–110

Once effective, the winding-up order operates for creditors and contributories. Proceedings against the society may require leave of the Registrar. The liquidator presents accounts for audit during the tenure and prepares a final report showing how the process was conducted, claims settled, costs incurred and any surplus. The Registrar terminates proceedings under section 109 and directs the statutory final-meeting/report process. Section 110 governs surplus assets.

## Conclusion

The process balances creditor protection and orderly dissolution with procedural fairness to the society. The winding-up order, liquidation administration and final termination are separate stages; deregistration should not be used as a synonym for all three.

**Authorities:** MCS Act, 1960, ss.102–110.
