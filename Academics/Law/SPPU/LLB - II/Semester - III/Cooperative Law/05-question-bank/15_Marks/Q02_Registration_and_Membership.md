# Q2. Explain registration of a cooperative society and the principal rules governing membership under the MCS Act, 1960.

## Introduction

Registration gives a cooperative society its statutory identity and enables it to operate as a body corporate. The MCS Act, 1960 regulates its objects, promoters, application, bye-laws and membership.

## Registration

1. **Objects — s.4:** a society may be registered to promote members' economic interests or general welfare, or public welfare, in accordance with cooperative principles. Registration may be refused on the statutory grounds of unsoundness, harm to cooperative development or conflict with policy directives.
2. **Liability — s.5:** registration may be with limited or unlimited liability.
3. **Conditions — s.6:** the general rule is at least ten qualified persons from different families residing in the area of operation. The Act provides exceptions and class-specific conditions; a federal society requires at least five societies as members. Unlimited-liability societies must satisfy the statutory locality condition.
4. **Application — s.8:** promoters submit the prescribed application, four copies of proposed bye-laws, prescribed fee and required information. Signatures/authorisations depend on the proposed society's form.
5. **Registrar's decision — s.9:** a compliant application is to be decided within two months. If not disposed of, it is referred to the next higher officer or State Government, which has a further two months. Deemed registration follows only if the higher authority also fails to dispose of the application within that further period.
6. **Certificate and classification — ss.10, 12:** certificate is conclusive evidence of registration unless cancelled; the Registrar classifies societies.
7. **Bye-laws — ss.13–14:** bye-laws and amendments must comply with the Act and Rules; amendments follow the statutory adoption and registration process.

## Membership

Sections 22–24 identify eligible persons and membership categories. Open membership under section 23 prevents refusal without sufficient cause to a duly qualified applicant and provides for appeal to the Registrar. The section also contains a procedure for deemed membership in specified non-response circumstances. Nominal/sympathizer members do not acquire a share in the society's profits or assets solely by that status. Associate rights depend on the Act and bye-laws.

Under section 25, membership may cease through accepted resignation, transfer of the whole share or interest, death, removal or expulsion, or dissolution of a corporate member. Section 26 requires the prescribed payment or interest before membership rights are exercised. Section 27 generally gives each member one vote, subject to statutory exceptions and qualifications. Section 32 provides inspection and copy rights for specified records. Expulsion under section 35 requires compliance with its grounds and procedure, including an opportunity to be heard and the necessary approval.

## Conclusion

Registration is a public-law gateway to corporate status; membership is regulated by statutory openness balanced with legitimate eligibility rules and responsible participation. Promoters and members must follow the Act, Rules and registered bye-laws together.

**Authorities:** MCS Act, 1960, ss.4–14, 22–27, 32, 35–36.
