# Q1. Explain State assistance to cooperative societies and the role of apex institutions.

The State supports cooperative societies through legislation, registration and supervision, direct or indirect capital participation, loans, guarantees, subsidies, training and development schemes. Under the MCS Act, State partnership and related funds are addressed principally in Chapter VI, sections 50 onwards. Aid can permit societies to establish services and infrastructure that small members could not finance alone.

Apex societies operate at a State-wide level to coordinate affiliated societies and provide services such as finance, marketing or technical support. Federal societies bring cooperative societies together as members. The National Co-operative Development Corporation (NCDC), under the NCDC Act, 1962, promotes and finances cooperative programmes. NABARD, established under the NABARD Act, 1981, supports rural development and eligible cooperative credit institutions through refinance and institutional development.

Assistance must be balanced with autonomy. If State support becomes administrative control, member ownership and democratic accountability may weaken. Sound governance, transparent conditions, professional management and audit help ensure that aid strengthens cooperative self-help.

**Conclusion:** State aid and apex institutions can expand cooperative capacity, but members must remain the foundation of the society's purpose and control.
