# Condensed Revision Notes: Modules 1–3

## Module 1 — Theory and principles

- Cooperative = voluntary association meeting common needs through joint ownership and democratic control.
- ICA principles: voluntary/open membership; democratic control; member economic participation; autonomy; education; cooperation among cooperatives; concern for community.
- Core features: service purpose, member participation, democratic governance, mutual responsibility and regulated autonomy.
- MCS Act: societies may be registered under s.4; limited/unlimited liability under s.5; body corporate effect under s.36; voting rule and exceptions under s.27.
- Compare cooperative with company (capital-linked), partnership (agreement/partners), trust (fiduciary purpose).
- Types: credit, producer/farmer/dairy, consumer, housing, marketing/processing, service.
- Risks: weak participation, undercapitalisation, politicisation, poor controls; safeguards include audit, training and transparent elections.

## Module 2 — History and Constitution

- 1904: Co-operative Credit Societies Act; 1912: broader societies/federations.
- 1919: cooperation transferred to provincial field; Bombay legislation followed.
- MCS Act 1960: Maharashtra's principal State framework; Rules 1961.
- State support: legislation, capital, loans, guarantees, subsidies, training and apex institutions.
- NCDC: statutory development and financing support to cooperatives; NABARD: rural development/refinance and institutional support.
- Article 19(1)(c): freedom to form cooperatives, subject to Art.19(4).
- Article 43B: Directive Principle for voluntary formation, autonomy, democratic control and professional management.
- 97th Amendment inserted Part IXB; *Rajendra N. Shah* held it invalid as applied to State cooperatives without State ratification; operative for multi-State societies and Union Territories.

## Module 3 — Registration and membership

- Ss.4–6: objects, liability and registration conditions. General rule: 10 persons from different families; statutory exceptions apply.
- S.8: prescribed application, four copies of proposed bye-laws, fee and information.
- S.9: Registrar's two-month period, then referral to higher authority for a further two months; deemed registration only after failure at the second stage.
- S.10: certificate is conclusive evidence unless registration cancelled. S.12: Registrar classifies societies.
- Ss.13–14: bye-law amendments must follow prescribed procedure and registration; bye-laws cannot override Act/Rules.
- Ss.15–21A: name, liability, amalgamation/transfer/division/conversion, reorganisation, reconstruction, deregistration.
- Ss.22–26: eligibility, open membership, nominal/associate/sympathizer membership, cessation and payment prerequisites.
- S.27: generally one member, one vote, with exceptions and qualifications.
- S.32: member inspection/copy rights. S.35: expulsion requires statutory process, hearing and approval.
- Don't conflate associate/active status; verify exact wording and bye-laws.
