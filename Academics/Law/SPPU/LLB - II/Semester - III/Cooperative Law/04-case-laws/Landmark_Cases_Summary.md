# Landmark Cases — Cooperative Law

## 1. *Union of India v. Rajendra N. Shah*

**Citation:** (2021) 9 SCC 1; 2021 SCC OnLine SC 474  
**Issue:** Whether Part IXB, inserted by the 97th Constitutional Amendment, was validly applied to State cooperative societies without ratification by half the State legislatures.  
**Held:** The Supreme Court held that the amendment affected the federal distribution of legislative power in relation to State cooperatives and required ratification under Article 368(2). Part IXB was invalid insofar as it applied to State cooperative societies. Its application to multi-State cooperatives and Union Territories was upheld.  
**Use in exam:** Essential authority for the current constitutional position; do not apply Part IXB's board/election provisions as controlling constitutional rules for Maharashtra State societies.

## 2. *Daman Singh v. State of Punjab*

**Citation:** (1985) 2 SCC 670.  
**Principle:** A member who joins a cooperative society is subject to the statute, rules and bye-laws governing the corporate body. The case is cited on the legal character of cooperative membership and statutory regulation.  
**Use in exam:** Explain that membership rights operate within the cooperative's statutory framework.

## 3. *Zoroastrian Co-operative Housing Society Ltd. v. District Registrar*

**Citation:** (2005) 5 SCC 632.  
**Principle:** Examined membership restrictions in a cooperative housing society's registered bye-laws and the interaction between freedom of association, bye-laws and public policy.  
**Use in exam:** Open membership and housing-society eligibility; pair with the current Act and constitutional developments.

## 4. *Thalappalam Service Co-operative Bank Ltd. v. State of Kerala*

**Citation:** (2013) 16 SCC 82.  
**Principle:** Considered when a cooperative is “substantially financed” or controlled by government for purposes of the Right to Information Act. Government regulation alone does not automatically make a cooperative a public authority; statutory tests must be met.  
**Use in exam:** Distinguish regulatory supervision from State ownership/control while discussing autonomy.
