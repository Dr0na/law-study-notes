# Module 6: Finance, Accounts and Audit

## 1. State aid and apex societies

The MCS Act provides for State partnership in cooperative societies, including direct and indirect participation, and related funds and arrangements (Chapter VI, ss.50 onwards). Other assistance may include loans, guarantees, subsidies, technical support and schemes under applicable law and policy. State aid can support societies that cannot raise adequate member capital, but it must not replace member participation or sound financial controls.

Apex societies operate at a State-wide or higher level to coordinate, finance or support affiliated societies. Federal societies bring together cooperative societies as members. Their exact legal status and functions depend on classification, registered objects and the applicable statute.

## 2. Funds and their use

The Act regulates share capital, deposits, borrowing, loans, surplus and reserves. Sections 43–45 regulate borrowing, loan policy and certain transactions with non-members. Sections 64–70 govern funds, profit appropriation, reserve and education/public-purpose contributions and investment.

- **Funds not divided (s.64):** society funds are not freely divisible among members as private property.
- **Appropriation of profits (s.65):** profits are calculated and appropriated according to the Act, Rules and prescribed order.
- **Reserve Fund (s.66):** the society must make the statutory reserve appropriation. The amount and exceptions must be checked against the current Act and rules; do not rely on a secondary guide's blanket “25%” without checking the applicable provision and society class.
- **Dividend (s.56 in the supplied Act text):** the Act restricts the amount of dividend. Some study guides cite a different section number or fixed cap; use the supplied current text and relevant amendments.
- **Education and public purposes (ss.68–69):** statutory contributions may be required in the manner prescribed.
- **Investment (s.70):** funds are invested only through the permitted statutory channels and subject to rules.

## 3. Accounts, registers and member access

Accurate books allow members and regulators to assess solvency, transactions and compliance. Societies must maintain accounts and statutory registers prescribed by the Act, Rules and bye-laws. Section 32 entitles members to inspect specified records at the society's office and obtain copies on written request and payment of prescribed fees. Section 38 requires a register of members. Records should be complete, current and traceable to supporting vouchers and resolutions.

Typical records include the member register, share register, cash book, ledger, minutes of general body and committee meetings, loan/deposit records, asset register, annual financial statements, audit reports and statutory returns. The precise list depends on the Rules and class of society.

## 4. Audit — s.81

Every society is subject to audit under section 81 by an auditor eligible under the Act and Rules, selected/appointed through the statutory process. Audit assesses whether accounts present a proper record of the society's transactions and financial position, and whether legal and bye-law requirements have been followed. The auditor reports irregularities, deficiencies, losses, overdue debts, fraud indicators and compliance issues as required by law.

An audit is not merely arithmetic checking: it is a member-protection and governance mechanism. However, audit does not replace internal controls or committee responsibility.

## 5. Rectification, inquiry and inspection

- **Rectification of defects (s.82):** the society and responsible officers must address defects noted in audit and submit the required rectification report within the statutory period.
- **Inquiry (s.83):** the Registrar may inquire into the constitution, working and financial condition of a society, on own motion or on a qualifying application.
- **Inspection (s.84):** the Registrar may inspect books of an indebted society on the application of a creditor, subject to the section's conditions.
- **Costs (ss.85–87):** the Act allocates inquiry/inspection costs and requires identified defects to be communicated to the society.
- **Surcharge (s.88):** where the statutory process establishes misapplication, retention, misfeasance or breach of trust, the Registrar may assess damages against responsible persons after the prescribed notice and hearing.

Audit, inquiry and inspection are related but distinct. Audit is periodic statutory examination; inquiry examines constitution, working or financial condition; inspection is a targeted examination under statutory triggers.

## 6. Why sound finance matters

Sound financial systems preserve member funds, support service delivery and sustain autonomy. Effective safeguards include separation of duties, dual authorisation, reconciliations, procurement controls, member access to records, timely audit, prompt rectification, conflict-of-interest disclosures and clear accountability for committee decisions.

## Exam answer frame

For audit and inspection, define each mechanism, state its statutory trigger and responsible authority, explain reporting and rectification, and conclude with member protection and accountability. For funds, proceed from borrowing and lending controls to profit appropriation, reserves, dividend, contributions and investment.
