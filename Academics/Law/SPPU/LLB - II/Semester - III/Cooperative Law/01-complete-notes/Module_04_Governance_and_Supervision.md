# Module 4: Incorporation, Duties, Privileges and Governance

## 1. Effect of incorporation — ss. 36–40

On registration, section 36 makes the society a body corporate by its registered name, with perpetual succession and a common seal. It may hold and dispose of property, enter contracts and sue or be sued. This separate legal personality allows the society to continue despite changes in membership. The Act also provides for the society's address, member register, inspection and evidentiary use of records (ss.37–40).

## 2. Rights, privileges, duties and restrictions of the society

The Act grants societies legal capacity and cooperative-specific protections, including rules on share interests, transactions, recovery and member records. It also restricts borrowing and lending, dealings with non-members, investment and use of funds, and requires statutory accounts, audit and returns. A society's powers arise from the Act, Rules and registered bye-laws; a bye-law cannot legalise an act prohibited by statute.

## 3. General body — s.72

Subject to the Act, Rules and bye-laws, the general body is the final authority of the society. It exercises this authority through properly convened meetings and decisions within its legal competence. The general body cannot dispense with statutory requirements or retrospectively validate an unlawful act simply by passing a resolution.

### Annual and special general meetings — ss.75–76

- **AGM (s.75):** must be held within the period specified by the Act after the close of the cooperative year. It considers the annual report, audited financial statements, audit rectification, appropriation of profits, committee matters, budget and other business. The precise date and extension provisions should be checked in the current text.
- **SGM (s.76):** may be called by the committee/authorised office holders or on a valid requisition by the prescribed fraction of members, and can be convened by the Registrar where the statutory conditions are met.
- **Conduct:** notice, agenda, quorum, chairing, voting, minutes and adjournment must follow the Act, Rules and bye-laws. A meeting decision is vulnerable if convened or conducted contrary to mandatory requirements.

## 4. Managing committee — s.73 and connected provisions

The committee manages the society's affairs subject to the Act, Rules, bye-laws and general body decisions. It is responsible for implementation, custody and prudent use of funds, keeping records, convening meetings, statutory filings and member service. Committee members must act within authority and for proper purposes; statutory accountability may include removal, disqualification, surcharge or other consequences.

Committee size, term, election method, reservations and eligibility are governed by the applicable current State law and Rules. Constitutional Part IXB provisions concerning board size, tenure, reservation and elections were held inapplicable to State cooperatives in *Rajendra N. Shah* for want of State ratification; use the MCS Act and current State rules for Maharashtra societies. Do not rely on a generic summary that assigns every society a uniform five-year term or one fixed reservation structure.

## 5. Election, supervision and no-confidence

The MCS Act provides for supervision of elections, including a State Co-operative Election Authority and statutory election procedures for specified societies. The applicable chapter, rules, class of society and electoral category determine the procedure. Disputes concerning elections are governed by the statutory forum and limitation rules; they should not be treated as ordinary internal complaints.

The Act also provides for no-confidence against specified officers, and for appointment, removal or disqualification of committee members. The correct resolution threshold and procedure depend on the section and class of society concerned.

## 6. Registrar's supervisory powers

The Registrar's powers include registration and classification; approval or registration of bye-law amendments; inquiry (s.83); inspection of books in specified circumstances (s.84); audit oversight (ss.81–82); enforcement of statutory obligations; seizure of records where authorised (s.80); assessment of damages/surcharge (s.88); and remedial action against committees or officers under provisions such as ss.77A, 78 and 78A where applicable. These powers are statutory, not unlimited administrative discretion: identify the enabling section, trigger, notice/hearing requirements and appeal/revision remedy.

An inquiry under section 83 may examine the constitution, working and financial condition of a society. It may be initiated by the Registrar or on a qualifying application. Inspection under section 84 is a separate mechanism and has its own statutory conditions. Do not conflate audit, inquiry and inspection.

## 7. Practical governance checklist

1. Confirm which body has authority: general body, committee, Registrar or election authority.
2. Identify the relevant statutory provision and registered bye-law.
3. Check notice, quorum, voting eligibility and minutes.
4. Give affected persons any hearing or notice required by law.
5. Maintain records and disclose required information to members.
6. Identify the appeal, revision or dispute forum and its limitation period.
