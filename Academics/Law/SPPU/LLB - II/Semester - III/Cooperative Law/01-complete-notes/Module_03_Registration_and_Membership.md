# Module 3: Preliminary Provisions, Registration and Membership

## 1. Preliminary framework

The Maharashtra Co-operative Societies Act, 1960 is the principal State statute for cooperative societies in Maharashtra. Section 2 supplies defined terms; section 3 provides for the Registrar and officers assisting the Registrar. Section 4 identifies societies that may be registered: societies promoting members' economic interests or general welfare, or public welfare, in accordance with cooperative principles, and societies facilitating their operations. Registration may be refused where the proposal is economically unsound, adverse to cooperative development or contrary to State policy directives.

## 2. Liability and conditions of registration

### Limited and unlimited liability — ss. 5–6

A society may be registered with limited or unlimited liability. The form must be reflected in its registered name. The general threshold in section 6 is at least ten persons, each from a different family, qualified under the Act and residing in the area of operation; the Registrar may prescribe a higher number for a class. The Act contains specific exceptions, including lift-irrigation societies with five or more persons. A society with unlimited liability must satisfy the same-town/village/group-of-villages condition. A federal society requires at least five societies as members. Always check the exact class-specific rule before stating a threshold.

### Application and decision — ss. 8–10

Promoters apply to the Registrar in the prescribed form, with four copies of the proposed bye-laws and the prescribed fee. The signatures and authorisations depend on whether the applicant is an ordinary or federal society. The Registrar examines compliance with the Act, Rules, other applicable law and policy directives and whether the bye-laws conflict with law.

Under section 9, the Registrar is to register a compliant society and its bye-laws within two months. If the application is not disposed of within that period, it is referred to the next higher officer (or the State Government where applicable), who has a further two months. Only failure at that stage results in deemed registration; a certificate then follows. The supplied study guides sometimes compress this into an automatic two-month deemed registration, which does not reflect the full sequence in the supplied Act text.

The registration certificate is conclusive evidence of registration unless registration is cancelled (s.10). The Registrar classifies the society under section 12; classification can be altered for recorded reasons.

### Bye-laws — ss. 13–14

Bye-laws regulate internal organisation, membership, services, governance and procedures. They are subordinate to the Act and Rules. An amendment passed in the manner prescribed by the Act and Rules is not valid until registered; the Act has a referral and deemed-registration sequence where the Registrar does not decide within the stated time. A bye-law cannot override legislation. The Registrar may direct an amendment considered necessary or desirable in the society's interest, after the statutory process and opportunity of hearing.

## 3. Structural changes — ss. 15–21A

- **Change of name (s.15):** general body resolution and Registrar approval; existing rights and proceedings continue.
- **Change of liability (s.16):** amendment of bye-laws and notice to members and creditors; affected persons have the statutory option to withdraw shares, deposits or loans before the change takes effect.
- **Amalgamation, transfer, division or conversion (s.17):** requires the statutory resolution, notice, Registrar approval and safeguards for members and creditors.
- **Reorganisation in public interest (s.18):** the Registrar may direct amalgamation, division or reorganisation in the circumstances and through the process specified in the Act, including consideration of objections.
- **Reconstruction (s.19):** statutory reconstruction mechanisms may be used where the society can be revived.
- **Deregistration (s.21A):** distinct from winding up. It concerns cancellation of registration on statutory grounds and has consequences for the society's assets and liabilities. Do not use “deregistration” and “liquidation” as synonyms.

## 4. Who may become a member — ss. 22–26

Subject to the Act, Rules and bye-laws, eligible members may include individuals competent to contract, firms, companies, other societies, local authorities and public trusts. The exact eligibility depends on the society's class and bye-laws.

- **Ordinary member:** admitted under the Act and bye-laws with membership rights and responsibilities.
- **Nominal or sympathizer member (s.24):** may be admitted on terms permitted by law/bye-laws; does not acquire a share in profits or assets by that status and ordinarily lacks member privileges.
- **Associate member:** rights and liabilities depend on the Act and bye-laws; do not assume all associates can vote or contest.
- **Active member:** the Act contains a participation/use concept relevant to specified rights. Check the current definition and the applicable bye-laws rather than importing descriptions from another State's statute.

Open membership under section 23 prohibits refusal without sufficient cause to a duly qualified applicant. Refusal can be appealed to the Registrar. The section also contains a route where an eligible person's application or payment is refused at the society level and rules for deemed membership if no decision is communicated within the specified period. The wording and timelines should be stated precisely from the current Act.

Membership ceases through accepted resignation, transfer of the whole share or interest, death, removal or expulsion, or dissolution of a corporate member, as applicable (s.25). Section 26 makes exercise of membership rights conditional on required payment or acquisition of the prescribed interest.

## 5. Rights, duties, privileges, disabilities and liabilities

### Rights and privileges

- vote subject to section 27 and eligibility restrictions;
- inspect specified records and obtain copies under section 32;
- participate in general meetings and seek accountability through the statutory governance process;
- transfer an interest only subject to section 29 and applicable bye-laws;
- receive the benefits and services for which the society exists, consistently with its rules.

### Duties and liabilities

- pay share capital, dues and other lawful contributions;
- comply with the Act, Rules, registered bye-laws and valid general body decisions;
- use services honestly and provide required information;
- participate responsibly in meetings and elections;
- bear liability according to the registered form, the Act and any statutory obligations.

Section 27 generally adopts one member, one vote, exercised personally, while the Act provides exceptions for certain corporate, institutional and federal members. It also contains qualifications and special restrictions, including a two-year voting ineligibility for an individual member from enrolment and a rule for certain agricultural credit defaulters. Avoid the simplified claim that every member immediately has an unconditional vote.

### Expulsion — s.35

Expulsion is a serious statutory measure for conduct detrimental to the society's interests or proper working. The statutory threshold, notice, opportunity to be heard, general body resolution and Registrar approval must all be followed. Because procedural details can be amended, quote the current section and Rules rather than memorising a threshold from a secondary guide.

## 6. Exam answer frame

For registration, structure the answer as: objects (s.4), liability and eligibility (ss.5–6), application (s.8), scrutiny and decision/deemed registration (s.9), certificate (s.10), classification (s.12), bye-laws (ss.13–14), then a brief note on structural changes. For membership, separate admission, categories, rights/duties, cessation and expulsion.
